Theodosian Code

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only complete English translation is by Clyde Pharr, in collaboration with Theresa Sherrer Davidson and Mary Brown Pharr, The Theodosian Code and Novels and the Sirmondian Constitutions: A Translation with Commentary, Glossary, and Bibliography (Corpus of Roman Law 1; Princeton University Press, 1952), with an introduction by C. Dickerman Williams; it was reprinted by The Lawbook Exchange (2001; ISBN 978-1-58477-146-3) and is available from that publisher. It is a complete translation, covering all sixteen books of the Code, the later laws (“Novels”) issued after it by Theodosius II and his successors, and the Sirmondian Constitutions. The Codex Theodosianus is the official collection of the general laws of the emperors from Constantine onward, compiled by a commission appointed by Theodosius II and promulgated in 438; each law is given in extract under a subject heading, with the names of the emperors, the official addressed, and the date. Its last book deals with religion: the Catholic faith, churches, bishops and clergy, heretics, Jews and Samaritans, apostates, and pagan sacrifices and temples, and it records step by step how Christianity was first privileged, then made the religion of the state, while the other cults were restricted and finally forbidden under ever heavier penalties. The Sirmondian Constitutions are a further set of sixteen laws, mostly on church matters, first published by Jacques Sirmond in 1631.
