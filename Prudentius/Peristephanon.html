Peristephanon

No public-domain English translation of this work is available online. The complete translation by Sister M. Clement Eagan, C.C.V.I., in The Poems of Prudentius, vol. 1 (The Fathers of the Church 43; Catholic University of America Press, 1962; ISBN 978-0-8132-0043-9), where it follows the Cathemerinon, appears to be in the public domain in the United States, since its copyright was not renewed; but it has not been made openly available online, and the copies on the Internet Archive can only be borrowed. The publisher still sells it as Poems, Volume 1 (ebook ISBN 978-0-8132-1143-5; paperback ISBN 978-0-8132-2762-7), available from the publisher. A complete translation by H. J. Thomson, with the Latin text on facing pages, is in vol. 2 of the Loeb Prudentius (Loeb Classical Library 398; London: Heinemann; Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1953), pp. 98–345, which can be read free online in the University of Toronto’s scan on the Internet Archive; its licence is not stated. An earlier complete translation, in Sister Mary Ellen Field’s thesis The Peristephanon of Prudentius: A Translation and Word Study (M.A., Boston College, 1937), was never published. The Peristephanon (“On the Crowns”) is a collection of fourteen lyric poems in a variety of metres in honour of martyrs, chiefly of Spain and Rome: Emeterius and Chelidonius of Calahorra, Lawrence, Eulalia of Mérida, the Eighteen Martyrs of Saragossa, Vincent, Fructuosus and his deacons at Tarragona, Quirinus of Siscia, Cassian of Imola, Romanus of Antioch, Hippolytus, Peter and Paul, Cyprian and Agnes, with a short poem on a baptistery built where martyrs suffered at Calahorra. The longest, the tenth hymn on Romanus, contains the well-known description of the taurobolium, the pagan rite of bathing in a bull’s blood (X.1011–1050). Other English translations: Len Krisak, Prudentius’ Crown of Martyrs: Liber Peristephanon, with introduction and notes by Joseph Pucci (Routledge Later Latin Poetry; Routledge, 2020; ISBN 978-0-8153-5327-0), available from the publisher; the taurobolium passage (X.1011–1050) is translated by C. K. Barrett in The New Testament Background: Selected Documents (London: SPCK, 1956), pp. 96–97. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 28 April 2009, who quotes Barrett’s version of the taurobolium passage, and 29 April 2009, on his unsuccessful request for a copy of Field’s thesis.)
