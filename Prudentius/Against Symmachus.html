Against Symmachus

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by H. J. Thomson, with the Latin text on facing pages, under the title “A Reply to the Address of Symmachus”, is in the Loeb Prudentius (2 vols; London: Heinemann; Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press): Book 1 in vol. 1 (Loeb Classical Library 387, 1949), pp. 344–401, and Book 2 in vol. 2 (Loeb Classical Library 398, 1953), pp. 2–97. Both volumes can be read free online in the University of Toronto’s scans on the Internet Archive: vol. 1 and vol. 2. Their licence is not stated. Prudentius wrote the two books of the Contra Symmachum in hexameters around 402–403, in reply to the plea (relatio) that the pagan senator Quintus Aurelius Symmachus had addressed to the emperor Valentinian II in 384 for the restoration of the Altar of Victory to the senate house. Book 1, opening with a preface on Paul’s shipwreck and the viper on Malta, attacks the pagan gods and their cults and praises Theodosius for leading the senate to Christ; Book 2, opening with a preface on Peter walking on the water, answers the arguments of Symmachus’s address one by one and closes with an appeal to the emperor Honorius to abolish the gladiatorial games. Other English translations: Sister M. Clement Eagan, in The Poems of Prudentius, vol. 2 (The Fathers of the Church 52; Catholic University of America Press, 1965; reissued as Poems, Volume 2, ISBN 978-0-8132-1872-4), available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 15 August 2015, who quotes Thomson’s version of the closing appeal against the gladiatorial games.)
