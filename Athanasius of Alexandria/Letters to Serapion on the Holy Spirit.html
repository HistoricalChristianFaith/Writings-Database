Letters to Serapion on the Holy Spirit

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by C. R. B. Shapland, The Letters of Saint Athanasius concerning the Holy Spirit, translated with introduction and notes (London: Epworth Press, 1951; also issued in New York by the Philosophical Library), is available free as a scan at archive.org, uploaded by Roger Pearse from a library copy. The uploader describes it as public domain because the US copyright was not renewed, but the scanned copy is the London edition (“All rights reserved”), so its licence is not established and we do not host it. Athanasius wrote these letters, probably around 359–361 during his third exile, to Bishop Serapion of Thmuis, who had told him of Christians in Egypt (Athanasius calls them “Tropici”, from their figurative reading of Scripture) who accepted the Son’s divinity but held the Holy Spirit to be a creature, one of the ministering angels. Athanasius answers them from Scripture, arguing that the Spirit belongs to the one undivided Trinity and is no creature. Shapland keeps the traditional numbering of four letters but prints the second and third together as a single letter. Other English translations: Mark DelCogliano, Andrew Radde-Gallwitz and Lewis Ayres, Works on the Spirit: Athanasius the Great and Didymus the Blind, Popular Patristics Series 43 (St Vladimir’s Seminary Press, 2011; ISBN 978-0-88141-379-3), complete. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 29 April 2010, and 22 May 2010.)
