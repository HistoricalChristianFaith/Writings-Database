Chronographia

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete chronicle was translated by Cyril Mango and Roger Scott, with the assistance of Geoffrey Greatrex, as The Chronicle of Theophanes Confessor: Byzantine and Near Eastern History, AD 284–813 (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1997; ISBN 978-0-19-822568-3), from de Boor's Greek text, with an introduction and commentary; it is available from the publisher. An earlier and partial translation by Harry Turtledove, The Chronicle of Theophanes: Anni Mundi 6095–6305 (A.D. 602–813) (University of Pennsylvania Press, 1982; ISBN 978-0-8122-1128-3), covers only the years from 602 onward; Mango describes it as “highly inaccurate”. The Chronographia was written in Greek in the early ninth century by Theophanes (d. c. 818), a Byzantine monk and abbot in Bithynia who was imprisoned and exiled for opposing Iconoclasm. It continues the world chronicle of George Syncellus and sets out events year by year, from the accession of Diocletian in 284 to 813. It is a principal source for Byzantine history in the seventh and eighth centuries, the so-called dark age of Byzantium. Through an Eastern source, it also follows the rise of Islam and the reigns of the caliphs, and it includes an early Greek account of the life of Muhammad.
