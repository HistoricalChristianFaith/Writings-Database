Catena on Mark

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first extended English translation, by William R. S. Lamb, is The Catena in Marcum: A Byzantine Anthology of Early Commentary on Mark (Texts and Editions for New Testament Study 6; Brill, Leiden, 2012; ISBN 978-90-04-22431-5), which translates the whole of the catena as printed by J. A. Cramer, with a long introduction on the manuscripts, the question of authorship and the history of scholarship; it is available from the publisher. The catena on Mark is the earliest anthology of patristic comment on Mark’s Gospel, put together around the end of the fifth century or the beginning of the sixth from extracts of earlier writers, above all John Chrysostom, joined into a running commentary and transmitted in the margins of Greek Gospel manuscripts. Cramer printed the Greek text in 1840, in the first volume of his Catenae Graecorum Patrum in Novum Testamentum, under the name of Cyril of Alexandria, which a few manuscripts give; it has more usually been ascribed to Victor of Antioch, a presbyter, though Lamb doubts that it was the work of any single compiler. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 November 2013.)
