The Ecclesiastical Chronicle

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first complete English translation is by David Wilmshurst, Bar Hebraeus: The Ecclesiastical Chronicle. An English Translation (Gorgias Eastern Christian Studies 40; Piscataway, NJ: Gorgias Press, 2016; ISBN 978-1-4632-0535-5), with the Syriac text on facing pages, a forty-page introduction, five maps, a bibliography and an index; it is available from the publisher. The Chronicon Ecclesiasticum is the church half of the history written by Gregory Bar Hebraeus (1226–1286), maphrian of the Syriac Orthodox Church, as a companion to his secular Chronography. Its first part follows the patriarchs of Antioch and the western, Jacobite church; its second turns east, to the catholicoi of the Church of the East and the Jacobite maphrians in Iraq and Persia. Bar Hebraeus carries the story down to his own day, and two later writers continue it to the end of the fifteenth century. Because it gives short notices of so many churchmen and their writings, it is a basic source for the history of Syriac literature; until Wilmshurst’s version it had been translated only into Latin (by J. B. Abbeloos and T. J. Lamy, 1872–1877). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 12 March 2016; an earlier post of 23 June 2011 quotes Wilmshurst’s version of one short passage, on Hunain ibn Ishaq.)
