The Chronography

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, E. A. Wallis Budge’s The Chronography of Gregory Abû’l Faraj, the Son of Aaron, the Hebrew Physician, Commonly Known as Bar Hebraeus (2 vols., London: Oxford University Press, 1932; reprinted with the Syriac by Gorgias Press, 2003), is offered free online by Robert Bedrosian, who presents it chapter by chapter in HTML on his site at rbedrosian.com (also preserved by the Wayback Machine) and as a searchable PDF at the Internet Archive, “solely for non-commercial educational/research purposes”; its licence is not stated. The Chronography (Chronicon Syriacum) is the secular half of the history written by Gregory Bar Hebraeus (1226–1286), maphrian of the Syriac Orthodox Church. It runs from the Creation to the author’s death, arranged by the succession of dynasties: the patriarchs and judges of Israel, the Hebrew, Chaldean, Median and Persian kings, the Greek and Roman rulers, the Arab caliphs, the Seljuks and, at greatest length, the Mongols, under whom Bar Hebraeus himself lived; the last ten years were added after his death, probably by his brother Bar Sauma. Bedrosian’s pages follow the pagination of Paul Bedjan’s Syriac text, which Budge gives in brackets. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 November 2008.)
