Scholia on the Old Testament

The text of this work is not yet hosted here. A public-domain English translation of its first part is Martin Sprengling and William Creighton Graham, Barhebraeus’ Scholia on the Old Testament. Part I: Genesis–II Samuel (Oriental Institute Publications 13; Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1931). It is free to read and download from the Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures (formerly the Oriental Institute) and at the Internet Archive. The volume was published in the United States in 1931 and its copyright was not renewed, so it is in the public domain there. It reproduces in facsimile the oldest manuscript of the work, a Florentine copy made in 1278, during the author’s lifetime, by his disciple John of Sarw. It gives a complete English translation of the scholia on Genesis to II Samuel, with a collation of the other manuscripts and notes. Bar Hebraeus (1226–1286), maphrian of the Syriac Orthodox Church, finished his Storehouse of Mysteries (Auṣar Rāzē) in about 1272/3. It is a set of short notes on the whole Bible, verse by verse, more philological than theological: it explains hard words and grammar, notes how the Syriac (Peshitta) reading differs from the Greek, Hebrew and other versions, and adds brief comments on the meaning. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 7 December 2012.)
