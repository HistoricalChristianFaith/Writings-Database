Books of Jeu

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only complete English translation, by Violet MacDermot, is in The Books of Jeu and the Untitled Text in the Bruce Codex (Nag Hammadi Studies 13; Brill, Leiden, 1978; ISBN 978-90-04-05754-8; reissued as an e-book in 2020, ISBN 978-90-04-43886-6), which prints Carl Schmidt’s Coptic text with MacDermot’s translation and notes; it is available from the publisher. It covers both Books of Jeu in full, together with the Untitled Text that shares the codex. The Books of Jeu are two Coptic Gnostic treatises, probably composed in the first half of the third century, preserved in the Bruce Codex (Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Bruce 96), which the Scottish traveller James Bruce bought in Egypt around 1769. The name is modern: Schmidt took it from a reference in the Pistis Sophia to “the two books of Jeu”, while the only title in the manuscript is The Book of the Great Logos corresponding to Mysteries. In them Jesus teaches his disciples the emanations of the treasuries of light, illustrated with diagrams and secret names, and gives them the baptisms, seals and passwords by which the soul passes the archons on its ascent. Schmidt’s Coptic text, with a German translation, appeared in 1892, and his German translation again in Koptisch-gnostische Schriften I (1905).
