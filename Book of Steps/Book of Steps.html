Book of Steps

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Robert A. Kitchen and Maartien F. G. Parmentier as The Book of Steps: The Syriac Liber Graduum (Cistercian Studies 196; Cistercian Publications, Kalamazoo, 2004; ISBN 978-0-87907-696-2), with an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher (now distributed by Liturgical Press). The Book of Steps is an anonymous collection of thirty Syriac discourses (memre), probably written in the late fourth or early fifth century, by a teacher addressing a Christian community that he divides into two grades: the "Upright", who live in the world, marry, and practise the active works of love, and the "Perfect", who renounce property and family for a life of prayer, fasting and teaching. Its author writes about the real tensions of that community and keeps urging his hearers on from the one step to the other. The work has no Syriac title; the Latin name Liber Graduum was given to it by Michael Kmosko, whose edition of the Syriac text with a Latin translation appeared in the Patrologia Syriaca (vol. 3, 1926). It was long suspected of Messalianism, though that charge is now usually doubted. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 August 2011.)
