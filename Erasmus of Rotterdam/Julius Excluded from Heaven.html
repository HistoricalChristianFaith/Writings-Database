Julius Excluded from Heaven

No modern public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete modern English translation, unattributed on the site but apparently that of Robert M. Adams (in Erasmus, The Praise of Folly and Other Writings, Norton Critical Edition, 1989; ISBN 978-0-393-95749-5), was posted free on the Theology Network website of UCCF: The Christian Unions; the site has disappeared, but the page is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. Its licence is not stated. An early public-domain English version exists: an anonymous Tudor translation printed in London by John Byddell in 1535 (STC 14842), The dyaloge bytwene Iullius the seconde, Genius, and saynt Peter, whose transcription by the Text Creation Partnership (EEBO-TCP A04714) is freely available under a CC0 public-domain dedication. The Julius exclusus e coelis is an anonymous Latin satire written about 1514, soon after the death of Pope Julius II (1513), and printed in 1517; it is traditionally attributed to Erasmus, who denied writing it. In it Julius, arriving at the gate of heaven with his Genius and his army of dead soldiers, tries to open it with the key to his money-chest; Peter questions him through a grating, and Julius proudly recounts his career as nephew of Sixtus IV, his sale of offices, his wars against Bologna, Venice, Ferrara and the French, and his defeat of a council summoned against him, until Peter refuses to admit him and Julius resolves to raise an army and take heaven by storm. Other English translations: Paul Pascal, The Julius Exclusus of Erasmus, with introduction and notes by J. Kelley Sowards (Indiana University Press, 1968), on the Internet Archive for borrowing; Michael J. Heath, “Julius Excluded from Heaven: A Dialogue”, in Collected Works of Erasmus 27 (Literary and Educational Writings 5; University of Toronto Press, 1986; ISBN 978-0-8020-5602-3 for vols. 27–28), available from the publisher.
