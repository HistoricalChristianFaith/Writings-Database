Hymns against Julian

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the four hymns by Judith M. Lieu, with an introduction and notes by Samuel N. C. Lieu, is included in Samuel N. C. Lieu (ed.), The Emperor Julian: Panegyric and Polemic (Translated Texts for Historians 2; Liverpool University Press, 1986; 2nd edition 1989; ISBN 978-0-85323-376-3), an anthology that also contains the panegyric of Claudius Mamertinus and part of John Chrysostom’s discourse on St Babylas against Julian; it is available from the publisher. The hymns are also translated complete by Kathleen E. McVey in Ephrem the Syrian: Hymns (Classics of Western Spirituality; Paulist Press, 1989; ISBN 978-0-8091-3093-1), available from the publisher. Ephrem wrote these four Syriac hymns soon after the emperor Julian (“the Apostate”) was killed on his Persian campaign in 363 and the city of Nisibis, Ephrem’s home, was surrendered to Persia by his successor Jovian. They celebrate the end of the pagan emperor and his persecution, contrast him with the Christian Jovian, and read the disaster at Nisibis and the failure of Julian’s plan to rebuild the Temple at Jerusalem as God’s judgement on paganism and on the Jews who welcomed him. The Syriac text was edited by Edmund Beck (Corpus Scriptorum Christianorum Orientalium, 1957), with a German translation. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 August 2010.)
