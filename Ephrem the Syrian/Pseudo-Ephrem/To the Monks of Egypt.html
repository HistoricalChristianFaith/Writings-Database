To the Monks of Egypt

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first ten of the fifty Exhortations to the Monks of Egypt (Sermones paraenetici ad monachos Aegypti, CPG 3942), one of the Greek works transmitted under the name of St Ephrem, were translated from the Greek by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash, with notes citing the editions of Thwaites (Oxford, 1709), Assemani (Rome, 1743) and Phrantzolas (Thessaloniki, 1990), and published on his website, anastasis.org.uk; the site has disappeared, but his introductory note and his translation of Exhortations 1–10 are preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. The site marks all its texts and translations as the copyright of Archimandrite Ephrem; no licence for reuse is given. The collection was known to Photius (Bibliotheca 196); Lash judges that in their present form the exhortations are certainly not by Ephrem, and notes some internal evidence of an Egyptian origin. Addressed to monks living in coenobitic communities, and especially to novices, the ten exhortations translated urge them to keep the commandments, to stay in the community rather than leave it before they have been tested, to guard against pride if they came from a high station in life, to persevere in manual work and in obedience, to refrain from judging the failings of their brothers, and not to lose heart when troubled by thoughts, since the struggle is only beginning. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 November 2018, and again on 12 October 2023.)
