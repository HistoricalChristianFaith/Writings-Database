Three Short Discourses

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the three discourses by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash, with a short introduction and notes, was posted free on his website (anastasis.org.uk, last updated 2009) as part of his series of translations from the Greek works attributed to St Ephrem; the site has disappeared, but the Three Short Discourses page and his introduction to the Greek Ephrem are preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. The page carries a copyright notice in the translator’s name and no open licence. The discourses belong to the large body of Greek writings transmitted under the name of Ephrem the Syrian (“Ephraem Graecus”), most of them products of later Byzantine monasticism rather than translations of Ephrem’s Syriac. They are three short pieces of monastic instruction, On Love, On Psalmody and On Prayer: the first urges forgiveness of one’s brother as the light burden that the Lord asks in return for the pardon of our own sins, the second praises the singing of psalms at work, at home and on the road, against the songs and dances of the Devil, and the third to unceasing prayer, not only in church but at every hour. Lash notes that they belong with two further discourses, on repentance and on the coming judgement, that the first seems to echo a hymn of the Byzantine Sunday office, and that the style of the third at times recalls the Ladder of St John of Sinai. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 November 2018.)
