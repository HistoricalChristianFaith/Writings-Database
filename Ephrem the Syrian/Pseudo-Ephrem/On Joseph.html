On Joseph

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash, “Sermon on Joseph the Most Virtuous”, with an introduction on the editions and early translations and notes on the Greek, Hebrew and Syriac, was posted as a PDF on his website among his translations of the Greek works attributed to St Ephrem; the site has disappeared, but his “On Joseph” page and the PDF are preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. The site states that all texts and translations on it are copyright to Archimandrite Ephrem. The sermon (Sermo in pulcherrimum Ioseph, CPG 3938) is one of the Greek texts (“Ephraem Graecus”) that go under the name of Ephrem the Syrian but are probably not his; it is printed in Assemani’s edition (vol. 2, Rome, 1743) and in Phrantzolas’s (vol. 7, Thessaloniki, 1998). The first five hundred lines or so are in verse of seven and eight syllables, the rest apparently in prose. It retells the story of the patriarch Joseph from Genesis, with long laments by Jacob and by Joseph himself, from his brothers’ betrayal and his slavery in Egypt to his rise to power and his reunion with his father, and ends with a doxology. An early Latin version, preserved in a ninth-century Munich manuscript (Clm 3516), was edited by L. Bailly in Sacris Erudiri 21 (1972–73).
