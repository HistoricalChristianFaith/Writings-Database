Sermon in Heptasyllables

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of this short sermon, one of the Greek works transmitted under the name of St Ephrem, was made by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash (1997) from the Greek text of the modern Thessaloniki edition by K. G. Phrantzolas (vol. 2, pp. 32–33), and published on his website, anastasis.org.uk, as the “Sermon in Heptasyllablics”; the site has disappeared, but the page is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. The site marks all its texts and translations as the copyright of Archimandrite Ephrem; no licence for reuse is given. As Lash explains in his introduction, the piece follows the hymn On the Fathers who have finished their course and may not originally have been separate from it, since its opening speaks only of “his weakness”, that is, the devil’s. Written in the seven-syllable metre associated with Ephrem, the sermon urges the brethren, knowing the Enemy’s weakness, to follow the way the Fathers walked, to purify their hearts so as to draw down the grace of the Spirit, and to flee the evil one and take refuge in Christ, whose light blinds the devil, so as to reach the city God has prepared for those who love him. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 November 2018.)
