On the Passion

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash, “Discourse on the Passion of the Saviour”, made from the Greek text published by K. G. Phrantzolas (Thessaloniki, 1988), with a short introduction, was posted on his website among his translations of the Greek works attributed to St Ephrem; the site has disappeared, but his “On the Passion” page is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. The site states that all texts and translations on it are copyright to Archimandrite Ephrem. The sermon is one of the metrical texts in Greek (“Ephraem Graecus”) that go under the name of Ephrem the Syrian but are probably not his; it is written in seven-syllable lines, the so-called metre of St Ephrem, and Lash, who restored the metre in a few places where the Greek editor had emended the text, believed it had never before been translated into English. The preacher, afraid to speak of so fearful a subject, follows the Lord through his betrayal, mocking, scourging, crucifixion and burial, describes the Holy Spirit going forth in the form of a dove and rending the veil of the Temple at the moment of his death, and ends with a long litany of “Glory to you” and a prayer to be delivered at the Judgement. The Russian Typikon appoints a sermon of St Ephrem “On the Passion of the Saviour” to be read at Matins on Great Friday, which may be this one. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 November 2018.)
