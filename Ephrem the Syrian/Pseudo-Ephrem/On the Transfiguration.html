On the Transfiguration

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash of the Sermon on the Transfiguration of our Lord and God and Saviour, Jesus Christ, made from the Greek, with an introduction, section numbers of his own and explanatory notes, was published on his website; the site has disappeared, but the page is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. No licence is given: the page states that its texts and translations are copyright to the translator. Lash believed his version to be the first in English. The sermon is a Greek prose work transmitted under the name of Ephrem the Syrian (“Ephraem Graecus”), printed in the eighteenth-century editions of Thwaites and Assemani and in the modern Thessaloniki edition of K. G. Phrantzolas; it is not by Ephrem, and some manuscripts attribute it to Isaac of Antioch. It opens with a commentary on the Gospel account of the Transfiguration, including a series of paragraphs beginning “He led them up the mountain”, and then uses the Father’s witness from the cloud (Matt. 17:5) as the starting point for a long defence of the two natures of Christ in the language of the Council of Chalcedon (451), with a series of antitheses on the two natures; Lash concludes that the sermon in its present form cannot go back to the fourth century and gives the impression of being a compilation. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 November 2018.)
