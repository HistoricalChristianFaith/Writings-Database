55 Beatitudes

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the fifty-five Beatitudes by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash, with a short introduction, was posted free on his website (anastasis.org.uk, last updated 2009) as part of his series of translations from the Greek works attributed to St Ephrem; the site has disappeared, but the 55 Beatitudes page and his introduction to the Greek Ephrem are preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. The page carries a copyright notice in the translator’s name and no open licence. The work belongs to the large body of Greek writings transmitted under the name of Ephrem the Syrian (“Ephraem Graecus”), most of them products of later Byzantine monasticism rather than translations of Ephrem’s Syriac. It is a series of fifty-five short sayings, each beginning “Blessed the one who…”, in the manner of the sayings of the desert fathers: they touch on many sides of the monastic life, such as keeping to one’s cell, purity of thought, tears of compunction, humility and the remembrance of death, without any fixed order, and towards the end turn increasingly to death and the coming judgement; the last, spoken in the first person, leads into a prose sermon on repentance which follows it in the Greek and is not included. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 November 2018.)
