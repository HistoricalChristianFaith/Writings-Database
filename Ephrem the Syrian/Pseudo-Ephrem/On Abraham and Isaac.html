On Abraham and Isaac

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the poem by Archimandrite Ephrem Lash, made from Silvio Giuseppe Mercati’s critical edition and with an introduction, was posted free on his website (anastasis.org.uk, last updated 2008) as part of his series of translations from the Greek works attributed to St Ephrem; the site has disappeared, but the On Abraham and Isaac page and his introduction to the Greek Ephrem are preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. The page carries a copyright notice in the translator’s name and no open licence. The poem belongs to the large body of Greek writings transmitted under the name of Ephrem the Syrian (“Ephraem Graecus”); the same Greek text is also found among the spurious sermons of John Chrysostom. Written in four-line stanzas in the Syriac “metre of Mar Ephrem” (lines of seven plus seven syllables), with Sarah’s lament in shorter lines, it retells the sacrifice of Isaac in Genesis 22, reading Isaac’s birth and offering as types of Christ’s virgin birth and Passion, and describes the moment of the sacrifice as it was shown in paintings. Much of it is identical with a text of Gregory of Nyssa printed in the Patrologia Graeca (vol. 46, cols. 565–576); Lash marks the shared passages and inclines to think Gregory’s text the earlier, since it lacks the poem’s explicitly Christological stanzas. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 November 2018.)
