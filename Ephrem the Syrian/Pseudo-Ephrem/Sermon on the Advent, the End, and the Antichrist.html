Sermon on the Advent, the End, and the Antichrist

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Lee W. Brainard (2021), made from the Greek text in K. G. Phrantzolas’s edition (Ὁσίου Ἐφραίμ τοῦ Σύρου ἔργα, vol. 4, Thessaloniki, 1992, pp. 111–128), with the page numbers of that edition in the text, is posted free on his Soothkeep website as “Ephraim the Syrian — Sermon on the Advent, the End, and the Antichrist”. No licence is given for the translation; the site’s footer reserves all rights. The translator marks his own additions in italics and square brackets, gives alternative renderings in parentheses, and highlights the passages he reads as referring to the tribulation and the rapture. The work (CPG 3946, Sermo in adventum Domini, et de consummatione saeculi, et in adventum Antichristi) is a Greek sermon transmitted under the name of Ephrem the Syrian (“Ephraem Graecus”). The preacher, calling himself “Ephraim the least”, describes the coming of the Antichrist, the “shameless and terrible dragon” who deceives the world with lying signs, demands to be worshipped and seals his followers with his mark; the famine, drought and flight of the last days; the faithful who hide in the mountains and deserts and are preserved by God; and finally the coming of Christ from heaven, the binding of the tyrant and his demons, and the judgement. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 27 March 2023.)
