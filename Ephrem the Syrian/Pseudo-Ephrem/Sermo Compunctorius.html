Sermo Compunctorius

No public-domain English translation of this work is available online. According to Archimandrite Ephrem Lash (“The Greek Writings Attributed to Saint Ephrem the Syrian”, Abba: The Tradition of Orthodoxy in the West, 2003, p. 90), the only English translation is an anonymous pamphlet, A Serious Exhortation to Repentance and Sorrow for Sin, and a strict and mortified Life; written about the Middle of the Fourth Century by St. Ephraim, the Cyrian [sic], Deacon of Edessa. Translated into English from the Greek and Latin compared (London: W. Bowyer, 1731), a complete translation of some fifty pages in small quarto, probably made by the printer William Bowyer himself from Thwaites’s Oxford edition of the Greek (1709) compared with the Cologne edition of Gerard Vossius’s sixteenth-century Latin translation. It is in the public domain by date, but it is not available online: only seven copies are known, one of them in Liverpool University Library. The work (CPG 3908; in Assemani’s edition, vol. 1, pp. 28–40) is a penitential sermon of the Greek Ephrem corpus (“Ephraem Graecus”), transmitted under the name of Ephrem the Syrian, calling its hearers to repentance, sorrow for sin and a strict, self-denying life; Lash suspected that this was the earliest work under Ephrem’s name to be translated into English, and noted that John Wesley, who read “the exhortations of Ephraim Syrus” to his hearers in Georgia, seems to have known it. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 7 November 2018.)
