Rhythm against the Jews

A public-domain English translation of this work exists but has not yet been transcribed here. It is the “Rhythm against the Jews, delivered upon Palm Sunday”, translated from the Syriac of the Roman edition (Assemani, vol. iii, p. 209) by J. B. Morris, Select Works of S. Ephrem the Syrian, Translated out of the Original Syriac, with Notes and Indices (Library of the Fathers; Oxford: John Henry Parker, 1847), pp. 61–83, which can be read in the scan at archive.org. Public domain (published 1847). A festal sermon in verse for Palm Sunday, it invites the congregation to the feast of the King’s Son who comes to them, calls on heaven, earth and sea to join in the Hosannas, and prays for the healing and unity of the Church; it then marshals the witness of the prophets against the Jews’ rejection of Christ, compares Israel to the Lord’s vineyard laid waste, and ends with the glories given to the Church as the new Jerusalem. Morris’s notes discuss the early history of the Palm Sunday feast in the Syriac churches. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 July 2012, who observed that the rhythm does not seem to be available elsewhere and might profitably be placed online.)
