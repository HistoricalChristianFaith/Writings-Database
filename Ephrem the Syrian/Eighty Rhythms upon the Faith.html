Eighty Rhythms upon the Faith

A public-domain English translation of this work exists but has not yet been transcribed here. It is the “Rhythms of St. Ephrem the Syrian upon the Faith, against the Disputers”, eighty rhythms translated from the Syriac of the Roman edition (Assemani, vol. iii, p. 1) by J. B. Morris, Select Works of S. Ephrem the Syrian, Translated out of the Original Syriac, with Notes and Indices (Library of the Fathers; Oxford: John Henry Parker, 1847), pp. 106–361, with extensive notes, which can be read in the scan at archive.org. Public domain (published 1847). With the seven rhythms of The Pearl (hosted here as The Pearl: Seven Hymns on the Faith), they make up the collection known as Ephrem’s Hymns on Faith, eighty-seven hymns in Edmund Beck’s edition. Written against those who pry into and dispute about the nature of the Son and his generation from the Father, in the setting of the fourth-century Arian controversy, the hymns urge that God is to be approached by faith, praise and silence rather than by investigation, and they draw on the names, types and symbols of Scripture and of nature as the ways in which God makes himself known. Other English translations: Jeffrey T. Wickes, The Hymns on Faith, The Fathers of the Church 130 (Catholic University of America Press, 2015; ISBN 978-0-8132-2735-1), all eighty-seven hymns, translated from Edmund Beck’s critical edition, available from the publisher. (The Select Works volume was noted by Roger Pearse, 30 July 2012.)
