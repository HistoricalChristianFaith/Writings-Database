Three Rhythms concerning the Faith

A public-domain English translation of this work exists but has not yet been transcribed here. It is the “Three Rhythms concerning the Faith”, translated from the Syriac of the Roman edition (Assemani, vol. iii, p. 164) by J. B. Morris, Select Works of S. Ephrem the Syrian, Translated out of the Original Syriac, with Notes and Indices (Library of the Fathers; Oxford: John Henry Parker, 1847), from p. 362, which can be read in the scan at archive.org; in his notes Morris cites them as contra Scrutatores, to distinguish them from the rhythms adversus Scrutatores that precede them in the volume. Public domain (published 1847). Beginning with Ephrem’s wonder at the boldness of those who think they can comprehend the Creator, the three pieces argue that God is revealed through his Son and is known by simple faith rather than by disputation, and they range over the faith of Abraham, the differing laws God gave to the patriarchs, to Israel and to the Church, the distinct Persons and single will of the Trinity, and the harm done by divisions in the Church. (The Select Works volume was noted by Roger Pearse, 30 July 2012.)
