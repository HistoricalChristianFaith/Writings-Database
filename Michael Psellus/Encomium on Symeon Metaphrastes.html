Encomium on Symeon Metaphrastes

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete, annotated English translation by Elizabeth A. Fisher, made from her critical edition of the Greek in Michaelis Pselli Orationes hagiographicae (Teubner, 1994), is published free online by the Center for Hellenic Studies in her Michael Psellos, On Symeon the Metaphrast and On the Miracle at Blachernae: Annotated Translations with Introductions (2014), with an introduction to the encomium; the Center states that it is published under a Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 licence. The translation’s earlier address on the Center’s site, which Pearse cites, is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine (“Encomion of his Excellency Symeon the Metaphrast”). In this speech Michael Psellos (1018–c. 1078) praises Symeon Metaphrastes, the tenth-century official who compiled the standard Greek collection of saints’ lives arranged by the church calendar: he describes Symeon’s noble birth at Constantinople, his learning, and his career at court in the imperial chancery and in foreign affairs, and then defends his rewriting of the older lives, which kept their facts but replaced their rough or faulty style. A short passage, unique of its kind, describes how Symeon worked, at the request of an emperor, with a team of stenographers who took down his dictation, copyists who wrote it out in full, and correctors who checked the finished texts. Other English translations: Elizabeth A. Fisher, “Encomium for Kyr Symeon Metaphrastes”, in Charles Barber and Stratis Papaioannou (eds), Michael Psellos on Literature and Art: A Byzantine Perspective on Aesthetics (Michael Psellos in Translation; University of Notre Dame Press, 2017; ISBN 978-0-268-10049-0), pp. 193–217, a slightly different version, available from the publisher.
