On the Word Cemetery and on the Cross

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Two complete English translations are free online. David M. Friel, “Chrysostom’s Homily on the Word Koimeterion and on the Cross: A Translation and Commentary”, Vigiliae Christianae 76 (2022; published online 2021), pp. 1–36 (doi:10.1163/15700720-12341486), gives the Greek text with a facing English translation and a commentary; a copy is posted on Academia.edu (a free login is needed to download it). John Sanidopoulos, “Homily on the Name ‘Cemetery’ and on the Cross of our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ”, is posted at the Mystagogy Resource Center (29 April 2025). Neither states an open licence. The homily (CPG 4337; in Migne De coemeterio et de cruce, Patrologia Graeca 49, cols. 393–398) was preached by Chrysostom at Antioch on Good Friday, at a eucharistic service held in the cemetery outside the city gates. He explains that the place is called a koimeterion, a “sleeping-place”, because since Christ’s death the dead are only asleep, and then celebrates the victory of the Cross, won by the same means through which the devil had conquered: a virgin, a tree and a death, Eve, the tree of knowledge and Adam’s death, answered by Mary, the Cross and the death of Christ. He alludes to the descent of the Holy Spirit at the eucharistic prayer and rebukes the congregation for their behaviour at communion. Other English translations: the passage on the victory of the Cross (chapter 2) is read in the Office of Readings in The Liturgy of the Hours (Catholic Book Publishing), in the Common of the Blessed Virgin Mary, excerpt only. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 September 2025, who traces the much-quoted passage to this homily.)
