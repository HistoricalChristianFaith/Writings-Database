On the Day of Christ's Birth

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Beth Elise Dunlop, “On the Day of the Birth of Our Savior Jesus Christ”, made from the Greek text in Migne’s Patrologia Graeca (49:351 ff.; CPG 4334), forms chapter VIII (pp. 180–201) of her doctoral dissertation Earliest Greek Patristic Orations on the Nativity: A Study Including Translations (Boston College, 2004; UMI 3122121), with an introductory chapter on the homily; the dissertation, which also translates the Nativity orations of Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa and Amphilochius of Iconium, is copyright © 2004 Beth Elise Dunlop. A scan of it is posted on the dec25th.info website (“The Earliest Greek Patristic Orations on the Nativity with Translations”, which links to the PDF); the site states that it is a copyrighted work that may be read but not copied. Preached at Antioch in the mid-380s, less than ten years after the feast of 25 December had been brought there from the West, the homily opens “That which, long ago, the Patriarchs travailed with, the prophets foretold, and the righteous desired to see, has come to pass”, and sets out to answer those who objected that the feast was a novelty: Chrysostom argues that the day was known in the West from the beginning, pointing to the census records kept at Rome and to the timing of Zechariah’s priestly service in Luke’s Gospel, before turning to the mystery of the Incarnation itself. It is Chrysostom’s genuine Christmas homily, and is not one of the five sermons in Maria Dahlin’s translation On Christmas, nor the same as the 2nd Homily On the birthday of our Savior. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 November 2011.)
