Letters from Exile

No public-domain English translation of these letters is available. Free draft English translations of a growing number of them, by the scholar who writes as “Inepti Graeculi”, are posted on Academia.edu, with an “Index of Translations (with quick links)” to the letters done so far (a free Academia.edu login is needed to download them); by January 2014 drafts of thirty letters had been posted, more than had ever been translated before, and the project was continuing. Their licence is not stated. About 240 letters of John Chrysostom survive, nearly all written during his second exile (404–407), from Cucusus in Armenia and from Arabissus, to friends, clergy, bishops and supporters at Constantinople, Antioch and elsewhere: he thanks them for their loyalty, asks for news and help, describes the hardships of his journey, of the Armenian winter and of the raids of the Isaurians, and encourages those suffering for his cause. Apart from the seventeen letters to the deaconess Olympias and a few others, such as the letter to Castus, Valerius, Diophantus and Cyriacus, which were translated in the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, almost none had been put into English. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 20 January 2014; see also his post of 13 December 2013.)
