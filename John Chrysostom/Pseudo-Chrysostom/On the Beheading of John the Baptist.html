On the Beheading of John the Baptist

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the pseudo-Chrysostom sermon In decollationem sancti Iohannis (CPG 4570; incipit “Πάλιν Ἡρωδιὰς μαίνεται”), made from the Greek text in Migne, Patrologia Graeca 59, was posted in 2022 by the author of the blog Aliens in This World (suburbanbanshee.wordpress.com) in two parts, Part 1 and Part 2, with key Greek words given in brackets, after an introductory post on the sermon and its misquotation. Its licence is not stated. The sermon is not by Chrysostom (Migne already printed it among the spurious works), and it has also been attributed to Ephrem and to John of Damascus. Taking as its theme the Gospel account of John’s imprisonment and death (Mt 14), it sets Herodias beside Jezebel and John beside Elijah, contrasting John’s freedom of speech with Herod’s weakness and the fury of the wicked women; it explicitly says that it speaks of evil women and not of good and virtuous ones, and goes on through Job’s wife, Delilah and the warnings of the prophets and wisdom books. Its second half turns to good women, the widow of Zarephath who fed Elijah and the Shunammite who received Elisha, and praises their hospitality. One sentence of the first part (“it seems to me that no evil wild animal in the world is comparable to evil women”) circulates in a distorted form as a saying of Chrysostom about women in general. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 22 September 2022.)
