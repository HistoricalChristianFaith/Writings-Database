Incomplete Commentary on Matthew

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by James A. Kellerman, edited by Thomas C. Oden, as Incomplete Commentary on Matthew (Opus imperfectum) in two volumes (Ancient Christian Texts; IVP Academic, 2010): Volume 1 (ISBN 978-0-8308-2901-9) and Volume 2 (ISBN 978-0-8308-2902-6), with an introduction and notes. The Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum is a long Latin commentary on Matthew in the form of fifty-four homilies, written in the early fifth century by an unknown author with Arian (Homoian) sympathies and handed down under the name of John Chrysostom. It is “incomplete” because the surviving text breaks off after Matthew 25 and lacks the sections on Matthew 8:11–10:15 and 13:14–18:35. In spite of its doctrinal origins it was widely read in the Middle Ages as a work of Chrysostom, and Thomas Aquinas quotes it often in his Catena Aurea. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 June 2009, among the forthcoming volumes of the series.)
