Catechetical Sermon on Pascha

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Two free English translations of the whole of Migne’s text were posted online: one prepared by André Lavergne for the worship.ca website (“An Easter Sermon”, posted Easter 1999, revised Easter 2001), and one by Frank Dobbs (“The Easter Sermon of John Chrysostom”) on the dobbscapital.com website. Both sites have since disappeared, but the Lavergne version and the Dobbs version are preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. Their licences are not stated. The sermon (Sermo catecheticus in pascha, CPG 4605; Migne, Patrologia Graeca 59:721–724, where it is printed among the works wrongly attributed to Chrysostom) is a short and apparently incomplete Easter homily, beginning (in Dobbs’s version) “If any be pious and a lover of God, let him rejoice in this fair and radiant festival”. Taking up the parable of the labourers in the vineyard, it invites to the feast all alike, those who came at the first hour and those who came only at the eleventh, those who have fasted and those who have not; it then proclaims Christ’s victory over death and Hades, which received a body and encountered God, and ends with the repeated cry “Christ is risen”. Under Chrysostom’s name it is read at the end of Paschal Matins in the Orthodox Church, and many other English versions circulate in service books and online. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 8 October 2012.)
