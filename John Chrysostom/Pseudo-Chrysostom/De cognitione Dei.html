De cognitione Dei

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A free draft English translation (2014, since revised) by the scholar who writes as “Inepti Graeculi”, made from the Greek text in the Patrologia Graeca (vol. 64, cols. 43–46), is posted on Academia.edu as “Ps.-Chrysostom, De Cognitione Dei (PG 64:43–46 = CPG 4703): Draft Translation” (a free Academia.edu login is needed to download it). Its licence is not stated. The work (CPG 4703; in Migne De cognitione Dei, et in sancta Theophania) is a short Greek homily transmitted under the name of John Chrysostom but not by him, preached on the eve of the feast of Christ’s baptism (Theophany): the preacher says that the coming of Christ brought mankind the true knowledge of God (θεογνωσία), turns briefly to the newly baptized, and invites the congregation to make a pilgrimage to the Jordan, though its references to Bethlehem and the Jordan are probably meant figuratively rather than as clues to where it was preached. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 24 January 2015.)
