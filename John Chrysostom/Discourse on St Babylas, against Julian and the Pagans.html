Discourse on St Babylas, against Julian and the Pagans

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Margaret A. Schatkin, from her critical edition prepared for Sources Chrétiennes, as “Discourse on Blessed Babylas and against the Greeks”, in Apologist (The Fathers of the Church 73; Catholic University of America Press, 1985; paperback ISBN 978-0-8132-1086-5), available from the publisher. A partial translation, of sections XIV–XIX only, which include the passage from Libanius’s monody on the temple of Apollo at Daphne that Chrysostom quotes, is in Samuel N. C. Lieu (ed.), The Emperor Julian: Panegyric and Polemic (Translated Texts for Historians 2; Liverpool University Press, 2nd edn 1989; ISBN 978-0-85323-376-3), an anthology that also contains the panegyric of Claudius Mamertinus and Ephrem the Syrian’s Hymns against Julian; it is available from the publisher. Written at Antioch around 378–379, the discourse (De sancto Babyla contra Iulianum et gentiles) answers pagan attacks on Christianity by retelling the story of Babylas, the bishop of Antioch who defied an emperor and died a martyr in the persecution of Decius: when his relics were brought to Daphne they silenced the oracle of Apollo, and after the emperor Julian had them removed the temple of Apollo was struck by fire. Chrysostom presents this as proof of Christ’s power over the pagan gods. It is a different and much longer work than the short homily On the Holy Martyr, S. Babylas. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 August 2010.)
