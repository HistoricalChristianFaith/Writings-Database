Praedestinatus

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Guido Stucco as Arnobius Iunior, Praedestinatus (Brepols Library of Christian Sources 6; Turnhout: Brepols, 2022; ISBN 978-2-503-59676-1), the first English translation, made from Franco Gori’s critical edition (2000), with the Latin text on the facing pages; it is available from the publisher. Stucco’s introduction discusses the authorship, date and setting of the work and follows its later use down to the seventeenth century, including Archbishop Hincmar of Reims’ use of it against the monk Gottschalk. The Praedestinatus is an anonymous Latin treatise of the fifth century, now generally attributed to Arnobius the Younger, a monk at Rome; Jacques Sirmond discovered it in a manuscript at Reims and printed it in 1643, taking its title from a reference the author makes to it in Book 3. Book 1 is a catalogue of ninety heresies, drawn largely from Augustine’s On Heresies but with additions of its own, among them an entry on the “Tertullianists”, and ending with Nestorianism and the “Predestinarians”; Book 2 is a tract setting out an extreme doctrine of predestination, presented as built on statements of Augustine; and Book 3 refutes that tract point by point. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 July 2023.)
