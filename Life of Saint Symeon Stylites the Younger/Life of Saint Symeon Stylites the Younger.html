Life of Saint Symeon Stylites the Younger

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. There is no complete English translation of the Greek Life (BHG 1689); two chapters (161 and 164, the second only in part) are translated in A. D. Lee, Pagans and Christians in Late Antiquity: A Sourcebook (Routledge; first published 2000, cited here from the 2013 printing), no. 7.2, pp. 135–6; a revised second edition appeared in 2015 (ISBN 978-1-138-02032-0) and is available from the publisher. The excerpt describes the official Amantius’s purge of pagans and heretics at Antioch around 555, when their books were burned and their idols hung up in the streets. Symeon the Younger (521–592) spent most of his life, from boyhood on, standing on pillars near Antioch, latterly on the “Wondrous Mountain” outside the city, where a great monastery grew up around him. The anonymous Life, in 259 chapters, was written by one of his disciples who had known him; it is an important witness to sixth-century Antioch. The Greek text was critically edited, with a French translation, by Paul van den Ven, La vie ancienne de S. Syméon Stylite le Jeune (Subsidia Hagiographica 32; Brussels, 1962–70). The Latin text in the Acta Sanctorum (May, vol. 5) renders the later Greek paraphrase by Nicephoros Ouranos; Roger Pearse gives his own English version of a passage from it (end of ch. 173 and ch. 174) in the post cited below. This Symeon is not to be confused with the elder Symeon Stylites (d. 459). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 23 November 2013; see also his post of 27 November 2013.)
