Commentary on Galatians

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete commentary was translated by Stephen Andrew Cooper as Marius Victorinus’ Commentary on Galatians: Introduction, Translation, and Notes (Oxford Early Christian Studies; Oxford University Press, 2005; ISBN 978-0-19-827027-0), the first English translation of the work; it is available from the publisher. Besides the annotated translation, Cooper’s long introduction sets the commentary against Victorinus’ writings in the Trinitarian controversy, discusses how the apostle Paul was portrayed in mid-fourth-century Rome, describes Victorinus’ method as an interpreter, and traces his influence on Ambrosiaster and Augustine. Victorinus, the celebrated teacher of rhetoric at Rome whose conversion in old age is told by Augustine in the eighth book of the Confessions, wrote in the early 360s the earliest Latin commentaries on the letters of Paul that survive; on Galatians he explains the letter verse by verse, pressing Paul’s argument that the Christian is justified by faith in Christ and not by keeping the Jewish law. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 September 2019.)
