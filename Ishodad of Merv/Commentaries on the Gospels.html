Commentaries on the Gospels

A public-domain English translation of this work exists but has not yet been transcribed here. Margaret Dunlop Gibson edited the Syriac text with a complete English translation, with an introduction by J. Rendel Harris, in The Commentaries of Isho‘dad of Merv, Bishop of Hadatha (c. 850 A.D.), in Syriac and English (Horae Semiticae V–VII; 3 vols.; Cambridge: University Press, 1911): the English translation of the commentaries on all four Gospels fills volume I, and the Syriac text is in volume II (Matthew and Mark) and volume III (Luke and John), all scanned at archive.org. Public domain (published 1911). The translation runs to about 290 closely printed pages, with marginal references and footnotes in Greek and Syriac, too long to be reproduced here in one go. Isho‘dad, a native of Merv and bishop of Hadatha in Assyria in the Church of the East, was put forward for the patriarchate in 852 but passed over. His commentary works through each Gospel passage by passage, drawing on earlier expositors, above all Theodore of Mopsuestia, “the Interpreter” (Harris’s introduction sets out the agreements with Theodore on John), and it preserves quotations from lost or rare works, among them readings of the Old Syriac Gospels, the Diatessaron of Tatian and Ephrem’s commentary on it, and, on Mark 15, a passage from Eusebius’s letter to Marinus on the hour of the crucifixion. Volume I also contains Gibson’s preface on the manuscripts she used and Harris’s list of agreements with the Old Syriac. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 24 February 2010.)
