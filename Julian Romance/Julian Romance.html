Julian Romance

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The newly discovered opening section of the Syriac text, preserved in the Vatican manuscript Vat. sir. 37 (fols. 168v–173r), was edited with an English translation by Marianna Mazzola and Peter Van Nuffelen, “The Julian Romance: A Full Text and a New Date”, Journal of Late Antiquity 16.2 (2023), pp. 324–377 (DOI 10.1353/jla.2023.a906772), together with a study of the work; it is available from the publisher. It is a partial translation: it covers only this opening section, which narrates the death of Constantius II and is missing from the manuscripts known before. The rest of the Romance, from the British Museum manuscript edited by J. G. E. Hoffmann (1880), was translated by Hermann Gollancz as Julian the Apostate (Oxford University Press, 1928); that older translation is in the public domain and can be read at the Internet Archive, but it was made before the opening section was found and does not contain it. The Julian Romance is a Syriac Christian novel about the reign of the emperor Julian the Apostate (361–363). In three parts it tells of Julian’s accession and his persecution of the Christians, his clash with Eusebius, bishop of Rome, and his Persian campaign, his death, and the accession of the Christian emperor Jovian. It has usually been dated to the sixth century and placed at Edessa; Mazzola and Van Nuffelen, who identify the Miscellaneous Chronicle of 640 as the source of most of its historical information, argue for the first half of the seventh century, and for a single author of all three parts. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 20 September 2023.)
