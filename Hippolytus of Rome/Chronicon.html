Chronicon

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the complete Chronicon, by T. C. Schmidt, made from Rudolf Helm’s edition (Die Griechischen Christlichen Schriftsteller 46), was posted free on Schmidt’s website chronicon.net in 2010, and a revised second edition with Nick Nicholas as co-translator, marked “second edition (rough draft)”, followed later that year; the site is gone, but the second-edition PDF is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. No licence is stated, and each page carries the translators’ copyright notice (©2010). Hippolytus finished the work in 234/235, under the emperor Severus Alexander. It counts the years of the world from Adam to his own day and sets out the descent of the nations from the three sons of Noah after the confusion of tongues at Babel, with long lists of peoples, lands, mountains and rivers and a sailing guide to the coasts of the Mediterranean (the Stadiasmus, now thought to be a separate source taken into the chronicle); it closes with lists of the patriarchs, judges, kings, prophets and high priests of Israel, a reckoning of the Passovers kept since Moses, the kings of the Persians and Macedonians, and the Roman emperors down to Alexander. The Greek survives only in part, in a Madrid manuscript, and the rest is recovered from Latin versions such as the Liber generationis of the Chronography of 354. Other English translations: T. C. Schmidt (with Nick Nicholas as co-translator of the Chronicon), Hippolytus of Rome: Commentary on Daniel and ‘Chronicon’ (Gorgias Studies in Early Christianity and Patristics 67; Piscataway, NJ: Gorgias Press, 2017; ISBN 978-1-4632-0658-1), the revised version of the same translation, complete, with an introduction. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 March 2010.)
