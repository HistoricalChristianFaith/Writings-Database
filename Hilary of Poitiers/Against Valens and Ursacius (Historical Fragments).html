Against Valens and Ursacius (Historical Fragments)

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The extant fragments were translated by Lionel R. Wickham, together with Hilary’s Letter to the Emperor Constantius, as Hilary of Poitiers: Conflicts of Conscience and Law in the Fourth-Century Church (Translated Texts for Historians 25; Liverpool University Press, 1997; ISBN 978-0-85323-572-9), with an introduction and notes, from Alfred Feder’s edition in CSEL 65 (1916); it is available from the publisher. The work, known also as the Historical Fragments or Collectanea antiariana Parisina, survives only in pieces: it was a dossier Hilary assembled against Valens of Mursa and Ursacius of Singidunum, the bishops whose influence with Constantius he blamed for the emperor’s campaign against Athanasius and the Nicene faith. What remains is chiefly the documents themselves, among them synodal letters and creeds of the councils of Serdica and Ariminum and letters of Pope Liberius and other bishops, linked by passages of Hilary’s own narrative and comment. The short Letter to Constantius, written while Hilary was in exile in the East, asks the emperor for a hearing in which to set out the faith. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 28 March 2018.)
