Commentary on Matthew

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete commentary was translated by D. H. Williams as Commentary on Matthew (The Fathers of the Church 125; Catholic University of America Press, 2012; ISBN 978-0-8132-0125-2), the first English translation of the work; it is available from the publisher. Probably Hilary’s earliest surviving work, written in the 350s before his exile to Phrygia, it is the oldest Latin commentary on Matthew to survive almost entire. It works through the Gospel passage by passage, reading the narrative chiefly as a figure of the history of salvation, in which the Jews’ rejection of Christ opens the way for the faith of the Gentiles, and it dwells on the relation of the Law to the Gospel and on justification by faith. It predates the Arian controversy in the West and so lacks the polemic of Hilary’s later writings, though it already insists against those who saw in Christ only a man that he is both God and man. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 May 2015.)
