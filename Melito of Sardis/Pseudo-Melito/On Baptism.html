On Baptism

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation is by A. I. Elanskaya, printed with her edition of the Coptic text as “The Treatise on the Symbolics of Baptism and the Elements” in The Literary Coptic Manuscripts in the A.S. Pushkin State Fine Arts Museum in Moscow (Supplements to Vigiliae Christianae 18; E. J. Brill, 1994; ISBN 978-90-04-09528-1), pp. 165–200 and plates 59–77, available from the publisher; it first appeared in her Coptic Literary Texts of the Pushkin State Fine Arts Museum in Moscow (Studia Aegyptiaca 13; Budapest, 1991), pp. 48–83. The 1994 volume can be borrowed from the Internet Archive’s lending library (archive.org), and Roger Pearse has reproduced Elanskaya’s translation of the eighteen surviving pages, with her brackets and editorial marks removed, in a blog post; its licence is not stated. The work survives only in a damaged two-column papyrus codex in the Pushkin Museum, once owned by the Egyptologist Vladimir Golenishchev and probably written in the Fayyum; its opening pages, with the title and the author’s name, are lost, and the order of the surviving pages is not certain. What remains is a homily on Christ’s baptism in the Jordan and on Christian baptism, explained through the four elements: fire, water, air and earth are opposed to one another yet held together in harmony, and man, made from the earth and given a soul from the Spirit, is washed in water and the Spirit and so becomes immortal and a son of God. It closes with a run of exhortations ending “you are born anew” and with Old Testament examples of cleansing water, among them Moses’ sweet spring and Naaman washing in the Jordan. Alin Suciu has argued, in an article forthcoming in Adamantius, that the homily is the lost On Baptism of Melito of Sardis (d. c. 180), pointing to a parallel with the one known Greek fragment of that work and to likenesses with Melito’s On Pascha; the attribution is a recent proposal. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 May 2026.)
