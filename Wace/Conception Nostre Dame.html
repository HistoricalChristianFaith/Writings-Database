Conception Nostre Dame

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete poem was translated by Jean Blacker, Glyn S. Burgess and Amy V. Ogden in Wace, The Hagiographical Works: The Conception Nostre Dame and the Lives of St Margaret and St Nicholas (Studies in Medieval and Reformation Traditions 169 / Texts and Sources 3; Brill, Leiden, 2013; ISBN 978-90-04-24705-5), where it occupies pp. 11–146, with an introduction and notes and printed with the Old French text; it is available from the publisher. The volume brings together all three of Wace's saints' poems and gives the first English translations of them. The Conception Nostre Dame is a twelfth-century poem in Old French octosyllabic verse by Wace, the Norman cleric of Caen better known for his chronicles Roman de Brut and Roman de Rou. It opens with the legend of how the feast of the Conception of the Virgin came to be kept in England, and then tells, largely from apocryphal sources, the story of Mary's parents Joachim and Anne, her birth, childhood and marriage to Joseph, and her death and assumption. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 December 2019.)
