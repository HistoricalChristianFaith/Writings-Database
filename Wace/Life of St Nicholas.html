Life of St Nicholas

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete poem was translated by Jean Blacker, Glyn S. Burgess and Amy V. Ogden in Wace, The Hagiographical Works: The Conception Nostre Dame and the Lives of St Margaret and St Nicholas (Studies in Medieval and Reformation Traditions 169 / Texts and Sources 3; Brill, Leiden, 2013; ISBN 978-90-04-24705-5), where it occupies pp. 235–353, with an introduction and notes and printed with the Old French text; it is available from the publisher. The volume brings together all three of Wace's saints' poems and gives the first English translations of them. La Vie de saint Nicolas is a twelfth-century Old French verse life of Nicholas of Myra by the Norman poet Wace. It is based mainly on the Latin Life by John the Deacon of Naples (late ninth century), in its original and interpolated forms, together with a number of episodes from popular legend. Among these is one of the earliest tellings of the miracle of the three clerks murdered by an innkeeper and raised to life by the saint (lines 213–226), which Wace gives as the reason why schoolboys keep the feast of St Nicholas with reading, singing and the recital of his miracles. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 December 2019.)
