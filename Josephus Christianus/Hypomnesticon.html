Hypomnesticon

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Robert M. Grant and Glen W. Menzies as Joseph’s Bible Notes (Hypomnestikon) (SBL Texts and Translations 41, Early Christian Series 9; Scholars Press, Atlanta, 1996; ISBN 978-0-7885-0195-1), with an introduction, notes identifying the sources and parallels, and a critical edition of the Greek text by Menzies; it is the first English translation. The book is out of print and no publisher’s page for it survives; library holdings are listed in WorldCat. Corrections to the translation are given in William Adler’s review in the Journal of Theological Studies 48 (1997). The Hypomnesticon (Greek Ἰωσήππου βιβλίον ὑπομνηστικόν, “Joseph’s book of notes”) is an anonymous Greek handbook of biblical history in five books and 167 chapters, probably compiled in the late fourth or early fifth century; its author was long called “Josephus Christianus”, though the title may simply mean that much of the material is drawn from the historian Flavius Josephus. Each chapter poses a question about the Old or New Testament and answers it with a list: the generations from Adam to Christ, the miracles of the prophets, the saints who were raised from the dead, the stations of the Israelites in the wilderness, the Jewish sects, the translators of the Hebrew scriptures, and so on. It survives complete in a single tenth-century manuscript at Cambridge (University Library Ff.1.24); the Greek text, with a Latin translation, was first printed by J. A. Fabricius in 1723 and is reprinted in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 106. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 20 September 2018.)
