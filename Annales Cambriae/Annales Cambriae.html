Annales Cambriae

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of a combined text of the annals, from 447 to 954, is free online in Paul Halsall’s Internet Medieval Sourcebook at Fordham University (1998); it follows the Harleian manuscript, the earliest surviving copy, and adds some entries from the later versions. The translator is not named (the page’s only source line cites James Ingram’s 1912 translation of the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, which does not contain these annals), so the printed translation behind it is unidentified; the Sourcebook allows copying for educational and personal use, but the licence of the translation itself is not stated. The Annales Cambriae (“Annals of Wales”) are a Latin chronicle kept in Wales, probably at St Davids, whose oldest version (the A text, in British Library Harley MS 3859, compiled in the mid-tenth century) records year by year brief notices of British, Irish and English events from the time of St Patrick to 954: the births and deaths of saints and kings, battles, plagues and synods, among them the battles of Badon and Camlann with their famous mentions of Arthur and Medraut, and the death of St David. Later versions (the B and C texts) continue the record into the thirteenth century. Other English translations: John Morris, Nennius: British History and the Welsh Annals (Arthurian Period Sources 8; Phillimore, 1980; ISBN 978-0-85033-297-1), available from the publisher; David N. Dumville, Annales Cambriae, A.D. 682–954: Texts A–C in Parallel (Department of Anglo-Saxon, Norse and Celtic, University of Cambridge, 2002). Henry W. Gough-Cooper’s editions of the Latin texts are published by the Welsh Chronicles Research Group. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 1 March 2024.)
