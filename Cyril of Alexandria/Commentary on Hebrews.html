Commentary on Hebrews

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the full surviving text is Commentary on the Letter to the Hebrews (Ankyunacar Publishing, Yerevan, 2021; ISBN 978-9939-850-51-1), which prints the Classical Armenian critical text with an English translation on facing pages, with indexes to both; the translator is not named in the publisher’s listing. It is available from the publisher. It covers the commentary on Hebrews 1–3, which is all that the Armenian version contains; the publisher suggests that Cyril may never have gone further. The previously known Greek and Syriac fragments are translated by David R. Maxwell, ed. Joel C. Elowsky, in Cyril of Alexandria, Commentaries on Romans, 1–2 Corinthians, and Hebrews (Ancient Christian Texts; IVP Academic, 2022; ISBN 978-0-8308-2918-7). The commentary was long thought lost apart from fragments in catenae and florilegia, until an Armenian translation of it was found in 2020 in three manuscripts in the Matenadaran in Yerevan; the Armenian text, edited by Hacob Keosyan, appeared the same year. Written after the outbreak of the Nestorian controversy in 428, it is directed in part against the exegesis and Christology of Theodore of Mopsuestia. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 20 July 2020; see also his post of 13 December 2021.)
