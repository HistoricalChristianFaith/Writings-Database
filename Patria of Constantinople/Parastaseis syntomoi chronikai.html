Parastaseis syntomoi chronikai

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Averil Cameron and Judith Herrin, in conjunction with Alan Cameron, Robin Cormack and Charlotte Roueché, as Constantinople in the Early Eighth Century: The Parastaseis Syntomoi Chronikai (Columbia Studies in the Classical Tradition 10; Brill, Leiden, 1984; ISBN 978-90-04-07010-3), with the Greek text, an introduction and a detailed commentary; it is available from the publisher. The Parastaseis syntomoi chronikai (“Brief historical notes”) is an anonymous Greek collection of short, often legendary notices on the statues and monuments of Constantinople, compiled in the eighth century and preserved in a single manuscript (Paris gr. 1336); much of its material was later taken up and reworked in the tenth-century Patria of Constantinople. It is a key witness to how the Byzantines saw the classical sculpture that still stood in their city. The Greek text is edited in Theodor Preger’s Scriptores originum Constantinopolitarum, vol. 1 (Leipzig, 1901), pp. 19–73. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 17 December 2013.)
