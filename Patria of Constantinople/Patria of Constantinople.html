Patria of Constantinople

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Albrecht Berger as Accounts of Medieval Constantinople: The Patria (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 24; Harvard University Press, 2013; ISBN 978-0-674-72481-5), with the Greek text on facing pages, an introduction and notes; it is the first complete English translation and is available from the publisher. It covers all four books of the collection and the account of the building of Hagia Sophia. The Patria is an anonymous Greek compilation, put together in the late tenth century and later circulated under the name of George Kodinos, of short notices, stories and legends about the origins, buildings and statues of Constantinople. Its four books treat the founding and early history of the city, its statues and monuments, its churches and other buildings, and the construction of Hagia Sophia under Justinian, drawing on older material such as Hesychius of Miletus and the Parastaseis syntomoi chronikai. The standard Greek edition is Theodor Preger’s Scriptores originum Constantinopolitarum (2 vols, Leipzig, 1901–1907); an older text attributed to Codinus, with a Latin version, is printed in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 157, from which Roger Pearse translated a few short notices on statues and on the imperial tombs in the Church of the Holy Apostles.
