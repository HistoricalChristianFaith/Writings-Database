Cura Sanitatis Tiberii

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Tuomas Levänen, made from the Latin text edited by Ernst von Dobschütz in Christusbilder: Untersuchungen zur christlichen Legende (Leipzig, 1899), pp. 157**–203**, is offered free on Academia.edu as a short paper, “Cura Sanitatis Tiberii – English Translation”; Levänen worked from an OCR of Dobschütz’s text made by Roger Pearse. Its licence is not stated. The Cura sanitatis Tiberii (“The healing of Tiberius”; CANT 69, BHL 4218–4220) is a Latin legend of the Pilate cycle, composed perhaps in northern Italy between the fifth and eighth centuries, and the earliest witness to the legend of Veronica’s image of Christ: the emperor Tiberius, gravely ill, sends Volusianus to Judea in search of Jesus, learns that Pilate has let him be put to death, and is healed by the image of his face kept by Veronica; the apostle Peter later confirms the truth of Pilate’s report about Jesus, and Pilate, condemned and banished, at last takes his own life. It survives in two recensions and is distinct from the related Vindicta Salvatoris (“The Avenging of the Saviour”) and Mors Pilati (“The Death of Pilate”).
