Correspondence

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete correspondence was edited and translated by Glen L. Thompson as The Correspondence of Pope Julius I: Greek and Latin Text and English Translation (Library of Early Christianity 3; Catholic University of America Press, 2015; ISBN 978-0-8132-2707-8), with an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher. It is the first full critical edition of these letters since that of the Maurist Pierre Coustant in the early eighteenth century, and gives the original text with a facing English translation of the six genuine letters, two written by Julius and four addressed to him, while its introduction surveys the many later writings falsely circulated under his name. Julius I was bishop of Rome from 337 to 352; all of his surviving correspondence concerns the Arian controversy, above all his defence of Athanasius of Alexandria and Marcellus of Ancyra against the eastern bishops who had condemned them. Julius’s two own letters, to the Eusebian bishops at Antioch and to the clergy and people of Alexandria, are quoted in full by Athanasius, and older public-domain translations of them can be read in this collection in Athanasius of Alexandria, Defence Against the Arians, Part 1 (chapter II, and §§52–53). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 December 2018.)
