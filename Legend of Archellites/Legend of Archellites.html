Legend of Archellites

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the Coptic (Sahidic) legend, by James Drescher, was published with the Coptic text and a commentary in Three Coptic Legends: Hilaria, Archellites, the Seven Sleepers (Supplément aux Annales du Service des Antiquités de l’Égypte, Cahier 4; Cairo: Institut Français d’Archéologie Orientale, 1947), pp. 83–99 (text pp. 14–31). The book can be read free online as a scan on the Internet Archive, and was formerly offered page by page as images on the Coptic Library site of calloflove.net, now preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine (the original domain no longer hosts the site). Its licence is not stated. Drescher translates the only complete text, Pierpont Morgan MS M 579 (ninth century), where the life is ascribed to “Eusebius, the historian of the city of Rome”. Archellites, the only son of a Roman prefect and his wife Synkletike, is sent to study at Athens and Beirut; after a shipwreck he turns instead to the monastic life at the monastery of Apa Romanus in Palestine, vowing never to look upon a woman. His mother, believing him drowned, learns where he is and travels to the monastery to see him; unwilling either to break his vow or to refuse her, he prays to die, and she is let in to find him dead. (Anthony Alcock’s translation of a separate tenth-century Coptic poem on Archellites was posted on the same occasion.) (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 July 2016.)
