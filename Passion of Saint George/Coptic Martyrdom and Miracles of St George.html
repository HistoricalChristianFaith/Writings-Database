Coptic Martyrdom and Miracles of St George

No public-domain English translation of this work is available here as text yet. A complete English translation of the Coptic texts is in the public domain: E. A. Wallis Budge, The Martyrdom and Miracles of Saint George of Cappadocia: The Coptic Texts Edited with an English Translation (Oriental Text Series 1; London: D. Nutt, 1888), whose translation fills pp. 201–331. A scan of the book can be read at archive.org; it has not yet been transcribed. The volume edits the texts from five Bohairic (Memphitic) and Sahidic manuscripts and translates four works: the Martyrdom of St George, presented as the record of his servant Pasikrates; an Encomium on the saint by Theodosius, bishop of Jerusalem; the nine Miracles of St George; and an Encomium by Theodotus, bishop of Ancyra. The Martyrdom belongs to the earliest, “apocryphal”, strand of the legend: the governor Dadianus and his fellow governors torture George for seven years, and he is killed and raised from the dead three times. Between his deaths he works wonders, makes wooden seats burst into leaf, converts Queen Alexandra and sends the demon in Apollo’s statue down into the earth, before fire from heaven consumes the governors and he is beheaded. Budge’s preface and footnotes compare the Greek, Latin, Syriac, Arabic and Ethiopic versions. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 July 2018.)
