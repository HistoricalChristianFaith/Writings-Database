Encomium of Phoebammon on St Colluthus

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English version of all the surviving Coptic fragments of the second encomium on St Colluthus, by Phoebammon, bishop of Akhmim (Panopolis), was published free online by Roger Pearse on his blog in 2026: “The encomium of Phoebammon on St Colluthus” (15 August 2026). It is a machine translation (ChatGPT and Google Translate), lightly edited, of Gesa Schenke’s German translation in Das koptisch hagiographische Dossier des Heiligen Kolluthos, Arzt, Märtyrer und Wunderheiler (CSCO 650, Subsidia 132; Louvain: Peeters, 2013), with sixteen notes summarised from hers; it is an English version of a German version, not a translation from the Coptic. Its licence is not stated. Phoebammon, who acted in the sixth century as deputy in Egypt for the exiled Patriarch Theodosius of Alexandria, delivered the eulogy at the consecration of a new shrine of Colluthus at Pneueit, a former pagan stronghold near Akhmim. Only four leaves of the single ninth- or tenth-century parchment manuscript survive: one in Paris, with the title summary and the opening of the sermon; two in Vienna (pp. 23–26 of the codex), where Phoebammon has Colluthus himself tell of his trial before the governor Arianus at Hermopolis, his torture by hanging from a persea tree weighted with a broken millstone, and his imprisonment; and a third in Vienna (pp. 69–70), with a miracle in which the childless couple Theognostos and Eudocia are sent by the saint from his shrine at Antinoë to the new one at Pneueit. A complete Arabic version, apparently never printed, survives in British Library Or. 4723. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 15 August 2026.)
