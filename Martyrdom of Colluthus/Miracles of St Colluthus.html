Miracles of St Colluthus

Translated by Roger Pearse (2026) from the Latin version by Agostino Antonio Giorgi of two mutilated miracle stories that took place at the shrine of St Colluthus, preserved on five Sahidic Coptic parchment leaves (numbered pp. 221–230) from the White Monastery at Sohag, the first pages from that library to reach Europe. They were published, in Coptic with Giorgi’s Latin, by Cardinal Stefano Borgia, Fragmentum Copticum ex Actis S. Coluthi Martyris (Rome, 1781). The opening of the first miracle is lost, and the second breaks off; the two stories run on in the manuscript without a heading between them. Pearse worked from the Latin, not the Coptic. Found via, and first published on, Roger Pearse’s blog: “Two miracle stories of St Colluthus from the White Monastery” (24 July 2026), which also prints the Latin version (omitted here). No licence is stated in the post. Words in square brackets are the translator’s; his two explanatory glosses, marked “**” in the post, are given here as notes 1–2.

MIRACLE 1

“…I will give you the healing of your eyes, and difficulty will not cause it to fail you, in the course [of events].” The man said, “I wish to find a woman who has recently given birth, so that I may complete the work which has been commanded to me by Saint Colluthus.”  The woman said, “I will fill your eyes with milk from my breasts.” Then that man, whose eyes were diseased, said to her, “Show kindness to me: behold, you see my torment and my affliction.”

Her husband said to her, because he remained unbelieving, “Do not do this, lest perhaps those who slander you should mock you.”  She said, “Mock me? Why? For I believe, through the faith of my noble lineage and the teaching of my Fathers, that if I fill his eyes with milk from my breasts, he will see through the power of Saint Colluthus.”  Then she said to him, “Have people ever seen me as someone worthy of ridicule? Was I ever a wanton woman? Or have they said in their hearts that a man has had relations with me other than intercourse with you, my husband?  Behold, I call you all as witnesses today, all who hear me. I swear to you by Saint Colluthus, and by the oblation of the Christians,1 and by the sufferings which Christ endured on the cross, that I have had no relations with any man since I was given in marriage. From my mother’s womb I came to my husband only. And when has my flesh been touched by a strange man? Has not the Lord heard me, seeing my need?”

And so, taking her milk, she filled the man’s eyes. And she said, “In the name of the power of Saint Colluthus the true doctor” and then his eyes poured out waters, and these flowed out abundantly.

Immediately after this, he opened them, saying, “I believe in you, Saint Colluthus!” and at the same time he cried out to his people, “Behold, I have received my sight, so that I can distinguish a little.”

The woman said to him, “Allow me to fill them again another time.” And the man blessed her, saying, “May the Lord preserve you until His hands reach you.”2 When she filled his eyes a second time, these [waters] flowed out more abundantly.

A little later he opened them again. He looked here and there and said, “Truly, this time I see well.”

Again, after a short while, the woman said to that man, “Allow me to fill them once more also; for I believe that you will have health.”  Then she signed her whole body and her breasts with the sign of the cross, in the name of Christ. She filled his eyes with milk a third time, saying, “In the name of God and of Saint Colluthus.” Then the woman said to him, “Close your eyes for a little while, and may the grace of God and of Saint Colluthus be with you.”

And this noble woman, confirmed greatly in faith, was strengthened because she saw the healing which had gradually been accomplished for him. After a little while he opened his eyes again. The household servants were present at night, and at that time he saw the light truly and perfectly.

Then that man rose up in great joy. He walked, washed his face in a basin, and cried out, saying, “You are great, to you I give glory, O God of Saint Coluthus, because I have been made worthy that you should grant me the grace of light, just as it was for me at the beginning.”  And all the people who had entered the martyr’s shrine, together with those who had gone out, came together. They looked upon him rejoicing greatly because he was seeing this light.

Indeed, that man also, who considered the wrong which he had done to his wife, praised her and confessed before Saint Colluthus, saying, “Behold, I have been healed in body and in my soul, because I have been made worthy to come to this place of yours.”  The woman’s parents also came with her brothers and her people, rejoicing, blessing and glorifying [God], saying, “Blessed are you among women, O our sister and our daughter, because today you have received glory in the midst of many, since you have put to shame the multitude of men and women through your nobility. For the multitude thought, O our daughter, that they would find fault with you because of your noble birth, your kindness, and your sweet speech.” At this time Saint Colluthus revealed the glory of his greatness and his nobility through the greatness of the healings which he performed for those who were sick and who believed in him.

After these things, each person went away with him to his own house.  They honoured the martyr’s shrine of Saint Coluthus: they ate and drank in his place, and they returned home giving glory to God and to Saint Coluthus.

MIRACLE 2

Hear also another great miracle which was performed through Saint Colluthus.

Again a mutilated man was brought forward who had absolutely no power to walk on his feet: likewise his hands were withered. He did not lift up his step, but moved along by dragging his body over the ground, just as little children go, crawling upon the earth, and he was brought into that holy place.  Then he lay in the martyr’s shrine, praying to God and calling upon Saint Colluthus for the healing of his body, that healing might be given to him.  And he groaned day and night, weeping many times, crying out in great anguish of spirit, he said, “Help me, physician and healer! For this reason I flee to you, my lord, and to this your holy martyr’s shrine, that you may have mercy upon my lowliness.” All the people were greatly distressed because of him, crying out, “Truly he suffers.”

After all these things, Saint Colluthus, the physician and accomplisher of healings, had mercy on him. He came to him in a vision at night. He stood over him and said to him, “Do you know me?” He answered, “No, my lord. Who are you, who appear surrounded by the greatness of glory?” He said to him in the vision, “I am Coluthus the physician. I heal all people who have faith, freely, and I desire to receive no payment from anyone whatsoever.” The man said to him, “My lord, may your mercy embrace me, for I am in very great anguish.”

Saint Colluthus said to him in the vision, “Rise at dawn. Walk using your hands and your feet, and enter the city. To any man or woman who meets you in the city say, ‘Where is the house of Mary the prostitute?’ They will show it to you. And if they ask you, ‘Why?’ say to them, ‘I wish to spend the night with her; I will give her her payment.’  Do not fear, and do not be ashamed. For a great gift and a great grace will come to that woman through you. She will abandon the shamefulness of her error; she will become honoured through this business of yours, when she hears of the mercy of your healing…”

1 [i.e. the mass]
2 [i.e. his purpose is complete]
