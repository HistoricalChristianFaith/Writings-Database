Life of Saint Nilus the Younger

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Raymond L. Capra, Ines A. Murzaku and Douglas J. Milewski, is The Life of Saint Neilos of Rossano (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 47; Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 2018; ISBN 978-0-674-97704-4), with a revised Greek text on facing pages, an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher. A second complete translation, The Life of Saint Nilus the Younger, was published by Scriptorium Press (Montreal, 2023; ISBN 9798864232491), the small press founded by Anthony Pavoni and Evangelos Nikitopoulos, who are presumably the translators, though the book names none; it is available from the publisher. The Greek Life (BHG 1370) was written in the years after the saint’s death by one of his monks, traditionally identified as his disciple Bartholomew. Nilus (c. 910–1005) was a Greek-speaking monk from Rossano in Calabria who lived as a hermit and abbot in southern Italy, moved north to escape the Arab raids, and at the end of his life founded the abbey of Grottaferrata near Rome; the Life is a major source for Italo-Greek monasticism and for the politics of tenth-century Italy, including Nilus’s dealings with the emperor Otto III. He is not the same person as Nilus of Sinai (Nilus of Ancyra). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 3 December 2024.)
