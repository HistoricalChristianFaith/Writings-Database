Against Secundinus the Manichaean

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the Contra Secundinum Manichaeum by Mark Vermes, made in 1997 as part of his M.A. thesis (University of Warwick) from the Latin text of Joseph Zycha (CSEL 25.2, 1892), is available free on Roger Pearse’s website, together with Vermes’s version of the letter of Secundinus to which it replies (that letter was later printed in Iain Gardner and Samuel N. C. Lieu, Manichaean Texts from the Roman Empire, Cambridge University Press, 2004). Both appear there by permission (the letter from the Cambridge volume, copyright Cambridge University Press; the treatise by permission of the translator), and Pearse states that they remain the copyright of CUP and Dr Vermes respectively, which is why he did not add them to his public-domain collection; no licence for reuse is given. Secundinus, a Manichaean “hearer” (not one of the sect’s elect) who did not know Augustine personally, wrote him a courteous letter reproaching him for his attacks on Manichaeism and urging him to return to it; Augustine’s answer, traditionally dated to about 405 (Vermes argues for 402), replies at length, setting the Catholic teaching on God, creation and the origin of evil against the Manichaean doctrine of two opposed principles. In his Retractations Augustine says that he preferred it to everything else he had written against that heresy. Secundinus’s letter is almost the only Latin writing by a Roman Manichaean to survive. Other English translations: Roland J. Teske, “Answer to Secundinus, a Manichean”, in The Manichean Debate (The Works of Saint Augustine I/19; New City Press, 2006; ISBN 978-1-56548-247-0), available from the publisher.
