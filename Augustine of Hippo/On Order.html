On Order

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Robert P. Russell, O.S.A., as “Divine Providence and the Problem of Evil”, in The Happy Life; Answer to Skeptics; Divine Providence and the Problem of Evil; Soliloquies (Writings of Saint Augustine, Volume 1; The Fathers of the Church 5; New York: Cima Publishing, 1948; reprinted by the Catholic University of America Press, ISBN 978-0-8132-1551-8), available from the publisher. The De ordine is one of the dialogues Augustine wrote in late 386 at the country estate of Cassiciacum near Milan, after giving up his post as a teacher of rhetoric and while preparing for baptism. In two books it records conversations with his pupils Licentius and Trygetius, his friend Alypius and his mother Monica about how everything that happens, evil included, falls within the order of divine providence without God being the author of evil. The second book turns to the order of learning, setting out the liberal arts as a path by which reason rises from bodily things to the contemplation of God and the soul. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 June 2019, who quotes Russell’s version of Book 2, chapter 4.)
