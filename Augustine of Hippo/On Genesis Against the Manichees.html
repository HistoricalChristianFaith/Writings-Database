On Genesis Against the Manichees

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Roland J. Teske, S.J., as “Two Books on Genesis against the Manichees” in On Genesis: Two Books on Genesis against the Manichees and On the Literal Interpretation of Genesis: An Unfinished Book (The Fathers of the Church 84; Catholic University of America Press, 1991; ISBN 978-0-8132-1088-9), available from the publisher. A second complete translation, by Edmund Hill, O.P., appears as “On Genesis: A Refutation of the Manichees” in On Genesis (The Works of Saint Augustine I/13; New City Press, 2002; ISBN 978-1-56548-175-6), available from the publisher, together with his two later works on Genesis. Written in two books around 388–389, soon after Augustine’s baptism and return to Africa, the De Genesi contra Manichaeos is the first of his several commentaries on the opening of Genesis. It answers the mockery that the Manichees, whose sect he had followed for nine years as a “hearer”, directed at the creation story: the first book goes through the six days of creation and the seventh day of rest, which it also reads as a figure of the ages of world history; the second treats paradise, the making of the woman, the serpent and the fall, largely in a figurative sense where Augustine judged a literal reading impossible. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 22 August 2020.)
