Letter to Firmus

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The letter was translated by Demetrius B. Zema and Gerald G. Walsh as “A Letter of St. Augustine Concerning the City of God”, printed as an appendix to their The City of God, Books I–VII (The Fathers of the Church 8; New York: Fathers of the Church, Inc., 1950; reprinted by the Catholic University of America Press, ISBN 978-0-8132-1554-9), available from the publisher. It is also translated by Roland J. Teske, S.J., as Letter 1A* in Letters 211–270, 1*–29* (The Works of Saint Augustine II/4; New City Press, 2005; ISBN 978-1-56548-209-8), available from the publisher. Both give the complete letter. It survives in two late medieval manuscripts of the City of God (Reims 403 and Paris, Sainte-Geneviève 2757) and was first published by Cyrille Lambot in the Revue Bénédictine in 1939. Writing to his friend Firmus, Augustine sends him the twenty-two books of the City of God, which he has reread, and explains how they may be bound: in two volumes of ten and twelve books, matching the work’s division into a refutation of paganism and a defence of Christianity, or in five volumes following its smaller sections. He asks Firmus to let one or two of the brethren at Carthage copy the books they still lack, promises to ask often how his reading is going, praises repeated reading as the key to understanding, asks how Firmus came to know his early books against the Academics, and encloses a summary of the whole work. The letter is a rare first-hand witness to how an ancient author arranged for his own work to be copied and circulated. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 August 2013.)
