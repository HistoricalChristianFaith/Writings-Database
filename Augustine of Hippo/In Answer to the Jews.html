In Answer to the Jews

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Sister Marie Liguori as “In Answer to the Jews”, in Treatises on Marriage and Other Subjects (The Fathers of the Church 27; New York: Fathers of the Church, Inc., 1955), pp. 387–417, with a short introduction; the volume is reprinted by the Catholic University of America Press (ISBN 978-0-8132-0967-8) and available from the publisher. Its chapter divisions differ from those of the Latin text in the Patrologia Latina (vol. 42, cols. 51–64). The Tractatus adversus Iudaeos is a short treatise in the form of a sermon, probably written after 425 since it draws on ideas from the City of God. Taking as its starting point Paul’s image in Romans 11 of the natural branches broken off the olive tree and the wild olive grafted in, it first answers the Jewish objection that Christians read the Law and the Prophets without keeping their precepts, and then gathers testimonies from the Psalms and the prophets to show that the Jews’ own Scriptures foretold Christ and the calling of the Gentiles. It ends by urging Christians to proclaim these testimonies to the Jews with great love and humility, not boasting against the broken branches.
