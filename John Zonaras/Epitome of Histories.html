Epitome of Histories

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The section of the Epitome running from Alexander Severus to the death of Theodosius the Great (Books XII.15–XIII.19), together with Zonaras’s prologue and postscript, was translated by Thomas M. Banchich and Eugene N. Lane as The History of Zonaras: From Alexander Severus to the Death of Theodosius the Great (Routledge Classical Translations; Routledge, 2009; ISBN 978-0-415-29909-1), with an introduction and commentary by Banchich; it is available from the publisher. It is a partial translation: the rest of the work has not been published in English. Shorter passages from the earlier books have been translated on the Sententiae Antiquae blog, and Zonaras’s summary of the lost early books of Cassius Dio is printed in English among the Dio fragments in Earnest Cary’s Loeb Classical Library translation of Dio (1914), which is online at LacusCurtius. John Zonaras, a high official at the court of Alexios I Komnenos who retired to a monastery and wrote there in the mid-twelfth century, compiled the Epitome in eighteen books as a history of the world from the creation to the death of Alexios in 1118, covering the Jewish people, Rome and Byzantium. Because he condensed earlier historians closely, often in their own words, the work preserves material from sources now lost, above all the missing books of Cassius Dio. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 22 June 2019.)
