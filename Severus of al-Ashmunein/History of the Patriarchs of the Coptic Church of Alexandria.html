History of the Patriarchs of the Coptic Church of Alexandria

A public-domain English translation of the first part of this work exists but has not yet been transcribed here. B. Evetts edited the Arabic text with an English translation as History of the Patriarchs of the Coptic Church of Alexandria in four parts of the Patrologia Orientalis (Paris: Firmin-Didot, 1904–1915): I, Saint Mark to Theonas, and II, Peter I to Benjamin I (PO 1, fasc. 2 and 4); III, Agathon to Michael I (PO 5, fasc. 1); and IV, Mennas I to Joseph (PO 10, fasc. 5). He stopped with the fifty-second patriarch, Joseph, who died in 849. Public domain (published 1904–1915). The printed volumes, with the Arabic above the English on each page, can be read in scans at the Internet Archive (PO 1, PO 5, PO 10), and Roger Pearse has placed Evetts’s English online, part by part, at tertullian.org, with a preface to the online edition; the four parts run to some six hundred printed pages, too long to be reproduced here in one go. The History is the chronicle of the Coptic (Miaphysite) patriarchs of Alexandria, told as a series of lives from the evangelist Mark onwards. Its compilation was traditionally credited to Severus (Sawirus ibn al-Muqaffa‘), bishop of al-Ashmunein (Hermopolis Magna) in the later tenth century; modern scholarship gives the Arabic compilation chiefly to Mawhub ibn Mansur ibn Mufarrij of Alexandria in the late eleventh century, working from older Coptic lives, and later writers carried it on into modern times. The early lives lean heavily on Eusebius and Coptic tradition, but from the sixth century the notices grow fuller and often rest on the reports of eyewitnesses: they follow the church through the quarrels after the Council of Chalcedon, the Persian and Arab conquests of Egypt and life under the caliphs, down to a vivid account of the overthrow of Marwan II, the last Umayyad caliph, in 750. The rest of the History, from Khael II (849) onwards, was translated by Yassa ‘Abd al-Masih, O. H. E. Burmester, Aziz Suryal Atiya and Antoine Khater as History of the Patriarchs of the Egyptian Church (Cairo: Société d’archéologie copte, 1943–1974), whose first volume reprints Evetts; its copyright status is unclear, and Pearse notes that the Cambridge History of Egypt (vol. 1, p. 211) calls that translation unreliable. Several of its parts were once posted online, and the index page is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 28 August 2007; see also his posts of 9 September 2008, 3 August 2009 and 2 October 2018, the last of which quotes Evetts’s version of the life of Theodosius and points out that he renders mal‘ab “amphitheatre” where the hippodrome of Alexandria is meant.)
