The Discourses

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by C. J. de Catanzaro as Symeon the New Theologian: The Discourses (Classics of Western Spirituality; Paulist Press, New York, 1980; ISBN 978-0-8091-2230-1), with an introduction by George A. Maloney, S.J., and a preface by Basile Krivochéine; it is available from the publisher. The thirty-four Discourses, also called the Catechetical Discourses or Catecheses, are the central work of Symeon the New Theologian (949–1022), abbot of the monastery of St Mamas in Constantinople, and were addressed to his monks, mostly at the morning office. In them he calls for repentance, tears, obedience to a spiritual father and the renunciation of the world, and insists that every Christian can and should come to a conscious experience of the Holy Spirit and of the divine light, drawing at times on his own experience. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 28 July 2012.)
