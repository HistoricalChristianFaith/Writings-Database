Ecclesiastical History

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first complete English translation of this church history (CPG 6034), also known as the History of the Council of Nicaea and, in G. C. Hansen’s critical edition (GCS NF 9, 2002), as the Anonyme Kirchengeschichte, was made for Glen L. Thompson’s Fourth Century Christianity website (Wisconsin Lutheran College) and is offered free there as one PDF per book: Book 1 (Nathanael J. Jensen, 2017), Book 2 (N. Jensen and R. Read, 2021) and Book 3 (R. Read and P. Balge, 2022), with an introduction page and an outline of the three books. Its licence is not stated. The work was written in Greek in the last quarter of the fifth century, probably soon after 476, by an author who tells us he was the son of a priest of Cyzicus; the name “Gelasius of Cyzicus” first appears in Robert Balfour’s editio princeps of 1599 and is widely doubted. Writing against the followers of Eutyches, the author sets out what the Council of Nicaea really taught: Book 1 covers Constantine’s rise, his vision and his victories over Maxentius and Licinius; Book 2 tells of Arius and the council itself, including long debates in which philosophers argue the Arian case and the orthodox bishops answer them, the creed, the canons and Constantine’s letters; Book 3 gathers further letters of Constantine and stories such as those of Helena, Frumentius and Edesius, breaking off with the emperor’s letter to the synod of Tyre (335). The author draws on Eusebius, Rufinus (whom he identifies with Gelasius of Caesarea) and lost sources, and parallels Socrates and Theodoret. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 September 2022; see also his post of 27 June 2017 on the translation’s beginnings.)
