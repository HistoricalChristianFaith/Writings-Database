Miracle of St George and the Dragon

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the Greek miracle story (BHG 687), by Stratis Papaioannou, appears in his anthology Saints at the Limits: Seven Byzantine Popular Legends (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 78; Harvard University Press, 2023; ISBN 978-0-674-29079-2), available from the publisher. The miracle is translated complete, with the Greek text on facing pages, as one of the miracles attached to Papaioannou’s early Greek Passion of George the Great Martyr (BHG 670a); the volume gathers seven popular Byzantine saints’ legends, with an introduction and notes on the text. This is the best-known of the miracles of St George, but a late one: it is missing from the oldest Greek collections of his miracles and was added to the tradition in the Middle Byzantine period. It tells how a dragon in a lake beside a pagan city demanded human victims, until the lot fell on the king’s daughter; George rode up, subdued the beast in the name of Christ, had the princess lead it into the city, and the king and his people were baptized. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 November 2023.)
