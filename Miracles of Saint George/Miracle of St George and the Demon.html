Miracle of St George and the Demon

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the Greek miracle story (BHG 687k), by Stratis Papaioannou, appears in his anthology Saints at the Limits: Seven Byzantine Popular Legends (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 78; Harvard University Press, 2023; ISBN 978-0-674-29079-2), available from the publisher. The miracle is translated complete, with the Greek text on facing pages, together with the dragon miracle and Papaioannou’s early Greek Passion of George the Great Martyr (BHG 670a); the volume gathers seven popular Byzantine saints’ legends, with an introduction and notes on the text. The story usually follows the dragon miracle in the manuscripts: as George leaves the city, a demon meets him on the road in a humble guise; George pins it inside a circle drawn with the sign of the cross, makes it follow him, and commands a great rock to split open, casting the demon into the fire that comes out of it, to be shut in and tormented there until the end of the age. A raw ChatGPT translation of the Greek, made by Roger Pearse, was posted in a comment on his blog (28 March 2025); it is not reproduced here.
