Poems

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the poems by W. B. Anderson, in Sidonius: Poems and Letters, vol. 1 (Loeb Classical Library 296; Harvard University Press and William Heinemann, 1936), which also contains Books 1–2 of the letters, can be read free on the Internet Archive (a scan of a University of Toronto copy). Its licence is not stated, and its copyright status is uncertain: no renewal of the US copyright has been found, but the translator was British (d. 1959) and the book was printed in Great Britain, so its US copyright may have been restored. The translation remains in print (ISBN 978-0-674-99327-3) and is available from the publisher. The poems (Carmina) of Sidonius Apollinaris (c. 430–c. 485), the Gallo-Roman nobleman who became bishop of Clermont, were almost all written before his ordination. The longest are three verse panegyrics, each with its own preface, delivered for the emperors Avitus, his father-in-law (456), Majorian (458) and Anthemius (468); that on Majorian is the main source for his reign. The rest are shorter occasional pieces for friends: wedding poems, verse letters, the description of a friend’s fortified country house, and a send-off for his own book. His letters are in this collection in O. M. Dalton’s translation. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 19 July 2012, who tentatively concludes that the translation is in the public domain in the USA; see also his post of 17 July 2012.)
