Life of Mary the Niece of Abraham

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Benedicta Ward translated the Latin Life of Mary, the niece of the hermit Abraham, from the Vitae Patrum in Harlots of the Desert: A Study of Repentance in Early Monastic Sources (Cistercian Studies 106; London: Mowbray, and Kalamazoo: Cistercian Publications, 1987; ISBN 978-0-87907-606-1), available from the publisher. The book is an anthology with commentary: each chapter studies one penitent woman of the early Church, and the chapters on the desert penitents end with new translations of their Lives. Mary, the niece of Abraham, has the sixth and last chapter (“Maria the Niece of Abraham: An Image of Salvation”, pp. 85–101). The other chapters cover Mary Magdalene, Mary of Egypt, Pelagia and Thaïs. The story comes from the Life of Abraham of Qidun, a fourth-century hermit near Edessa, which tradition ascribes to Ephrem the Syrian. Abraham brings up his orphaned niece in a cell beside his own. After many years of ascetic life she is seduced by a false monk, despairs, and runs away to a distant town, where she lives in a brothel. Abraham goes after her disguised as a soldier, takes a room with her as a customer, then reveals who he is and persuades her to come home, where she spends the rest of her life in penance. Other English translations: Helen Waddell, “The Life of Saint Mary the Harlot”, in The Desert Fathers (London: Constable, 1936), from the Latin; and Sebastian P. Brock and Susan Ashbrook Harvey, “Mary, the Niece of Abraham of Qidun”, in Holy Women of the Syrian Orient (The Transformation of the Classical Heritage 13; University of California Press, 1987, updated edition 1998; ISBN 978-0-520-21366-1), from the Syriac, available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 4 May 2012.)
