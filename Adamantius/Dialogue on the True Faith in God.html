Dialogue on the True Faith in God

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, by Robert A. Pretty (1904–1985), was published after his death as Adamantius: Dialogue on the True Faith in God (De recta in Deum fide), translated with commentary by Robert A. Pretty and edited for publication by Garry W. Trompf (Gnostica 1; Peeters, Leuven, 1997; ISBN 978-90-6831-893-7); it is a complete translation, with an introduction and commentary, and is listed by the publisher as sold out. The dialogue, ascribed in the manuscripts to an otherwise unknown Adamantius (a name also borne by Origen, though the author is hostile to Origen’s views), is a debate on which teaching is the true Christianity, held before the pagan Eutropius as judge: the orthodox Adamantius disputes in turn with the Marcionites Megethius and Marcus, with Marinus, a follower of Bardesanes, and with the Valentinian Droserius, and in the end Eutropius declares for the orthodox faith. It draws on lost works of Methodius and so was written around 300 or a little later; it survives in Greek and in a Latin translation by Rufinus, and is an important source for the teaching of these groups. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 14 August 2009.)
