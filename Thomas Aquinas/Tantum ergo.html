Tantum ergo

The Tantum ergo is made up of the last two stanzas of Thomas Aquinas’s hymn Pange lingua gloriosi corporis mysterium, written for the Office of Corpus Christi; it is also sung on its own at Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament. The Latin text is given as printed in The Golden Manual: being a guide to Catholic devotion, public and private, compiled from approved sources (London: Burns and Lambert, 1850), p. 711, transcribed from the archive.org scan; that book prints the Latin and Caswall’s translation side by side (in the 1854 edition, on p. 665). The first translation is by Edward Caswall, Lyra Catholica: containing all the Breviary and Missal hymns, with others from various sources (London: James Burns, 1849), p. 112, where these are the closing stanzas of his version of the whole hymn (“Sing, my tongue, the Saviour’s glory”); transcribed from the archive.org scan of that first edition. The second, by an anonymous member of the Society of Jesus, is from Select Hymns and Prayers. Translated from the original Latin into English lyric verse. By a member of the Society of Jesus. Lat. & Eng. (Dublin, 1852), pp. 43–44, as transcribed by Roger Pearse; it has not been checked against the scan. Public domain (published 1849 and 1852). Found via Roger Pearse’s blog. Other English translations: James Quinn, SJ, “Come, adore this wondrous presence” (1969).

Latin text

Tantum ergo Sacramentum
Veneremur cernui:
Et antiquum documentum
Novo cedat ritui:
Præstet fides supplementum
Sensuum defectui.

Genitori, Genitoque
Laus et jubilatio,
Salus, honor, virtus quoque
Sit et benedictio:
Procedenti ab utroque
Compar sit laudatio.

Translation by Edward Caswall (1849)

Down in adoration falling,
Lo! the sacred Host we hail;
Lo! o’er ancient forms departing,
Newer rites of grace prevail;
Faith, for all defects supplying,
Where the feeble senses fail.

To the Everlasting Father,
And the Son who reigns on high,
With the Holy Ghost proceeding
Forth from Each eternally,
Be salvation, honour, blessing,
Might, and endless majesty.

Translation by a member of the Society of Jesus (1852)

Let us worship, humbly bending,
This so glorious sacrament;
And let ancient rites, here ending,
Yield to worth, new rites present;
May faith too, assistance lending
Aid where sense proves impotent.

To the Father, ever heeding
Our petitions, glory be:
To the Son, on Calv’ry bleeding,
Raise the song of jubilee:
And of Him, from both proceeding,
Chaunt the praises equally. Amen.
