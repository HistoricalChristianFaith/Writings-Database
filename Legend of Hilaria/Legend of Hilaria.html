Legend of Hilaria

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the Coptic (Sahidic) legend, by James Drescher, was published with the Coptic text and a commentary in Three Coptic Legends: Hilaria, Archellites, the Seven Sleepers (Supplément aux Annales du Service des Antiquités de l’Égypte, Cahier 4; Cairo: Institut Français d’Archéologie Orientale, 1947), pp. 69–82 (text pp. 1–13; the other Coptic fragments in Appendix I, pp. 139–148). The book can be read free online as a scan on the Internet Archive, and was formerly offered page by page as images on the Coptic Library site of calloflove.net, now preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine (the original domain no longer hosts the site). Its licence is not stated. Drescher translates the only complete Coptic text, Pierpont Morgan MS M 583 (A.D. 848). The legend, presented as the work of Apa Pambo, priest of Scetis, tells how Hilaria, elder daughter of the emperor Zeno, leaves the palace in a soldier’s dress, sails to Alexandria and goes on to Scetis, where she is received as a monk and lives for years as “Hilarion the eunuch”; when her sister Theopiste falls ill with a demon and is sent to the monks to be healed, Hilaria cares for her in her cell and cures her, and when Zeno summons the monk to Constantinople she reveals herself to her parents before returning to the desert, where her secret becomes known only at her death. An older, public-domain English rendering covers only part of the Coptic tradition: A. J. Wensinck, Legends of Eastern Saints, Chiefly from Syriac Sources, vol. II, The Legend of Hilaria (Leiden: Brill, 1913), translates the fragmentary Coptic text then known (it begins partway through the story) together with the Arabic, Syriac and Karshuni versions; it is on the Internet Archive. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 July 2016.)
