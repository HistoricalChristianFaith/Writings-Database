Translation of the relics to Bari

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation of this anonymous Greek account of the removal of St Nicholas’s body from Myra to Bari (Translatio Barim, BHG 1361b; Greek text in G. Anrich, Hagios Nikolaos, vol. 1, 1913, pp. 435–449), by J. McGinley and H. Musurillo, is reproduced free online in Paul Halsall’s Internet Medieval Sourcebook (formerly at legacy.fordham.edu), from the reprint by Gerardo Cioffari in Bollettino di S. Nicola 10, Studi e testi (Bari, October 1980), pp. 3–17. Its licence is not stated: the Sourcebook notes that the Bari reprint reproduces Appendix B of Eugene R. Whitmore, Saint Nicholas, Bishop of Myra (Saint Nicholas of Bari): The Genesis of Santa Claus (1944), pp. 28–36, where the translators are described as professors at Georgetown University, and suggests that the text may therefore be in the public domain, but this has not been confirmed. The account tells how, in the reign of the emperor Alexius Comnenus, merchants and sailors of Bari bound for Antioch landed at Myra, forced their way past the monks guarding the tomb, broke open the coffin, which they found full of fragrant oil, and carried off the saint’s bones, outstripping the Venetians who had planned the same thing; it describes the lament of the monks and the citizens of Myra on the shore, and then follows the voyage of 1087 harbour by harbour across the Aegean towards Italy, with the signs and visions by which the saint was believed to show his approval. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 January 2016.)
