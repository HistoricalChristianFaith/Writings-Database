Life of Shenoute

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete Bohairic Coptic Life was translated by David N. Bell as The Life of Shenoute by Besa (Cistercian Studies Series 73; Cistercian Publications, Kalamazoo, 1983; ISBN 978-0-87907-873-7), with an introduction by the translator; it was the first English translation of the Life, and is available from the publisher. Besa, Shenoute’s disciple and his successor as head of the White Monastery near Atripe in Upper Egypt, wrote the Life in Sahidic Coptic after his master’s death in the mid-fifth century, as a memorial of his teacher. The Sahidic original survives only in fragments; the full text is preserved in a Bohairic version (Vatican Coptic 66), from which Bell translates, and there are also Arabic, Syriac and Ethiopic versions, the Arabic much expanded. It presents Shenoute as a prophet and wonder-worker: his calling and training, his visions, his struggles with demons and pagans, his rule over his monks, and his journey to the Council of Ephesus. (A short Syriac epitome of the Life is in this collection as the Syriac Life of Shenoute, under Shenoute the Archimandrite.) (Noted by Roger Pearse, 20 May 2009.)
