Life of Markos the Athenian

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the Greek Life (BHG 1039–1041), by Stratis Papaioannou, appears in his anthology Saints at the Limits: Seven Byzantine Popular Legends (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 78; Harvard University Press, 2023; ISBN 978-0-674-29079-2), available from the publisher. The Life is translated complete, with Papaioannou’s edition of the Greek text on facing pages; the volume gathers seven popular Byzantine saints’ legends, with an introduction and notes on the text and its manuscripts. The legend is told by the monk Serapion, who is led by a vision into the remote desert to a mountain where he finds the aged hermit Mark. Mark tells him that he was born in Athens and studied philosophy there, that after his parents’ death he gave up everything, and that he has lived there for ninety-five years without seeing another human being, naked, battling demons and at last fed by an angel; after their conversation he dies, and Serapion buries him in his cave. It belongs to the same family of desert tales as the Life of Onnophrius, in which a traveller finds a hidden holy man just before his death. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 November 2023.)
