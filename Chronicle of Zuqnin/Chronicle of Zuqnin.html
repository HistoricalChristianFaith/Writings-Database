Chronicle of Zuqnin

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Part III of the chronicle was translated by Witold Witakowski as Pseudo-Dionysius of Tel-Mahre: Chronicle (known also as the Chronicle of Zuqnin), Part III (Translated Texts for Historians 22; Liverpool University Press, 1996; ISBN 978-0-85323-760-0), with an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher. It is a partial translation, covering Part III only. Parts III and IV together were translated from the Syriac by Amir Harrak as The Chronicle of Zuqnin, Parts III and IV, A.D. 488–775 (Mediaeval Sources in Translation 36; Pontifical Institute of Mediaeval Studies, Toronto, 1999; ISBN 978-0-88844-286-4), with an introduction and notes; see the publisher’s catalogue page (archived copy). Neither covers Parts I and II. Roger Pearse’s own English of the opening of Part IV, made from Chabot’s French, is on the page Part IV (from Chabot) in this folder. The chronicle is an anonymous Syriac world history in four parts, from the Creation to 775, written by a monk of the monastery of Zuqnin near Amida and preserved in a single manuscript (Vatican Syriac 162, with a few leaves in the British Library); it was long wrongly ascribed to the patriarch Dionysius of Tel-Mahre. By the author’s own account, Parts I and II follow Eusebius as far as Constantine and Socrates as far as Theodosius II; Part III, running to the 570s, draws on John of Ephesus, much of whose lost second part of the Ecclesiastical History survives only here, and it also takes in the whole of the Chronicle of Pseudo-Joshua the Stylite; Part IV is the author’s own account of the Arab conquests and of life in Mesopotamia under the Umayyad and early Abbasid caliphs, including famine and plague. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 May 2017; see also his post of 5 July 2010.)
