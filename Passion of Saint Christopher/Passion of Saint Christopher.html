Passion of Saint Christopher

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the Greek Passion of Christopher the Cynocephalus (BHG 309), by Stratis Papaioannou, appears in his anthology Saints at the Limits: Seven Byzantine Popular Legends (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 78; Harvard University Press, 2023; ISBN 978-0-674-29079-2), available from the publisher. The Passion is translated complete, with Papaioannou’s edition of the Greek text on facing pages; the volume gathers seven popular Byzantine saints’ legends, with an introduction and notes on the text and its manuscripts. In this early Greek form of the legend Christopher is a cynocephalus, a dog-headed man from a race of cannibals, who cannot speak the language of the Romans until God grants him speech in answer to prayer. In the persecution of the emperor Decius he preaches openly, wins over the soldiers sent to arrest him, is baptized with them by Bishop Babylas of Antioch and given the name Christophoros (“Christ-bearer”), and converts the two women sent to seduce him; after surviving a series of tortures he is beheaded. The later Western legend of the giant who carried the Christ child across a river grew out of this story. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 November 2023.)
