Epistle on Easter

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Kurt M. Simmons gives the Latin text of the long version (recension A), from Paul de Lagarde’s Mittheilungen, vol. 4 (1889), pp. 274–282, with an English translation under the heading “Council of Caesarea Concerning the Pascha”, at the end of his essay “December 25th and the Epistle of Theophilus” (2023) on his website dec25th.info; the essay was also published in Bibliotheca Sacra 180 (2023), and a copy was first posted on Academia.edu. Its licence is not stated beyond the site’s “All rights reserved”. The work, also called the Acts of the Council of Caesarea or De ordinatione feriarum paschalium, presents itself as the record of a synod held under Theophilus, bishop of Caesarea in Palestine, around AD 190, to settle how and when Easter should be kept. It is generally regarded as one of the computistical texts composed in Ireland around AD 600 under the names of earlier fathers in support of the local Easter reckoning, and it survives in four recensions in some three dozen manuscripts, often among the spurious works of Bede. The long version opens with an account of how the Gauls kept Easter on a fixed date, arguing (in Bainton’s rendering) that “as the birth of the Lord on whatever day it falls is kept on December 25th”, so Easter should be kept on 25 March; this is the source of the oft-repeated claim that Theophilus commanded Christmas to be kept on 25 December. Simmons’s essay argues that this report may preserve genuine early tradition. A translation of only the opening sentences of recensions A and B, by Roland H. Bainton, is in the Journal of Biblical Literature 42 (1923), p. 112. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 28 December 2021, updated 8 May 2023.)
