Description of the Church of the Holy Apostles

No public-domain English translation of this work is available online. The complete work was translated by Glanville Downey, with the Greek text and a commentary, as “Nikolaos Mesarites: Description of the Church of the Holy Apostles at Constantinople” (Transactions of the American Philosophical Society, n.s. 47.6, American Philosophical Society, Philadelphia, 1957, pp. 855–924); its US copyright does not appear to have been renewed, but no freely viewable copy is online: it is on JSTOR (subscription) and can be borrowed from the Internet Archive. A newer complete translation, with an introduction and commentary, is in Michael Angold, Nicholas Mesarites: His Life and Works (in Translation) (Translated Texts for Byzantinists 4; Liverpool University Press, 2017; ISBN 978-1-78694-006-3), which translates all of Mesarites’ published works; it is available from the publisher. Nicholas Mesarites, a churchman of Constantinople who later became Metropolitan of Ephesus, wrote this rhetorical description (ekphrasis) around 1200, shortly before the city fell to the Fourth Crusade. It is the fullest account of Justinian’s cross-shaped Church of the Holy Apostles, destroyed after 1453: it describes the building and its mosaics of the life of Christ, the teaching that went on in its precincts, and the mausolea of Constantine and Justinian where the emperors were buried. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 18 December 2013.)
