Letter on Syriac Orthography

The public-domain English translation of this work has not yet been transcribed here. George Phillips edited the Syriac text from the British Museum manuscript Add. 12,178 with an English translation and notes in A Letter by Mār Jacob, Bishop of Edessa, on Syriac Orthography; also a Tract by the Same Author, and a Discourse by Gregory Bar Hebræus on Syriac Accents (London: Williams and Norgate, 1869), where the translation of the letter fills pp. 1–12; it is in the public domain (published 1869), and a scan of the book is at archive.org. Because the letter is about how Syriac words should be spelled and pointed, the English is full of Syriac words, which the scan’s text layer cannot read, so the translation has not yet been reproduced here. In the letter Jacob writes to George, bishop of Sarug, and through him to every scribe who copies his books: readers, he complains, understand the craft of writing better than the copyists who practise it; he forbids them to change his spellings or his diacritical points, lists words and particles that must be written or separated in a particular way, compares superfluous or misplaced points to a body with too many or misplaced members, and asks that the letter be set before the middle volume of the cathedral homilies (Homiliae cathedrales) of Severus of Antioch, which he had translated, and sent with it to the abbot Julian. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 7 March 2011.)
