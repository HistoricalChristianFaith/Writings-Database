Life of Alexios the Man of God

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of this Greek version of the legend (BHG 51n), by Stratis Papaioannou, appears in his anthology Saints at the Limits: Seven Byzantine Popular Legends (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 78; Harvard University Press, 2023; ISBN 978-0-674-29079-2), available from the publisher. The Life is translated complete, with Papaioannou’s Greek text on facing pages, as one of seven popular Byzantine saints’ legends; the volume has an introduction, which the editor has made available on Academia.edu, and notes on the text. The legend of Alexios, the “Man of God”, tells of the only son of the wealthy Roman senator Euphemianos, who leaves his bride on their wedding night, gives away what he has and lives as a beggar in the East; he later comes back to Rome and lives for years unrecognized as a beggar in his father’s own house, and only at his death is it discovered who he was. Growing out of a Syriac story of an anonymous “man of God” of Edessa, it became one of the most widely told saints’ legends in Greek and Latin. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 November 2023.)
