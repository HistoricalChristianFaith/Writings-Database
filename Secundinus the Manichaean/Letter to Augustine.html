Letter to Augustine

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the letter of Secundinus to Augustine (Epistula Secundini ad Augustinum) by Mark Vermes, made in 1997 for his M.A. thesis at the University of Warwick from the text edited by Joseph Zycha (CSEL 25.2, 1892) on the basis of the only manuscript, the tenth-century Codex Carnutensis, is available free on Roger Pearse’s website, together with Vermes’s version of Augustine’s reply, Against Secundinus the Manichaean. Vermes’s translation of the letter was later printed in Iain Gardner and Samuel N. C. Lieu, Manichaean Texts from the Roman Empire (Cambridge University Press, 2004; ISBN 978-0-521-56822-7), available from the publisher; Pearse posted it with the permission of Cambridge University Press, whose copyright it remains, and its licence for reuse is not stated. Secundinus, a Manichaean “hearer” (not one of the sect’s elect) whom Augustine did not know even by sight, wrote him a courteous letter reproaching him for his attacks on Manichaeism and urging him to come back to the faith he had abandoned. Augustine answered with his Against Secundinus; the exchange is traditionally dated to about 405, but Vermes redates it to 402. The letter is one of the very few Latin writings by a Manichaean to survive. Other English translations: Roland J. Teske, “A Letter of Secundinus, a Manichean, to Augustine”, in The Manichean Debate (The Works of Saint Augustine I/19; New City Press, 2006; ISBN 978-1-56548-247-0), available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 3 September 2012.)
