Commentary on Hosea

Latin source: Jerome's commentary on Hosea, in three books addressed to Pammachius, each with its own prologue (written at Bethlehem in 406), from the critical text of M. Adriaen, Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 76 (Turnhout, 1969), pp. 1–158, verified against the page images and collated with Vallarsi's edition (Venice, 1766); the edition's few evident misprints are translated in their evident sense, and its critical apparatus is not translated. Each lemma gives Hosea in Jerome's own version from the Hebrew and then, after "LXX:", in his Latin of the Septuagint; all Scripture is translated as Jerome quotes it, from his own Latin. Italics mark the lemmata and the other Scripture he quotes. Headings follow the verse numbering of English Bibles, with the Latin numbering added where it differs (2:21-23 and 13:16-14:9). Psalms keep the Latin (LXX) numbering, with the Hebrew number in parentheses. Greek is kept as printed, with an English gloss in parentheses where Jerome gives none, and Hebrew words keep Jerome's own transliteration; the Origenian signs in two Septuagint lemmata are kept. This English translation is released into the public domain.

Prologue

If in the explanations of all the prophets we need the coming of the Holy Spirit, so that they may be opened by the revelation of him by whose prompting they were written; and if in Isaiah and in the Apocalypse we read of a sealed book, which the scribes and Pharisees, who boast that they know the letters of the law, cannot read, because it is sealed, and no one could be found who could unlock its mysteries except the lion of the tribe of Judah, whom God the Father has sealed: how much more, in the explanation of the prophet Hosea, must the Lord be prayed to, and must we say with Peter: Explain this parable to us. Especially since he who wrote it himself testifies at the end to the obscurity of the volume: Who is wise, and he will understand these things, understanding, and he will know them? This we ought to take not as something impossible but as something difficult, according to that saying of the Gospel: Who, do you think, is the faithful and prudent servant whom the Lord has set over his household? For who would not at once, at the very front of the book, be scandalized and say: Hosea, first of all the prophets, is commanded to take a harlot as his wife, and he does not object? He does not even pretend to be unwilling, so as to seem to do a shameful thing against his will; but he carries out the command gladly, as though he had longed for it, as though he regretted having given himself to chastity for a long time; whereas we read that holy men, at the Lord's command, refused things more honorable and without sin. Moses is sent to Pharaoh and appointed leader of the Israelite people; and yet, not from weakness but from humility, he answered: Provide another whom you may send. Jeremiah says that he is a boy, so as not to rebuke Jerusalem in her sin. Ezekiel is commanded to make one loaf baked under the ashes from every kind of pulse and seed, and to cook it in human dung; and he says: By no means, Lord, for nothing unclean has ever entered my mouth. And Hosea, hearing from the Lord: Take a fornicating wife, does not knit his brow, does not testify to his grief by pallor, does not show his shame by a changed blush on his cheeks; but he goes to the brothel and leads the harlot to his bed. And he does not initiate her into the chastity of a matron; rather he proves himself a voluptuary and a profligate. For he who cleaves to a harlot becomes one body with her. Hearing these things, then, what can we answer except that prophetic word: Who is wise, and he will understand these things, understanding, and he will know them? Hence we too must say with David: Unveil my eyes, and I will consider the wonders of your law, so that the king may bring us into his chamber, and take away from us the veil which was placed before the eyes of Moses in the reading of the Old Testament. For as soon as the Lord had said: Father, into your hands I commend my spirit; and saying this he breathed his last, the veil of the temple was torn, and all the secrets of the Jews lay open. And the fountain that went forth from the house of David entered the sea of the wilderness; and the true Elisha seasoned the barren and deadly waters with the salt of his wisdom and made them life-giving. And the Marah of the law, which is interpreted bitterness, was sweetened by the wood of the gibbet. For we understand who Judah the patriarch is, chosen to be king, and we do not wonder why a holy man goes in to Tamar as to a harlot; why Samson, which is interpreted sun, loved Delilah, which is translated poor, and, mocked on her account and dying, slaughtered thousands of his adversaries; why Salmon begot the just man Boaz from Rahab the harlot, Boaz who, covering Ruth the Moabitess with the wing of his cloak, carried her over from lying at his feet to the head of the Gospel. What reason there is that, though David had so many wives, he made no other the successor of his kingdom except the one who was born of Bathsheba; so that not only harlots but even adulteresses seem to please God. Hence in this very prophet too we read that, joined first to a harlot, he is coupled secondly to an adulteress, the Lord saying to him: Go again, love a woman beloved of a friend, or loving evil things, and an adulteress. This is the woman, harlot and adulteress, who in the Gospel washed the Lord's feet with her tears, wiped them with her hair, and honored them with the ointment of her confession; and when the disciples were indignant, and above all the betrayer, that it had not been sold and its price distributed for the food of the poor, the Lord answered: Why do you trouble the woman? She has done a good work on me; the poor you will always have with you; but me you will not always have. And lest we should think what she had done a light thing, and that the pistic nard, that is, the most faithful ointment, was to be referred to something else and not to the Church, he gives us an occasion for understanding; and he promises great rewards to great faith, saying: Amen, amen I say to you, wherever this Gospel is preached in the whole world, what she too has done will be told in memory of her. This is the harlot of whom the Lord speaks to the Jews: Amen I say to you, harlots and publicans will go before you into the kingdom of God. For you were unwilling to receive the Son of the householder, the Lord of the vineyard, who planted it, when he was sent to you. This harlot received with kindness my two spies, most valiant young men, of whom I sent one to the circumcision and the other to the Gentiles; she hid them with zeal, lifted them up onto the roof, and covered them with stalks of flax. That flax, cut down by persecutions and washed in baptism, changes its color, and from dark is turned to white. Nor is it surprising if we recall that these things were done as a figure of the Lord Savior and of the Church gathered from sinners, since he himself says in this same prophet: I will speak to the prophets; I have multiplied visions, and in the hands of the prophets I have been likened; so that whatever the prophets are commanded to do is to be referred to my likeness. His wife is an Ethiopian, against whom Aaron and Mary, the carnal priesthood of the Jews and the prophecy that serves the letter, murmur and offend God; she it is who says in the Song of Songs: I am dark and beautiful, daughters of Jerusalem. Otherwise, if we contend that all the things which are commanded for a reason, to be done as a likeness, really happened, then Jeremiah too, girded with a loincloth, a woman's garment, went among innumerable nations, the Assyrians and the Chaldeans, the peoples most hostile to the Jews, to the Euphrates, and there hid his loincloth, and, returning after a long time, found that it had rotted so as to be of no use. How could he go out and travel so far when Jerusalem was besieged, with siege works built all around it, ditch, rampart and forts? He who, when he once wished to go out to Anathoth, his little village situated at the third milestone from the city, was both seized at the gate and led back to the princes, and, beaten as a traitor, was thrown into prison. If that was in figure, because it could not happen, then this too is in figure, because if it happened, it would be most shameful. But you will answer: When God commands, nothing is shameful; and we will say: God commands nothing except what is honorable, nor by commanding shameful things does he make honorable what is shameful. But because we know that God wills nothing except what is honorable, he commanded this, which is honorable. I have spoken of these things at greater length at the beginning, so as first to solve the most difficult question, and, passing with God's help the rock set in the very mouth of the passage, to run down into safe waters. For the rest, I am not unaware, dearest Pammachius, that it has been passed over by many churchmen; and of those who attempted to expound it, among the Greeks I found Apollinaris of Laodicea, who, though in his youth he had left brief little commentaries both on this prophet and on others, touching the senses rather than unfolding them, was asked afterwards to write more fully on Hosea; that book has come into our hands, but he too, through excessive brevity, cannot lead the reader to a perfect understanding. Origen wrote a small book on this prophet, to which he gave this title: Περὶ τοῦ πῶς ὠνομάσθη ἐν τῷ Ὠσηὲ Ἐφραΐμ; that is: Why Ephraim is so called in Hosea, wishing to show that whatever is said against him is to be referred to the person of the heretics. And another volume, ἀκέφαλον καὶ ἀτέλεστον, which lacks both head and end. I have also read a very long treatise of Pierius, which he poured out on the opening of this prophet on the day of the vigil of the Lord's passion, in an extempore and eloquent discourse. And Eusebius of Caesarea, in the eighteenth book of the Εὐαγγελικῆς ἀποδείξεως (the Demonstration of the Gospel), discusses certain matters concerning the prophet Hosea. Hence about twenty-two years ago, when, at the request of your holy and venerable mother-in-law, or rather your mother, Paula (for the one is the name of the flesh, the other of the spirit; she always burned with love for monasteries and for the Scriptures), I was at Alexandria, I saw Didymus, the most learned man of his time, and heard him often; and I asked him to complete what Origen had not done, and to write commentaries on Hosea; and he dictated three books to me, at my request, and five others also on Zechariah. For on that prophet too Origen wrote only two volumes, expounding barely a third part, from the beginning of the book up to the vision of the four-horse chariots. I say these things so that you may know whom I have had as forerunners in the field of this prophet; whom, however, to confess it to your prudence simply and not proudly — as a certain one of my friends is always hissing — I have not followed in everything; so that I might be a judge of their work rather than its translator, and might say what seemed right to me on each point, and what I received from the teachers of the Hebrews, barely one or two of them. Such a one is now a rare bird even among themselves, since all devote themselves to pleasures and money, and take more care of the belly than of the breast; and they think themselves learned in this, if in the shops of physicians they disparage the works of everyone. But now it is time that, setting down the words of the prophet, we discuss what is written.

Book One

1:1

The word of the Lord which came to Hosea son of Beeri. LXX: likewise. The Word of the Lord (which in the beginning was with God the Father, and the Word was God) came to Hosea son of Beeri, so as to make the prophet too a god, as the Savior says: If he called them gods to whom the word of God came, and the scripture cannot be broken, him whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world you tell: You blaspheme; because I said, I am the Son of God. So that, just as God makes gods, and stands in the synagogue of gods, and in the midst he judges between gods; and, though he himself is the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world, says to the apostles: You are the light of the world, so too the Savior himself may make his prophet a savior. For Hosea, in our language, means savior, a name which Joshua son of Nun also had before his name was changed by God. For he was not called — as is wrongly read in the Greek and Latin manuscripts — Ause, which means nothing at all; but Hoshea, that is, savior; and Lord was added to his name, so that he might be called savior of the Lord. This savior, then, is the son of Beeri, that is, of my well; such wells Abraham, Isaac and Jacob dug, and the foreigners were always trying to fill them in. Between a well and a pit, that is, a cistern, there is this difference: a well has perpetual waters, flowing from a living spring; a cistern, which keeps water cool, holds waters that are external and brought in from outside. Hence God speaks through the prophet Jeremiah: They have forsaken me, the fountain of living water, and dug for themselves cisterns which cannot hold water. Of this fountain the psalmist cries out to God: With you is the fountain of life; in your light we shall see light. Hence some think that Beeri is interpreted my light; but the former is the true translation.

In the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, Hezekiah, kings of Judah; and in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, king of Israel. LXX: likewise. Azariah, who is also called Uzziah, of the stock of David, reigned in Jerusalem over the two tribes, which were called Judah, for fifty-two years; his son Jotham succeeded him in the kingdom, and he too reigned for sixteen years; after him his son Ahaz reigned, likewise for sixteen years. After Ahaz his son Hezekiah reigned for twenty-nine years, in whose sixth year the ten tribes, which were called Israel, were captured by Shalmaneser king of the Chaldeans and settled in the mountains of the Medes. From this it is evident that, while Hosea, Isaiah and Joel, and Amos and Obadiah, and Jonah and Micah were prophesying, who were his σύγχρονοι (contemporaries), the kingdom of the ten tribes came to an end, which from the first king, Jeroboam, to the last, Hoshea, lasted two hundred and fifty years. At the time when Uzziah began to reign over Judah, Jeroboam, the great-grandson of Jehu, was reigning over Israel, Jehu to whom the Lord had promised that his offspring would reign to the fourth generation, because he had struck down the two impious kings of Judah and of Israel. We say this to show you briefly that the prophet Hosea prophesied both before the captivity of Israel and after its captivity, and that, treating it as near as well as present and past, he both announced it as coming, and mourned it as it fell upon them, and, for the correction of Judah, went back over it once past; and this we shall try to prove in the prophet himself according to the history. Uzziah is interpreted strength of the Lord; Jotham, consummation and perfection of the Lord; Ahaz, power; Hezekiah, command of the Lord. These reigned among the people of Judah, whose name is confession. Moreover, in Israel, which had made idols for itself and had been separated from the people of God, reigned Jeroboam son of Joash, which is interpreted χρονισμός, that is, temporality, or delay; because he loved the world and thought that to linger long in it was eternal life, delighting not at all in things to come but in things present.

1:2

The beginning of the Lord's speaking in Hosea. LXX: The beginning of the word of the Lord to Hosea. Because, as we said above, Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, in whose reigns they prophesied, are set at the head of the title of other prophets too, he therefore now says that among all these the Lord spoke first in Hosea, and afterwards to the rest. But it is one thing for the Lord to speak in Hosea, another to speak to Hosea. In Hosea, he does not speak to Hosea himself, but through Hosea to others; but when he speaks to Hosea, he is signified to address his discourse to Hosea himself. Others, however, do not want Hosea to have been the first of all the prophets on the ground of what is said: The beginning of the Lord's speaking in Hosea; but hold that it shows that the Lord spoke these things which follow first to Hosea.

And the Lord said to Hosea: Go, take for yourself a wife of fornications and children of fornications, for the land, fornicating, will commit fornication away from the Lord. LXX: And the Lord said to Hosea: Go, take for yourself a wife of fornication and children of fornication, for the land, fornicating, will commit fornication behind the Lord. The Hebrew word zanunim means not a fornicating woman and fornication, as most suppose, but many fornications. From this it is shown that this woman, whom the prophet takes in marriage, had committed fornication not once but quite often, so that the more sordid she is, the more patient is the prophet who married such a wife. And as for what is added: Take for yourself also children of fornications; for it is understood ἀπὸ κοινοῦ (from what is common, i.e. understood with both parts): Take for yourself a wife of fornications, and take for yourself children of fornications. Both can be understood: that he both receives the children whom the fornicating woman had earlier conceived from fornication, and himself begets offspring from the harlot, who are therefore to be called children of fornication, because they were begotten of a harlot. Nor is the prophet to be blamed, to follow the history for the moment, if he converted a harlot to chastity, but rather praised, because he made a good woman out of a bad one. For he who remains good is not himself polluted if he is joined to one who is bad; but he who is bad is turned to good if he follows the examples of the good. From this we understand that the prophet did not lose his chastity by being joined to a fornicating woman, but that the fornicating woman took on the chastity which she did not have before; especially since blessed Hosea did not act for the sake of luxury, nor of lust, nor of his own will, but obeyed the command of God, so that what we read done carnally in him we might show to have been done spiritually in God, who took to himself the Synagogue, that is, the people of the Jews, enslaved to fornication and lust. To her the Lord speaks through Ezekiel: And you, harlot, hear the word of the Lord. For her breasts were broken in Egypt, and she was wallowing in blood, and had been polluted up to the crown of her head, so that there was no part of her body and limbs which did not bear the stain of shame. Such a woman the Lord covered with his cloak and joined to his embraces; he gave her honey and oil and fine flour to eat, and clothed her in the most precious garments; he placed ornaments of gems on her neck; he adorned her ears with gold and most precious stone; he also furnished bracelets for her arms, so that she might practice good works. And nevertheless, despising this generosity and goodness of her husband, forgetful of her former shame, she followed her lovers, the Chaldeans and Assyrians and Egyptians, who are of great flesh. Of the type of the Savior and the Church we spoke in the little preface: that he took for himself a fornicating wife, who previously served idols. But if anyone contentious, and especially of the Gentiles, refuses to accept that it is said figuratively, and mocks the prophet for being joined to a fornicating woman, let us set against him what Greece is wont to praise and the schools of the philosophers sing in chorus. On what ground do they extol that most learned man Xenocrates, who made Polemon, a most dissolute youth, drunk and crowned with ivy among harp-girls and pipers and shameless women, obey wisdom, and changed a most shameful young man into the wisest of philosophers? Why do they raise Socrates to heaven, who transferred Phaedo, after whose name a book of Plato is called, from a brothel, where through the cruelty and avarice of his master he served the lust of many, into the Academy? And whatever they answer about these masters of philosophy, we apply to the defense of the prophet. Let this be said against the heathen and those who are like the heathen. But to our own, who nevertheless wish to accept the truth, let us briefly show this, from what is said: For the land, fornicating, will commit fornication away from the Lord: that it is not so much that the prophet was joined to a harlot as that the whole human race withdrew from fellowship with the Lord. Also, since "all the earth" is not added, it can be taken here as Judea, or properly Samaria and Israel, that is, the ten tribes, which, at the time when these things were being said, had withdrawn from the Lord.

1:3-4

And he went and took Gomer daughter of Diblaim; and she conceived and bore him a son. And the Lord said to him: Call his name Jezreel, for yet a little while and I will visit the blood of Jezreel upon the house of Jehu; and I will cause the kingdom of the house of Israel to cease. LXX: And he went, and took Gomer daughter of Diblaim; and she conceived and bore a son. And the Lord said to him: Call his name Jezreel, because yet a little while and I will avenge the blood of Jezreel upon the house of Judah; and I will cause the kingdom of the house of Israel to cease. The prophets make their promises about the coming of Christ and the calling of the Gentiles many ages later in such a way as not to neglect the present time, lest they seem not to teach an assembly called together for another purpose about the things that are at hand, but to trifle about things uncertain and future. And so this Gomer too, daughter of Diblaim, who is taken as wife by Hosea, and conceives by him, and bears him a son named Jezreel, which is interpreted seed of God, in vengeance for whose blood he overturns the kingdom of Jehu (or, as an error has wrongly prevailed, of Judah), is to be referred to the calling of the nations in such a way as to fit that time in which she is recorded to have borne a son. And not to keep the reader's eagerness waiting with a long treatment: these two women, of whom one is called Gomer and is a harlot, and bears three children, first Jezreel, second, a girl, who is called: Without Mercy, and third a male, who is himself also called: Not My People; and the other woman, who is hired for fifteen pieces of silver, and a cor, and half a cor of barley, and is called an adulteress, are referred to Israel and to Judah, that is, to the ten tribes which were in Samaria under king Jeroboam, who was of Ephraim, and to Judah, which reigned in Jerusalem from the stock of David. These are the two women who in Zechariah are said to have the wings of a hoopoe, or of a kite, or of a heron, and to go into the land of Shinar, where Babylon was founded. These women Oholah and Oholibah signify under the name of two sisters; these are shown in the two rods which Ezekiel joins into one rod. And because we are writing commentaries, not broad volumes, reserving for each section its own explanation in its place, let us now discuss only the present section. Gomer is interpreted τετελεσμένην, that is, consummated and perfected; others think that θώρακας, that is, breastplates, is signified. There are some who suppose it means measure, or bitterness, which they would say rightly if it did not have the letter gimel. Diblaim means παλάτας (cakes of pressed figs), of which there is a very great abundance in Palestine, and which the prophet Isaiah is commanded to apply to the ulcer of king Hezekiah. It is a mass of rich dried figs, which they shape after the manner of bricks, tread and press together, so that they may remain long unspoiled. Therefore Israel too, consummated and perfected in fornication, a daughter of pleasure, which seems sweet and pleasant to those who enjoy it, is taken as wife by Hosea as a type of the Lord Savior; and from her a son of God is first begotten, that is, Jezreel; and this is the metropolis of the ten tribes, in which Naboth was killed, on account of whose blood Jehu is raised up, who destroyed the house of Ahab and Jezebel. But because Jehu himself too, the avenger of just blood, walked in the ways of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel commit fornication and set up golden calves in Dan and Bethel, his kingdom too is said to be overturned; under his great-grandson Jeroboam Hosea began to prophesy; when he died, his son Zechariah succeeded to the kingdom; and in the sixth month of his rule Shallum, born of another stock, killed him. For this reason it is now said: Yet a little while and I will visit the blood of Jezreel, that is, the slaughter of my people, upon the royal house of Jehu, which at that time ruled over Israel. Nor is it surprising if the house of Jehu is overturned, since even the kingdom of the house of Israel, that is, of the ten tribes, was to be utterly destroyed after not many years. For from Zechariah son of Jeroboam, whose ancestor was Jehu, to the ninth year of Hoshea, under whom the ten tribes were led into captivity, forty-nine years are counted. And once Zechariah, who was the last of the stock of Jehu, was killed, the kings of Assyria at once took Reuben and Gad and the half tribe of Manasseh, which were beyond the Jordan; and then many cities of Samaria, and then all Naphtali, and at the last all the remaining tribes. In place of Jehu, in the common edition Judah is read; but this seems to me to have grown up not through the fault of the Seventy translators but through the ignorance of the copyists, who, not knowing Jehu, wrote Judah, which was more familiar. The type of the seed of God, and the vengeance for his blood, is referred to the Lord's passion, on account of which both the house of Judah and the kingdom of all Israel are said to be overturned.

1:5

And on that day I will break the bow of Israel in the valley of Jezreel. LXX: likewise. When I have avenged the blood of Jezreel upon the house of Jehu, and have destroyed the kingdom of Israel through the victory of the Assyrian, then on that day and at that time I will break all the strength of the army of Israel in the valley of Jezreel. We said above that Jezreel, which is now near Maximianopolis, was the metropolis of the kingdom of Samaria; near it are very broad plains, and a valley of exceeding vastness, which stretches for more than ten thousand paces. In this valley, when battle was joined, Israel was cut down by the Assyrians, that is, the ten tribes, which, on account of Jeroboam of the tribe of Ephraim, who first made a schism among the people, were called Ephraim. Sometimes, on account of Joseph, who was the father of Ephraim, it is called Joseph; sometimes Samaria, which was itself the other metropolitan city of the ten tribes, and which was afterwards called Augusta by Augustus Caesar, that is, Σεβαστὴ; in it the bones of John the Baptist are laid to rest. After the division, then, of the two and the ten tribes, because of the very great part of the multitude which followed Jeroboam, the ancient name Israel remained with the ten tribes. And on account of the tribe of Judah, which reigned in Jerusalem, the other tribes were called Judah; and at the same time the truth of the type is also unfolded. For just as, on account of the blood of Naboth, which was shed in Jezreel, the house of Ahab was destroyed, so that the prophecy of Elijah might be fulfilled; so, on account of the blood of the true Jezreel, that is, of the seed of God, the kingdom of the Jews was destroyed. In all the prophets indeed, but especially in Hosea, the ten tribes are referred to the heretics, whose multitude is very great; but the two tribes, which are called Judah, hold the person of the Church, and reign under the stock of David. The bow of the heretics, therefore, of whom it is written: The sons of Ephraim, bending and shooting the bow, turned back in the day of battle, will be broken in the valley of the seed of God, about whom they held lowly and earthly opinions.

1:6-7

And she conceived again and bore a daughter; and he said to him: Call her name: Without Mercy; for I will no more have mercy on the house of Israel, but with forgetting I will forget them. And on the house of Judah I will have mercy; and I will save them in the Lord their God; and I will not save them by bow and sword, and by war, and by horses, and by horsemen. LXX: And she conceived again and bore a daughter, and he said to him: Call her name: Without Mercy; for by no means will I any more have mercy on the house of Israel, but opposing I will oppose them; but on the sons of Judah I will have mercy, and I will save them in the Lord their God; and I will not save them by bow, nor by sword, nor by war, nor by horses, nor by horsemen. After the bow of Israel was crushed and broken in the valley of Jezreel, and the kingdom of the ten tribes was forsaken, so that they were led into captivity, there is no longer born a Jezreel, that is, the seed of God, nor a son of the male sex; but a daughter, that is, a woman, of the frail sex, and one exposed to the insults of the victors; and she is called: Without Mercy. For she was led away captive for this reason, because she did not have the mercy of God. And the indignation of the Lord must be considered, in that he says he will by no means have mercy any more on the house of Israel, but will blot them out of his memory with an eternal forgetting; for to this day they serve the kings of the Persians, and their captivity has never been released. But to the house of Judah he promises mercy, and says that he saves them in the Lord their God, either in himself who speaks; or the Father saves in the Son, according to what is written: The Lord rained from the Lord. For when Israel was handed over to the Assyrians, he saved them from the hand of Sennacherib, not by bow and sword and war and by a multitude of horsemen, but by his own strength, when he sent an angel and struck down from the army of Sennacherib king of the Assyrians in one night one hundred and eighty-five thousand. According to the type, we say that those who, on account of the blood of the seed of God, are called: Without Mercy, and dared to say: His blood be upon us, and upon our children, serve the Romans to this day; but that the house of Judah, namely those of the Jews who confessed the Lord, were saved not by the strength of an army but by the preaching of the Gospel. What we have interpreted of Israel and Judah, both according to the history and according to the type, let us refer to the conventicles of the heretics and to the Church of the Lord Savior: that, while they are left without mercy and lose their kingdom, the Church has prevailed by the power of her God.

1:8-9

And she weaned her who was Without Mercy; and she conceived and bore a son; and he said: Call his name Not My People, for you are not my people and I will not be yours. LXX: likewise. He who was called the seed of God, turned into a female and, because of the weakness of his strength and the offense of God, led into captivity, since he did not have the mercy of the Lord, is said to have been weaned not as a son but as a daughter; for the man had already lost his vigor. Whoever is weaned withdraws from the mother, no longer feeds on the parent's milk, and is sustained by food from outside. So also Israel, cast off by the Lord, hemmed in by the straits of captivity, and in Babylon sustained by unclean foods, is called not the people of God, and is struck with the eternal sentence of a foreign nation, so that he is called Not My People and is disowned forever. This we can rightly understand both of the whole people of the Jews, which, handed over to captivity because of the offense against the seed of God, lost its kingdom and its province and is called not the people of God, and also in the person of the heretics. Hence also the prophet Amos, who was of the tribe of Judah and of the little village of Tekoa, is compelled to return to his own country, so as not to prophesy in a foreign kingdom and in Samaria.

4:5b-6

By night I have made your mother silent.

My people has fallen silent, because it had no knowledge; because you have rejected knowledge, I too will reject you, so that you may not serve as priest to me; and you have forgotten the law of your God, I too will forget your sons. LXX: I have likened your mother to the night, and my people has been likened to one not having knowledge; because you have rejected knowledge, I too will reject you, so that you may not serve as priest to me; you have forgotten the law of your God, and I too will forget your sons. He calls them mother and sons, not because the mother is one thing and the sons another; but just as the Lord, speaking to the people of the Jews, said: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kill the prophets and stone those who are sent to you, how often I wished to gather your sons, as a hen gathered her chicks under her wings, and you would not? Not that Jerusalem was one thing and her people another; for he was not speaking to the rubble of the city and its timbers and stones apart from the people. So the multitude of the people and the whole crowd of the Hebrew nation to which he speaks is called mother; and the sons are either the individuals of the people, or those scattered through the towns and villages. In the night, then, and in the darkness of captivity and of grief and of pressing distress Israel is handed over, and its people falls silent in eternal silence; because it did not have knowledge of the law, nor did it keep the commandments of God, and it received back what it had done. For she herself rejected the law of God, and therefore lost the priesthood forever, worshiping the golden calves in Dan and Bethel; and because she forgot the law of God and gave herself over wholly as a slave to Egyptian idols, therefore the Lord too will forget her sons, handing them over to eternal captivity: For whoever does not know will not be known; and in the psalms we read: They did not know, nor did they understand; they walk in darkness. All the things that are said to the ten tribes we can refer to the heretics, who have left the kingdom of David and Jerusalem, that is, Christ and the Church; and therefore they are covered with eternal night, and do not have the knowledge of God, and are rejected by the Lord, so that they may not serve as priests to him, and he never remembers the sons whom they have begotten, because they have become alien sons to him.

4:7-9

According to their multitude, so have they sinned against me; I will change their glory into disgrace, and they will eat the sins of my people, and will lift up their souls to their iniquities; and it will be, as the people, so the priest; and I will visit upon him his ways, and I will repay him his thoughts. LXX: According to their multitude, so have they sinned against me; I will turn their glory into disgrace; they will eat the sins of my people, and in their iniquities they will take their souls, and it will be, as the people, so also the priest; and I will avenge upon him his ways, and I will repay him his thoughts. As many men as Israel had, so many altars did it build to demons, in whose victims it sinned against me. Therefore their glory, in which they gloried for themselves and preferred idols to God, I will change into disgrace, so that both priests and peoples are taken captive. For indeed the priests eat the sins of my people, the priests of whom it is written: Who devour my people as a meal of bread. And they eat the sins of my people for this reason, that they consent to the crimes of those who offend; for when they have seen them sinning, they not only do not reprove them, but praise and extol them, and proclaim them blessed. Of these Isaiah too speaks: My people, those who call you blessed lead you astray, and trip up the paths of your feet. Of these the psalmist also cries out: For the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul, and the unjust man is blessed. Hence the people and the priest alike will bear the sentence of an indignant God; because he will visit not only their works, which are called ways, in which they walk, but also the thoughts with which they deliberated in order to do such things. For not only the work, but also the thought of an evil work will pay the penalty. Concerning the heretics the understanding is easy: that the more numerous they are, the more they sin against God and glory in their peoples; and therefore they deceive the unhappy, so as to eat the sins of the people, and through sweet words to devour the houses of widows. For when they see some people sinning, they say: God seeks nothing else but the truth of faith; if you guard it, he does not care what you do. For by saying these things they lift up their souls in their iniquities, so that they not only do not repent nor are humbled, but rejoice in their crimes and walk with neck held high. Hence both the people and the priest, both those who have been taught and those who taught, will be bound by an equal judgment.

4:10-12

And they will eat and will not be satisfied; they have played the harlot and have not ceased, since they have forsaken the Lord in not observing; fornication and wine and drunkenness take away the heart. My people has inquired of its wood, and its staff has declared to it. For the spirit of fornications deceives them, and they have played the harlot away from their God. LXX: And they will eat and will not be filled; they have played the harlot and will not be set straight, because they have forsaken the Lord, so as not to observe; the heart of my people has taken up fornication and wine and drunkenness; they inquired by auguries, and by their rods they made announcement to it; for they have been led astray by a spirit of fornication and have played the harlot away from their God. Pleasure is insatiable, and the more of it is taken, the more hunger it creates in those who use it. On the contrary, blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be satisfied. As justice satisfies, so iniquity, having no substance, mocks with deceit those who eat empty things, and leaves the bellies of those who devour it empty. They have played the harlot and have not ceased. Their strength fails in fornication, and the desire to fornicate does not rest. The ten tribes too played the harlot with the idols of Jeroboam son of Nebat; and they forsook their God, not keeping what he had commanded when he said: You shall worship the Lord your God and serve him alone, but this is to be read ἐμφατικῶς (emphatically): Fornication and wine and drunkenness take away the heart. For just as wine and drunkenness make him who has drunk no longer master of his mind, so fornication and pleasure pervert the sense and weaken the spirit, and make of a rational man a brute animal, so that he pursues eating-houses and brothels and the dens of lusts. And when the heart has thus been moved from its place, it thinks wood and stones are gods and worships the works of its own hands. Hence the prophet too, as if astonished and full of wonder, declares: My people, which was once called by my name, has inquired of wood and of rods; a kind of divination which the Greeks call ῥαβδομαντείαν (divination by rods). Hence we also read in Ezekiel that Nebuchadnezzar mixed his rods against Ammon and Jerusalem, and the rod came out against Jerusalem; and the cause of this madness is the spirit of fornication, which deceived them, so that they played the harlot away from their God. Now fornication signifies idolatry, according to what we read in Jeremiah: And they committed adultery with wood and stone, and I said: After they have played the harlot with all these, return to me; and she did not return with her whole heart, but in falsehood. And again: You have forsaken me and said: I will go upon every high hill, and I will pour myself out in my fornication under every leafy tree. And in the psalm it is said: You have destroyed all who play the harlot away from you. For the beginning of fornication is the invention of idols. The heretics are never satisfied with their error, nor do they cease from the foulness of fornication, and daily, by not keeping the law and the holy Scriptures, they forsake the Lord, they rave and become drunk; and having lost the judgment of the mind, they worship idols which they have fashioned from their own heart, and are possessed by the spirit of fornication.

4:13a

On the tops of the mountains they sacrificed, and on the hills they burned incense; under the oak and the poplar and the terebinth, because its shade was good. LXX: On the summit of the mountains they sacrificed, and on the hills they offered victims; under the oak and the poplar and the shady trees, because the shelter was good. In the law it is commanded that no sacrifice be made to the Lord except in the place which the Lord God shall choose; and that a grove and trees not be planted beside the altar, lest, namely, a promiscuous and pleasure-loving religion overturn the austerity of the one and true religion. On the contrary, Israel sacrificed on the mountains and burned incense on the hills, loving the high places of the earth because it had forsaken God on high, and seeking the shade it had lost the truth. This is what we read of the several kings: Nevertheless he did not take away the high places; the people still offered victims and sacrificed on the high places, which in Hebrew is called bama. The heretics promise themselves loftiness of dogmas, and they sacrifice under the oak and the poplar and the terebinth, unfruitful trees, which have not the fig and the vine under which the saint is said to rest. But at times they take up for themselves the terebinth, which according to Isaiah has no leaves, so that they may seem to imitate the example of Abraham.

4:13b-14

Therefore your daughters will play the harlot, and your brides will be adulteresses.

I will not visit upon your daughters when they have played the harlot, and upon your brides when they have committed adultery; since they themselves consorted with harlots and sacrificed with the effeminate; and the people that does not understand will be beaten. LXX: Therefore your daughters will play the harlot and your brides will commit adultery, and I will not visit upon your daughters when they have played the harlot and upon your brides when they have committed adultery; because they themselves also mingled with harlots and offered victims with the initiated; and the people that understands clung to the harlot. The word cadesoth, which Aquila translated ἐνηλλαγμένων, Symmachus ἑταιρίδων, the Seventy τετελεσμένων, Theodotion κεχωρισμένων, we have rendered 'effeminate', so as to lay open the sense of the word to the ears of our own people. These are they who today at Rome, serving the mother not of the gods but of demons, are called Galli, because the Romans, in honor of Attis — whom the harlot goddess had made a eunuch —, made over men of this nation, cut off from lust, as her priests. And the men of the nation of the Gauls are unmanned for this reason: that those who had captured the city of Rome may be struck with this disgrace. Idolatry of this kind existed in Israel, the women especially worshipping Baal-peor for the size of his obscene member, whom we can call Priapus. Hence also King Asa took away the high places from the people, and priests of this kind, and deposed his mother from her august rule, as Scripture testifies, saying: And Asa did what was right in the sight of the Lord, as David his father, and he took away the effeminate from the land, and he purged all the filth of the idols which his fathers had made. Moreover he also removed Maacah his mother, so that she should not be chief in the rites of Priapus and in his grove which she had consecrated; and he overturned its cave, and broke the most foul image, and burned it in the brook Kidron. But the high places he did not take away; nevertheless the heart of Asa was perfect before God all his days. But it must be known that in the present passage he calls cadesoth the harlots, ἱερείας, that is, priestesses, made over to Priapus. In other places, however, we read cadesim for men cut off from lust, as Isaiah says: And mockers will rule over them, for which in the Hebrew is written: And cadesim will rule over them, which we have rendered 'effeminate'. But Aquila, translating ἐνηλλαγμένους, that is, changed, wished to show this: that they had changed their nature and from men had become women. Symmachus called them ἐταιρίδας, properly harlots. The Seventy, τετελεσμένους, that is, consecrated and initiated, to show that they were worshippers of idols. Theodotion, κεχωρισμένους, that is, separated from the people, men who seemed to themselves to have something more than the common crowd. We have discussed the word briefly; now let us return to the sense of the section. It is a grave displeasure, after you have sinned, not to merit the wrath of the Lord. Israel had played the harlot away from the Lord his God and been led astray by a spirit of fornication; therefore his daughters and brides also play the harlot and are left in their crime without any punishment, so that what the sons and the wife had done to their true parent and husband, they may feel in their own children and wives, and from their own pain understand the pain of God, who is so angry that he does not strike the offenders at all. The Apostle too, writing to the Romans, testifies to this in mystical speech: For, saying that they were wise, they became fools; and they changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of the image of corruptible man, and of birds, and of four-footed beasts, and of serpents. And because they had played the harlot with idols, they are struck with such a sentence: For this reason God gave them up to the desires of their heart, to uncleanness, so that they dishonor their bodies among themselves, who exchanged the truth of God for a lie; and they worshipped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed for ever. Amen. But that we may know that visitation is brought by God, as upon the wounded and the sick, for this purpose, that as by cautery and most bitter potions they may recover health and be free of diseases, let us hear God speaking through the prophet: I will visit their iniquities with the rod, and their sins with stripes; but my mercy I will not scatter from him. He, therefore, who is loved is corrected; he who is neglected is left to his sins. And so great was the number of fornicators in Israel that the vengeance was one that despaired of amendment. For what is more shameful than for men to be joined to the worship of harlots, and with the effeminate to offer the victims of their own lust? But what he adds: the people that does not understand will be beaten; for which the Seventy translated: And the people that understands clung to the harlot; this signifies that Israel is beaten in captivity and afflicted with various blows, so that through pain it may receive discipline. Concerning the heretics the understanding is easy: their daughters and brides play the harlot, that is, the souls which they have begotten in error and betrothed to their own dogmas. Men of this kind are unworthy of God's correction, since all their worship is fornication, and the shameful are mingled with the shameful; and therefore they will be beaten, so that at some time through blows they may understand what God they should seek. When you see a sinner abounding in riches, boasting of his power, rejoicing in health, delighting in his wife, surrounded by a crown of children, and that which is written being fulfilled: They are not in the labor of men, and with men they will not be scourged, say that in him the prophet's threat has been fulfilled: I will not visit upon your daughters when they have played the harlot and upon your brides when they have committed adultery.

4:15-16

If you play the harlot, Israel, let Judah at least not sin; and do not go into Gilgal; and do not go up to Beth-aven, nor swear: The Lord lives; for like a wanton cow Israel has turned aside. Now the Lord feeds them like a lamb in a broad place. LXX: But you, Israel, do not be ignorant, and Judah, do not go into Gilgal, do not go up to the house of ὢν, and do not swear: The Lord lives; for like a cow stung by the gadfly Israel has gone mad. Now the Lord feeds them like a lamb in a spacious place. For 'house of ὢν', in certain copies, and especially in Theodotion, 'house of iniquity' is read, which Aquila and Symmachus translated 'house of ἀνωφελοῦς', that is, useless, which profits nothing and by another word is called an idol. Now it is Bethel, which formerly was called house of God; after the calves were set up in it, it was called Beth-aven, that is, house of uselessness and house of the idol, which we have expressed as it is read in the Hebrew. But it seems to me that the reason why both the people of Israel in the wilderness made for themselves the head of a calf to worship, and Jeroboam son of Nebat fashioned golden calves, was this: that what they had learned in Egypt, that ἄπιν (Apis) and μνεῦιν (Mnevis), who are worshipped under the figure of oxen, are gods, they might keep in their own superstition. Let us state the sense of the section: If once, O Israel, you have been deceived by error and have mingled yourself with harlots, so that whoever filled the hand, either his own or the king's, by offering and giving him gifts, became a priest of the high places; at least you, Judah, who possess Jerusalem, and have Levites according to the law, and perform the ceremonies of the temple, ought not to follow the examples of your former sister Oholah in fornication, and worship idols along with God. Do not go into Gilgal, of which we read in this same prophet: All their wickedness is in Gilgal; in which Saul was anointed king; in which the people, coming out of the desert, first encamped and were purified by the second circumcision. From that time the error of a contrary religion grew up in a celebrated place. And do not go up to Beth-aven, that is, the place which was once called Bethel, because after golden calves were set up there by Jeroboam son of Nebat, it is called not House of God but house of the idol. For which I wonder why the Seventy translated 'house of ὢν', unless perhaps, by a common error, in place of the letter iod in the middle, which is flanked on either side by the letters aleph and nun, they supposed uau, which differs only in size. Nor swear: The Lord lives. For I do not want mention of my name to be made by your mouth, which is polluted by the remembrance of idols. For as a wanton cow, casting off the yoke of the law, so Israel, that is, the ten tribes, has turned aside from the service of the Lord. For 'wanton cow' the Seventy translated παροιστρῶσαν, one stung by the oestrus and the asilus, which is commonly called the tabanus. Of this Vergil also tells in the third book of the Georgics: "whose Roman name is asilus, the Greeks turned it oestrus, so calling it; fierce, sounding harshly; terrified by it, whole herds scatter through the woods; the air, shaken, rages with bellowing, and the woods and the bank of dry Tanagrus". Since, therefore, Israel has gone mad and, struck by the spirit of fornication, has raved with incredible fury, therefore not long afterward, but while I prophesy, while breath rules these limbs, the Lord feeds them like a lamb in a broad place. Because he had taken the metaphor from the wanton cow, or from the gadfly, he keeps it in what remains, so that he calls the captivity among the Assyrians, and the dispersion of the people of Israel in the very broad land of the Medes, the pasturing of a flock and of lambs, as in a very broad plain and in a spacious land. Concerning the heretics the understanding is ready at hand, to whom, or of whom, it is said: If once you play the harlot, heretic, at least you, churchman, do not sin, nor go into Gilgal, the conventicles of the heretics, where the sins of all are revealed, and like swine they wallow in the mire. Nor think that you are going up to the proud and arrogant fictions of false dogmas. For there is not the house of God, but the house of the idol. Nor swear by the name of Christ, whose majesty you have polluted by mingling it with idols. For like a cow stung by the gadfly, the heretics have been struck by the burning arrows of the devil and have abandoned the knowledge of the law; therefore they will be pastured on the broad and spacious way that leads to death, and the patience of the Lord, the good shepherd, will nourish them for destruction.

4:17-19

Ephraim is a partaker of idols; let him go; their banquet is set apart; in fornication they have played the harlot; his protectors loved to bring disgrace; the spirit has bound him in its wings; and they will be put to shame by their sacrifices. LXX: Ephraim, a partaker of idols, has set stumbling blocks for himself, he has provoked the Canaanites; playing the harlot they have played the harlot, they have loved disgrace from their roaring; you are a whirlwind of the spirit in its wings, and they will be put to shame by their altars. Ephraim, the tribe from which came Jeroboam son of Nebat, who first set up the golden calves in Bethel and Dan, is king of the ten tribes. O Judah, then, to whom I said above: If Israel plays the harlot, let Judah at least not sin, hear my counsel, do not spurn the words of the prophet: since Ephraim is once for all a friend and partaker of idols, let him go, lest you follow his impiety, whose worship and religion and food are set apart from your banquet. For they serve idols once for all, and sacrifice to demons, and play the harlot daily; and they love their fornication; indeed, its princes and protectors, that is, the kings, loved to bring disgrace upon the people, that is: by the vice of the princes the unhappy people took up the worship of idols, whose unclean spirit has bound Israel in its wings and does not allow him to fly about freely. They will therefore be put to shame in their sacrifices, and in their confusion they will take on the disgrace of the princes. Symmachus, for what we have translated: the spirit has bound him in its wings, rendered it into Greek in this way: as if someone were to bind the wind in the wings of the wind; that is, so as to assert that both the princes and the people, or rather both the demons and Israel, are joined together, the vain with the vain and the empty with the empty. For wind and spirit among the Hebrews are called by one word, rua. That which the Seventy set down: he has provoked the Canaanites, is not found in the Hebrew. Yet it can be interpreted thus, so that we say that Israel had such zeal in the worship of idols that he did not imitate the Canaanites, that is, the heathen, but provoked them to imitate his own error. This same thing can also be referred to the heretics, and it is said to Judah, that is, to the churchman: since Ephraim, which is interpreted καρποφόρος (fruit-bearing), promises himself a false richness of doctrine and fruitful dogmas, and is once for all a friend of demons, let him go and despise him, especially since their sacrifices are set apart from your sacrifices. For this is what he says: their banquet is set apart; they have played the harlot once for all, and their princes have deceived the unhappy people; and in place of the worship of God they have steeped them in the disgrace of idols, and the spirit of the devil has bound them in its wings, they who are carried about by every wind of doctrine and cannot remain with a steady foot in the Church. They truly will be put to shame in their sacrifices, because their bread is the bread of mourning. And what is said, he has provoked the Canaanites, can in the same sense be referred to the heretics as well: that most of the heretics have invented things so foul and unspeakable, and have such unclean sacrifices, that idolatry is inferior to them. Or at least, because Canaanite is interpreted merchant or μετάβολος, that is, one who transfers, all who make the house of the Father a house of trade, and seek gains from the peoples, and transfer the truth of the Church into a lie, are to be called Canaanites.

5:1-2a

Hear this, O priests, and attend, house of Israel, and house of the king; listen with your ears, for the judgment is for you; since you have become a snare to the watchtower and a net spread over Tabor, and you have turned aside the victims into the depth. LXX: Hear this, O priests, and attend, house of Israel, and house of the king; perceive with your ears, for the judgment is against you, because you have become a snare to the watchtower; and like a net spread over Itabyrium, which those who take the prey have fixed fast for the hunt. The priests of the ten tribes are summoned to judgment, and the kings: not that the priests are of the tribe of Levi, but that they are called priests by the people. Hence too the priests of Baal, the four hundred and fifty prophets whom Elijah, and afterwards Jehu son of Nimshi in the presence of Jonadab son of Rechab, killed, were called priests; but Israel too, that is, the people, is summoned to judgment; and no one is excepted, so that priests and people and kings, who had led the people astray and appointed the priests, may hear together what they have done and why they are handed over to their enemies. I set you, he says, as watchmen and princes among the people, and placed you on the lofty summit of dignity, so that you might guide the wandering people. But you have become a snare, and are to be called not so much watchmen and princes as hunters. For you spread a net over Mount Tabor, which the Seventy rendered Ἰταβύριον (Itabyrium), having this custom, that they inflect Hebrew names in the Greek tongue; just as Edom, that is, Esau, and Seir they always translate Idumaea. Now Tabor is a mountain in Galilee, situated in the plains, round and lofty, and it is bounded evenly on every side. Of this mountain we read in the psalm too: Tabor and Hermon will exult in your name. This mountain the Seventy also rendered Itabyrium in Jeremiah; on it birds are commonly caught with snares. And the victims, he says, you have turned aside into the depth, so that no one should repent, nor the slain lift up his head. Both the kings and the priests prevented the people from going to the temple at Jerusalem; therefore they are said to have turned aside the victims into the depth. According to tropology, the people is accused so that it may not think itself free of the crime if it was led astray by kings and priests, whom we understand as the princes of the heretics. The princes themselves are also accused, because like a snare they caught the people and, though set as watchmen in the Church, led them into error. And on Mount Tabor, lofty and most beautiful, which is interpreted coming light, they laid ambushes, so as to drag all whom they had deceived down into the depth and sacrifice them to demons, and turn aside the victims into the abyss, lest they should ever come to their senses, lest they should look up to the Church, the house of God. Some think that Tabor is interpreted pit, that is, cistern; and that it fits the present sense that the heretics have dug a pit and fallen into the ditch which they made.

5:2b-4

And I am the instructor of them all.

I know Ephraim, and Israel is not hidden from me, for now Ephraim has played the harlot, Israel is defiled; they will not give their thoughts to return to their God, for the spirit of fornications is in their midst, and they have not known the Lord. LXX: For I am your teacher. I have known Ephraim, and Israel has not departed from me, for now Ephraim has played the harlot, Israel is stained; they have not given their thoughts to return to the Lord their God, for the spirit of fornication is in them. But the Lord they have not known. O priests and kings, who have deceived my people; and you, O people, who have been deceived: you have sinned so grievously that you have not only slain the victims, but have sent them into the depth of hell. Do not think that by the greatness of your crime you have departed wholly from me. I am your teacher, or rather your instructor, who desire to amend, not to punish; and to save, not to destroy. I, he says, know Ephraim, that is, Jeroboam, by whom the people was seduced, and all the kings who followed him both in rank and in crime; and Israel is not hidden from me, that is, the people of the ten tribes, because when the king played the harlot, Israel was defiled. Finally, they will not return to the Lord, because what they wanted they have found, and the spirit of fornication, which according to the Apostle works in the sons of unbelief, possesses their captive hearts; therefore, while he reigned, they did not know the Lord, or rather they forgot their Creator. Nor will they give their thoughts to repent, they who are always advancing in perdition. For the spirit of fornication with which they played the harlot in the Church and departed from the true marriage moves in their midst; therefore they have not known the Lord.

5:5

And the arrogance of Israel will answer in his face; and Israel and Ephraim will fall in their iniquity, Judah also will fall with them. LXX: And the injury of Israel will be humbled into his face; and Israel and Ephraim will be weakened in their iniquities, Judah also will be weakened with them. The word gaon the Seventy and Symmachus translate as injury, Aquila and Theodotion as pride. Whatever, therefore, Israel has done, whether acting proudly against the Lord or venerating idols to the injury of the Creator, will answer in his face, so that he may not go away unpunished, but that his insolence may be humbled; and that the people and the kings may fall together in their iniquity, or be weakened, so that those who were strong in crime may be compelled, now weak, to return to the Lord. And this will befall not only Israel and Ephraim, that is, the ten tribes and their kings, so that they are led into captivity; but Judah also, that is, the two tribes which reigned in Jerusalem, will follow the footsteps of the captives, so that those whose crimes they imitate they may imitate also in punishment. The heretics have pride as the mother of their iniquity, while they constantly boast that they know higher things and rave in insult against the Church. But their arrogance will be weakened, and the people and the teachers will fall together; Judah also, who seems to be in the house of God, and dwells in the Church not in mind but in body, and holds in error the same opinion as the heretics, in vain lays claim to the name of churchman, because he too must be punished with the heretics. We run quickly over what is plain, so that we may linger over the more obscure.

5:6-7

With their flocks and with their herds they will go to seek the Lord and will not find; he has been taken away from them; they have transgressed against the Lord, for they have begotten strange sons; now a month will devour them with their portions. LXX: With sheep and calves they will go to seek the Lord and will not find him; he has turned aside from them, because they have forsaken the Lord; because they have begotten strange sons for themselves, now rust will devour them, and their lots. Not only Israel and Ephraim will go with flocks and herds to seek the Lord, but Judah also, of whom it is written above: Judah also will fall with them; all of them having this one contest, that they strive to appease with victims him whom they have offended by their transgressions; and they do not find him whom they seek, because he too has departed from those who depart from him. Especially since he says elsewhere also: Shall I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats? And again: I will not accept calves from your house, nor goats from your flocks. And in Isaiah: Burnt offerings of rams, and the fat of lambs, and the blood of goats and bulls I do not want. For they have transgressed against the Lord, because, playing the harlot with idols, they have begotten sons not to God but to demons. Others think that what is meant here is what is said in Ezra, a long time afterwards, when they took foreign wives, begetting children from them, and afterwards were compelled to put them away. But here it is better to take as strange sons those who were begotten in the error of idols, or those whom they led through the fire and consecrated to idols. Since, therefore, they have done this, not long afterwards, not after endless spans of years, nor, as I was formerly accustomed, foretelling things to come long after, but now and at this present time the Assyrian and the Chaldean will come; and they will devour them with their portions, or lots, that is, with the possessions which they received in the division of the land by the measure of the line. Where we said: a month will devour them, the Seventy translated rust; whereas rust, that is, ἐρυσίβη, is certainly called hasil, as they themselves also said in the prophet Joel: What the locust left, the rust has eaten, that is, hasil. But month is called hodes; accordingly Aquila translated new moons, that is, Kalends; Symmachus and Theodotion month; and the sense is: month by month the enemy will come and will lay everything waste. Let us read the books of Kings and Chronicles, and we shall find that under King Pekah, who reigned over the ten tribes, Tiglath-pileser king of the Assyrians came and carried a great part of the people of Samaria away to the Assyrians; at which time among the Greeks it was the second year of the first Olympiad; and among the Latins — Rome not yet founded — Amulius was reigning at Alba in his twentieth year, he whom Romulus afterwards drove from the kingdom. The heretics suppose that they appease God by a multitude of sacrifices; and churchmen, that by alms they redeem the sins in which they persist; whereas every sacrifice blots out past crimes, not present ones; therefore they do not find the Lord, who is taken away from such men and withdraws far off. These, indeed, have transgressed against God, and have begotten strange sons not to Christ but to the devil; therefore at all times their works are accursed, and all that they do the rust of bloody color lays waste, because they are next to blood and slaughter. Now rust is properly said to come down in the night dew and to stain the grain, still milky in the ear, with the color of vermilion or of red ochre; and fittingly, according to the ἀναγωγήν (anagogy), it lays waste the lots of the heretics, of whom it is said: Their lots will not profit them.

Book Two

He who sails often sometimes suffers a storm; he who frequently sets out on the road either endures the attacks of robbers or at least fears them; and in every art both glory and detraction arise, as with favorable and adverse winds, while either friends praise more than is deserved or enemies disparage more than is just; and you would rarely find one who, in either direction, is led not by favor or hatred but by the fairness of the matter. This I see happening to me as I toil in the explanation of the Scriptures. For some despise it as trifling, and look down with wrinkled nose on whatever we have said; others, out of hatred of our name, consider not the matter but the persons, and approve the silence of others rather than our zeal. There are those who assert that we act boldly in having taken up a work which none of the Latins before us has dared to attempt. Certain men think themselves eloquent and learned in this, if they disparage another's work, and they judge not what they themselves can do, but what we cannot. But you, Pammachius, who commanded us to do this, must needs be the supporter of your own command, and trample with the evangelical foot the Amafiniuses and Rabiriuses of our time, who out of good Greek make Latin that is not good, and, themselves tongueless, translate the most eloquent men; you must burn the viper and the scorpion with the cautery and crush them with your sole, as in the fables of the poets, and pass by the dogs of Scylla and the deadly songs of the Sirens with a deaf ear; so that we may be able alike to hear and to know what Hosea the prophet foretells, for the explanation of whom we shall dictate the second book. And although I rejoice in your support, and am glad that in the first city of the world I have a defender who is first both in nobility and in religion, yet I would rather wish that to befall me which Livy writes of Cato, whose glory no one profited by praising nor harmed by censuring, since men endowed with the highest talents did both. He means M. Cicero and C. Caesar, of whom the one wrote praises of the aforesaid man, the other censures. For while we live and are contained in a fragile vessel, the zeal of friends seems to help us and the reproaches of rivals to harm us. But after earth has returned to its own earth, and pale death has taken away both us who write and those who judge of us, and another generation has come, and, the first leaves falling, a green forest has grown up in their place, then talents alone are judged, without the dignity of names; nor does he who is going to read consider whose is what he is going to read, but of what kind it is; whether he be a bishop or a layman, emperor and lord, soldier and slave; whether he lie in purple and silk or in the meanest cloth, he will be judged not by the difference of honors but by the merit of his works.

Blow the horn in Gibeah, the trumpet in Ramah; howl in Beth-aven, behind your back, Benjamin; Ephraim will be in desolation in the day of rebuke; among the tribes of Israel I have shown faithfulness. LXX: Blow the trumpet upon the hills, sound upon the high places, proclaim in the house of ὤν; Benjamin has fallen out of his mind, Ephraim has become a desolation in the days of rebuke; among the tribes of Israel I have shown faithful things. The months one by one, or the rust, will devour Ephraim and Israel and Judah with their portions. Therefore I command you who hear to sound not with a loud voice but with a clear horn; for clear hearing is needed, so that all who are round about may hear. Blow the horn in Gibeah, whose etymology the Seventy translated as hills; and the trumpet in Ramah, which is interpreted high places; now these are two cities in the tribe of Benjamin, neighbors to one another, that is, Gibeah, in which Saul was born, and Ramah, which is next to Gibeah, situated at the seventh milestone from Jerusalem, and which the king of Israel tried to seize, in order to shut off the going out and the coming in of the tribe of Judah. In Gibeah, therefore, and in Ramah, sound out with a clear horn and trumpet; of these the horn is a shepherd's instrument and is made of curved horn; hence it is properly called in Hebrew sophar, in Greek κερατίνη. The trumpet, however, is made of bronze or of silver, and sounds in wars and at solemn festivals. Over Beth-aven, which was once called Bethel, and is in the tribe of Ephraim, where the golden calf was, there is need not of blaring and sounding but of howling, because captivity is near. And he said beautifully that Beth-aven is behind the back of Benjamin; for where the tribe of Benjamin ends, not far off, in the tribe of Ephraim, this city was founded. For this reason, then, I say and command: Blow the horn in Gibeah, and the trumpet in Ramah, and howl in Beth-aven, because the royal house of Ephraim, or Ephraim himself, that is, the dominion of Israel, will now be in desolation, and captivity is close at hand. In the day of the rebuke and punishment of the ten tribes of Israel I have shown my words to be faithful, the words I threatened through the prophets, so that what I had foretold in word I might prove in deed. Some, following the Seventy, who said house of ὢν, have interpreted Beth-aven as the city of the sun; and, calling Christ the sun of justice, they would have his city be the Church. This displeases me; for the house of the idol, which Aquila translated useless house, cannot be referred to the Church even according to tropology. But let us say this: that the heretics, who in Gibeah and Ramah promise themselves lofty knowledge, are bidden to howl in the house of the idol, and are not before the face of Benjamin, which is interpreted son of the right hand, but behind his back, where he has no eyes. For all the boasting of Ephraim, which is interpreted fruitfulness, will shortly be in desolation; and when the day of judgment, he says, and the time of rebuke has come, I will show by deeds that my words are not empty. Where we translated behind your back, Benjamin, the Seventy rendered Benjamin has been moved in mind, always fitting ἔκστασιν, that is, ecstasy of mind, to Benjamin, the churchman. Hence also in the sixty-seventh (68th) psalm it is said: There is Benjamin, a youth, in ecstasy of mind. And in the blessings of Jacob, under the person of Benjamin, of which tribe the apostle Paul was, we read it written: Benjamin is a ravening wolf; in the morning he will eat the prey, and at evening he will give food. For he who in the beginning persecuted the Church afterwards bestowed the nourishment of the Gospel on believers throughout the whole world. Hence also Saul, who was of the tribe of Benjamin, prophesied in ecstasy of mind all day until evening among the band of the prophets.

5:10

The princes of Judah have become like those who take up the boundary; upon them I will pour out my wrath like water. LXX: The princes of Judah have become like those who move boundaries; upon them I will pour out my onrush like water. When Ephraim and Israel had been led into captivity and their land reduced to a wilderness, the princes of Judah, who ought to have wept, and to have exhorted their own people to withdraw from idolatry lest they too should suffer the like, began to rejoice and be glad that the land of those tribes lay open to them to possess more widely; and they became like those who take up the boundaries of Israel, passing beyond their own borders and desiring to extend their kingdom and possession into the places that once were Israel's. Therefore the Lord says: Upon these very princes of Judah too the Babylonians will come, and will thus overwhelm them like an onrush of water, not by their own strength but by my indignation. He says also to the princes of Judah, that is, of the Church, that they ought not to exult and to reckon the perdition of the heretics their own salvation, but rather to lament that those have perished. Hence also the apostle Paul teaches churchmen not to glory over the breaking of the Jewish branches, but rather to fear lest they too be broken. And in another place: Who, he says, is made to stumble, and I do not burn? Otherwise, upon such princes, who glory in the miseries of others and think they stand precisely because others fall, the Lord will pour out his wrath like water. For wrath the Seventy translated ὅρμημα, that is, onrush; and some think it is to be taken as a blessing, according to what we read elsewhere: The onrush of the river makes glad the city of God. But it is more correctly taken as wrath — as all except the Seventy have translated —, especially since the word pouring out goes with wrath, as the prophet says to the Lord: Pour out your wrath upon them, and let the fury of your wrath take hold of them. The princes of Judah move the boundaries which their fathers set when they exchange the truth for a lie and preach something other than what they received from the apostles.

5:11

Ephraim suffering wrong, broken in judgment, because he began to go after filth. LXX: Ephraim has oppressed his adversary, has trampled on judgment, because he began to go after vain things. If Ephraim is impious, and on account of his impiety will be in desolation, how is it now said: Ephraim suffering wrong, broken in judgment? For he who suffers wrong and is broken has his judgment unjustly overborne, all the more since a just cause is set down why he was handed over to captivity. For there follows: because he began to go after filth, that is, after idols, which are compared to filth. What he says, then, is this: Ephraim is oppressed by the Assyrians, first by Pul, then by Tiglath-pileser, afterwards by Shalmaneser; not that those who oppressed him were just, and that he was therefore handed over to them, but that those who were once my people, since I abandon them, are handed over to punishment; and in this there seems to be wrong, not in the eyes of God, who passes a just sentence, but of those who endure the torments, while they are yielded up to adversaries worse than themselves. And the Lord gives the reason why in this respect they endure wrong, and are handed over to worse men, and justice is not kept toward them: because Ephraim, that is, Jeroboam, began to go after idols and to forsake God; for it was he who fashioned the golden calves. According to the translation of the Seventy, Ephraim, that is, Jeroboam, oppressed his adversary Rehoboam, that is, the tribe of Judah, and oppressed him in this, that, having deserted Jerusalem and the temple, he followed the images of Egyptian filth. Concerning the heretics the sense is plain: that by their false sophisms and by dialectical art they often oppress churchmen; but when they have done this, they follow not the cleanness of the true faith but the filth of falsehood; and, handed over to the devil and his angels, they seem to themselves to suffer wrong, and that the truth of judgment has been broken in their case. In another way: Israel is laid waste, and all his labors are plundered when the Assyrian comes, and he loses God as his protector; and he is broken in judgment for this reason, that he followed idols.

5:12

And I am like a moth to Ephraim, and like rottenness to the house of Judah. LXX: And I am like a disturbance to Ephraim, and like a goad to the house of Judah. In the Hebrew language a moth is called As; recob is rottenness and decay; of which the one consumes garments, the other wood; for which the LXX translated ταραχὴν, that is, tumult or disturbance, and goad. And it must be noted that the moth is referred to Ephraim, and rottenness to the house of Judah. Just as, therefore, the moth consumes garments, and rottenness or decay consumes wood (both of which happen over a long time), so God, giving the ten tribes, and afterwards the two, room for repentance over a long time, and calling them to salvation, while they treasure up for themselves wrath in the day of wrath, becomes as it were a moth and rottenness; not that God is a moth or rottenness, or a disturbance or a goad, but that to those who suffer punishment all these things seem so. First, therefore, Ephraim and Israel were consumed, then the house of Judah also followed; not Judah himself, but his house; otherwise there is reserved from the race of Judah the one for whom it was laid up. And he was the expectation of the nations. But when the heretics, who are understood as Ephraim, have been punished, Judah too, that is, those who remain with the Church and are held fast by either the errors or the vices of the heretics, will be subject to a like sentence.

5:13

And Ephraim saw his sickness, and Judah his bond; and Ephraim went to Assyria and sent to the avenging king; and he will not be able to heal you, nor will he be able to loose the bond from you. LXX: And Ephraim saw his infirmity, and Judah his pain, and Ephraim went to the Assyrians, and sent envoys to King Jareb; and he will not be able to free you, nor will he make the pain cease from you. "Envoys" is not found in the Hebrew. And where the Seventy put Jareb, we, following Symmachus, have translated "avenger"; for Aquila and Theodotion rendered "judge". That Jareb signifies avenger and judge, the name of Gideon shows: when the worshipers of Baal demanded him for punishment, because he had overthrown the grove and the altars of Baal, his father answered: Let Baal avenge himself, or let Baal judge him; and he was called, it says, Jerubbaal, that is: let Baal avenge himself. Ephraim, therefore, perceiving his infirmity, and Judah his bond, because he had been bound together with the ten tribes in sin, sought help not from God, who was able to loose them, but from the king of the Assyrians. We read that under King Menahem, who ruled over the ten tribes, Israel sent gifts to the Assyrians; and that Judah, under King Ahaz, begged the protection of Tiglath-pileser, king of the Assyrians, who, since God opposed them, were not able to free them, nor to loose the bond of captivity. We can also refer the bond, for which in the Hebrew is written mezur, and which Aquila rendered ἐπίδεσιν or συνδεσμὸν, that is, a binding together or a conspiracy, to that time when Rezin and Pekah son of Remaliah laid waste many thousands of men of the tribe of Judah, in that Judah in vain begged against the two kings the help not of God but of the Assyrians. Some, according to tropology, refer Ephraim and Judah to the heretics and to churchmen; because both they and Judah, bound together by the bonds of sins, according to what is written: Each one is bound fast by the cords of his own sins, sent to Assyria and to the avenging king, that is, to the devil, of whom we read: That you may destroy the enemy and the avenger. And because they did not entreat the true helper or judge, therefore he made them remain in the pain of sickness and in the bonds of their offenses. I have read in someone's commentaries that King Jareb is interpreted as Christ. And because there follows: He will not be able to heal you, he argued from this that Christ cannot heal the heretics or the sinners among churchmen in the time of judgment, where there is no mercy, according to what is written: But in hell who will confess to you? And that he cannot heal or free them is by no means through his own weakness, but through the desert of those who have asked for help too late. Just as the Lord too is said not to have been able to do signs in his own country; and the cause why he was not able, he explains: Because, it says, they did not believe in him. Let him have said these things; let us interpret the avenging king in a bad sense. In place of what we have expounded as Jareb, that is, avenger, others wrongly read Iarim, with the letter mem, which is translated "forests"; whence Kiriath-jearim is interpreted "village of forests".

5:14-15

For I am like a lioness to Ephraim, and like a lion's whelp to the house of Judah. I, I will seize and go; I will carry off, and there is none who may rescue. Going, I will return to my place, until you fail and seek my face. LXX: For I am like a panther to Ephraim, and like a lion in the house of Judah. And I will snatch, and go, and carry off, and there will be none who may rescue; I will go and return to my place, until they perish and seek my face. While they go to Assyria and send to the vindicating or avenging king, who will not be able to heal them nor to loose the bond that binds them, I will show that, when I oppose, all the help of men is vain; for I will be like a lioness to Ephraim, and like a lion's whelp to the house of Judah. For lioness, which in Hebrew is called sohel, the Seventy rendered panther, which is said alike in Greek and in Latin, and can be taken both as the name of a beast and as "every beast"; so that whatever is savage in beasts you may recognize in the indignation of God. Nothing is swifter than the panther, nothing stronger than the lion; in the panther is signified the swift destruction of the kingdom of Samaria by the Assyrians; and in the lion is shown the most mighty kingdom of the Chaldeans, which some time afterwards came against Jerusalem and Judah. And because he had said that he was a lioness or a panther, and a lion, he keeps the metaphor, and says: I, I will seize and go; I will carry off, and there is none who may rescue. It is asked: if, when God seizes, carries off and holds, no one can snatch from his hands, according to what is written: No one can snatch out of the hand of my Father, how was Judas snatched from the hand of God by his betrayal? To which we will briefly answer that no one can snatch from the hand of God; but that he who is held can, by his own will, fall out of the hand of God. And as for what follows: Going, I will return to my place, by the place of God we ought to understand his magnificence and majesty; so that he no longer, by way of dispensation, descends to men, is angry, has mercy, forgets, becomes like a panther, is turned into a lion, is changed into beasts; but disregards human affairs and allows those whom he formerly protected to be subjected to their enemies, so that they may waste away, and fail, and perish, and afterwards seek the face of the Lord, and say: Make your face shine, and we shall be saved. And: Show us, O Lord, your mercy, and grant us your salvation. To the heretics too, and to a negligent Church, God is turned into a panther and a lion; and he will take from them the prey which they formerly snatched from the Church, so that those who had perished while free may be saved by being captured; and he will by no means dwell in the conventicles of the perverse, but will return to his place, of which he says: I am in the Father and the Father in me, and he will spurn and despise them, until they fail in their impiety and seek through repentance him by whom they had been abandoned. Others think that the place of God is heaven, to which God, offended by those who dwell on earth, returns, and will make them perish who, by the greatness of their sins, have turned the most merciful Lord into the rage of wild beasts against themselves.

6:1-3

In their tribulation they will rise early to me: come, and let us return to the Lord, for he has seized and he will heal us; he will strike and he will cure us; he will make us live after two days; on the third day he will raise us up, and we shall live in his sight; we shall know and we shall follow, that we may know the Lord. LXX: In their tribulation they will watch early for me, saying: let us go and return to the Lord our God, for he will scourge and he will heal us; he will strike and he will cure us; he will make us whole after two days; on the third we shall rise again and we shall live before him and we shall know; let us pursue, that we may know the Lord. Therefore God has handed Ephraim and Judah over to captivity, and there is none who can snatch from his hand, and he will return to his place until they fail; and they will seek his face, so that him whom they had not felt as gracious and present, they may seek again as angry and absent; and in their tribulation, when the light of repentance has risen for them, they may rise early to him, according to what we read in Isaiah: In tribulation I remembered the Lord. And in the first of the psalms of ascents: To the Lord I cried when I was in tribulation, and he heard me. And when they have risen early to the Lord, what will they say? Come, and let us return to the Lord. They are not content with their own salvation, but urge one another on to return to the Lord whom they had left, whom they had deserted because of their sins, by whom they had been abandoned. For he has seized and he will heal us; he who had said above: I, I will seize and go, will strike and heal us. For what we have said, "he will cure", all alike translated μοτώσει (will dress with lint); now properly μότα is the name for the little linen pads that are inserted into wounds, to eat away the putrid flesh and draw out the pus; and it belongs to the art of physicians to heal great wounds over a long time and to restore health through pain. The Lord therefore strikes and cures us; for whom the Lord loves he rebukes, and he chastises every son whom he receives; not only does he cure, but he makes alive after two days, and on the third day, rising again from hell, he raises up with himself the whole race of men. And when he has cured those who were struck, and made alive those who were cured, and raised up those who were made alive, then we shall live in his sight, we who, while he was absent, lay dead. And we shall live in his sight; we shall know him and follow him with all zeal, that we may know the Lord, by whose rising on the third day we have risen. By these words is explained what we have often already pointed out, that both Israel and Judah, that is, the ten and the two tribes, will then have one shepherd, and David as king, when they have believed in the Lord who rises again; and that the Jews vainly promise themselves dreams of a thousand years, since the salvation of all has been promised on the third day, on which the Lord rose again from hell. The Hebrews interpret the second day as the coming of their Christ, and the third day as the judgment, when they are to be saved. Granting this, let them answer us what the first day is, that is, the first coming of the Savior. And when they cannot answer, let us conclude that the first day is, according to what they themselves would have, the coming of the Savior in humility, the second in glory, the third in the guise of one who judges. But those who accept the second and the third testify that they have lost the first, because a second and a third cannot be so called without a first.

His going forth is prepared like the dawn, and he will come to us like the early rain, and the late rain to the earth. LXX: We shall find him prepared like the morning, and he will come to us like the early and the late rain to the earth. Come, he says, and let us return to the Lord, for he who has seized also will heal us, will strike and cure us, and will make us live after two days, and on the third day will raise us up; he himself and no other is prepared for us like the morning and the dawn. Of whom too is the title of the twenty-first (22nd) psalm: For the morning taking up; although in the Hebrew it is written: For the morning hart, because, having slain death and the twisting and ancient serpent, he longs to climb to the mountains; and he himself, the darkness scattered, rises for us as the sun of justice, to illuminate our blindness. And beautifully he says: his going forth is prepared. Of which, according to tropology, we read in the eighteenth (19th) psalm: And he, like a bridegroom, goes forth from his chamber. He is not only called morning, and dawn, and daybreak; but he will come to us like the early rain, and the late rain to the earth. We receive Christ as the early rain when the foundations of faith have been laid in us, and we shall receive him as the late rain when, the crops being ripe, we shall gather eternal fruits and be stored away in the Lord's granaries. Therefore the Jews, who did not receive the early rains and cast their seed without rains, will not receive the fruit of the crops in the last time. This is the rain of which the Lord makes a promise, saying: I will give you the early and the late rain. According to allegory, the early rain is given when we know in part; the late rain is given when that which is perfect comes. And the Lord is always prepared for those who rise early, who can say: I will rise at daybreak. And: O God, my God, to you I watch from the light. My soul has thirsted for you.

6:4-5

What shall I do to you, Ephraim? What shall I do to you, Judah? Your mercy is like a morning cloud, and like the dew passing away in the morning; for this reason I have hewn them by the prophets, I have slain them by the words of my mouth, and your judgments will go forth like the light. LXX: What shall I do to you, Ephraim? What shall I do to you, Judah? But your mercy is like a morning cloud, and like the dew passing away in the morning; therefore I have cut down your prophets, I have slain them by the words of my mouth, and your judgment will go forth like the light. When he says: What shall I do to you, Ephraim? What shall I do to you, Judah? he shows the affection of a parent toward lost sons. According to what we read in Isaiah: What is there that I ought to have done further for my vineyard, and have not done for it? And in Micah: My people, what have I done to you, and in what have I been troublesome to you? Answer me, for I brought you out of the land of Egypt, I freed you from the house of servants, and I sent before your face Moses, and Aaron, and Miriam. What then shall I do to you, Ephraim, what shall I do to you, Judah? Your mercy, with which I have always had mercy on you, has passed away like a morning cloud, and like the dew rising in the morning, which is dried up when the sun has risen. For already captivity is near, already I see you led in chains to the Assyrians and the Babylonians; I have hewn you by the prophets and threatened you with terrible words, I have brought out the scalpel, fire and cautery; so that you who despised me when I was merciful might fear me when offended; and I have slain the negligent by the words of my mouth, so that I might punish sinners by the terror of words before captivity was at hand. And all these things I have done so that the truth of the judgment with which I am about to judge you might appear, and no one might doubt that you have justly suffered what you suffer. For what is I have hewn them by the prophets, the Seventy translated: I have cut down your prophets, understanding the false prophets themselves to have been slain by the Lord; so that those who had been the cause of error, by promising prosperity, might, when slain, be turned into an occasion of salvation. And the sense is: Lest you should say: We believed the prophets; I have slain even them, so that every occasion of sinning might be taken away from you. We read in the book of Kings that three hundred prophets of the Baalim were slain under Elijah, and another countless multitude under Jehu, who overthrew the house of Ahab. We perceive these same things to have been said also to the heretics, and to the true Judah, who is to suffer the like: that the Lord calls them to mercy and desires that they return to salvation. But they pursue the delights and refreshments of this age, like a cloud and dew that quickly passes away; to whom it is said in the Gospel: Fool, this night they require your soul of you; and the things you have prepared, whose will they be? And that rich man clothed in purple, who despised Lazarus lying before his gates, learned that all he had enjoyed had passed away like a cloud and dew. But God always slays the prophets of the heretics, when he threatens them with eternal punishments, and takes them away from the true life, and leaves them to the death of their crimes. But let us love that cloud which is everlasting, and which protects us from the heat of this world, sitting upon which the Lord came into Egypt and broke all the images of the Egyptians. Let us love that dew of which Moses speaks: Let my words come down like the dew. And of which Isaiah says: The dead will rise again, and those who are in the tombs will be raised up; for the dew that is from you is their health. There are some who think that the prophets who were slain are holy men, because on account of a sinful people even prophets were slain and handed over to their enemies.

6:6-7

For I desired mercy and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings; but they, like Adam, have transgressed the covenant; there they have dealt treacherously against me. LXX: For I desire mercy and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings; but they are like a man passing over the covenant. What follows in the Seventy: There Gilead despised me, a city that works vain things, and the rest, must be fitted to the following section; let us discuss what we have set down: I have hewn them by the prophets, I have slain them by the words of my mouth, I have threatened grievous things, so that I might have mercy on the penitent, so that I might stretch out my hand to those who have fallen and are rising. For I do not delight in sacrifices and victims, and in a multitude of burnt offerings. My victims and burnt offerings are the salvation of those who believe, the conversion of sinners. But they have imitated Adam, so that what he had done in paradise, passing over my covenant and law, these did on earth. And there, that is, in paradise, all have dealt treacherously against me, in the likeness of the transgression of Adam. For it is no wonder if what went before in the parent is condemned in the children also. Daily God calls to repentance both those who are outside the Church and those who sin while dwelling in the Church, and says to them: I desire mercy and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings. But they offer sacrilegious bread, and give alms, and appear to pursue humility; these, then, if they are truly done, I interpret as burnt offerings. But since they have abandoned the knowledge of God, in vain, with the head of faith cut off, do they boast that they have the other members; for they have transgressed the covenant of God in the Church, just as Adam transgressed in paradise; and they show themselves imitators of their ancient parent, so that, as he was cast out of paradise, so these too may be cast out of the Church.

6:8-9

Gilead, a city of those who work an idol, supplanted by blood, and like the jaws of robber men (or: of a robber; for it can be read either way). LXX: There Gilead despised me, a city that works vain things, and troubles the water, and your strength is that of a pirate man. We read that in Ramoth-gilead Jehu was anointed king, he who mingled blood with blood, and overthrew the house of Ahab, and before sunrise ordered heaps of the heads of his sons to be set up; in this city across the Jordan, in the possession of the tribe of Gad, an idol was consecrated; it was inhabited by priests, for it too was a city of fugitives. Therefore, the more celebrated it was and the greater its authority, because it had been assigned as the portion of the priests, so much the more was it, for Israel dwelling across the Jordan, the beginning of idolatry and of all evils, so that those who had sinned first were the first to be taken captive by the Assyrians. And since that province itself is full of robberies, he compares them to robbers: as those lie in wait for travelers, so the priests lay in wait for the simplicity of the people. Moreover, according to tropology, Gilead is interpreted 'migration of testimony'; and it despises God when it twists the testimonies of the Scriptures into perverse dogmas, and all its works are vain, and it troubles the waters of the Church, and out of the purest springs makes muddy and filthy streams, which stain rather than cleanse the baptized. And all the strength of this city is like that of a pirate man, while it imitates the devil, who in the sea of this world, through which ships pass, lies in wait for those who strive to reach the harbor. Finally, Symmachus translated more plainly, saying: And your jaws like those of a man lying in ambush. Of pirates of this kind we read in Job: There is no delay for pirates. For although in the present world they seem to vaunt themselves, and to trouble the waters, and to work vain things, and to practice piracy, yet there is no delay of the punishments, which will swiftly overtake them.

A partner of the priests who kill on the way those going from Shechem, because they have wrought wickedness. LXX: The priests hid the way of the Lord, they killed Shechem, because they wrought iniquity. Symmachus translated this passage thus: The fellowship of the priests on the way were killing Shechem. Theodotion in this way: The priests hid the way, they were killing on the back. Aquila: The partnership of the priests on the way were slaying shoulders; and when we sought their meaning according to the history from a Hebrew, it was explained to us thus: The priests of Bethel, or rather the fanatics of Beth-aven, at the times of Passover and Pentecost and Tabernacles, when one had to go through Shechem, which today is called Neapolis, to Jerusalem, where alone it was lawful to sacrifice victims, used to post robbers along the road to lie in wait for those going, so that they might worship the golden calves in Dan and in Beth-aven rather than God in Jerusalem and in the temple. But when he says: The partnership and fellowship of the priests, it signifies their conspiracy and their agreement in the bad sense. But if we read, as we have translated: partners of the priests, it must be referred, he says, to Gilead, which works an idol; and it is supplanted by blood, because it followed the impiety of the priests and is given over to robberies and blood. This is what they may say; let us say that the heretics shut off the way, so that we may not go from Shechem, that is, from good works, to Jerusalem, that is, into the Church. These are like the jaws of robber men, and they kill those who desire to go along the way of this world to the truth. Shechem is interpreted ὦμοι, that is, shoulders; in shoulders we understand work, and all the false priests hide the way, and kill men with evil works, so that they may not reach Jerusalem. And that the shoulder signifies work, this shows: Put your heart on your shoulder, that is, turn what you understand into works. And of Issachar we read that he put his shoulder under to labor, and is a farmer.

6:10-11

Of this crime and terrible horror Jeremiah too speaks: The heaven was astounded at this, and the earth shuddered exceedingly. For what is more horrible than that the ten tribes suddenly went over to the worship of idols? Hence it is said to their metropolis: Take away your calf, Samaria, in which Ephraim first played the harlot, that is, Jeroboam of the tribe of Ephraim; and while he played the harlot, Israel was defiled, namely the people of Samaria, which for the most part held the common name of Israel. Hence the word is turned to Judah also: You too, Judah, set a harvest for yourself; and the sense is: Do not think yourself secure because Israel is led away captive; you too, prepare your crops to be reaped; for not much later you will be led captive to Babylon, and the time of your reaping will come. And when the Chaldeans have reaped you, I will turn back again the captivity of my people, and under Cyrus king of the Persians and Artaxerxes I will bring my people back. And note how pointedly the captivity of Judah and its return are prophesied together; but of Israel, that is, of the ten tribes, there is silence even now, and if ever something favorable is said, it is put off to the coming of Christ. But in the house of the heretics we see a horrible thing daily, the teachers first playing the harlot, and the people who are led on by them being defiled. Judah too, that is, the Church, is commanded that it too should prepare for itself, because of its sins, a harvest, or a vintage, when the time of judgment comes. But it is pardoned, and the Lord promises it forgiveness, because whom he loves he rebukes, and he chastises every son whom he receives, so that, tested and purged, he may store him in his treasuries. Some refer what is said: Judah, begin your vintage, or, set a harvest for yourself, to the good sense, so that, Israel having been punished, this one receives the fruits of its works, according to what is written: Those who sow in tears will reap in joy. To us the former sense is more pleasing.

7:1

LXX: likewise. Israel often received many wounds of idolatry, and above all that one when in the desert they cast the head of a calf and said: These are your gods, Israel, who brought you out of the land of Egypt. Hence I, who prefer the repentance of the sinner to his death, and who afterwards say in the Gospel: The healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are sick, have tried to heal the wounds of my people. And while I was treating these with every art, so that the wretched people might be cured, suddenly Jeroboam of the tribe of Ephraim arose, to make the golden calves, and the malice of Samaria, which followed the impious king, was uncovered; for both king and people wrought a lie, that is, an idol. For just as an image is contrary to God, so a lie is contrary to the truth. And the king himself entered like a thief to the people of Israel, and like a robber plundered the unhappy people of God of their help. And the sense is: When I wished to blot out the old sins of my people on account of their ancient idolatry, Ephraim and Samaria found new idols. Thieves lie in wait and deceive by hidden fraud; robbers boldly carry off what belongs to others. For those who steal, steal by night and in darkness. For they cannot strip those whom they have taught of the garment of Christ unless they have led them out of the Church and made them walk in the perverse way of their doctrines. By the thieves and robbers who came before the Lord we ought to understand not Moses and the prophets, who are always praised by the mouth of the Savior, but the false prophets; and afterwards the heretics, who were not sent by the Lord but came of their own will.

7:2-3

And lest perhaps they say in their hearts that I have remembered all their wickedness, now their devices have surrounded them; they have been done before my face. LXX: That they may sing together as those who sing in their hearts: I have remembered all their wickednesses; now their thoughts have surrounded them, they have been done against my face. Lest perhaps, he says, they say in their hearts: God has brought old sins back upon us, and we are paying for the offenses of our fathers; they ate the sour grape, and our teeth were set on edge; for that reason I will recount in the following discourse what they have done now and at present, while I look on, and what they do daily; and I will show them their devices, or the thoughts with which they have most zealously pursued evil, and the things they have done in my presence, not fearing my face. But what we read in the Seventy: that they may sing together as those who sing in their hearts, is to be referred to this: that the thief entered, or the robber plundered outside, for this reason, lest, remaining in their former riches and garments, they should reject agreement with the thief and robber; but that, when they have been plundered, they should sing together with them and be made of one heart. Therefore let them receive what they have done, and all their thoughts or works will not merit my regard. The heretics too cannot plead old sins against God, since daily they add new impiety to their ancient works, and perish with the lost, and are bound by their own errors, and, though they think they are hiding from the Lord, are not able to escape his eyes.

In their wickedness they made the king glad, and in their lies the princes. LXX: In their wickednesses they made the kings glad, and in their lies the princes. He sets out what they did before his face: In their wickednesses they made King Jeroboam glad, and in their lies the princes who had been set over the people under Jeroboam. The heretics too, in the wickedness of their works, have made the king, the devil, glad, and in the lies of their perverse dogmas, the princes, no doubt those of this world, whose false wisdom God destroys. Among the heretics we can call king the one who first invented the heresy, and princes those who, set over the peoples of the heretics, claim for themselves a false priesthood. And at the same time it must be noted that the opposing powers and the rulers and princes of this darkness rejoice in our sins.

7:4

All are adulterers, like an oven heated by the baker; the city rested a little from the mixing of the leaven, until the whole was leavened. LXX: All are adulterers, like an oven burning for baking, to the scorching of the flame, from the mixing of the leaven, until the whole was leavened. Those who in their wickedness made the king glad, and in their lies the princes, are all adulterers; and like an oven they were heated by Jeroboam with the fire of idolatry, so as to bake the bread of impiety; and he, when he had sent the burning of error into their souls, like a furnace and an oven first heated with fire, rested a little, so as not to force the people but to leave them to their own will, until the whole lie was leavened; for whatever is done by compulsion is quickly undone, but what we seize upon by our will endures. Therefore here too, since he had taken the metaphor from an oven, which is heated for baking loaves, he keeps it in the rest, so as to show, in the mixing of the leaven, the consent of the whole people; by which, namely, both king and people fell with equal ardor into idolatry. No one doubts that the hearts of the heretics are heated with the fire of the devil, so that the bread of the Antichrist may be baked in them; therefore at first they rest quiet in the Church and speak in secret, and promise all things peaceable, so that the cancer may creep little by little into the peoples, and the leaven of their doctrine; which the Lord too understood when he says: Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees; when it has swollen in the hearts of the deceived, then they break out into open madness; and in them is fulfilled what is said by the apostle John: They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would surely have remained with us.

All night he slept baking them; in the morning he himself was kindled like a fire of flame; all were heated like an oven and devoured their judges; all their kings have fallen; there is none among them who cries to me. LXX: The day of your kings. The princes began to rage from wine; he stretched out his hand with the pestilent, because their hearts were kindled like an oven, while they were cast down headlong all night. Ephraim was filled with sleep; morning came; he was set on fire like a flame of fire. All were heated like an oven, a burning fire, and they ate up their judges. All their kings have fallen; there is not among them one who calls on me. An obscure passage, and one needing the close attention of the reader, so that we may first learn the history. Israel and the city of Samaria rested a little, having received into themselves the heat of error, until the whole lump became like leaven, and rose and burst out; and the people, swelling up, shouted at the doors of King Jeroboam and said: This is the day of Jeroboam our king, this is the feast day which our ruler has appointed for us; this we celebrate, this we sing, on this we exult and play, on this we worship the golden calves. When the people shouted, the princes were not angry, as some think; but they too began to rage from wine and to lose the understanding of their mind, to forget God and to dash themselves against the wood of idols. When the king saw this, namely that the people were shouting and saying: This is the day of our king, and that the princes, like drunken and frenzied men, did not know what they were saying, he stretched out his hand to the mockers, he joined his assent to those who mocked him with empty praises. These mockers, when their king lay in wait for them and was leading them away from their God, offered him their heart like an oven, for him to kindle them and make them burn with the flames of idolatry. For he assented to the people for this reason: because he understood that they had turned to error with their whole mind. And what follows: All night he slept baking them; in the morning he himself was kindled like a fire of flame, signifies this: after he sent fire into the oven of their heart, and saw them raging, and that there was no one to resist his will, he slept all night, that is, he was secure; he kept himself in darkness while they were being baked and were making the bread of impiety. Hence afterwards he rose in the morning, and showed the flame of his crimes in open madness, so that they passed over, no longer by stealth but shamelessly, from the worship of God to the ceremonies of idols. What more? All, like an oven, were heated with the fire of idolatry, and devoured their judges, so that even one who could have been good by nature and could have remembered the religion of the Lord, seeing both princes and people subject to the calves and thinking them gods, is himself also devoured by the crime. Finally, all the kings of Israel fell, and walked in the ways of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel sin, and none was found who abandoned the idols and returned to God. These things we have spoken according to the tradition of the Hebrews, more boldly than knowingly, leaving the credit for what is said to its authors. Now let us pass to the spiritual understanding: Unhappy peoples who are led astray by the king, the devil, and by his princes; or who have taken up other solemn feasts from the prince of a heresy and from his leaders, leaving the Church and trampling on the truth of the faith, are accustomed to shout and say: This is the day of our king; for example, of Valentinus, of Marcion, of Arius and of Eunomius. Those set over them, hearing this, are not made drunk with wine, lest the sin be thought a light one, but they rave from the wine of which Moses writes in the song of Deuteronomy: Their wine is the fury of dragons, and the incurable fury of asps; for these eat the food of impiety and are made drunk with the wine of iniquity. Of this the Apostle also says: Do not be drunk with wine, in which is debauchery. And in Proverbs we read: Let princes not drink wine, lest they forget wisdom and be unable to judge rightly. Hence, both peoples and leaders having been deceived, the prince stretches out his hand to the mockers, or the pestilent, such as were the sons of Eli, and of whom we read in the first psalm: He has not sat in the chair of the pestilent; of whom it is said: Cast out the pestilent man from the council, and strife will go out with him; whose hearts were kindled, so that they might dash down those whom they had deceived. For this, according to the Seventy, is what cataracts signify, which do not lift up on high but dash down below. And what he says: All night Ephraim was filled with sleep, shows that the heretics, sleeping, do not see the light of the sun of justice. For those who sleep, sleep at night, because their sense is weighed down. And of these we read in the psalms: They have slept their sleep and found nothing. Their hearts are heated by various passions: anger, love, avarice; and they devour their judges, either whatever virtues they may be able to have in their mind, or the senses by which they distinguish evil from good. Or else it must be said that the leaders of the heretics are devoured by their own peoples, so that they themselves are devoured by the assent of those whose houses they devour for the sake of base gain. All the princes of the heretics have fallen; although they cry to the Lord, there is none who calls on his name. For everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved. Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those who call on his name; they called on the Lord, and he heard them, he who does not hear the kings and princes of the heretics, because there is none among them who cries to the Lord.

And such was its madness that it did not know it had been devoured; or at least it was unaware of the cause for which it had been handed over to those who devoured it. Finally, right up to old age, that is, right up to the last captivity, it persisted in the error it had begun. Hence the pride of Israel will be humbled, not after a long time, but now and in the present; for this is what he means by before his face; and it was being humbled because it had lifted itself up and trusted not in God but in the size of its army, and because God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble; for pride, that is, gaon, the Seventy, after their custom, translated ὕβριν, that is, outrage. And because he had said above: Ephraim has become a loaf baked under the ashes which is not turned over, and this seemed ambiguous and did not make clear enough what he meant, he now puts it more plainly: They have not returned to the Lord their God; and they have not sought him in all these things. For if they had returned to the Lord their God, they would surely have heard God speaking through Jeremiah: Return to me, and I will return to you. And though they had done so much, they did not seek him whom they had lost through their own fault. But, to pass to another sense: though Ephraim ought to teach the peoples and draw them to his own likeness, he himself is mingled with the peoples and becomes like them; according to what was said above: As the people is, so will the priest be. Or Ephraim is mingled with the peoples and the nations, so that all heretics differ in nothing from the error of the Gentiles. And he who was once a leader in the Church has become a loaf baked under the ashes, surrounded on every side by unclean ash and the heat of the fires, so that he did not return to the Lord but persisted in the error he had begun. Demons devoured his strength, for these are the strangers and the enemies of all Christians, and he did not know it, thinking his adversaries friends, and judging those who devoured him to be his table-companions; yes, and grey hairs were spread over him, or rather they blossomed, that is, he went astray for a long time; and nonetheless he was unaware of his old age and his decrepitude, of which it is written: What grows old and ages is near to destruction. And if it is said of a just man and a churchman: A man's grey hairs are his wisdom, why should it not be said of an unjust man and a heretic: A man's grey hairs are his folly? Of this old age Daniel spoke to the elder: You who have grown old in evil days. Hence also in the book of the Shepherd — if, however, anyone cares to accept the reading of it — the Church appears to Hermas first with a grey head, then as a young girl and a bride adorned with her tresses. And though the pride of the heretics is humbled, or the insolence which they daily offer to churchmen, they do not return to the Lord; but in all these things they by no means seek him, because they have been heated like an oven, and they have not sought him; they have devoured their judges, and the kings fell together; nor is there anyone who cries to the Lord. All these things they suffered so that they might seek the Lord, whom they refused to seek.

7:11-12

And Ephraim has become like a dove led astray, without heart. They called upon Egypt, they went off to the Assyrians; and when they have set out, I will spread my net over them, I will bring them down like a bird of the sky, I will strike them according to the report of their assembly. LXX: And Ephraim was like a foolish dove without heart. He called upon Egypt, and they went off into the Assyrians as they will go. I will cast my net over them, I will bring them down like the birds of the sky; I will correct them in the hearing of their tribulation. The Lord commands in the Gospel that we be simple as doves and shrewd as serpents, so that, imitating the simplicity of doves and the shrewdness of the serpent, we may neither be able to harm others nor suffer snares from others; but by simplicity and prudence may show ourselves balanced men, because prudence without goodness is malice, and simplicity without reason is called folly. Ephraim therefore has become like a dove led astray, in Hebrew photha; by Aquila and Symmachus it is called θελγομένη or ἀπατωμένη, that is, enticed or deceived; and by the Seventy foolish or senseless; for ἄνους can express both. And he is fittingly called a dove led astray, or foolish, because there is also a dove that is wise, which says in the psalms: Who will give me wings like a dove, and I will fly and be at rest? Whose wings are silvered, and the hinder parts of whose back are in the greenness of gold. But Ephraim, a foolish dove and without heart, is shown to be of so brutish a mind that, calling upon Egypt, he went on to the Assyrians. For he who begged the help of the Egyptians is led away captive by the Assyrians. Egypt is a staff of reed: whoever leans on it, it will at once crush him, and, broken and shattered, it pierces the hand of the one who rests upon it. And so that God might show that by changing places we cannot escape his eyes, and that we are always ruled by the power of God: When, he says, they have set out into the Assyrians, even there I will spread my net, and if they have been lifted up like birds, I will bring them down from there. But I will bring them down, not to destruction, but to strike them as sons; and I will strike them not with the greatness of punishments, but with fear, so that, hearing of the punishments of old, they may be corrected by terrors alone. The question is asked why Ephraim is compared not to other birds but to the dove. The other birds hasten to protect their young even at the peril of their own life; and when they see a hawk, a snake, a raven or a crow approaching their nest, they fly about hither and thither, and hurl themselves at the attacker with their bites, and tear it with their claws, and testify to a parent's grief with plaintive cries; the dove alone does not grieve for its young when they are taken away, and does not look for them; and to it Ephraim is rightly compared, because he does not perceive that his people is being laid waste piece by piece, but is careless of his own safety. And what he says: I will strike them according to the report of their assembly, can also mean this: just as by a common counsel they all fashioned idols, so, when I am angered, they will all be laid waste together. The teachers of contrary dogmas, who have abandoned Christ, the Wisdom, and have gone out from the Church, we can rightly call a foolish and heartless dove, since, desiring earthly things, they have been handed over to the Assyrians. And when they have set out from the Church, the Lord spreads his net, woven from the testimonies of the Scriptures and the skilful discourse of wisdom, so as to bring down to low places those who lift themselves up against the knowledge of God and, like a bird, fly to the heights; and to correct them by threats and by the hearing of punishments, so that, corrected, they may not perish for ever.

7:13-14a

I redeemed them, and they have spoken lies against me, and they have not cried to me in their heart, but they howled on their beds. LXX: Woe to them, because they have withdrawn from me; they are manifest, because they have acted impiously against me; but I redeemed them; yet they have spoken a lie against me, and their hearts have not cried to me, but they howled on their beds. Concerning the heretics the interpretation is easy: they have everlasting woe, because they have withdrawn from God; and they are wretched, because they have abandoned their Creator, who redeemed them with his own blood; and they themselves speak lies against him, composing the impious dogmas of falsehood; and they do not cry out in their hearts, but always howl in their conventicles, which he fittingly calls beds, and the lairs of wild beasts. Such men cannot say: Every night I will wash my bed, with my tears I will water my couch; but they wallow in the filth of lusts, they give themselves to debauchery, and whatever they say, and think they are saying in praise of God, is the howling of wolves and the din of raving Bacchantes. Rarely does a heretic love chastity, and whoever pretend to love purity, like Mani and Marcion, and Arius and Tatian, and the restorers of the old heresy, promise honey with a poisoned mouth. For the rest, according to the Apostle, the things they do in secret it is shameful even to speak of.

And as they were from the beginning, before I called them through Abraham and afterwards through Moses and Aaron, when they were without the yoke and the knowledge of the law and were mingled with all the nations, so even now they have returned to their former state, so as to be carried without yoke and bridle over precipices, and to be changed into a deceitful bow; so that the bow which God had bent against the adversaries they themselves turned against their own Lord, and shot against him the arrows of blasphemies. Hence their princes, who deceived the unhappy people, will fall by the sword because of the fury of their tongue, with which they dared to call the golden calves gods, so that they did in the land of promise what they had learned in Egypt, worshiping the Egyptian Apis and venerating all their monstrosities. For so also in the wilderness, when they had come out of Egypt, they derided the Lord, saying: These are your gods, Israel. And: Would that we were in the land of Egypt, where we sat by the pots of meat, and so on. On wheat and wine, and the false mysteries of the body and blood of Christ, who says in the Gospel: Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone. And in another place: I am the true vine. And: Unless you drink my blood. Over this wheat and wine, then, the heretics are cut asunder, and build diverse tabernacles for themselves; or they are cut off from the body of the Church, and pretend to meditate on the law of God and to ruminate on it. But in doing this they withdraw from the Lord, who taught them in the Church and gave them strength with which to fight against the adversaries. But they devised malice against the Lord, building up most impious heresies, and returned to the state of the Gentiles, so as to be without the knowledge and the yoke of God; or they returned to nothing, not that they ceased to be, but because, in comparison with him who speaks to Moses: Go, say to the people of Israel: He who is has sent me, all who think against the Lord are said not to be. According to what we read in Esther, in the Seventy only. Do not hand over your scepter to those who are not: there is no doubt that it means idols. For if God is truth, whatever is contrary to truth is a lie, and is called nothing. This fits the heretics, who, instructed from the holy Scriptures, turn the words of the law and the prophets and the Gospel against the Lord; and they are like a deceitful or perverse bow. A deceitful and perverse bow is one that strikes the one who aims it, and wounds its own master. Or they have become like a bent bow, always ready for battle and contentions, to the subversion of their hearers. Hence their princes, that is, the heresiarchs, are struck by the Lord's sword because of the madness of their tongue, with which they blasphemed the Lord; doing in a Church falsely so called the same thing they did at the time when they dwelt in the Egypt of the world, that is, when they were Gentiles. For all the questions of the heretics and of the Gentiles are the same, because they follow not the authority of the Scriptures but the sense of human reason.

8:1-4

Let there be a trumpet in your throat, like an eagle over the house of the Lord; because they have transgressed my covenant and violated my law. They called upon me: My God, we know you, Israel. Israel has cast away the good; the enemy will pursue him. They have reigned, and not from me: they have been princes, and I did not know it. Of their silver and gold they have made idols for themselves, that they might perish. LXX: In their bosom like earth, like an eagle over the house of the Lord, because they have transgressed my testament and acted impiously against my law. They called upon me: God, we know you; because Israel has turned away from good things. They pursued the enemy, they reigned for themselves and not through me; they have been princes, and they did not make it known to me; of their silver and their gold they have made idols for themselves, that they might utterly perish. What we have translated: let there be a trumpet in your throat, for which in the Hebrew is written alechcha sophar, Aquila and Symmachus and Theodotion translated likewise. The Seventy alone said: in their bosom like earth, and what this means is uncertain. For some think that bosom is taken in two ways: both as the lower part of a garment, from the genitals to the feet; and as the colpus of the sea, that is, a gulf, for example of the Adriatic and the Ionian and the Propontis; and that the false teachers, who followed the blasphemy of Egypt, keep in their bosom all things that serve lust, and earthly things; or at least that in the harbor and refuge of their voyage they acquire no precious merchandise, but earthly things. But let us, following the Hebrew truth, weave on the sequence of the explanation we began. The prophet is commanded, and it is said to him: Let there be a trumpet in your throat, that is, raise your voice so that it is like a trumpet, that very many may hear, because very many have sinned. And when you have raised your voice, say this with a shout: like an eagle over the house of the Lord; and the sense is: Nebuchadnezzar will come with all his army so swiftly, so quickly, that he will imitate the flight of an eagle hastening to its prey; and he will come not to some other place but to Jerusalem, in which the temple of God stands, to destroy and overturn it. Of this eagle Ezekiel too speaks more fully: of great wings and feathers and talons, which has the course for entering into Lebanon, that is, into the temple of God, according to the prophet Zechariah, in whom it is written: Open your gates, Lebanon, and let fire devour your cedars. But this, O prophet, which I say and command you: Let there be a trumpet in your throat, so that you may cry aloud and say, like an eagle over the house of the Lord, that the king of the Chaldeans is coming, I command for no other reason than that they have transgressed my covenant and abandoned my ceremonies. Hence in the time of necessity and distress, when the captivity comes, they will call upon me and say: My God, we know you, Israel; we who are called Israel know you and acknowledge you, and we keep the ancient name of Jacob, who pleased you, so that we are called Israel. To them the Lord replied: How are you called Israel, when Israel has cast away the good, that is, his Lord, from whom he was called Israel? Therefore, because Israel cast away the good, the enemy, that is, the Assyrian, will pursue him and seize him, who, having abandoned me as king, asked for a king for themselves, as the other nations also had, and acted against my will. Finally, Samuel for this reason sets before them the harsh commands of a king, and says that their sons and daughters will serve kings, so that they might return to the Lord, the most merciful king; but they became princes without the will of God. And this crime was not enough for them, unless they doubled the outrage with a greater impiety, turning into idols the silver and gold which they had received for riches and adornment. Therefore Saul became king not by the will of God, but by the error of the people. And because he did not have the root of piety, as soon as he began to reign he was inflamed with impiety. What he says: They have reigned, and not from me; they have been princes, and I did not know it, can also be taken of Jeroboam son of Nebat, and of the other princes who succeeded him in power. Nor, because God, angry with Solomon, willed his kingdom to be divided, did Israel therefore do well in accepting a king. For according to the precepts of the law it ought to have asked the Lord whether he wished this to be done. For of the Savior too it is said that the Son of man must indeed be betrayed, but woe to him through whom he is to be betrayed. The devil, who longs to set his nest above the stars of heaven, we very often read called an eagle: If you are exalted like an eagle, from there I will bring you down, says the Lord. The eye too that mocks its father and despises the old age of its mother will be dug out by ravens, and the young of eagles will devour it; losing the clear light of its vision through the devil and the demons. Hence it is commanded in the law that we should not eat the eagle. This eagle, then, comes upon the conventicles of the heretics, which were once the house of the Lord; and it comes because they have transgressed his covenant and abandoned the law of God; and in vain they call upon him and say: You are my God, and we know you, we who are called Israel, seeing God, and are called by the Christian name. In vain, he says, they do these things, since they have set up kings for themselves, and acted against my will, and have as princes my adversaries, whom I do not know, because they do not deserve knowledge of me. Their silver too and their gold, and whatever they could possess by nature, words and thoughts, they turned into idols which they fashioned from their own heart; and they turned them, not in order to perish; but because they turned them, therefore they perished. For they did not do it with this purpose, that they might perish; but because they did it, therefore they perished.

8:5-6

Your calf is cast off, Samaria. My fury is kindled against them. How long will they be unable to be cleansed? For it too is from Israel; a craftsman made it, and it is not God, for the calf of Samaria will become spiders' webs. LXX: Cast off your calf, Samaria; my fury is roused against them. How long will they be unable to be cleansed in Israel? And this a craftsman made, and it is not God, because your calf, Samaria, was leading astray. In the place where we have put spiders' webs, in the Hebrew is written sababim, with the letter iod as the penultimate; not, as some falsely think, sababum, that is, with uau, which the Seventy and Theodotion translated πλανῶν, that is, leading astray and deceiving; Aquila, wandering or turned about; Symmachus, inconstant or unstable, that is, ἀκαταστατῶν; the Fifth edition, ῥεμβεύων, roving and fluctuating. We have learned from a Hebrew that sababim properly denotes the threads of spiders flying through the air, which perish even while they are seen, and are dissolved into atoms and into nothing. And to these the calf of Samaria is rightly compared, which at that time the people worshiped because of the greatness of its value, since it was of gold. What he had said above: Of their silver and their gold they have made idols for themselves, that they might perish, he now explains more plainly: Your calf is cast off, Samaria, my fury is kindled against them; either against the calves, because they had made two; or against the inhabitants of Samaria, who worshiped them. Moreover, as for what we read in the Seventy: Cast off your calf, Samaria, he exhorts its inhabitants, not of one city but of all the ten tribes, which are called Samaria — for otherwise there were no calves in the city of Samaria, but in Dan and in Bethel — to cast off the calves against which God is angry; or ἀποτρίψεται, that is, let it rub them away; so that it may little by little rub away and cleanse those which it had worshiped for a long time. And when they do not listen, he turns to others, and speaks as though to a third person: how long will they be unable to be cleansed? What madness is this, he says, that, while I give room for repentance, they refuse to turn to health? And because he had said: Cast off your calf, Samaria, he explains who this calf is: for it too is from Israel; not from others, he says, from the nations, have you received the calf, as Baal and Ashtaroth from the Sidonians, as Chemosh from the Moabites and Moloch from the Ammonites; but you yourselves, and your king Jeroboam, made in Israel what you had learned in Egypt. Or what sort of god is that which is formed by the hand of a craftsman? Finally, as the threads of spiders dissolve into the wind, so the calf of Samaria will be reduced to nothing. God casts off the calves of the heretics and of Samaria, who say that they keep the precepts of the law; which calves cling to the ground and work in the earth, not in the spirit, nor lift their eyes to heaven; and therefore the fury of the Lord is kindled against them, and he wonders what this great perversity is, that they will not abandon the idols which they have fashioned for themselves, and love heretical filth instead of the cleanness of the Church. These calves Israel, who pretends that he sees God, did not receive from the other nations; but he himself forged for himself out of the holy Scriptures the depravity of his understanding, and he is the craftsman of his own god, which will quickly perish and will imitate spiders' webs, which are broken at a light touch.

8:7

For they will sow the wind and reap the whirlwind; there is no standing stalk in it, the shoot will make no flour, and if it should make any, strangers will eat it. LXX: For they have sown things corrupted by the wind, and their overthrow will receive them; a sheaf without the strength to make flour, and if it should make any, strangers will eat it. He compares the calf of Samaria to spiders' webs; therefore he keeps the metaphor in what follows, so that what he had called spiders' webs he compares to wind and whirlwind, and to stalks that do not stand; and if they do stand, they have no flour; and if, he says, they should make flour, it will be devoured by others. And since the sense is common both to the heretics and to those in Samaria who fashioned idols, it must be discussed in common. These men sow the wind, or seeds corrupted by the wind, which have no pith, which the Greeks call ἐντεριώνην, and therefore, sowing empty things, they receive what is void and empty; nay rather, sowing in the flesh, they reap corruption from the flesh, and are carried about by every wind of doctrine. And when they have sown the wind, they reap whirlwinds and storms; and first of all, no stalk, that is, no straw, will come from these seeds, nor will they be able to have any appearance of a fruitful crop. But if it should rarely happen that they seem to have something like the Church's dogma, that very shoot and ear will make no flour. Into three measures of this flour the woman of the Gospel puts leaven, so that the spirit by which we perceive, and the soul by which we live, and the body by which we walk, may be brought together into one Holy Spirit, according to the Apostle: In him we live, move and are. But if it should rarely happen to the heretics that their sowing too makes flour, from this flour will be made a loaf baked under the ashes, which is not turned over, and which strangers will eat. Hence he now also says: And if it should make flour, strangers will eat it. But we ought to understand as strangers those of whom it is written: Strange sons have lied to me. And in the eighteenth (19th) psalm: From my hidden sins cleanse me, O Lord, and from strangers spare your servant. If they have not had dominion over the righteous man, then he will be unblemished and will be cleansed from the greatest sin.

8:8

Israel has been devoured; now he has become among the nations like an unclean vessel. The Seventy translated "useless" for "unclean"; the rest likewise. The unclean, or useless, vessel the Hebrews call a chamber pot, which we are accustomed to use for receiving and throwing away excrement. To this uncleanness he compares the idolaters and the heretics, who are mingled with the Gentiles while they do not keep the truth of God, and, having been made a vessel unto honor, have been turned into a vessel of dishonor. For what is more unclean than a demonic spirit and the dogmas of the heretics, which have mingled them with the pagans? Such was Jeconiah, enslaved to idols, of whom God speaks through Jeremiah: Jeconiah is dishonored, like a vessel of which there is no use. On the contrary Paul, who could say: Or do you seek a proof of Christ who speaks in me? is called a chosen vessel, of gold and of silver, because he had wisdom and eloquence, with which he preached the Gospel of Christ. And when he says: has been devoured, or swallowed up, it signifies that, mingled with idols and with the nations, he has lost the proper name of Israel and of Christian.

8:9-10

For they themselves have gone up to Assyria, a wild ass alone by himself. Ephraim have given gifts to lovers; moreover they have hired the nations for pay; now I will gather them, and they will rest a little while from the burden of the king and of the princes. LXX: For they themselves have gone up to the Assyrians; Ephraim has sprouted within himself, they have loved gifts; therefore they will be handed over among the Gentiles; now I will receive them, and they will rest a little, that they may anoint kings and princes. Israel, he says, has been devoured, and has become like a useless, or unclean, vessel, of which no potsherd remains in which water could be drawn, or some small bit of fire. And because he has become an unclean vessel, therefore they have gone up to the Assyrians, imitating the solitary wild ass; not at all as sheep fed by the Lord, but wickedly abusing their freedom, and led away into captivity; and the prophet bewails them and says: Ephraim have given gifts to the Assyrians and have hired their lovers for pay; of whom Ezekiel writes: To all harlots payments are given, but you have given payments to your lovers, and the contrary has happened in you. And when they have given presents to the Gentiles, and have hired the nations for pay to help them, they will be gathered for battle, and, bound, will be led away together. And because they love to offer gifts to their adversaries, therefore for a little while they will obtain the favor of not paying tribute to the king and the princes, until they come to the Assyrians, where they will by no means pay taxes and tribute as free men, but will be reduced to the utmost slavery. As for the heretics, we do not doubt that, going to the Assyrians according to the error of their mind, they think that they are ascending, not descending. Hence it is said to them through Isaiah: What has happened to you now, that you have all gone up onto empty roofs? The prince of these Assyrians is the great mind. Hence Ephraim has sprouted within himself, judging in his presumption that he has grown. Or he has become a solitary wild ass, so as to enter not the planted fields of the Church but the deserts of the devil. He has loved gifts in return for his error, promising himself rewards, or doing everything for the sake of shameful gain. Or at least he has given gifts and payments to his lovers, the demons, and when he has done this, he will be handed over to the Gentiles. For just as the nations worship bodily images, so these men think to be gods the idols which they have fashioned out of their own heart, and therefore they will be reckoned among the number of the nations. But if, he says, now and in the present age they repent, and I receive them, they will cease to set up over themselves the little king and his princes; so that we may understand the little king to be the devil, in distinction from the great king, who can have no fellowship with Belial, but, as soon as he has been received, drives the little king and his princes out of the heart of believers. According to the Hebrew, however, those who had before been scattered, and had hired the nations for pay, will be gathered into the Church of God, and will rest from the burden of the king, about whom the Apostle rebukes those cut off from the Church, saying: Without us you reign; and would that you did reign, and from the princes whom they have, set up in the synagogues of the devil.

8:11-13a

For Ephraim has multiplied altars for sinning; altars have become for him an offense. I will write for him my manifold laws, which have been reckoned as foreign; bring victims, bring; they will sacrifice flesh and eat it, and the Lord will not accept them. LXX: For Ephraim has multiplied altars, for sin, altars are beloved to him. I will write for them a multitude; his ordinances have been reckoned as foreign, beloved altars; for if they have sacrificed victims and eaten flesh, the Lord will not accept them. Led, he says, into captivity, they will rest for a little while from the burden of the king and the prince. And they will suffer this because Ephraim, their prince, has multiplied altars, not on which to sacrifice to the Lord, but on which to join sins to sins; and these altars, that is, places of sacrifice, will be turned for him into an offense, so that the more of them there are, the more his crimes are multiplied. Then, as he had said above: What shall I do to you, Ephraim? What shall I do to you, Judah? as though hesitating and seeking with what remedy he might heal the sick man, and by what counsels he might draw the sinner back to salvation. I will write for them again, he says, the laws which I had given before through Moses. But what profit is there in writing still others, when he has despised those which he received before? Is it not contempt of God when, though I commanded that there should be one altar in Jerusalem, they fashioned idols on all the mountains and hills with which to provoke the Lord? They also made altars for this reason, not to please me, but so that, when many victims had been sacrificed, they might eat their flesh, according to what the Lord says in the Gospel: Amen, amen, I say to you, you seek me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate of the loaves and were filled. For they put all their zeal for victims into devouring the sacrifices, not into pleasing God through them; nor will the Lord accept those which they sacrificed not to him, but to their own belly and gullet. Now the Apostle teaches that there is one altar in the Church, and one faith, and one baptism; deserting this, the heretics have fashioned for themselves many altars, not for appeasing God, but for a multitude of offenses. Therefore they do not deserve to receive the law of God, since they had earlier despised those laws which they had received. And if they have said anything from the Scriptures, it is to be compared not with the divine words, but with the thoughts of the pagans. These men sacrifice many victims and eat their flesh, deserting the one victim of Christ; nor do they eat the flesh of him whose flesh is the food of believers. Whatever they do, feigning the order and rite of sacrifices, whether they give alms, or promise chastity, or feign humility and deceive all the simple with pretended blandishments, the Lord will accept nothing of sacrifices of this kind.

8:13b-14

Now he will remember their iniquity and will visit their sins; they will be turned back into Egypt.

And Israel has forgotten his Maker and has built shrines, and Judah has multiplied fortified cities; and I will send fire upon his cities, and it will devour his dwellings. LXX: Now he will remember their iniquities, and will avenge their injustices; they have turned back into Egypt, and Israel has forgotten him who made him, and they have built shrines, and Judah has multiplied walled cities; and I will send forth fire upon his cities, and it will devour his foundations. Between ἀνομίαν, that is, iniquity, and sin there is this difference, that iniquities are before the law, sin is after the law; and for those who persist in their offenses, the Lord will remember their iniquity, which they had committed before the law; but their sins he will not remember, but will take vengeance on them. And he will both remember their old iniquities and visit their former sins for this reason, that they have gone back into Egypt, either asking for help or worshiping the same gods by which they had earlier been led astray, ἄπιν (Apis) and μνεῦιν (Mnevis). For Israel has forgotten his Maker and has built shrines on the high places, consecrating whole hills and mountains, and shady trees, to Baal and Ashtaroth and other idols. Judah too, understanding that Israel had departed from the love of God and that their sins had been visited, was not converted to the Lord, but trusted in the fortification of his cities, which the Lord says he will destroy, devouring them down to his foundations. His: there is no doubt that it signifies Judah; although some read their foundations, that is, of the cities, in place of his. But according to anagogy, those are called iniquities, that is, both ἀνομίαι and ἀδικίαι (injustices), which we committed before baptism and which have been forgiven us in baptism; but sins are those which we have done after baptism, of which it is also written in the psalm: Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. All of these will be imputed to the heretics, so that both the old iniquities and the new sins are brought back upon them. For those who had gone out of Egypt through the confession of Christ have gone back into Egypt through faithlessness. Israel has forgotten his Maker, and, having despised the Creator, has fashioned another god for himself. Judah too, that is, the churchman, in evil works, or in a perverse interpretation of the holy Scriptures, has built himself fortified cities, not with the help of God, but with skillful falsehood; and the Lord says that he will set them on fire with the fire of his spirit, and will devour his βάρεις, that is, great houses built in the manner of towers, and will overturn the foundations badly laid, so that they may not be able to set up sacrilegious shrines against God. Some take the cities fortified by Judah in a good sense, and they try to temper what seemed contrary to this sense, I will send fire upon his cities, and it will devour his dwellings, in such a way that, after what is perfect has come, what is in part is destroyed. What we read according to the LXX translators, ÷ and in Assyria they ate unclean things :, is not found in the Hebrew, and therefore it must be marked beforehand with an obelus. We can, however, say that the Israelites, longing for Egypt, were taken captive by the Assyrians and there ate unclean things, according to Ezekiel, who describes them as having eaten food offered to idols in Chaldea, food so polluted with the filth of idols that it is compared to human dung. The heretics too, whose princes are the Assyrians — of whom we have often spoken — eat unclean things among them, while they are defiled by their filth.

9:1-2

Do not rejoice, Israel, do not exult like the peoples, for you have played the harlot away from your God; you have loved a payment upon all the threshing floors of wheat. The threshing floor and the winepress will not feed them, and the wine will deceive them. LXX: Do not be glad, Israel, and do not rejoice like the peoples, for you have played the harlot away from your God; you have loved gifts upon every threshing floor of wheat. The threshing floor and the winepress did not know them, and the wine has deceived them. Those who have departed from God, when they have come into the depth of sins and have despaired of their salvation, will despise everything. Finally Israel too, departing from the law of God and worshiping idols, says that he is one nation out of many nations, rejoicing and congratulating himself that he has departed from the knowledge of God and is a nation mingled with the rest; and therefore he now rebukes them and says: Do not be glad, do not rejoice, and do not think that you are such as the other nations are. For one who does not know God is punished in one way, one who departs from God in another, because the servant who knows the will of his master and does not do it will be beaten with many stripes. You have reckoned many threshing floors and winepresses as the payment for your fornication, so that you might enjoy an abundance of all things; therefore the threshing floor and the winepress will not produce wheat and wine, and the winepress will deceive them, or will deny its wine, with which they thought they would be made drunk. We read that under Ahab king of Israel, and Elijah the prophet, there was a most severe famine in Samaria, so that mothers fed on the corpses of their own sons. At that time, according to the letter, the threshing floor and the winepress did not feed them, and the wine deceived them, and they wasted away in want. It is said also to the heretics, that they should not exult and rejoice and think themselves like the other nations. For those did not believe in God; but these, under the name of God, worship idols and play the harlot away from their God; and they multiply for themselves many threshing floors and countless winepresses, and they eat the wheat from which the bread of mourning is made, and drink the wine of Sodom, which is trodden out with the gall of asps. And because they prepare for themselves many winepresses and many threshing floors, therefore they will not eat from the true and only threshing floor, nor drink from the winepress which the Lord Jesus trod; but whatever they think they have will be corrupted by falsehood.

9:3-4

They will not dwell in the land of the Lord; Ephraim has returned into Egypt, and among the Assyrians he has eaten what is polluted. They will not pour out wine to the Lord, and their sacrifices will not please him; like the bread of mourners, all who eat it will be defiled, because their bread, their own soul, will not enter into the house of the Lord. LXX: They did not dwell in the land of the Lord; Ephraim dwelt in Egypt, and among the Assyrians he ate what is unclean; they did not pour out wine to the Lord, nor did their sacrifices please him, because it is to them the bread of mourning; all who eat them will be defiled; because the loaves of their souls will not enter into the house of the Lord. Not only did the threshing floor and the winepress fail to feed them, and the wine lie in the land of Israel, when for three years and six months everything perished, but the inhabitants themselves also will depart from the land of the Lord; and they will be led into a foreign land, so that they may not dwell in the holy land which they have polluted with their fornications. Ephraim has returned, he says, into Egypt, and among the Assyrians he has eaten what is polluted. From this place some have added above: and among the Assyrians he has eaten what is polluted, which is not found in the Hebrew, and about which we have already spoken. But when they are in Chaldea without a temple and without an altar, they will not pour out wine to the Lord, but to demons; and they will not please him, those who pour out to foreign gods, and who are held in captivity and among the Assyrians eat things sacrificed to idols, and are like the bread of mourners. For it is not lawful to eat of the sacrifices of mourners, and if anyone eats, what was offered becomes unclean. The Greeks call the suppers of mourners περίδειπνα; we can call them parentalia, from the fact that they are offered for dead parents. And not only he who has offered, but also he who has eaten of food of this kind will be unclean; for their bread, that is, the food which they offer, will not enter into the house of the Lord, which has been destroyed, which has been burned with Babylonian fire; but it will be for their own soul. And the sense is: they provide for their own gullet and their own pleasure; but polluted things do not please me. They will not dwell in the land of the Lord who have departed from the Church, and have returned in mind into Egypt, and eat the sacrifices of the Assyrians, that is, of demons; nor do they pour out to the Lord the wine with which they have been made drunk unto luxury, and neither the things they offer nor they themselves who offer please him. The sacrifices of the heretics are the bread of mourning and of tears; for all that they do will be turned into weeping. Nor will they be able to hear: Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted; but on the contrary they will hear: Woe to those who laugh, for they shall mourn. Whatever they offer, they offer not to God but to the dead, namely to those who have contrived wicked heresies; and whoever eats of their victims will be defiled. The blind will be led into the pit by the blind. Whatever they do, they do for the sake of delights, to deceive the people, to devour the houses of widows. We can call the bread of mourning the deadly words with which they speak iniquity against the Lord; and this bread does not enter into the house of God; for the conventicles of the heretics are called not the house of God, but dens of robbers.

9:5-6

What will you do on the solemn day, on the day of the festival of the Lord? For behold, they have set out from the devastation; Egypt will gather them, Memphis will bury them; the nettle will inherit the desirable things of their silver, the bur will be in their tents. LXX: What will you do on the day of assembly and on the day of the solemnity of the Lord? Therefore behold, they will go from the misery of Egypt, and Memphis will receive them, and Michmash will bury them; destruction will possess their silver, thorns will be in their tents. When the day of captivity comes, he says, and the most savage enemy falls upon you, what is my solemnity? What victim do I hold as propitious? That he avenges me on my enemies, and puts a limit to wrong, and scourges the impious sons. What then will you do on the day of the festival of the Lord? Answer. And while they are silent, he answers himself, or rather he beholds with divine eyes what they are going to do. Behold, he says, when misery and devastation pressed upon them, and the Assyrian and the Chaldean were eager to bind them as captives, they fled to Egypt. There Memphis will bury them, which at that time was the metropolis of Egypt, before Alexandria, which was formerly called No, received both the greatness of a city and its name from Alexander of Macedon. But what is said in the Seventy: Michmash will bury them, is not found in the Hebrew; but rather mamad, which is called desirable. From which it is clear that they were deceived by the similarity of the letters daleth and chaph, and in place of mamad, which all have translated desirable, they thought it was Michmash, a city of Egypt. We can also take what is said: For behold, they have set out from the devastation; Egypt will gather them, Memphis will bury them, of the tribe of Judah; when, after Gedaliah, whom Nebuchadnezzar had set over the land, was killed by Ishmael, the remnant of the people fled over to Egypt with Jeremiah the prophet, and there, when the Chaldeans afterwards pursued them, they were either taken captive or buried. And by the desirable things of their silver, which the nettle possesses, we shall understand country houses and all the adornments of country houses, which are bought at the price of silver. And what follows, the bur will be in their tents, signifies a long devastation, so that where once there were houses, there burs and nettles and thorns spring up. It is said also to the heretics: When the solemn day comes, what are you going to do? For solemn day Aquila has translated time. From which it is manifest that it does not mean a festal day, but the time of retribution; for at once there follows: The days of vengeance have come, the days of your retribution have come. Behold, you have been laid waste by many enemies: the Assyrians and the Chaldeans have slaughtered you, you have fled to the world, and have been made like the other nations; there Memphis will bury you, which is interpreted from the mouth; and the sense is: you will receive according to your blasphemies, and what you have spoken you will feel in your punishments; the desirable things, that is, the dogmas which you had composed for yourselves with crafty eloquence, which is what silver signifies, the nettle will possess, which will consume you with eternal burning; and the bur or thorn will be in your tents; for thorns spring up in the hands of those who have been made drunk with the Babylonian cup. And in the Gospel we read that shameful thoughts and the cares of this world, and the vices that cling to us, are called thorns, which choke the grain as it rises among the grasses.

9:7

The days of visitation have come, the days of retribution have come; know, Israel, that you are a foolish prophet, an insane spiritual man; because of the multitude of your iniquity, and the multitude of madness. LXX: The days of vengeance have come, the days of your retribution have come, and Israel will be afflicted like a raving prophet, a man who had the spirit; because of the multitude of your iniquities, your madness has been multiplied. In this place too there is the usual error: for where we have translated: know, Israel, that is, Israelites, and in the Hebrew is read iadau, the Seventy translated: and will be afflicted, taking the letter iod for vau, and reading res for daleth; of which the one means knowledge, the other affliction or malice. Therefore the days of visitation have come, of which he had said above: What will you do on the solemn day, on the day of the festival of the Lord? The days of retribution have come. O Israel, now recognize your own words, you who used to call the prophet who told you true things, and prophesied by the Holy Spirit, a fool and insane, according to what the princes said to Jehu in Ramoth-gilead: Why did this madman come to you? Therefore, because of the multitude of your iniquities, in which you have long run riot in wickedness, recognize that it is not my prophet, but you, who are a madman, you who labored to this end, that you might trample on my words. For madness Aquila rendered ἐγκότησιν, which in Latin we can call either wrath or the memory of a grievance. The day of vengeance and retribution some interpret as the day of judgment, when Israel will be afflicted, who now boasts that he sees God, and is not ruled by the Holy Spirit, but is carried about in various directions by a demonic one, not knowing what he says, and calling the Son of God a creature, denying that the Holy Spirit is God; and again asserting that the good God is one, the creator of the world another; and their madness is manifold, because their iniquities too were very many. Whom we have called a foolish prophet, the Seventy have translated a false prophet. And lest, by often repeating the same things, we seem to distrust the reader's prudence, we briefly advise that whatever is said in this prophet about Israel and about Ephraim is to be referred to the heretics, who, truly raving, speak lies against God.

9:8-9

The watchman of Ephraim with my God, the prophet, has become a snare of ruin over all his ways; madness in the house of his God; they have sinned deeply, as in the days of Gibeah; he will remember their iniquity and will visit their sins. LXX: The watchman of Ephraim with God, a prophet; a crooked snare over all his ways; they contrived madness in the house of God; they have been corrupted according to the days of the hill; he will remember their iniquities; he will take vengeance upon their sins. God gave princes for this purpose, that they might correct the people when it transgressed, and draw it back to the right road. Whence he also says to Ezekiel: I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel. Therefore Jeroboam was given among the people as a watchman, and as a prophet with my God, that is, with God, who speaks these things to Hosea. But he, according to what is written above: You have become a snare to the watch and a net spread out over Tabor; and you have turned aside victims into the depth, is even now called a snare of the people of Israel, because all fall in his snare, especially since he has set up, or contrived, madness in the house of God, that is, made the golden calf in Bethel, for this is interpreted house of God; and has sinned so deeply in wickedness, and has thus been sunk in the abyss of impiety, that he surpasses the crime which was once committed in Gibeah, when they killed the wife of the Levite returning from Bethlehem by unlawful intercourse. We can also take the days of Gibeah as that time when, in place of God, they chose for themselves a king from the city of Gibeah, that is, Saul. And he says that they have now sinned much more greatly by choosing Jeroboam and worshiping idols than at the time when they chose Saul; for here idolatry too was joined to schism, but there the worship of God remained among the people. Therefore he will remember their iniquity, he who is now thought, because of his patience, to have forgotten, and he will visit their sins, and the wounds which have long festered. Searching the ancient histories, I cannot find that any have rent the Church and led peoples astray from the house of the Lord, except those who had been appointed by God as priests and prophets, that is, watchmen. These, then, are turned into a crooked snare, placing a stumbling block in all places, so that whoever enters by their ways falls, and cannot stand in Christ, and is drawn away by various errors and carried by crooked paths to the precipice. These are the watchmen of Ephraim, who have contrived madness in the house of the Lord, that is, in the Church, or in the holy Scriptures, interpreting them perversely, or at least in each one of the believers, who is most rightly named the house of God. Therefore they have been corrupted and have perished according to the days of the hill, when they spoke iniquity against the heights and went up on vain roofs. God will remember their iniquity, by which they acted wickedly against their neighbor, leading him out of the Church, and he will visit their sins, by which they have sinned against their own souls. This is what we read in the psalm: Beside the path they have set a stumbling block for me. For unless one has seen the path of God, that is, has heard the name of Christ, he will not enter upon it. Therefore the heretics too, under the name of Christ, have set snares beside the path, so that he who believes that he walks in Christ, of whom we read that he himself is the way, may tread also in the snares of those who have contrived madness in the house of God.

9:10

Like grapes in the desert I found Israel; like the first fruits of the fig tree at its top I saw their fathers; but they went in to Baal-peor and were estranged unto confusion; and they became abominable like the things they loved. LXX: As a grape in the desert I found Israel, and as an early fig on the fig tree I saw their fathers; but they went in to Baal-peor, and were estranged unto confusion; and they became abominable like the beloved. For which in other copies we read: and the beloved became as abominable, which agrees better with the truth. When the whole world was a desert and did not have the knowledge of God, I found, he says, the people of Israel like a grape in the wilderness; and how he found it, he says: Like the first fruits of the fig tree, at its top I saw their fathers. Therefore the people was found in Abraham and Isaac and Jacob. And note the exactness of the language: the parents are seen, the people is found; and in both there is the vine and the fig tree, under which he who trusts in the Lord is said to rest. But they, when they had been brought out of Egypt, committed fornication with the Midianites, and went in to Baal-peor, the idol of the Moabites, whom we can call Priapus. Indeed, Baal-peor is interpreted (that is, the idol of lust) as having in its mouth, that is, at its top, a skin, so as to display the shamefulness of the male member. And because they went in to Baal-peor, therefore they were estranged from God, made over as slaves to their confusion, that is, to the idol; so that they worshiped that for whose sake they had departed from God, according to what is written: For by whatever one is overcome, of that he is also the slave; and just as those who serve the gullet have their belly as their God, so those who serve lust have Baal-peor as their God. And they became, he says, abominable, like the things they loved, according to what is written in the psalms: Let those who make them become like them, and all who trust in them, so that they are called not only idolaters, but idols. Moreover, the Lord says in his passion: I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the nations there was no man with me. And in the psalm: Save me, O Lord, for the holy one has failed. When the whole world was held in sin, the nations did not know God, and Israel had rejected him whom it had known before, the Lord found, first in the apostles and in apostolic men, Israel, the Christian people, and seeing God with the mind; and he was satisfied with their sweetest fruits, the grape and the fig, which, if they are found in the desert and when the time is not yet ripe, are of greater delight because of their rarity. But they, that is, Israel, who take to themselves the Christian name — for it is not to be understood of the fathers — went in to the idol Baal-peor, which has a skin in its mouth. For whatever the heretics speak is deadly, and separated from the living word of God. Or they went in to lust; for it is difficult to find a heretic who loves chastity; not that he ceases to put it forward on his lips, but that he does not keep it in his conscience, saying one thing and doing another; whence they have both been estranged from God, and have glory in their confusion, and have become abominable, they who were formerly loved in their fathers. But if we wish to read: they became abominable like the beloved, which however is not found in the Hebrew, we say that the Gentiles, who are abominable, have become so, in the same way as the heretics also, who were formerly loved in their fathers; that is, so that both the one and the other are equally abominable and guilty of the fault.

9:11-13

Ephraim has flown away like a bird; their glory from birth and from the womb and from conception. And even if they rear their sons, I will make them childless among men. But woe to them also when I depart from them. Ephraim, as I saw, was Tyre, founded in beauty; and Ephraim will lead out his sons to the slayer. LXX: Ephraim has flown away like a bird; their glory in birth and in travail and conception; for even if they have reared their sons, they will be without sons among men, for woe to them also; my flesh is from them. Ephraim, as I saw, has offered his sons for a capture; and Ephraim, to lead out his sons to slaughter. In this place the translators greatly disagree among themselves. For in the place where we said: woe to them when I depart from them, the Seventy and Theodotion translated: woe to them, my flesh is from them; and seeking the reason why there is such great variety, I seem to myself to have found this: My flesh is said in the Hebrew tongue basari; again, if we say: my departure, or my turning aside, it is said basori. The Seventy and Theodotion, therefore, in place of what is my departure and my turning aside, rendered: my flesh. Again, where we have put: Ephraim, as I saw, was Tyre, the Seventy translated θήραν, that is, a hunt, or a capture; Aquila and Symmachus and Theodotion, the hardest rock, that is, flint, which in the Hebrew tongue is called sur; and if we read Sor, it means Tyre. But the Seventy translators, thinking because of the similarity of the letters res and daleth that it was not res but daleth, read sud, that is, a hunt, or a capture; whence Bethsaida too is called the house of hunters. We have spoken of the diversity of the translators; let us return to the sense. Ephraim, that is, the ten tribes, has flown away like a bird into captivity and has departed from its place. And he called it a bird to show its swift passage into Babylon. But if we read, like a bird their glory has flown away, we say this, that the help of God has departed from them and flown away. And what follows, from birth and from the womb and from conception, can be understood in two ways: that the glory which has flown away from Ephraim departs also from their birth and from their womb and from their conception, that is, it deserts their sons and abandons their descendants. Or at least we say that Israel had all his glory in his multitude, and in this he thought himself greater than Judah his brother, because he was set over ten tribes, the other over two. Whence the Lord says that even if they rear their sons and gather together a multitude of children, they will be handed over to death; and then a true woe will fall upon them, when God has departed from them. And consequently he sets forth what Ephraim, who is now deserted, once was: so beautiful, he says, was he, and so walled about with the help of God, as Tyre is girded by the sea, or at least like a very hard crag which, fixed in the earth, scorns all storms and does not care about whirlwinds and winds. But he himself, that is, Ephraim, or Tyre, which was founded in the fullness of the sea, will lead out his sons to captivity. Many refer this section to the times of Hazael, who besieged Samaria and afflicted it with famine for a long time, so that the besieged thought it lighter to perish by the sword than by starvation. But let us say according to tropology that Ephraim, that is, the heretics, has departed from the Church like a bird, and has all his glory in birth and in the womb and in conception, if they have begotten many sons, whom the Lord threatens, even if they have been reared, to be punished as well, and not by any other whatever, but by the Lord himself, since they have begotten sons of fornication; and a true woe will be upon them when God has departed from them. And at the same time he unfolds what Ephraim was: when he was in the Church, he says, he was beaten by the temptations of this world as Tyre is by the waves of the sea, and yet he could suffer nothing adverse, because he had the foundation of Christ, upon which a house that is built cannot be overthrown. But now he leads out his sons to the slayer, that is, to the devil. And he said finely: he leads out, that is, he makes them go outside from the Church. We can call the sons of Ephraim the worst thoughts and the dogmas contrary to the truth, which the Lord slays with the spirit of his mouth, and he does not suffer them to have such children, and he abandons them to eternal destruction. But what we read in the Seventy: My flesh is from them, Ephraim, as I saw, has offered his sons for a hunt, we understand thus: If Christ is the head of the body, that is, of the Church, we are all members of Christ and of the Church. He, then, who departs from the Church tears the body of Christ; therefore Ephraim was the flesh and a member of the Lord the Savior. But he offered his sons for a hunt, and for the hunt of those of whom it is written: Our soul has been snatched like a sparrow from the snare of the hunters. And he led out his children from the Church to slaughter, or to be wounded, so that they are wounded by those who shoot burning arrows, so as to strike and burn at the same time.

9:14

Give them, O Lord; what will you give them? Give them a womb without children and dry breasts. LXX: likewise. If we wrongly abuse the things that God has given as a blessing, and they are turned to the opposite of the purpose for which they were given by him, it is expedient that they be taken away from us. Finally, the tongue was given for praising the Lord God and for speaking the things that are good. If anyone abuses it for blasphemy, against him the psalmist prays to the Lord: Let the deceitful lips be made dumb, which speak iniquity against the righteous in pride and in abuse. And in another place: May the Lord destroy all deceitful lips and the boastful tongue. Since, therefore, Ephraim gloried in the womb, and in conception, and in childbirth, and in the multitude of his peoples, the prophet prays to the Lord and says: Give them, O Lord. And he answers himself: What will you give them? And at once he adds: Give them a barren womb and dry breasts, so that they may have no grounds for pride, so that in the very thing in which they are accustomed to glory, in that they may be confounded. That this can also be understood of the teachers of contrary dogmas is evident, who glory in the multitude of their peoples, and in those children whom they have reared for destruction, in order to lead them out of the Church and lead them in to the murderer. For the devil slaughters as many as the sons the heretics have begotten in error. Of a soul of this kind it is said: Blessed is the barren woman who is undefiled, who has not known the bed in sin. For blessed is the churchman who, in comparison with the heretic, has not begotten children in error. And in another place we read: It is better to have no children, with virtue. For from an unlawful union the seed will perish; and though they be of long life, they will be counted as nothing, and their old age at the last will be without honor; for the fruitful multitude of the ungodly is useful for nothing; nor indeed ought we to think that he prayed for a barren womb and dry breasts in a bodily sense.

9:15

All their wickednesses are in Gilgal, for there I held them in hatred. LXX: likewise. In Gilgal Saul was anointed king, while Samuel announced the wrath of God to the people. There, he says, I held them in hatred, and by asking for a man as their king they withdrew from my rule. Or because Gilgal is a place of idolatry: there they committed all their crimes. But since Gilgal is interpreted revelation, or κυλισμοὶ, that is, wallowing-places, he says that all the wickedness of the heretics is to be revealed at that time when God gives them a barren womb and dry breasts, and they see their own shame. And those who boasted through pride that they had climbed to the heights will be rolled down to the earth, or dragged down to hell. Truly the heretics are worthy of God's hatred, who speak a lie against God, of whom he says in what follows:

Because of the malice of their devices I will cast them out of my house. There follows: I will not again love them; all their princes are departing, or disobedient. The Seventy have the same. And of the heretics indeed there is no doubt that they have been cast out of the house of God, and that he will not again love them as long as they remain in error, and that all their princes are departing from God, or disobedient, like Valentinus, Marcion and the rest. We can call the princes of the heretics demons, who truly departed from the Lord and are called princes, according to what the Lord says in the Gospel: The prince of this world will come and will find nothing in me. And the Apostle says that we fight against the powers and principalities and the rulers of this darkness. But it is asked according to the history how he cast them, that is, the ten tribes, out of his house, since they were not in the house of God? But we shall call the house of God either the holy land, into which they were brought, or the false name of Israel, or the fact that prophets were sent to them as to the people of God. But that he does not again love them, and that all the kings of Israel were departing from God, is evident; for to this day they remain in captivity. Others think that what is written, I will cast them out of my house, refers to the kingdom of Judah, because they too were to be led into captivity. But how can I will not again love them be fitted to them, since afterwards they were brought back to Jerusalem; and all their princes are departing, since we read that David, Asa, Jehoshaphat, Hezekiah and Josiah were righteous kings? Hence we must pass on to the times of Christ, because at his coming they were cast out of the house of God, and are in no way saved as Israel, but as the Christian people. Hence also the Lord made himself a whip of cords and cast them out of the temple, because they had made his Father's house a house of trade.

9:16-17

Ephraim is stricken; their root is dried up; they will bear no fruit at all; and even if they beget, I will slay the most beloved of their womb. God will cast them away because they did not hear him; and they will be wanderers among the nations. LXX: Ephraim has suffered pain in his roots; he is withered, he bears no fruit at all; for even if they beget, I will slay the desirable things of their womb. God will reject them because they did not hear him; and they will be wanderers among the nations. He takes a metaphor from a tree, which, if its roots have withered, cannot bear fruit, and if it produces a little, will at once dry up in the very blossom. He speaks, moreover, of Ephraim, whose root has withered because he lost God, in whom he had been founded, or did not deserve to have his fathers Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, in whom he had sent down his root; and therefore he does not produce the fruit of righteousness; and even if he should produce it: I will slay, he says, the most beloved of his womb, according to what he had said above: If they rear their sons, I will make them childless among men; hence also God cast them away and made them go as captives. And they will be wanderers among the nations. We can say this same thing also of all the Jews: because their princes departed from God, stirring up the people to demand him for death, therefore he cast them out of his house and will not again love them any more. He struck their root and dried it up, and they will bear fruit no more; and even if they do, and seem to meditate on holy Scripture and the law, and bring forth from their own heart, like most beloved sons, something of knowledge and doctrine, with the Lord opposing, they will be cut down. For the God of all the prophets cast them away, because they did not hear him; and they will be wanderers among the nations, having no altar, no seat, no city of their own. Hence David too says in the psalm: Do not slay them, lest at any time my people forget: scatter them by your power. And in another place: According to the multitude of their impieties cast them out, for they have provoked you, O Lord. Of this tree we also read in the Gospel: Already the axe is laid to the roots of the trees. Every tree that does not bear good fruit will be cut down and cast into the fire. That the heretics cannot bear the fruits of the virtues, because they have lost the Lord, in whom, according to the Apostle, they ought to have been rooted and founded, no one doubts; and even if they do bear them, and beget some by the fruitfulness of their womb, with the Lord opposing, they will die. Or, because their fruits are all the things they invent and beget from their own heart, these will wither and perish; and it will be plain to all that a dry root cannot bring forth crops. These will be cast away, or rather have been cast away, by God, because they did not hear him saying: Do not move the boundaries which your fathers set. And therefore they will be wanderers among the nations, passing over now to these opinions, not to those; since what they have once found does not please them, but they always exchange the old for the new and imitate the errors of the heathen.

10:1

Israel is a leafy vine, its fruit was made equal to it; according to the multitude of its fruit it multiplied altars, according to the richness of its land it abounded in images. LXX: Israel is a leafy vine, abundant fruit in it; according to the multitude of its fruits it multiplied altars, according to the good things of their land they will build monuments. For leafy vine, Aquila translated ἕνυδρον, which we can call watery, or ἔξίτηλον, because it loses the flavor of wine. Symmachus has ὑλομανοῦσαν, one that has grown entirely into leaves. Vines that have not been pruned by the vinedresser run riot into shoots and leaves, and the moisture that they ought to have changed into wine they squander on the empty ambition of foliage and leaves; a vine of this kind is ruinous to farmers. Such was Israel, growing in the multitude of its peoples, and not rendering fruit to God the farmer. Let us also put it another way: the leafy vine, or according to the Seventy εὐκληματοῦσα, that is, having good layers and fruitful shoots, bore many clusters, and the fruitfulness of the grapes equaled the size of the branches; but she who before was such, before she offended God, afterwards turned the abundance of fruits into a multitude of offense, so that the more peoples she had, the more altars she built, and surpassed the abundance of the land by the number of idols. For images, the Seventy rendered στήλας, which we call statues or monuments, which properly belong to demons or to dead men. So also the heretics, while they were planted in the Church and growing in the house of God, were called the vineyard of Sorek and bore most abundant fruits; but afterwards, the more numerous they became, the more altars they multiplied for themselves, so that instead of the one altar that is true they built many altars for their error, and according to the richness of their land they abounded in images. The land of the heretics is fruitful: receiving from God keenness of understanding and of talent, so that they might turn the goods of nature to the worship of God, they made idols for themselves out of them. For no one can build a heresy unless he is of ardent talent and has gifts of nature which were created by God the artificer. Such was Valentinus, such was Marcion, whom we read to have been most learned. Such was Bardesanes, whose talent even the philosophers admire. These, then, turned the good things of their land into monuments of the dead, because all their doctrine refers not to the living but to the dead, both to those whom they worship and to those whom they deceive.

10:2

Their heart is divided; now they will perish, he himself will break their images, he will lay waste their altars. LXX: They have divided their hearts; now they will perish; he himself will dig under their altars; their monuments will be afflicted. The Hebrews hand down a tale of this kind, confirming their surmise by the authority of the Scriptures: as long as both the kings and the people alike worshiped the golden calves and were in agreement in impiety, captivity did not come. The last king of the ten tribes was Hoshea, of whom it is written that he did indeed do evil in the sight of the Lord, but not like the kings of Israel who were before him; in whose ninth year Shalmaneser, king of the Assyrians, took the people of Israel, and led them away to the Assyrians, and made them dwell by the river Gozan in the cities of the Medes. It is asked, therefore, why they were not taken captive under the worst kings, but under one who had begun in some part to turn to better things? To this they adduce the following: at first the people excused itself and said: We obey the commands of kings, and we cannot resist their tyranny; we worship the calves that we are compelled to adore. But in the days of Hoshea it was ordered by that same king that the calves should not be worshiped with such zeal, but that whoever wished should go to Jerusalem and sacrifice to God in the temple; this decision, they say, the people opposed. And this is what he now says: their heart is divided, that is, the heart of the king and of the people, and with no excuse remaining, now they will perish and be handed over to eternal captivity; for as soon as the people separated from the king, destruction came. And what follows: he himself will break their images, he says of God: and he will lay waste their altars; not that God himself did this with his own hand, but that his will was fulfilled through enemies. That the hearts of the heretics are divided among themselves, and that they fight with contrary opinions, even they themselves do not deny, since they hold different views. Hence they will be destroyed, and the Lord will break, or dig under, their images or altars, which they have fashioned from their own heart; and he will lay waste the monuments by which each of them is called by his own name; and they have called their names upon their lands, so that they are said to belong in no way to the Church of Christ, but to this man or that.

10:3-4

For now they will say: We have no king; for we do not fear the Lord, and what will a king do for us? Speak words of a useless vision, and you will strike a covenant, and judgment will spring up like bitterness over the furrows of the field. LXX: Therefore now they will say: We have no king, because we did not fear the Lord, but what will a king do for us? Speaking words, lying pretexts; he will draw up a testament, judgment will spring up like grass upon the wilderness of the field. After God has broken the images of Israel, and laid waste their altars or statues, and the final captivity has come, they will say: We have no king. And lest they think that the sentence is put off for a long time, he added: Now they will say, when they are laid waste, when they perceive that Hoshea, their last king, has been taken from them; but the king has been taken from us for this reason, because we did not fear the Lord, the true king; for what could a man as king profit us? Say what you will, sigh after your old errors, promise yourselves prosperity, which will be turned into its opposite; you will strike a covenant, not at all with God, but with a lie. And after the covenant, which the Seventy translated testament, there will spring up for you not a fruitful crop of grain, not even barley, the food of beasts of burden, not various pulses, not vines that sweat out their fruit into must; the trees will not put forth fruits that change the moisture of the earth into various flavors; but bitterness will spring up for you, or rather the judgment of bitterness, or ἄγρωστις, which in Latin we render as grass. For it is a kind of plant like a reed, which at each of its joints sends a shoot upward and a root downward, and these shoots and twigs in turn are the seedbeds of another plant, and so in a short time, unless it is dug out to its deepest roots, it makes whole fields like thickets of briars. Indeed, even if some dry part of it, provided it has a joint, falls upon cultivated ground, it fills everything with grass. We have said these things according to the LXX translators; but in the Hebrew ros is written, which is translated as bitterness, that is, the judgment of bitterness, of which the Lord also speaks in the Gospel: For judgment I came into this world; and of others it is written that they will receive greater judgment. The disciples of contrary dogmas too, when their lies have been crushed and their altars and groves overthrown, will say too late: We do not have the kings who formerly ruled over us, by whose deceptions we did not fear the Lord; for what did it profit us to follow those whose help we do not feel in our need? They will say these things seeking some excuse, so that they may seem to have erred not through themselves but through very bad teachers. Hence also the LXX translated their words as false excuses, which the prophet shuns, saying: Do not incline my heart to words of malice, to make excuses in sins. We gladly applaud our own vices, and, overcome by pleasures, we plead the weakness of the flesh or the harsh commands of our elders; hence the words and visions of the heretics will be useless. And they will strike a covenant, not at all with God, but with bitterness, which, when the day of judgment has come, will spring up over the furrows of their field; so that those who sowed in joy may reap in tears; those who laughed may weep; those who had consolation may mourn.

Book Three

I am not unaware, Pammachius, that I am hammering out a most difficult little work on the twelve prophets, one certainly left aside by the Latins, and one that may more readily expose our rashness than display our knowledge. But because we can refuse nothing to you when you urge us, or rather command us, and because, in proportion to the means of those who bring their gifts into the treasury of God, the two small coins of the poor woman surpassed the riches of many, whatever we can do we pay, first to God, then to you, who are God's; and we always remember that little verse: "Pollio himself also makes new songs." And while I rejoice that you are my open supporter by the right of friendship, I greatly fear the silent judgment of your learning, and I fear you when you praise more than my adversaries when they disparage. For in them rivalry takes away credit, and they are to be called not so much judges as accusers. But you, who love, deliver your verdict not at all on persons but on things; although love too admits error, and that Θεοφράστιον (saying of Theophrastus) is a fine one, which Tullius rendered more according to the sense than according to the word, τυφλὸν τὸ φιλοῦν περὶ τὸ φιλούμενον, that is, "the judgments of lovers are blind." Nevertheless, rather err on this side, so that you slip not from hatred but from love. We are dictating the third book on the prophet Hosea, and we have come as far as the cows of Beth-aven; and as we spread the sails of interpretation, you ought to say that prophetic word: Come from the four winds of heaven, O spirit, so that with swift course, passing the greedy rocks of those who lie in wait, we may carry the Lord's merchandise, while shipwreck rages on every side, to the safest harbors.

10:5-6

The inhabitants of Samaria worshiped the cows of Beth-aven, for its people mourned over it; and its temple-keepers exulted over it in its glory, because it departed from it. For indeed it too was carried into Assyria, a gift to the avenging king; shame will seize Ephraim; Israel will be put to shame in his own will. LXX: With the calf of the house of ὢν those who dwell in Samaria will sojourn, for its people mourned over it. And as they provoked him, they will rejoice over its glory, because it was carried away from it; and binding it for the Assyrians, they bore gifts to King Jareb in the house. Ephraim will receive shame, Israel will be put to shame in his own counsel. What Beth-aven is, for which the LXX translated house of ὢν, and who King Jareb is, which is interpreted avenger, we have discussed more fully above. In Beth-aven, then, that is, Bethel, the inhabitants of Samaria worshiped the golden cows; and he called them in mockery not calves of the male sex but cows, that is, females, so that, plainly, Israel worshiped not only calf gods but cow goddesses. And to show that by the cows of Beth-aven the one calf in Bethel is to be understood, he did not add, the people mourned over them, but over it, that is, over the golden calf. But if the people mourned, why did its temple-keepers exult over it? The Hebrews relate that the golden calves were taken away by the priests by theft, and that bronze gilded ones were put back in their place. When, therefore, the people mourned in the time of need and distress that the golden calves too, among the other gifts, had been sent by the king of Israel to the Assyrian kings, and above all to King Sennacherib, the temple-keepers exulted, because their fraud could in no way be proved or detected. And this is what he says: its temple-keepers, that is, the calf's, exulted over it in the glory of the people, that is, in the calf, which they held as their glory; because it had departed from it, that is, from the people, and had been carried over to the Assyrians. And that we may know, they say, that this is what is meant, the following verse shows it clearly: For indeed it was carried into Assyria, a gift to the avenging king. And at once there follows: Shame will seize Ephraim, and Israel will be put to shame in his own will, or in his own counsel. For the fraud of the gilded calves, once detected, is reported to the king of Israel by letter, and the very thing by which they had thought to please is that by which they are put to shame most of all; and they offend those to whom they had sent the gifts, who think that this was accomplished not by the theft of the priests but by the fraud and counsel of the kings. We read in the book of Kings that Menahem, king of Israel, sent to Pul, king of the Assyrians, a thousand talents of silver, that his hand might be with him, that is, that he might give him help; and some think that among these the golden calves too were carried off. For Jareb in the present place Symmachus translated ὑπερμαχητῆ, that is, to the protector and defender. According to the spiritual understanding we must labor at how we may fit everything to the heretics. The cows of Beth-aven, or of the house of ὢν, which is interpreted toil, were worshiped by the heretics, who boasted that they dwelt in the keeping of the commandments of God, that is, in Samaria; and on the day of judgment, when judgment will spring up like bitterness upon the furrows of the field, the people will mourn over it, that is, over the calf, and over the perverse dogma which it thought to be God. But of the temple-keepers too he did not say, will exult, but, exulted, referring it to past time, over their glory, with which they had once fabricated their glorious things, because the people had departed from God, or because the very glory of God had departed from the people, by whom it had been abandoned. And whatever the heretics say and compose in fine language, they send as gifts to their king, the devil, referring everything to him; whence shame will seize them for ever, and they will be put to shame in their own wills. A certain man has left it written in his commentaries, both above and in the present place, that King Jareb, that is, the avenger, is to be understood as Christ. This displeases us altogether. For it is impious that what according to the history will be understood of the Assyrian king should according to tropology be referred to Christ.

10:7-8

Samaria has made her king pass away like foam upon the face of the water, and the high places of the idol, the sin of Israel, will be destroyed. The bur and the thistle will come up upon their altars; and they will say to the mountains: Cover us; and to the hills: Fall upon us. LXX: Samaria has cast out her king like brushwood upon the face of the water, and the altars of ὦν, the sins of Israel, will be taken away. Thorns and thistles will come up upon their altars; and they will say to the mountains: Cover us; and to the hills: Fall upon us. For foam, which the LXX and Theodotion translated φρύγανον, that is, brushwood, dry grasses and withered twigs, which are got ready for the furnace and for burning, Symmachus put ἐπίζεμα, wishing to indicate the upper waters of a boiling pot, rising into foam and bubbles, which the Greeks call πομφόλυγας. As, then, the foam that is upon the water quickly dissolves, so the kingdom of the ten tribes will swiftly be ended; and the high places will perish, that is, bamoth, of which it is written: But still the people were sacrificing and burning incense on the high places. Now these high places are those of ὦν, or auen, that is, of the idol, which is interpreted useless; and this idol and useless thing is the sin of Israel. But once the idol and its high places have been scattered: The bur and the thistle will come up upon their altars. A sign of utter desolation, so that not even walls and the last traces of buildings are left. At that time they will say to the mountains: Cover us; and to the hills: Fall upon us. This the Lord says is to be fulfilled in the last time of the Jewish captivity. Whatever, then, is now said against the ten tribes, or against all Israel, let us know that it can also be transferred τυπικώς (typologically) to the whole people, so that when the Romans had taken Jerusalem and overthrown the temple, or when the day of judgment comes, as others suppose, they say, fearing with great horror, to the mountains: Cover us; and to the hills: Fall upon us, preferring to die rather than to see the things that bring death. But the Samaria of spiritual wickedness too, which had divided itself from the people of God, made its king pass swiftly away, namely the speech of the heretics and their doctrine, like foam, or brushwood, upon the face of the water: the one, even while it is seen, suddenly dissolves; the other is easy to lift from the surface of the waters and cast into the fire. Such are the heretics, swelling with foaming words and mixing precepts destined for burning into the baptism of Christ and into his words. All these will pass away, and the grand words on which they labored, which is what ὦν is interpreted, will at once be scattered, the words in which Israel sinned; and so great will be the desolation of that most wicked doctrine that thorns and thistles will come up upon their altars. That thorns and thistles spring up where there is no cultivation of the fields is plain to all. These are the thorns that choke the seed and do not let it grow, which spring up in the hand of the drunkard, and which Israel produced instead of the grape. For the Lord waited for it to produce grapes, and it produced thorns, or the wild vine, which has the likeness of the grape and twists the mouths of those who eat it with its bitter taste. Therefore, when the time of judgment comes and all things have been overthrown, they will say to the mountains, which they formerly thought lofty, and to their former teachers: Cover us; and to the hills: Fall upon us. But because with the mountains he put cover, and with the hills: fall, something more sacred must be explained. To the mountains, that is, to the saints, who have a true and not a feigned height, they will say: Cover us. For blessed are those whose iniquities are covered. And to the hills, which have no natural height, which they once thought had something of loftiness, they will speak: Fall upon us; for the mountains will cover, and the hills will fall; and these things will happen out of fear and incredible dread, through which both mountains and hills will be humbled.

10:9-10

From the days of Gibeah Israel has sinned; there they stood. The battle against the sons of iniquity will not overtake them in Gibeah. According to my desire I will chastise them; peoples will be gathered against them when they are chastised for their two iniquities. LXX: Since the hills have been, Israel has sinned; there they stood. War did not overtake them on the hill against the sons of iniquity; he came to chastise them, and peoples will be gathered against them when they have been chastised in their two iniquities. From the day on which Benjamin shamefully and cruelly killed the Levite's wife in the city of Gibeah, all Israel has sinned against me; not because it avenged the wrong and punished the crime with blood, but because it leapt to battle out of a husband's grief and would not avenge the sacrilege against its own God, in that it disregarded the ephod and teraphim in the house of Micah, which were worshiped as idols. Here, then, Israel stood; there it checked its step, so as to walk no further in the ways of the Lord. Therefore the battle, or captivity, will not overtake them on account of Gibeah, as they themselves suppose; for there they did well to pursue the sons of iniquity; but I will chastise them, he says, and instruct them with the whole will of my mind, and I will gather against them a multitude of peoples, because they committed two iniquities, avenging a man and neglecting the wrong done to their God. Or two iniquities, because they sinned first in the idols of Micah, second in the calves of Jeroboam. Or at least we can call the two calves of Samaria, in Dan and Bethel, the two iniquities, of which Jeremiah too speaks: My people have done two most evil things; they have forsaken the fountain of living water, and dug for themselves broken cisterns, which cannot hold water. These two iniquities broke out against two commandments of the Decalogue, in which it is said: I am the Lord your God; you shall have no other gods besides me. The Seventy interpreted Gibeah as hills. From the days of the hills, then, Israel has sinned, when it abandoned the mountains of the Church and went down to the hills, or heretical slopes, thinking itself more learned than the Church is and to have found something more sublime: there they stood, that is, they persisted in error. And what follows, The battle will not overtake them on the hill, some have interpreted thus: because they have begotten sons of iniquity, and, withdrawing from the Church, have begun to be on the hills, when persecution comes the battle will not overtake them, since the devil is unwilling to attack his own. Others thus: Since from the days of the hills Israel has sinned, and stood there and could walk no further, must it not be overtaken by battle on the hills? Ought not churchmen to fight against it, to destroy them over the sons of iniquity? If they are overtaken and overcome, they will no longer be able to beget. And at the same time the Lord promises that he will chastise and instruct them, so that, when the teachers have been overcome, their own disciples, whom they had deceived before, may be gathered against them and see the chastisement on account of the two iniquities: because they both abandoned the Church, the fountain of the Lord, and dug for themselves broken cisterns, namely the caves of the heretics, which cannot hold the waters, that is, the doctrine of the Savior and the mystery of baptism.

10:11

Ephraim is a heifer trained to love threshing, and I passed over the beauty of her neck; and I will mount upon Ephraim; Judah will plow, Jacob will break up the furrows for himself. LXX: Ephraim is a heifer — or a cow; for what in Hebrew is called egla, that is, μόσχος (calf) and δάμαλις (heifer), signifies both —; Ephraim, then, is a heifer taught to love contention; but I will come upon the beauty of her neck; I will lay upon Ephraim and I will keep silent about Judah; Jacob will be strengthened for himself. This passage, or rather everything that follows this section, is wrapped in great obscurities. Hence let both we who try to explain it and the prudent reader with us pay attention, so that, if not the truth, which is most difficult, we may at least be able to track down a conjecture of what is probable. The divine word has this custom, to express the truth of the history through tropology and metaphor. Ephraim, then, is like a cow or a heifer which from her youth has learned to tread the threshing floor and to drag iron wheels over the heaps of grain, so that the chaff may be separated from the wheat; and she has not only learned it, but through excessive habit has begun to love what she was taught. And I, he says, passed over the beauty of her neck. The Hebrew word abarthi, that is, I passed over, especially when it is said by God, always signifies blows and adversities. Indeed the destroyer too is recorded to have passed through in Egypt. Because, then, Ephraim, the cow or heifer, loves to tread the threshing floor, I, he says, passed over the beauty of her neck, and, laying a yoke on it, I tamed the swelling muscles of her neck. Why should I mention the yoke of the law? I myself mounted upon her, and while I was laboring thus, Judah, that is, the two tribes, began to cleave the fields with the plowshare and to turn the earth back into furrows. And while Ephraim bore the yoke and Judah plowed, Jacob broke up the furrows for himself. Here by Jacob, as distinct from Israel and Judah, let us understand the twelve tribes, in that they began to break the clods with hoes and to crumble the earth, so that, softened, it may receive the seed, and after a little while a fruitful crop may sprout. For threshing, or threshing floor, the Seventy translated contention, and the sense is: Because Ephraim will not receive the yoke of the law, I will pass over and mount upon the beauty of her neck, so that the contentious and wanton cow may learn to labor, which she does not want. But Judah will plow of his own accord, because he has the temple; and he abides in the law, so that all the twelve tribes vie with one another in preparing the fields for sowing. And what follows according to the same Seventy, I will lay upon Ephraim and I will keep silent about Judah; Jacob will be strengthened for himself, can have this sense: On Ephraim, who is contentious and will not bear the burdens of the law, I will lay captivity. Judah, however, I will leave for a little while and say nothing about him; but whoever, whether of Ephraim or of Judah, keeps my precepts will be strengthened for himself and will be called Jacob. According to ἀναγωγήν (anagogy) this can be said: that Ephraim, who was instructed in the law of God so as to tread the threshing floor of the Scriptures and to meditate on it day and night, began to love contention, to throw off the yoke of the law, and to contend against the churchmen to the subversion of the hearers. Hence the Lord will either press down with the yoke the neck that is lifted up and promises itself lofty things, or, passing over, will himself trample it with his feet and mount upon him, so that he may know that he has a Lord. But Judah, that is, the churchman, will plow, persevering in the work he has begun. Or: I will keep silent, he says, about Judah. It is not, after all, the healthy who need a physician, but those who are sick. But Jacob, which is interpreted supplanter, who daily supplants vices and sins, and receives his brother's birthright, and is heir of his father's possession, and sleeps in Bethel, which is interpreted house of God, will break up the furrows and the clods, so that the earth may receive the scattered seed in its soft bosom, and may yield a hundred measures of barley, or, as it is in the Hebrew, a hundredfold. For it is not to be believed that the patriarch Isaac devoted himself to barley and not to wheat. To this day the churchman Jacob breaks into pieces the clods of the history and the hardness of the letter, and divides them spiritually, so that they can yield spiritual fruits. This indeed we read that the Lord too did, breaking into pieces the five loaves of the law, which the people could not eat whole, so as to give them through the hands of the apostles to believers to eat. And what he says according to the Seventy, Jacob will be strengthened for himself, shows that everyone who labors labors for himself, so as to gain everlasting fruits.

10:12

Sow for yourselves in righteousness and reap in the mouth of mercy. Renew for yourselves the fallow ground; but it is the time of seeking the Lord, when he comes who will teach you righteousness. LXX: Sow for yourselves in righteousness, gather for yourselves the vintage of the fruit of life; light for yourselves the light of knowledge, for it is time; seek the Lord, until the fruits of righteousness come to you. He keeps up the metaphor taken from farmers, once he has begun it. He had said that Ephraim, a trained heifer, loves and cherishes the threshing of the threshing floor, and that he had mounted upon her neck, and that Judah had plowed, and that Jacob had broken up the furrows, or the clods. Now he commands them to sow for themselves through repentance, and to sow in righteousness, that is, in the law, and to reap in mercy, that is, in the grace of the Gospel; for there it is: An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth; here we read: Whoever strikes you on the right cheek, offer him the other as well. And when you have sown in righteousness and reaped in mercy, renew for yourselves the fertile fallow lands. And he gives the reasons why they should sow, why reap, why renew the fertile fallow lands. It is the time, he says, of seeking the Lord, when he comes, Christ and Savior, who will teach you righteousness, which you now hope for in the law: For Christ is the end of the law unto righteousness for everyone who does good. Where we said, reap in the mouth of mercy, the Seventy translated, gather for yourselves the vintage of the fruit of life; and reaping suits seed better than the vintage does; but the fruit of life is he who is also the tree of life. And where we put, renew for yourselves the fallow ground, they rendered, light for yourselves the light of knowledge, so that from the works and commandments of the law we may deserve to have knowledge, according to what we read in a certain book: You have desired wisdom; keep the commandments, and the Lord will give it to you. For whoever turns the commandments into works sows in righteousness and will reap from it the fruits of life. Whence we read elsewhere too: The commandment of the Lord is bright, enlightening the eyes. And Isaiah says to the Lord: Your precepts are a light upon the earth. And in another place: From your commandments I have gained understanding. Those too who have been separated from the Church and take to themselves the false name of Christians are commanded to repent and to receive both testaments: to sow righteousness in the Old, to reap mercy in the New; and to light for themselves the light of knowledge, or to renew for themselves the fallow lands, and to seek the Lord, who can teach them true righteousness, and to destroy the false teachers, from whom they learn not righteousness but iniquity.

10:13a

You have ploughed impiety, you have reaped iniquity; you have eaten the fruit of lying. LXX: Why do you keep silent about impieties, and you have gathered the vintage of his iniquities; you have eaten lying fruit. I am compelled against my will to discuss rather often the peculiarities of the Hebrew language; for we do not, after the manner of the rhetoricians, repeat maxims, build up words, and rouse our hearers or readers by declamations to our own praises. But what is obscure, above all in a foreign tongue, we strive to explain to men. Above, where we translated: Judah shall plough, in the Hebrew is read ieros, with iod as the first letter; the Seventy, thinking it uau, translated: and shall I keep silent? Now too in the Hebrew is written arasthem, which we have rendered you have ploughed, for which the Seventy translated: why do you keep silent; by the same error as above, rendering silence for ploughing. And this is the sense of what is said: Over the neck of the heifer Ephraim, contentious and loving to thresh, I passed; and I went up, so that, while Judah ploughed and broke up the furrows, Jacob might tread the threshing floor and bear the heat of the sun. And I admonished them to sow in righteousness and to reap in mercy, and to make for themselves fallow ground; and to know that that is the time for seeking the Lord in which he will come who will teach us righteousness. While I commanded this and willed to receive from the glad fallow lands the fruits of righteousness and mercy, they ploughed impiety, because they acted impiously against the Lord, forsaking the Creator and worshipping idols; and they reaped iniquity, receiving from evil seed evil fruits, from which they made not only bread baked under the ashes, but deceitful and lying bread, which would deceive the one who eats it with an empty hope. Of this kind are the heretics, who plough with composed speech and protect or keep silent about impiety, lest it seem to be impiety, but be believed to be piety. Hence, because they said in their heart: There is no God, they are corrupt and have become abominable, they also reaped, or gathered the vintage of, iniquities. For as avarice is the root of all evils, so the root of all sins and crimes is impiety, and whoever has ploughed, or sown, it will reap iniquities. Those therefore who have ploughed impiety and reaped iniquities have eaten the fruit of lying, preaching to the deceived peoples all things that are false, so that they do not seek the true bread which came down from heaven, but the bread of lying, which chokes and kills those who devour it.

Because you have trusted in your ways, in the multitude of your mighty men,

a tumult will rise up among your people, and all your fortresses will be laid waste; as Salmana was laid waste by the house of him who judged Baal in the day of battle, the mother dashed down upon the sons, so has Bethel done to you from the face of the malice of your wickednesses. LXX: Because you have hoped in your chariots, in the multitude of your strength, destruction will rise up among your people, and all your walled places will depart; as the princes of Salmana from the house of Jerubbaal in the days of war dashed the mother down upon the sons, so will I do to you, house of Israel, from the face of your wickednesses. Therefore you have eaten the fruit of lying, and in all that you set before yourselves an empty hope has deceived you; because you have trusted, O Ephraim, in the ways of your idolatry; for these are your ways; and in the multitude of your mighty men, having your hope not in God but in the strength of an army. And so a tumult will rise up among your people, which in Hebrew is called Saon, that is, the noise and roaring of a howling army; and at their shouting all your fortresses will be laid waste; and the places you thought fortified and safe will lie open to the enemy and will be laid waste just as Salmana, the prince of the Midianites, was laid waste and crushed, who was killed by the house of Jerubbaal. There is no doubt that he means Gideon, who, from the fact that he destroyed the temple of Baal and cut down the grove, and Baal could not avenge himself, obtained the surname Jerubbaal, that is, let Baal avenge himself; so that, as Salmana killed the sons in front of their mothers, the mothers themselves also being killed afterwards, so too your sons, O Ephraim, may be killed in your presence, you who are yourself also to be slaughtered. As, therefore, Salmana was laid waste by Jerubbaal, whom some wrongly think to be Jeroboam son of Nebat, who ruled over the ten tribes, and was laid waste, as is contained in the Hebrew, by Arbel, which signifies the very same thing as Jerubbaal, but in a shorter and more eloquent expression; so has Bethel done to you, O Israel, from the face of your wickednesses, Bethel, where you set up the golden calf and worshipped the gods of Egypt. For Bethel, which is interpreted house of God, the LXX translated house of Israel, which is not found at all in the Hebrew. We have escaped, after a fashion, from the rugged places; now, spreading our sails for the deep, let us cross the sea of allegory. Because you have trusted, O Ephraim, in the ways, or in the chariots, of yours, of which it is written: Some in chariots and some in horses; but we will be magnified in the name of the Lord our God; and you have trusted in the multitude of your mighty men, whom you have strengthened for yourself by false knowledge; therefore a tumult and noise will rise up among your people. For whatever the heretics say has no voice that unfolds meanings, but only tumult, clamor and noise. And all your fortresses, or the things that are walled, will be laid waste —, for they have been fortified and built not with the testimonies of the Scriptures, but with the art of dialectic and the arguments of the philosophers —; as Salmana was once laid waste by Gideon, the mother being killed upon her sons. This history the eighty-second (83rd) psalm also recalls, where it records that among the other leaders of Midian there was also Salmana, saying: Do to them, O Lord, — no doubt meaning those who have entered into a pact or covenant against God —, as to Midian and to Sisera. And after the rest: Make, he says, their princes like Oreb and Zeeb, and Zebah and Salmana, all their princes who said: Let us possess the sanctuary of God by inheritance. And what follows, So has Bethel done to you from the face of the malice of your wickednesses, is properly fitted to the leaders of the heretics: that Bethel has done this to them, the one they themselves call Bethel, that is, the house of God, and a false Church; so that the sense is: So will your Church do to you, which you call the house of God; but from the time it is held by you, it must be called Beth-aven, that is, house of the idol, because of the multitude of your wickednesses.

11:1-2

As the morning passes, the king of Israel has passed away; because Israel was a child, and I loved him, and out of Egypt I called my son; they called them, so they went away from their face. They sacrificed to the Baalim and offered sacrifice to images. LXX: In the morning they were cast away, the king of Israel was cast away; because Israel was a little child, and I loved him, and out of Egypt I called his sons. As I called them, so they themselves went away from my face. They sacrificed to the Baalim and burned incense to carved images. He sets out the same sense under different figures. He who had said above: Samaria has made her king pass away like foam upon the face of the water, because foam and bubbles floating on the surface of the waters are quickly dissolved, now puts the same thing under another likeness. For as the rising of the dawn, the first light and the beginning of the day, which is called morning, passes quickly between the nearness of night and of the sun, so that the night ends and the day grows bright, so the king of Israel, that is, of the ten tribes, will swiftly pass away. And he sets out the benefits that God conferred upon him. While he was a boy, he says, and a little child, and was held captive in Egypt, I loved him so much that I sent my servant Moses, and called out of Egypt my son, of whom I said in another place: Israel is my firstborn son. And because Israel is indeed spoken of in the singular but understood in the plural, as are people, Ephraim and Judah, since in the singular number there is a great number contained in this number, he recalls the old history: that he called them through Moses and Aaron, who called them to go out of Egypt; but they, called by them, withdrew from their face, turning their backs on him and showing the hardness of their mind by the gesture of their body. Nor was it enough for them to despise those who called them, unless they also sacrificed to Baal and burned incense to carved images, or idols. We read that they first worshipped Baal under Ahab king of Israel, who took as his wife Jezebel, daughter of the king of Sidon, and brought the Babylonian and Phoenician idol over into Samaria. Therefore he joins in one discourse sins distant in time: how first they were called out of Egypt and named sons, then in the wilderness withdrew from God, worshipping Baal-peor rather than God, and afterwards in the holy land served the Baalim and Ashtaroth and the other idols. And we understand that the heretics pass away like the first light of dawn, and their king, the devil, or the heresiarch; whom in their infancy, — when they believed in the Church, and were little ones, and were reckoned under the name of Christ —, God loved; and he called them out of the tribulation and darkness of Egypt. And he called them through the apostles and the teachers of the Church. And when they had been called, he says, by my leaders, they withdrew from their face and worshipped Baal-peor, that is, they served their own vices and lust, and afterwards they sacrificed to the Baalim and to images which they had fashioned for themselves. For each of the heretics has his own gods, and whatever they have feigned they set up as a carved and a molten image. Where we have said: Out of Egypt I called my son, the Seventy translated: Out of Egypt I called his sons, which is not found in the Hebrew; and no one doubts that Matthew took his testimony from this place according to the Hebrew truth. Therefore let those who disparage our translation produce the scripture from which the evangelist took this testimony and interpreted it of the Lord Savior, when he was brought back from Egypt into the land of Israel. And when they cannot find it, let them stop wrinkling the forehead, knitting the brow, curling the nose and snapping their fingers. The emperor Julian, in the seventh volume of what he vomited forth against us, that is, against the Christians, slanders this place, and says that what was written of Israel the evangelist Matthew transferred to Christ in order to mock the simplicity of those of the Gentiles who had believed. To him we will answer briefly: first, that Matthew published his Gospel in Hebrew letters, which none could read except those who were of the Hebrews. Therefore he did not do it in order to mock the heathen. But if he wished to mock the Hebrews, he was either foolish or unskilled: foolish, if he invented an open lie; unskilled, if he did not understand of whom these things were said. The volume itself clears him of folly, since it is composed prudently and in order; unskilled we cannot call him, whom we know from other testimonies of the Scriptures to have had knowledge of the law. It remains for us to say that the things which go before τυπικῶς (typologically) in others are referred to Christ according to truth and fulfillment; which we know the Apostle did with the two mountains, Sinai and Zion, and with Sarah and Hagar. For it is not that Sinai is not a mountain and Zion is not; that Sarah did not exist and Hagar did not exist, because the apostle Paul referred these to the two covenants. So, therefore, this which is written: Israel was a little child, and I loved him, and out of Egypt I called my son, is indeed said of the people of Israel, which is called out of Egypt, which is loved, which at that time, after the error of idolatry, was called as an infant and a little child; but it is perfectly referred to Christ. For Isaac too was a type of Christ, in that he himself carried the wood of his coming death; and Jacob, in that he had as wives Leah, sore of eye, and Rachel, the beautiful. In Leah, who was the elder, we understand the blindness of the Synagogue, in Rachel the beauty of the Church; and yet those who were in part types of the Lord Savior are not to be believed to have done in his type all the things they are related to have done. For a type indicates a part; if it went before as a whole in the type, it would no longer be a type, but ought to be called the truth of history. These things we have said briefly, as is fitting in commentaries; now let us return to the rest.

And I will be to them as one who lifts the yoke upon their jaws, and I inclined toward him that he might eat. LXX: And I bound Ephraim together, I took him up upon my arm; and they did not know that I was healing them in the corruption of men; I stretched them out in the bonds of my love. And I will be to them as a man giving slaps upon his jaws; and I will look upon him, prevailing over him. The Hebrew and the edition of the Seventy translators differ greatly from each other. Let us try, therefore, to weave the history according to the Hebrews, and ἀναγωγὴν (anagogy) according to the LXX. He who had said above: Israel was a child, and I loved him, and out of Egypt I called my son, and afterwards added what wickedness he committed: They sacrificed to the Baalim and offered sacrifice to images, now relates with what love he loved Israel, according to what we read in Deuteronomy: The Lord your God carried you, as a man is wont to carry his little son, in all the way by which you walked, until you came to this place. And in another place: He spread out his wings and took him up, and carried him on his shoulders. I, he says, who was a father, became a foster-father, and I myself carried my little one in my arms, lest he be harmed in the wilderness; and lest he be terrified either by heat or by darkness, by day I was a cloud, by night a pillar of fire; so that those whom I had protected I might enlighten and heal with my light; and when they had sinned and made for themselves the head of a calf, I gave room for repentance, and they did not know that I was healing them, and closing over the wound of idolatry in the space of forty years, and restoring them to their former health. And I healed them because of the cords and bonds of love with which I had bound Abraham, Isaac and Jacob to myself. For in place of Adam, Aquila and Symmachus and the Seventy and Theodotion translated all men, so as to say: with the cords of men I will draw them, with the bonds of love. And what follows, I will be to them as one who lifts the yoke, for which Symmachus translated: and they thought that I was putting a yoke upon their jaw, is taken in two ways: either I took from them the yoke of all the nations round about, or they thought my law a most heavy weight of a yoke. And I gave him manna as food in the desert, for him to eat; for this is what he says: and I inclined toward him that he might eat; for which Symmachus translated: and I inclined food toward him. Not that God inclined toward him, but that he made the food of manna incline toward him. Otherwise: I loved them so much, and was so merciful a shepherd, that I myself carried the sick sheep on my shoulders; but they did not know that I was healing them by my passion, and that I, who am the lover of all men, was drawing them to believe with the bonds of love, according to what is written in the Gospel: No one comes to me unless the Father who sent me draws him. And they thought my light yoke most heavy; and I inclined toward them, leaving the kingdoms of the heavens, so as to eat with them, having taken the form of a man; or I gave them my body to eat, I myself both the food and the guest at table. Let us pass to the spiritual understanding, according to the Seventy translators only; lest, if we wish to expound both, according to the history and according to ἀναγωγὴν, we stretch out the size of the book. While they sacrificed to the Baalim which they had fashioned from their own heart, and, when I called, fled from my face, — for so it stands in the Seventy —, I, the most merciful Lord, bound the feet of Ephraim, that they might not flee farther from me; for this is what συνεπόδισα signifies. And I bound them with the testimonies of the Scriptures and the disputation of the teachers of the Church, so that he might hold them, bound through patience, in his arms, while they did not understand that the patience of God was the occasion of their salvation. Hence, in the corruption of men, that is, of the teachers who had deceived them, I extended the warmth of faith to those contracted by the cold of faithlessness, and bound them, resisting as they were, with the bonds of my love. And because they did not run of their own accord, but were dragged along bound with ropes, I struck their jaws a little with slaps, not punishing but correcting and amending. A judge tears the flesh, tortures with cords, torments with scourges and fire. But he who is a father strikes his wanton son with the palm of the hand. And he finely did not say: I will be beating them with slaps, but: as a man striking the jaws with the hand. And God strikes his straying sons with the threat of punishments, with the reading of the Gospel and the testimonies of the prophets. And when he has so struck on the jaw as to knock the bread and doctrine of the heretics out of the polluted mouth, then he looks upon him, while the beaten son says to him: Look upon me and have mercy on me. And again: Look and hear me, O Lord my God. And when he has looked upon him, he will prevail, or it will profit them, that is, he will overcome the adversaries and make servants out of runaways. Or he will give true and sweet food to those who formerly devoured the lies and most bitter food of the heretics.

The sword has begun in his cities and will consume his chosen ones; and it will eat their heads, and my people will hang in suspense for my return. But a yoke will be laid upon them all together, which will not be taken away. LXX: Ephraim will dwell in Egypt, and Assyria itself is his king, because he would not be converted. And the sword has grown weak in his cities, and has rested in his hands, and they will eat of their own thoughts; and his people hangs from its sojourning, and God will be angry over his precious things, and will not exalt him. When he says: he will not return into the land of Egypt, he shows that he desires to return but cannot go. For Israel desired to return, begging for help from the Egyptians; but he was taken in possession by the Assyrian, who captured him and ruled over him by the right of the victor; and he suffered this because he would not be converted nor repent. Or at least let us say that he returned into the land of Egypt when in the holy land he worshipped the gods of the Egyptians; or it is to be taken in the sense in which it was said above: They called upon Egypt, they went away to the Assyrians. And so the sword has begun in his cities, or will rush in, as Aquila translated, or will wound, as Symmachus rendered. And see how great is the weight of miseries: not that fields or possessions and the countryside are laid waste, but that the enemy enters the midst of the cities and consumes his chosen ones, or his arms, as Symmachus translated, which in Hebrew is called baddau. And when the sword has consumed the chosen and the princes, or the strength of the army, and has devoured either their heads or their counsels, so that they can find no protection at all, then the wretched common people, who would not return to me, will wait for my return to them. And they will repent late, while the enemies lay everything waste. And so, because great sins must be punished with great torments, he will lay upon those of the people who are left, — their king and princes having been cut down by the Assyrian sword, — the most heavy yoke of servitude, and he will lay it on them all together, which will not be taken away according to the letter, unless it is taken away spiritually in Christ. According to the Seventy, Ephraim dwelt in Egypt, saying that he held the holy land and the Church of the Lord Savior; but by vices and sins and the perversity of his faith he always lingered in Egypt. Because, therefore, he dwelt in Egypt, the great mind, the Assyrian, will be his king; for he would not return to the Church, and, having lost power, that is, Christ, who is the power of God and the wisdom of God, he has always lived in languor; and he was weak, subject to all the demons and passions; therefore the sword, that is, spiritual knowledge or the word of a churchman, laying waste and subduing, will always be at work in his cities, which he impiously built against God; and the sword itself will rest in his hands, so that, slain by another, he cannot slay another nor lift his hand against the adversary. Finally they will receive, and will devour, according to their own counsels. But the unhappy people and the untaught crowd will sigh for their ancient homeland and will feel themselves to be captive, or will hang in suspense in their sojourning, not knowing what to do and ignorant where to turn. And God is angry over their precious things, namely the gold and silver which they had received from him, of which we have often spoken, and he will by no means set free him who has fallen by his own fault. This according to the LXX; and we will fit the same sense to the Hebrew as well.

11:8-9

How shall I give you up, Ephraim, shall I protect you, Israel? How shall I give you up like Admah, set you as Zeboiim? My heart is turned within me, my repentance is troubled together. I will not execute the fury of my wrath, I will not turn to destroy Ephraim, because I am God and not man; the Holy One in your midst, and I will not enter the city. LXX: What shall I do to you, Ephraim, shall I protect you, Israel? What shall I do to you? I will set you as Admah and as Zeboiim; my heart is turned in him; my repentance is troubled together. I will not act according to the wrath of my fury. I will not abandon Ephraim to be destroyed, because I am God, and not man; holy in you, and I will not enter the city. In that place where we and the LXX translated: shall I protect you, Israel? in the Hebrew is written amaggenach, which Aquila translated ὅπλῳ κυκλώσω σε, that is: I will surround you with a shield. And while we thought this was to be understood in a good sense and signified protection, from the edition of Symmachus a contrary sense is set before us, for he says ἐκδώσω σε, that is: I will hand you over. From the translation of Theodotion too, not favorable but adverse things are shown: ἀφοπλίσω σε, which signifies: I will strip you, and take from you the ὅπλον, that is, the shield with which I had protected you before; and this sense better suits the Lord when he threatens. What he says, then, is this: since they would not be converted, and Assyria became their king, and the sword will devour cities and princes and people, and a yoke will be laid upon them which will not be taken away from them; and because the sentence seemed harsh, leaving them no room at all for repentance, now God speaks to Israel with a parent's affection: What shall I do to you, Ephraim? how shall I strip you of my help? What shall I do to you? By what art shall I correct you? With what remedy shall I be able to heal you? I will set you as Admah and Zeboiim, which are two of the five cities, as we read in Genesis: Sodom and Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim and Bela, which is Zoar, and in the Syrian tongue it is called Zoara. I will therefore set you and turn you into a wilderness, and destroy you down to cinders and ashes, as I destroyed Admah and Zeboiim. And when he had pronounced a harsh, nay a cruel sentence, again he is overcome by mercy through a parent's affection, and softens the severity of the judgment with a father's tenderness. For he says: My heart is turned within me; my repentance is troubled together. As soon as I said that I would set Ephraim and Israel as Admah and Zeboiim, my bowels were moved. I repented of destroying for ever my people of old; therefore I will not act according to the fury of my anger, nor will I be changed from my clemency so as to destroy Ephraim. For I do not strike in order to destroy for ever, but in order to amend. My cruelty is the occasion of repentance and of mercy. God, indeed, am I, and not man. Man punishes for this end, that he may destroy; God chastises for this end, that he may amend. The Holy One in your midst, and I will not enter the city, that is: I am not one of those who dwell in cities, who live by human laws, who think cruelty to be justice, for whom the highest law is the highest malice; but my law and my justice is to save those who have been corrected. We can also say otherwise: since Cain the parricide first built a city in the name of his son Enoch, the Lord does not enter a city of this kind, which was built by crime and blood and parricide. But if we wish to read: How shall I give you up, Ephraim, shall I protect you, Israel? it is to be understood thus: What shall I do to you? Are you worthy of protection, you who have done such things? It must also be noted that where something is said against Judah, that is, the people of God, it is not Admah and Zeboiim that are set down, but Sodom and Gomorrah. For we read in Isaiah: Hear the law of God, you princes of Sodom; and: Give heed to the word of the Lord, you people of Gomorrah. In the Gospel too, of the city which has not received the apostles, when they shake off the dust of their feet, it is said that in the day of judgment it will be more tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrah than for that city. And the prophetic word is directed to Jerusalem: Sodom has been justified by you. We are therefore given ground to suspect that Sodom and Gomorrah were the leaders in sin, and that Admah and Zeboiim followed their example; because the mighty will suffer torments mightily; and the servant who knows the will of his lord and does not do it will be beaten with many stripes. Hence churchmen too, if they are held fast by the same crimes as the heretics, will be subject not to the torments of Admah and Zeboiim, which are the lesser, but to those of Sodom and Gomorrah, which are said to be of greater crimes. To the heretics also, and to the people deceived by them, the Lord says that unless they repent they will be set as Admah and Zeboiim, so as to have no hope of salvation. Again, as a most merciful father, he says that he changes his sentence and repents of having said such things, so as to call them too to conversion and to repentance. I will not act, he says, in my fury, I will not destroy Ephraim. As far, he says, as lies in me, as far as I desire, if he corrects his error by the truth, if he loves me more than the leaders of heresy; God, indeed, am I, and not man: I will stretch out my hand to the fallen, I will call the wandering to salvation. And because I am holy, for that reason I will not enter the city, that is, the conventicles and cities of the heretics. Those who go out of their cities I gladly receive; into their cities I will not enter. This which he said, I will not enter the city, and what follows according to the LXX, I will walk after the Lord, some have interpreted so as to say that the people answered the Lord, and that the sense is: Because your heart is turned within you, and you have not dealt with us according to our sins, but imitate your own clemency, and do not punish our offenses, and promise that you, holy and merciful, will dwell in our midst, therefore I too will not enter the city of evil men, nor will I be of the number of sinners; but I will walk after the Lord our God. The Hebrews, however, explain it thus, in the person of God: I will not abandon you, I will not go to another nation; nor will I enter another city.

They will walk after the Lord; he will roar like a lion, for he himself will roar; and the sons of the sea will tremble, and they will fly like birds out of Egypt, and like a dove from the land of the Assyrians; and I will settle them in their houses, says the Lord. LXX: I will walk after the Lord; as a lion he will roar, for he himself will roar; and the sons of the waters will tremble, and they will fly like a bird out of Egypt, and like a dove from the land of the Assyrians; and I will settle them in their houses, says the Lord. When the Lord promises prosperous things, the people will be converted to him, and will walk after the Lord, because the Lord will roar like a lion. Of this the prophet Amos also makes mention: The Lord will roar from Zion, and from Jerusalem he will utter his voice. And he roars when he says: as Admah, I will set you as Zeboiim. The Hebrews refer these things to the coming of Christ, whom they hope is yet to come. We show that they are already accomplished, because both from Egypt and from the Assyrians, that is, from the east and the west, and from the north and the south, they have come, and daily they come, who sit at table with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. And we can call sons of the sea, or of the waters, those who have been caught in the Lord's dragnet and drawn out of the sea of this world. And when they have been taken from death into life, they will be settled in their houses, which the Gospel calls barns, in which the chosen grain, separated from the chaff, is stored. It is said to be the nature of lions that when they growl and roar, all living creatures tremble, and, their step fixed, cannot move: so great is the terror, so great the dread. And so the Lord too, when he roars and thunders like a lion and utters his voice, all the birds and every winged creature will shudder, and they will go to their nests, that is, to their houses, in which the Lord will dwell with them. Let us also say it another way: when the true lion growls, the false lion, who according to the apostle Peter is our adversary, will at once fall silent, and every perverse dogma will be unable to open its mouth; and those who had before been taken captive by him, set free by the roar of the lion and its terrible threat, will follow the Lord their God. Then the sons of the sea, or of the waters, will fear, who had been begotten in the bitterness and in the salt waters of the heretics; and taking wings, they will fly like birds out of Egypt, and like a dove from the land of the Assyrians, and they will say: Who will give me wings like a dove, and I will fly and be at rest? We know that Egypt, that is, Mesraim, is interpreted tribulation and distress; the Assyrians also, directing, or, as we judge better, rebuking. From these, therefore, the heretics will be set free when they begin to dwell in their houses and say to their evil parents: Your house will be left to you deserted.

But Judah, that is, the churchman, is not proud, is not puffed up with heretical swelling, but is humbled with God, and with the choir of the saints is faithful and strong, while he builds his house upon the rock, which no storm can shake. The LXX translated very differently: that Ephraim and the house of Israel and Judah surrounded God in falsehood and in impiety, and that God is of such great clemency that he does not cut off from them the hope of salvation, but knows them and is ready to call the people a holy people and the people of God, which is now perverted by impiety. According to tropology also: that the Lord wills both heretics and sinful churchmen to be saved, and all to be called by his name. Therefore the judgment of God is with Judah, and visitation upon Jacob; according to his ways and according to his devices he will repay him. LXX: And there is judgment for the Lord with Judah, that he may punish Jacob; according to his ways and according to his devices he will repay him. In the womb he supplanted his brother, and in his labor he prevailed with God, and he was strengthened with the angel, and was able; they wept and entreated me; in the house of ὢν they found me, and there it was spoken to them. We can understand Judah as the churchman, who is rebuked by the Lord because he is not mindful of his former benefits toward him, but daily joins sins to sins; and he sets forth what those benefits are. When the Church, he says, was bringing you to birth in faith, you supplanted your brother, the Jew or the Gentile, and received his birthright; and in your strength you were directed with the angel, either overcoming the adverse powers, or strengthened by the blessings of the angel, who is himself God; and you prevailed in figure against the angel, so that you might prevail against men, and you were strengthened. Therefore, O churchman, you who are called Judah, and confessing, turn daily through repentance to your Lord; and if perhaps you have sinned, imitate the prophet who says: I have labored in my groaning; every night I will wash my bed, I will water my couch with my tears. That is, which of those whom you thought your protectors will be able to bring help to your captivity and final servitude? Where is your king, of whom you said to Samuel: Appoint a king over us to judge us, as the other nations also have. And when he spoke against it, you answered: By no means; but there shall be a king over us, and we too shall be like all the nations, and our king will judge us, and go out before us, and fight for us. Let him, then, of whom you had promised yourself that he would fight your wars, now come to your aid in your need, and free all your cities from servitude. Where are your judges? Where are your kings? For you said: Give me a king and princes; and so I gave you Saul as king in my fury, so much so that in the days of harvest I displayed rain, contrary to the nature of the province of Judea. And I took away, he says, the king in my indignation, namely Zedekiah, so that the one I had given with fury I might take away with indignation. Others think that the king given in fury was Jeroboam son of Nebat, and the one taken away in indignation Hoshea, the last king of the ten tribes. This which we have expounded, I gave you a king in my indignation, and I took away the king, the Hebrews refer to the future tense. At that time, he says, when you were saying: Give me a king and princes, I answered you through Samuel that I would give you one in my fury and would take him away in my indignation. Every heretic is lost and given over to corruption; for whoever corrupts the temple of God, the Lord will destroy him; and he has help in nothing else but in the mercy of God alone, which he obtains by repentance. His king and judges are the devil and the demons, or all the princes of perverse dogmas, who will not be able to free them in the time of need and distress, who were given in fury and will be taken away in indignation; not that the Lord wished them to have such kings, otherwise he would not take away those whom he had given of his own accord, but because he let them go after their own wills, so that, eating, and with their flesh grown fat, they might be nauseated and vomit it out through their nostrils, and begin to hate those whom they followed with such zeal.

13:14-15

From the hand of death I will free them, from death I will redeem them. I will be your death, O death; I will be your bite, O hell. Consolation is hidden from my eyes, because he divides between brothers. LXX: From the hand of hell I will free them, from death I will redeem them; where is your cause, death? Where is your sting, hell? Consolation is hidden from my eyes, because he will divide between brothers. According to both understandings, both of Ephraim, that is, of the ten tribes, and of the heretics, who could not hold out in the crushing of their children when the pains as of a woman in labor came, the Lord promises that he will free them from the hand of death and redeem them from death. Now he calls the hand of death the works by which it kills, according to what is written: Death and life are in the hand of the tongue. And the Lord freed all and redeemed them in the passion of the cross and the shedding of his blood, when his soul went down into hell and his flesh did not see corruption; and to death itself and to hell he spoke: I will be your death, O death. For this is why I died, that you might die by my death. I will be your bite, O hell, you who devoured all in your jaws. And seeing the hard necessity of death, and that there is no man who lives and does not see death, the most merciful Father remembers the ancient sentence, that in Adam we all die. Or the prophet, understanding his own frailty and the condition of human flesh: Consolation, he says, is hidden from my eyes; and the sense is: I am not able to be consoled; whatever I conceive in my mind cannot soften my grief, as I see names most dear to one another severed by death; hell itself, he says, divides between brothers. Whatever, then, separates brothers is to be called hell; and above all the harlot woman, who, calling the unwise man to herself, says in the wealth of prudence: Touch gladly the hidden loaves, and the sweetness of stolen water; and the unwise man does not know that the earth-born perish with her and run into the depth of hell. For whatever is not permitted is desired the more, and what is sweet through rarity is turned into bitterness through constancy. And honey drips from the lips of the harlot woman, which for a time makes the jaws of the unwise man fat, but at the last is found more bitter than gall, and sharper even than a two-edged sword. Whoever is earth-born, and is not born from heaven, is killed by her embraces and bound with the bands of her couches; and the feet of folly lead down those who make use of her, with death, to hell. Now between death and hell there is this difference: death is that by which the soul is separated from the body; hell is the place in which souls are shut up, whether in refreshment or in punishments, according to the quality of their merits. We have said this to show that death does what the harlot woman does. For death divides brothers, and the woman does this too. Under brothers understand every bond of love, so that mother also is divided from daughter, and father from son, and brother from brother. That death is one thing and hell another, the psalmist also shows, saying: There is no one in death who is mindful of you; but in hell who will confess to you? And in another place: Let death come upon them, and let them go down alive into hell. Where we have translated: I will be your death, O death; I will be your bite, O hell, the LXX rendered: Where is your cause, O death? where is your goad, O hell? In place of which the Apostle put: Death is swallowed up in strife? Where, O death, is your strife? Where, O death, is your sting? And expounding the force of the testimony he added: Now the sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law; but thanks be to God, who has given us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ. Therefore what he interpreted of the resurrection of the Lord, we neither can nor dare interpret otherwise. Death and hell can also be taken as the devil, who was slaughtered by the death of Christ, of whom it is also said in Isaiah: Death, growing strong, has devoured. And afterwards there follows: The Lord has taken away every tear from every face. But the two brothers divided from each other by death some understand, according to the history of that time, as Israel and Judah, so that what was then prefigured in part may now be perceived in the whole, and Israel and Judah are to be freed and redeemed together with the whole human race. In the place where the LXX rendered: where is your cause? and we said: I will be your death, Symmachus translated: I will be your stroke. The Fifth edition and Aquila: where are your words?, for in Hebrew it is written dabarach; they read dabar, that is, word, in place of deber, which is interpreted death, according to what we read in Isaiah: The Lord sent death into Jacob, and it came into Israel, that is, deber, in place of which we translated: The Lord sent a word into Jacob, and it came into Israel, that is, dabar. For sting also, which we rendered bite, Symmachus translated ἀπάντημα, that is, an encounter, and Theodotion and the Fifth edition a stroke and a shutting-in.

The Lord will bring a burning wind rising from the desert, and it will dry up its veins, and lay waste its spring, and he will plunder the treasure of every desirable vessel. LXX: The Lord will bring a burning wind from the desert upon them, and will dry up its veins, and lay waste its springs; he will parch its land and all the desirable vessels. I have read in the commentaries of a certain writer that the burning wind which the Lord will bring from the desert is the one that struck the house of Job at its four corners and made it fall upon his sons as they feasted, and that it is one of those winds which we read in the Gospel blow and come with whirlwind, rains and floods to overturn the house that is built upon the rock, or upon the sand. This by no means seems right to me; for it is not written in Job that the Lord brought the wind from the desert; rather, the name of the Lord is passed over in silence, so that the wind from the wilderness, which had come against the holy man of its own will, can be taken as a hostile power; and the winds that overturn the foundations of houses certainly cannot be referred to the good side. It remains that we understand the burning wind, which the Lord will bring rising from the desert, as the one of whom we also read in Habakkuk: God will come from the South, and the Holy One from Mount Paran, which is certainly situated in the wilderness and in the south. And in the Song we read: Where do you pasture, where do you lie down at midday? This burning wind, then, to dry up the veins of death and parch its springs, the Lord will bring rising from the desert; but from the desert of the human race, in which even the devil, seeking rest, could not find it. Or we understand the desert as the virginal womb of holy Mary, because without human seed it sprouted no shoot, but a rod, simple and most pure and fruitful in its oneness, brought forth that flower which says in the Song of Songs: I am the flower of the field and the lily of the valleys. And beautifully, both in Isaiah and in the present place, it is called a flower rising and a wind rising, because from the lowliness of the flesh he climbed to the heights, and led us with himself to the Father, saying in the Gospel: When I have been lifted up, I will draw all things to myself. He himself will rise like a root from an uninhabitable land, and death will by no means come upon him, but he will come upon death; for death found in him no path for its power, and this is what is said in Proverbs: It is impossible to find the tracks of a serpent upon a rock. And the rock itself speaks in the Gospel: Behold, the prince of this world will come, and will find nothing in me. He will dry up the veins of death and lay waste its springs. The veins and springs and sting of death are named sins by the Apostle; and when these have been dried up, death itself also will be dried up. And what follows: He will plunder the treasure of every desirable vessel, is taken in two ways: either because they are desirable to those who dwell in death, or by the desirable vessels that were held in the treasury of hell we understand the saints, whom, bound as they were, the Lord perhaps plundered and carried off from hell, and led with him into paradise like most precious vessels. For treasure the LXX translated land; there is no doubt that the land signifies death. And in the psalms we read: I believe that I shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living. And according to the Gospel: The meek will possess the land. By contrast we must take it that the land of hell is not the land of the living but of the dead, which is plundered and laid waste when, by the death of Christ, the souls bound in hell are set free. According to tropology, in those same commentaries — of which we spoke above — we read that the burning wind is understood as the devil and the individual heresiarchs. This does not please us; for the devil cannot dry up the veins of death and the springs of error, since he himself is the source and beginning of the dead. Therefore the burning wind is to be understood as the word of the Church, which parches all the dogmas of the heretics and brings them to nothing, and plunders and scatters those who had been gathered together in death by the doctrine of the heretics.

13:16 (Latin 14:1)

Let Samaria perish, because she has provoked her God to bitterness; let them perish by the sword; let their little ones be dashed and her pregnant women be ripped open. LXX: Samaria will be destroyed, because she has resisted her God; they will perish by the sword, and let their sucklings be dashed against the rock, and those with child be torn apart. We have often said that the ten tribes are called Samaria from the capital city Samaria, which is now called Augusta, that is, Sebaste, from the name of Augustus. And why the city was called Samaria we read in the book of Kings. The prophet, then, commands, and, to speak more truly, speaks in the optative mood, that Samaria should perish. For though God was preparing such great goods for her, she acts against God and follows rather the images of demons. Symmachus, however, did not say let her perish, but μεταμελήσει, that is, she will repent, and she will regret her error, that she turned the sweetest God into bitterness, so that her warriors perish by the sword, her little ones and sucklings are dashed to the ground, and her pregnant women and those with child are torn apart unto death. All this we must believe befell her in the time of captivity and distress, when they lost their homeland, and those who escaped the sword were led away into perpetual servitude. Concerning the heretics the understanding is easy: they are called Samaria because they boast that they keep the precepts of God, not that they had kept his laws, but because they say that they are this, after the likeness of the schism of the Novatians, who themselves too call themselves καθαροὺς, that is, clean, though they are the most unclean of all, denying repentance, through which sins are cleansed, according to what is written: You will wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow. And in Isaiah: Wash yourselves, be clean. Now by washing he does not mean baptism, but all repentance, which washes away the filth of sins. Let such a Samaria, then, perish, because whatever it says fights against its God and turns his clemency into cruelty, so far that those among it who are men and have come to the full age of malice are cut down by the spiritual sword. And those who are little ones and sucklings, let them be dashed against the rock, of whom we also read in the psalm: Blessed is he who will take and dash his little ones against the rock. Her pregnant women too, and those with child, who have conceived from evil seed, will be torn apart for this reason, that they may not produce most wicked offspring. Something of the kind is also set before us in the Gospel: Woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse in those days; that is, in the days of tribulation and distress. And the warriors of Samaria are killed by the sword, and the sucklings are dashed, and those with child are torn apart, so that, the evil seed perishing and its tares burned up, the wheat alone may remain, to be stored in the Lord's barns.

14:1-3 (Latin 14:2-4)

Return, O Israel, to the Lord your God, for you have fallen in your iniquity. Take words with you, and return to the Lord; say to him: Take away all iniquity and receive the good, and we will render the calves of our lips. Assyria will not save us, we will not mount upon a horse; nor will we say any more: Our gods, the works of our hands; for you will have mercy on the orphan who is in you. LXX: Return, O Israel, to the Lord your God, for you have been weakened in your iniquities; take words with you and turn back to the Lord, say to him, so that you may not take up iniquity, but may receive good things; and we will render the fruit of our lips. Assyria will not save us, we will not mount upon a horse; by no means will we say any more: Our gods, to the works of our hands; he who is in you will have mercy on the orphan. Now that Samaria and its men are perishing, and its little ones and pregnant women have been slain, dashed down and ripped open, all Israel is called to repentance; so that he who has been weakened, or has fallen in his iniquities, may return to the physician and recover health, or that he who had fallen may begin to stand; and he is taught how he ought to repent. Take, he says, words with you, that is, prayers and the confession of sins, and return to the Lord as much by words as by works; and say to him: Take away all iniquity, leave in us nothing of our sickness and of our former ruin, lest the revived seedlings of an evil seed sprout again; and receive, he says, the good. For unless you take away our evils, we cannot have a good to offer you, according to what is written elsewhere: Turn away from evil and do good; and we will render, he says, the calves of our lips. For the calves, which in Hebrew are called pharim, the Seventy translated fruit, which is called pheri, through the likeness of a deceptive word. But the calves of the lips are praises to God and thanksgiving: For a sacrifice to God is a troubled spirit. Therefore, fleshly victims being already rejected at that time, the offering acceptable to God is pure confession. Those who say that they will render the calves of the lips, and will sing the praises of God with unceasing voice, also promise something else: that they will by no means have hope in the Assyrians, nor upon Egyptian horses, because a horse is deceptive for safety, and that they will no longer worship the works of their hands, the golden calves which they cast in Dan and Bethel; and therefore they say: By no means will we say to the work of our hands: our gods, for you will have mercy on the orphan who is in you; that is, on the people of Israel, of whom you had said: Israel is my firstborn son. And: I have begotten sons and exalted them, but they have despised me. And in another place: Foreign sons have lied to me. Now he is called an orphan because he has lost God as his father. A certain writer has explained the orphan as him who has withdrawn from an evil father, the devil, and who is therefore lifted up by the mercy of God. To every perverse dogma also the prophet speaks daily, and calls its followers to repentance, saying: Return to the Lord your God, you who have fallen, or have grown weak, having lost the health of the Lord; take words with you, the true confession of faith, and say: Take away the iniquity that dwells in our heart, and receive the good of faith. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. The calves and victims, or the fruit of the lips, are to believe in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, and in the passion and resurrection of the Lord; and whoever has offered this to him will by no means hope in the Assyrian king, of whom we have often spoken. Nor will he mount upon the horse which the Lord commands by no means to be multiplied, the horse with which Pharaoh was drowned together with his cavalry. For every heretic, through pride, mounts horses which he himself has bred in his error. And by no means will they any longer say to the works of their hands, which they themselves have fashioned with artful eloquence: our gods. The glutton's god is his belly, the covetous man worships mammon, the heretic, the dogma he has invented. Whoever deserts all these, that is, Assyria and the horse and the works of his hands, will return to the Lord, and will appease his father, by whom he had been cast off.

14:4-8 (Latin 14:5-9)

I will heal their breaches, I will love them freely, for my fury is turned away from him; I will be like the dew. Israel will spring up like the lily, and his root will break forth like that of Lebanon; his branches will spread, and his glory will be like the olive, and his fragrance like that of Lebanon. Those who sit in his shade will be converted, they will live on wheat and will spring up like the vine. His memorial like the wine of Lebanon. Ephraim, what more have I to do with idols? I will hear him and direct him; I, like a green fir tree; from me your fruit has been found. LXX: I will heal their dwellings; I will love them openly, for my anger is turned away from them; I will be like the dew. Israel will flower like the lily and will send out his roots like Lebanon; his branches will spread, and he will be like a fruitful olive, and his fragrance like that of Lebanon; they will turn back and sit under his shade; they will drink and be inebriated with grain, and his memorial will blossom like the vine, like the wine of Lebanon, Ephraim. What more has he to do with idols? I have humbled him and I will strengthen him, I am like a dense juniper; from me your fruit has been found. To those who have turned to repentance and, like an orphan, acknowledge the father whom they had left, God answered: I will heal the breaches, or their dwellings, in which they had been wounded, or broken, or in which they had dwelt badly: I will love them freely; which the LXX translated ὁμολόγως, plainly and openly or without any doubt. Now the Lord loves those who love him, of whom he also says in another place: I love those who love me. For I, who before was angry with them because of the sins they had committed, will now have mercy because of my clemency. And I will be to them like the dew; so that with my dew I may quench the Babylonian furnace and the oven of the blazing fire, I who also through the patriarch Isaac spoke to my servant Jacob: Of the dew of heaven shall your dwelling be. For just as the Lord becomes to believers light, way, truth, bread, vine, fire, shepherd, lamb, door, worm, and the rest; so for us who need his mercy, and burn with the fevers of sins, he is turned into dew, he to whom Isaiah says: For the dew that is from you is their health. And in the song of Deuteronomy Moses speaks: Let my words descend like the dew. And when the Lord has sprinkled us with his dew, and has watered the dryness of our breast with his rains, we will spring up, nay, we will flower like the lily, imitating the Lord the Savior, who says in the Song of Songs: I am the flower of the field and the lily of the valleys; and he speaks to his bride, who has no wrinkle or spot: As the lily in the midst of thorns, so is my nearest one in the midst of the daughters. And when we have grown in the Lord, we will send out our roots like the trees of Lebanon, which sink their root into the depths as far as they rise with their top into the air, so that they are shaken by no storm, but stand firm in their steady bulk. The branches of these trees stretch this way and that, so that the birds of heaven may come and dwell in them. And lest perhaps we should think, because he had said: his root will break forth, or: he will send out his roots like Lebanon, that he was speaking of cedars and unfruitful trees, he compares the holy man, converted to the Lord, to the fruit-bearing olive, the man who says in another place: But I am like a fruitful olive in the house of God. Its fruit the five wise virgins prepared for themselves; by it the swelling of wounds is soothed, the limbs of the sick find rest, light is kindled in darkness, those who contend in the contest are anointed. This olive will have a fragrance like that of Lebanon, or of frankincense, which is a kind of incense: ὁμωνύμως (by the same name) among the Greeks and the Hebrews both the mountain and frankincense are so called; or at least like that of Mount Lebanon, which, most fertile and green, is covered with the densest foliage of trees, so that the olive can say: We are the good fragrance of Christ. But those who have been converted to the Lord will receive the reward of their conversion, that they may sit in his shade and say: Under his shade I rested and sat, and his fruit is sweet in my mouth. And when they have sat in his shade, those who before had been dead will live; or, according to the Seventy, they will drink and be inebriated with wheat, that is, with an abundance of all things. That inebriation here signifies not the overturning of the mind but a plenty of all things, that little verse declares, saying: You have visited the earth and inebriated it. And the banquet of Joseph, at which he is said to have inebriated his brothers. And the Lord, speaking to the apostles: Eat, friends, and drink, and be inebriated, brothers. Or, because the Lord himself is the grain and the vine, whoever believes in him is said to be inebriated. Finally there follows: And it will flower like the vine, his memorial like the wine of Lebanon. Now we can call wine of Lebanon wine mixed and seasoned with incense, so that it has a most sweet fragrance; or the wine of Lebanon is the wine poured out to the Lord in the temple, of which we read in Zechariah under the name of Lebanon: Open your gates, O Lebanon. Since, therefore, so great an abundance of all things is to come, O Ephraim, everyone who repents and who has begun to be mine, put away idols, despise images; for I am he who humbled you, and I will exalt you; or, I will hear and direct, and I will make him like a green fir tree, so that of him it may be said in the psalm, according to the Hebrews: The fir tree is its house. Or at least, I will be like a dense juniper, so that he may rest under my shade. Of ἀρκεύθοις, that is, of junipers, according to the Seventy translators, Solomon is recorded to have made the doors of the temple, because Christ, through whom we come to the Father, has this nature, that he always flowers, always bears new fruits, and never lays aside his greenness. This juniper, for those who rest under its shade, so that they may not be struck by the burning of this world and the heat may not strike their head, as it once struck Jonah's too, gives fruits, and provides not only rest to those who sleep and sit, but also fullness to those who eat. Whatever we have interpreted according to ἀναγωγὴν (anagogy) of the coming of the Lord the Savior and of the conversion of the true Israel, this can be referred as much to heretics and Jews as to the Gentiles and to every perverse dogma, so that when they have repented they may obtain pardon. If, therefore, the fullness of the promise was fulfilled at the coming of the Savior, and is fulfilled daily in the Church, we must believe that it will be fulfilled more fully when, perfection having come, what is now in part will be done away. We must note what we have often said: that the salvation of Israel and of the return to God, and the redemption from captivity, are to be taken not carnally, as the Jews think, but spiritually, as is most truly proved.
