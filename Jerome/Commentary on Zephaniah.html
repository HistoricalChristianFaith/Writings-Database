Commentary on Zephaniah

Latin source: Jerome's commentary on Zephaniah, in one book addressed to Paula and Eustochium (written at Bethlehem about 392–393), from the critical text of M. Adriaen, Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 76A (Turnhout, 1970), pp. 655–711, verified against the page images and collated with the text of Vallarsi as reprinted by Migne, with the edition's evident misprints corrected. Each lemma gives Zephaniah in Jerome's own version from the Hebrew and then, after "LXX:", in his Latin of the Septuagint; all Scripture is translated as Jerome quotes it, from his own Latin. Italics mark the lemmata and the other Scripture he quotes. Psalms keep the Latin (LXX) numbering, with the Hebrew number in parentheses. Greek words are kept as printed, with an English gloss in parentheses where Jerome gives none; Hebrew words keep Jerome's own transliteration, and the two Latin words he discusses (nugas, excetra) stay beside their English. This English translation is released into the public domain.

Prologue

Before I take up Zephaniah, who is ninth in the order of the twelve prophets, it seems I must answer those who think I deserve to be laughed at because, passing over men, I write above all to you, O Paula and Eustochium. If they knew that Huldah prophesied while men were silent, and that Deborah, a judge and a prophetess alike, overcame the enemies of Israel while Barak was afraid; and that Judith and Esther, in a type of the Church, both slew the adversaries and delivered Israel, which was about to perish, from its peril; they would never crook their hand into a stork behind my back. I say nothing of Anna and Elizabeth and the other holy women, whose little fires, like those of the stars, the bright light of Mary hides. I will come to pagan women, so that they may see that even among the philosophers of the world it is the differences of minds, not of bodies, that are wont to be considered. Plato introduces Aspasia disputing; Sappho is written of together with Pindar and Alcaeus; Themista philosophizes among the wisest men of Greece; Cornelia of the Gracchi, that is, your own, the whole crowd of the city of Rome admires; Carneades, the most eloquent of philosophers, the sharpest of rhetoricians, who had been accustomed to rouse applause among men of consular rank and in the Academy, did not blush to dispute on philosophy in a private house with a matron listening. Why should I mention the daughter of Cato, the wife of Brutus, whose virtue makes us not marvel so greatly at the constancy of her father and her husband? History, Greek as well as Latin, is full of the virtues of women, and of such as would demand whole books. For me, since another work presses upon me, let it be enough to have said at the end of the prologue that the Lord, when he rose again, appeared first to women, and that they were the apostles of the apostles, so that men might blush not to seek the one whom the frailer sex had already found.

1:1

The word of the Lord which came to Zephaniah the son of Cushi, the son of Gedaliah, the son of Amariah, the son of Hezekiah, in the days of Josiah the son of Amon, king of Judah. LXX likewise. The Hebrews hand down that whenever the father or grandfather of any prophet is set in the title, they too were prophets. Hence Amos also, one of the twelve prophets, who had said: I am not a prophet, nor the son of a prophet, but a herdsman of goats, plucking sycamore fruit, does not have his father's name in the title. If this is true, Zephaniah the prophet, whom we are now attempting to expound, was begotten by a prophetic custom, so to speak, and of a glorious stock of ancestors; for he had as father Cushi, as grandfather Gedaliah, as great-grandfather Amariah, as great-great-grandfather Hezekiah, and he himself, like the last charioteer, completed such a four-horse team. Some have translated the name of Zephaniah as 'watchtower', others as 'the secret of the Lord'. So whether it is interpreted 'watchtower' or 'the hidden thing of the Lord', both suit the prophet. For it is said also to Ezekiel: Son of man, I have set you as a watchman for the house of Israel, and in another place: The Lord will not do anything unless he has revealed his teaching to his servants the prophets, and the title of Psalm 9 is rendered 'for the hidden things of the son'. This prophet, then, who was on the watchtower and set on the heights, and knew the mysteries of the Lord, was the son of Cushi — which is interpreted 'humility', or 'my Ethiopian', of which we shall treat later —; he had also as grandfather Gedaliah, who is called 'the greatness of the Lord', and as great-grandfather Amariah, who is himself also rendered 'the word of the Lord', and as great-great-grandfather Hezekiah, which sounds 'the strength of the Lord'. So from the strength of the Lord was born the word of the Lord, and from the word of the Lord was born the greatness of the Lord, and from the greatness of the Lord was born humility, so that when someone has arrived at the perfect, he may say: I am not worthy to be called an apostle, and that in the psalms: Lord, my heart is not exalted, nor are my eyes lifted up. Up to this point, running as if on a downward slope and over level ground, we have stumbled on the fact that Cushi is also interpreted 'my Ethiopian'. For after such great virtues, how could the name of an Ethiopian sound in praise? And indeed, if Scripture had said Cush, that is, 'Ethiopian', the question would seem insoluble; for Cush was born of Ham. But in that it says Cushi, that is, 'my Ethiopian', a mystery seems to sound; namely, that he who was once an Ethiopian, turned to repentance — according to what is said: Ethiopia will stretch out her hand first to God, and in another place: Before him the Ethiopians will fall down —, may say with the bride in the Song of Songs: I am black and beautiful, daughter of Jerusalem. We read also in Jeremiah that Ebed-melech the Ethiopian eunuch pleased God; and in the Acts of the Apostles that the Ethiopian eunuch of Queen Candace had such zeal for the Scriptures and the law of God that he read in his carriage, and came to Jerusalem to worship the Lord in his temple. Hence such faith is crowned with a worthy reward, and Philip the evangelist is sent to him, and at once he is taught, believes, is baptized and is saved. And he is not set down only as a eunuch, but with the addition of man: a eunuch, a man, an Ethiopian. For because he was a eunuch of Christ, and had made himself a eunuch for the sake of the kingdom of heaven, for that reason he had not lost the name of man. Rightly also does Zephaniah, as the son of Cushi, that is, of the Ethiopian, write in the following parts of the book about the repentance of the Ethiopians: Across the rivers, he says, of Ethiopia, from there they will bring victims to me. This much on the genealogy of Zephaniah, who prophesied in the days of Josiah. Now just as those who were enlightened by Elijah are called the days of Elijah, so too are they called the days of Josiah, who had lifted himself up to the Lord, since Josiah is interpreted 'the elevation of the Lord'; and he was a just man, and the history of Kingdoms and of Chronicles also writes of his praises. And he had as father Amon, as grandfather Manasseh. We read that Manasseh, after many crimes and after the captivity in Babylon, repented, and, turned to better things, obtained the mercy of the Lord. Hence he called his son ἐπώνυμον (named after it) of his faith, by which he had believed God, that is, Amon; for Amon is interpreted 'faith'. And at the same time consider that by no means, as above, are the kings of the ten tribes, that is, the kings of Israel, set down, but only the kings of Judah. For the ten tribes had already been led away into captivity by the Assyrians under King Hezekiah, the father of Manasseh. This much in the preface and in the title, on Zephaniah's lineage and time. Now let us see what the prophecy itself also contains.

1:2-3

Gathering I will gather all things from the face of the earth, says the Lord, gathering man and beast, gathering the bird of the heaven and the fish of the sea; and there will be ruins of the ungodly, and I will destroy men from the face of the earth, says the Lord. LXX: Let it fail with failing from the face of the earth, says the Lord; let man fail and the cattle; let the birds of the heaven fail, and the fish of the sea; and the ungodly will be weakened, and I will remove the wicked from the face of the earth, says the Lord. What we have set down in the Seventy, And the ungodly will be weakened, was added from the translation of Theodotion; in its place Symmachus translated: And stumbling blocks with the ungodly, so that 'will be gathered' or 'will fail' is understood; and the Fifth Edition: And weakness with the ungodly will fail. We ought therefore, following our custom, first to weave the history, and afterwards to discuss the loftier things. For no one doubts that the last reign of the two tribes, which were called Judah and Benjamin, was under Josiah. For after he was killed, his sons who reigned afterwards, and his grandsons, are to be thought not so much to have reigned as to have been held in mockery by the king of Egypt and by the Chaldeans, and tormented by various captivities and deaths. Since, then, the people could excuse themselves by their bad kings and say: We wish to serve God, but we are prevented by the kings, a just king is given; and while he was zealous with the zeal of the Lord, and the people nonetheless persevered in the worship of idols, a just cause of wrath is brought forward by the Lord, and there is prophecy of the overthrow of Jerusalem, and the captivity of Judah, and the victory of Nebuchadnezzar. And the Lord says through the prophet: Never again will I grant repentance, but I will consume everything from the face of the earth; neither man nor beast nor bird nor the fish of the sea will remain. For even the brute animals feel the wrath of the Lord; and that, when cities are laid waste and men killed, there comes about a desolation and scarcity of beasts too, and of birds and fish, Illyricum is witness, Thrace is witness, witness is the soil on which I was born, where, apart from the sky and the earth and the growing brambles and the thickets of the woods, all things have perished. And this, says the prophet, will happen because the multitude of the ungodly was too great. So the ungodly will fall and men will be destroyed, and there will be a desolation upon the face of the earth. But we can also take this very thing of the consummation of the world: that men and cattle and birds and the fish of the sea and all things will fail, and the ungodly will be weakened, and iniquity will be removed from the face of the earth. But if we wish to understand something deeper as well — because of what is said by the Seventy: Let it fail with failing from the face of the earth —, and to take failing in a good sense, according to that saying: And failing, Abraham died in a good old age, an old man and full of days, and was gathered to his people; and from what Scripture relates of Isaac and Jacob, we shall see how they fail with failing from the face of the earth, and that they fulfil this precept: Who, having their conversation in heaven, and in the flesh not warring according to the flesh, because they know that those who live in the flesh cannot please God, do everything, as far as in them lies, that they may not be in the flesh but in the spirit; and withdrawing from the earth they say: He has raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ. But if anyone should set against our taking Let it fail with failing from the face of the earth in a good sense that which is written of Ishmael: These are the years of the life of Ishmael, a hundred and thirty-seven, and failing he died, and was gathered to his kind, we shall answer him, first, that Ishmael himself too was a son of Abraham, and received gifts and portions from his father, according to his measure; then, that it is written absolutely: Failing he died, and there is not added what is written of Abraham: In a good old age, an old man and full of days, and was gathered to his people. Or of Isaac: And the days of Isaac which he lived were a hundred and eighty-five years, and failing Isaac died, and was gathered to his kind, an old man and full of days. And also of Jacob: And Jacob ceased charging his sons, and lifting his feet upon the bed he failed, and was gathered to his people. From this we understand that it is one thing merely to fail, and another, along with failing, to have many virtues together. And what it had first said in general, Let it fail with failing from the face of the earth, divine Scripture afterwards divided into parts: let man fail, and the cattle, let the birds of the heaven fail, and the fish of the sea. There are four things which are commanded to fail: first man, the rational being, then three which are subject to man, the cattle, and the birds, and the fish, which I think are set down also in Psalm 8: Moreover also the beasts of the field, the birds of the heaven and the fish of the sea, which pass through the paths of the sea. But what it had first said: all sheep and oxen, it separated as the chief of the cattle, and did not wish to number them with the cattle that remained. So let man fail, let the cattle fail, let the birds of the heaven fail, let the fish fail; and it did not say: let the wild beasts fail, let the creeping things of the earth fail. For these ought not to fail but to perish; but let those things fail which can receive correction. Just as the ways of women had failed Sarah, and Abraham is commanded to listen to whatever Sarah should enjoin. One fails as a man if he despises human things, and no longer dies as a man, and hears: I have said, you are gods. Another fails as cattle who, climbing to higher things, is not accused by the prophetic word: Man, when he was in honour, did not understand; he was compared to the senseless cattle, and was made like them. He fails like a bird of the heaven who makes himself the wings of an eagle, and returns rich to the house of his teacher, forsaking all poverty. He fails like a fish of the sea who, caught in the nets of the Lord, is separated along with the good fish. When these things have been accomplished according to the Lord's precept, the ungodly will be weakened, not having as much strength as before. But the wicked will also be removed — he did not say 'will be killed', but 'will be removed' —, so that, converted to better things, carried over from ungodliness and iniquity to godliness and justice, they may begin to be what they had not been before. These things according to tropology. For we ought to set down the interpretation of our elders as well. It will now be in the reader's judgment whether he wishes what has been said to sound severity or clemency.

1:4-6

And I will stretch out my hand over Judah and over all the inhabitants of Jerusalem; and I will destroy from this place the remnants of Baal, and the names of the temple-keepers with the priests, and those who worship the host of heaven upon the roofs, and worship and swear by the Lord, and swear by Milcom; and those who turn away from behind the Lord, and who have not sought the Lord nor searched for him. LXX: And I will stretch out my hand over Judah, and over all who dwell in Jerusalem; and I will remove from this place the names of Baalim, and the names of the priests with the priests, and those who worship the host of heaven upon the roofs, and those who swear by the Lord, and swear by their king, and those who turn aside from the Lord, and who do not seek the Lord, and who do not hold fast the Lord. After the ruin of the ungodly and the removal of the wicked from the face of the earth, it is said consequently against Judah and against Jerusalem in the person of the Lord: And I will stretch out my hand over Judah and over all the inhabitants of Jerusalem. For the stretching out of the hand shows the gesture of one who strikes. And I will destroy from this place the remnants of Baal. Not that, according to the LXX, the names of Baalim are to be utterly rooted out, but that, according to the Hebrew, he threatens to remove his worshippers from the people, the few who had remained in Judah and Jerusalem from the slaughter by the enemies. The names also of the temple-keepers with the priests, because Judah and Benjamin had come to such impiety that in the temple of the Lord — according to what Ezekiel writes, and the fourth book of Kingdoms (2 Kings) shows — they set up a statue of Baal, which the Lord calls the image of jealousy, and in the same sanctuary they venerated the idols and the Lord alike. Hence, pointedly, he called the priests of the idols not priests but τεμενίτας, that is, temple-keepers or fanatics, which in Hebrew is called acchumarim. Both the temple-keepers, then, and those once priests of God, and those who on the housetops worshipped the host of heaven, the sun and the moon and the rest of the stars; and those who swore by the name of the Lord and by the name of the idol of the Ammonites, Milcom — which was turned by the LXX into 'king' —, the Lord will remove from the place of Jerusalem, or will destroy; for it is understood ἀπὸ κοινοῦ (in common). And I will destroy from this place the remnants of Baal, and I will destroy the names of the temple-keepers with the priests, and I will destroy those who worship the host of heaven upon the roofs, and I will destroy those who worship and swear by the Lord, and swear by Milcom, and I will destroy those who turn away, or turn aside, from behind the Lord, and who do not seek him, or do not hold him fast. Now Israel turn away behind the Lord, who, abandoning his worship, swear by Milcom, and worship the host of heaven, and venerate Baal, the idol of the Sidonians. Up to this point the sense of the history has been expounded; let us see the anagogy as well. Because of the Lord, who arose from the tribe of Judah, and because of Jerusalem, in which Judah reigned, that is, the Lord and Savior, let us say that when iniquity has been multiplied, and the love of many has grown cold, and at the Lord's coming faith appears rare on the earth, so much that even the elect of God are tempted, then the Lord stretches out his hand for the punishment of sinners over Judah, who seems to himself to confess the name of the Lord, and over Jerusalem, the Church, which has obtained its name from peace, and removes from the Church the names of Baalim, which is interpreted 'in the higher places'. The Lord will remove the names of vainglory and of false admiration which have their place in the Church, in which, according to James, the one who has a gold ring is venerated, and the poor man is despised, when at the arrival of a judge and a senator, and generally of all the rich, the whole people rises up, and to the holy poor man not even a place to stand is granted among the throngs of the powerful and the concessions they grant; and also the names of the priests with the priests, who vainly applaud themselves in the name of bishop and in the dignity of the presbyterate, and not in their work. Hence, pointedly, he does not say 'and the works of the priests with the priests', but 'the names', those who only display the false names of dignities and destroy their own names by evil works; and those who worship the host of heaven upon the roofs, who are lifted up against the knowledge of God, and, promising themselves a feigned knowledge, refer everything that happens in the world to the risings and settings of the stars, and follow the errors of the astrologers; and those who worship the Lord and Milcom, who think they can serve the world and the Lord alike and satisfy two masters, God and mammon; who, as soldiers of Christ, entangle themselves in worldly business, and offer the same image to God and to Caesar, and, though they say they are priests of Christ, consecrate their sons to Milcom, that is, to their king. For rightly do they have a man as king who have lost the Lord as king; and those who through evil works turn aside from the Lord and do not seek him, they hold fast their sins while he flees from them. And if anyone wishes, according to the interpretation of the names Judah and Jerusalem, to understand this very thing of each one's soul, he will not err: that the Lord removes all the things we have mentioned either at the consummation of the world or at the departure of each person, when they will hear: Fool, this night your soul will be taken from you. And he stretches out his hand over him who does not confess the Lord, and over him who boasts that he has the mind of peace, to remove and destroy from such a Jerusalem all pride, and false worship of God, and the errors of various doctrines, and servitude to God and the world alike, and, through daily sins, aversion from the Lord and neglect of God.

1:7

Be silent before the face of the Lord God, for the day of the Lord is near, for the Lord has prepared a victim, he has sanctified his called ones. LXX: Fear before the face of the Lord God, for the day of the Lord is near, for the Lord has prepared his sacrifice, he has sanctified his called ones. Where the LXX translated fear, and we have set down be silent, in the Hebrew there is an interjection of one commanding silence, which the comic poets also often use; but silence is also enjoined absolutely on all, because the day of the Lord is to come. And by the day of the Lord let us understand the day of captivity and of vengeance on the sinful people, and the victim of the overthrow of Jerusalem, and the sanctification of those whom he has dedicated to slaughter, according to what is said in Jeremiah: Sanctify them for the day of their slaughter. And the sense is: The captivity foretold long ago is coming against the ungodly people; it is already near. For the prophecy is woven under King Josiah; once he has been killed, fear before the face of the Lord God, for the day of the Lord is near, for the Lord has prepared his sacrifice, he has sanctified his called ones. Utter devastation is coming, of which it is said in Ezekiel too: The end, he says, has come; the end has come, and so on. This sacrifice pleases me; these victims I have sanctified for myself. And what he says, He has sanctified his called ones, can be taken also of the Babylonians, whom, for vengeance on the people, he even calls his servants, as they avenge the wrong done to him. I have called, he says, Nebuchadnezzar my servant. And in the same volume he calls him not only servant but also dove: From the face of the sword of the dove. Moreover, according to tropology, since the face of the Lord is against those who do evil, to destroy the memory of them from the earth, and the day of judgment is near — because in comparison with eternity the whole time of this world is short —, or the departure of each person; let all fear and be silent, lest the face of the Lord — of which the holy one says: The light of your countenance is sealed upon us, O Lord — consume the hay, the stubble and the wood of sins. For the Lord has prepared his victim, the whole mystery of Leviticus, when through fire and the shedding of blood and the true offering those who are to be saved will be saved, and the called will be sanctified. Some of our writers understand the day of the Lord and his victim and the sanctification of the called of the coming of the Savior, when the Lamb was sacrificed, and in his blood the apostles were sanctified, and the rest who were called through them.

1:8-9

And it will be on the day of the Lord's victim, I will visit upon the princes, and upon the king's sons, and upon all who are clothed in foreign apparel. And I will visit everyone who arrogantly enters upon the threshold on that day, who fill the house of the Lord their God with iniquity and deceit. LXX: And it will be on the day of the Lord's victim, and I will take vengeance upon the princes, and upon the house of the king, and upon all who are clothed in alien garments; and I will take vengeance openly upon all who are in the porches on that day, who fill the house of the Lord their God with ungodliness and deceit. On the day of the Jewish captivity, when the whole people is to be sacrificed, the Lord will visit both upon the princes, who drank strong drink in the morning, and upon the king's sons, either all of the royal stock or certainly, properly, the sons of Josiah, whom we read were either killed or captured; and upon all who are clothed in foreign apparel, that is, who instead of the worship of God venerated idols. And upon all who arrogantly enter upon the threshold on that day, that is, against the proud, who with a certain disdain and haughtiness of rank ascend the steps of the temple and the threshold of the sanctuary. Moreover, where we translated: Who arrogantly enter upon the threshold, it can be understood according to the Hebrew as 'who leap over the threshold'; and this is to be taken historically: I will avenge upon those who, according to the first book of Kingdoms (1 Samuel), do not tread on the threshold of the idols, serving superstitions, who have filled the house of the Lord their God not only with the worship of idols but with iniquity and crime and every lie, so that to the error of religion there might be joined also iniquity toward subjects and lying toward neighbours. But since we have once begun to expound tropologically as well: The Lord will visit, at the coming and passion of the Savior, that is, on the day of the victim of his Son, upon the high priests and priests of the Jewish people and upon the royal house. For up to that time the kings of Judah from the stock of David continued, according to the prophecy of Jacob: A prince shall not fail from Judah, nor a leader from his thighs, until he comes for whom it is laid up, and he will be the expectation of the nations. For after the Lord's victim the kingdom was taken from the Jews. And upon all, he says, who are clothed in alien garments, who have withdrawn from the protection and clothing of God and are covered with their own error. And I will take vengeance openly upon all who are in the porches, that is, who have gone out from the temple of God; and though they ought to have been within, because of their sins they have gone outside and withdrawn from the Church of God, filling his temple with ungodliness and deceit. Let this be understood of the first coming of the Savior. But since we have once expounded it of the consummation of the world and the day of judgment, which all interpret as the day of the Lord, we ought to know that at that time the Lord will visit upon the princes and upon the shepherds, who eat the milk of the sheep and, shearing the wool, have no care for the breaking of the flock; and upon the king's sons, who boast that they are Christians and glory that they are sons of Christ the king; and upon all who are clothed in alien garments. The garment of the king's sons and the clothing of the princes is Christ, whom we receive in baptism, according to that saying: Put on Christ Jesus, and: Put on bowels of mercy, of kindness, of humility, of meekness, of patience, and so on. In these we are commanded to be clothed with the new, heavenly man, according to our Creator, and to cast off the clothing of the old man with his works. Though, then, we ought to be clothed with such garments, instead of mercy we are clothed with cruelty, instead of patience with impatience, instead of justice with iniquity. And to say it once for all, instead of virtues with vices; that is, instead of Christ with the Antichrist. Hence it is said of a man of this kind: And he was clothed with cursing as with a garment. The Lord will also avenge most openly at his coming even upon those who, though they ought to have been in the Church with good works, have cast themselves out because of their most wicked way of life; and, handed over to Satan, they abide in the porches, or rather not even in the porches but before the porches, which is said more expressively in Greek, ἐπὶ τὰ πρόπυλα. And he will avenge upon all who fill the Church with various iniquities and sins, and with ungodliness and lying, and mingle blood with blood. But if we wish to take this very thing of the souls of individuals, let us understand by the princes and the house of the king the λογισμοὺς, that is, the thoughts and senses, and the soul itself, which ought to be the lodging of the king; and according to the exposition above let us also refer the alien clothing, and all that follows, to each one of the believers who, though they ought to have been clothed with Christ and always to abide within, have covered themselves with the various garments of sins, and, gone out from the Church, that is, from the congregation of the saints, have filled the temple of their body, instead of with virtues, with iniquity and deceit.

1:10

And it will be on that day, says the Lord, the sound of a cry from the Fish Gate, and wailing from the Second, and a great crushing from the hills. LXX: And it will be on that day, says the Lord, the sound of a cry from the gate of those who pierce, and wailing from the Second, and a great crushing from the hills. On the day of the Lord's victim, when he stretches out his hand over Judah and over all who dwell in Jerusalem, and a hostile army has besieged it, there will be the sound of a cry from the Fish Gate and wailing from the Second, and a great crushing from the hills. They called the Fish Gate the one which leads to Diospolis and Joppa, and of all the roads of Jerusalem it was the nearest to the sea; of it Ezra too relates: But the Fish Gate the sons of Hassenaah built; they covered it, and set up its doors and locks and bars. And what he says, And wailing from the Second, signifies the gate of the second wall in the same region, of which it is written also in the book of Kingdoms: And Hilkiah the priest went, and Ahikam, and Achbor, and Shaphan, and Asaiah, to Huldah the prophetess, the wife of Shallum son of Tikvah, son of Harhas, keeper of the garments, and she dwelt in Jerusalem in the Second. And by the great crushing from the hills he speaks of Mount Zion and the higher part of the city, because when the heights and the citadel of the city have been taken, the descent to the lower parts is easier. But if we wish to take that day with which the Lord threatens, as above, as the day of judgment, at that time when the Ancient of days is to sit, and the books are to be opened, and the consciences of each are to be laid bare, then the sound of a cry will be fulfilled from the gate of those who pierce. For the first gate will be that of the eyes, when our sins will be displayed to us before our eyes, and all the pomp and image of our old crimes and vices and luxury will be brought out into the open. Then that will be true which is written: Behold the man and his works before his face. His conscience, then, will torment him, and after he has cried out, pierced to the heart, from the first gate, that of the eyes, he will wail also from the second, which we can understand as the ears. For it is through these senses above all, by which the vices had slipped in, that their punishment will be felt, when we discern what we have done and, hearing the discourse of the learned one and the whole order of our sins, we are driven to wailing, and whatever had been lofty in us is crushed, and was unknown to us because of blindness and deaf ears. Or certainly when lofty words and instruction coming from on high will crush and break us, and this will be fulfilled in deed: I roared from the groaning of my heart, so that there may be in us a sacrifice to God, a troubled spirit; in us, who are men and have not committed sins so great as to be compared to mountains, the hills are crushed. But in the devil and his angels the heights of the mountains will be crushed. Many think that what we have referred, according to the history, to the times of the Babylonians is to be understood of the first coming of the Savior, when, because of their excessive sins and the clamour of the people crying out: His blood be upon us, and upon our children, Jerusalem was surrounded by an army, and by two bears, namely Vespasian and Titus, the crowd of mocking boys was consumed. This understanding indeed suits our faith better, but in such a way that we know it can also agree with the earlier history, or certainly that the earlier captivity is a type of the second and complete overthrow of Jerusalem. This too must be noticed — since adagim in the Hebrew plainly sounds not 'the gate of those who pierce' but 'the Fish Gate' —: allegorically, the Fish Gate is in Jerusalem, through which are brought in the good fish that have been separated from the bad, and those who have remained outside will mourn as the rest go in. Or certainly at the end of the world and at the consummation those will mourn from the first gate, the Fish Gate, who have not kept their baptism; those will mourn from the second who have not done worthy repentance for their sins. And there will be a great crushing upon the hills, who were not even bowed down for their sins so as to submit their necks and bewail their crimes. For through these two gates, of baptism and of repentance, there is either entry or return into Jerusalem, that is, into the Church of God.

1:11

Wail, inhabitants of the Mortar; all the people of Canaan has fallen silent. LXX: Lament, you who inhabit the one cut to pieces, for all the people of Canaan has been made like. The Mortar, which in Hebrew is called machtes, and which was rendered by Aquila εὶς τὸν ὅλμον (into the mortar), must be read with its first syllable not short, lest we think it means σφαῖραν (a ball), but long, so that we may know it is said of the mortar in which grain is pounded, a hollow vessel, suited also to the use of physicians, in which, properly speaking, ptisans are usually crushed. Someone may say that he has understood what the Mortar means, but wishes to know why it has been taken up in the present place. Because there was once a description of the captured city, and it is said: A voice of crying from the Fish Gate, and wailing from the Second, and a great crushing from the hills; now the same order of description is kept, and the wailing is spoken of those who dwell in the valley of Siloam. And Scripture has beautifully not said, you who inhabit the valley, you who inhabit the hovel, but you who inhabit the Mortar, namely because, just as grain is pounded when the pestle strikes from above, so the army, rushing out from the Fish Gate, and from the Second Gate, and from the hills, will run down upon you. Now he has called the people of the Jews the people of Canaan, according to what we read in Daniel: Seed of Canaan and not of Judah; and to Jerusalem: Your father an Amorite, and your mother a Hittite; and in another place: Canaan, in your hand, a balance of iniquity. But if we wish to weave a tropology according to both translations, those are rightly roused to wailing and lamentation who dwell in the uttermost lees of sins, and, sunk in the depths of their crimes, say: I am stuck fast in the mire of the deep, and there is no, he says, substance. Hence it is also added: You who inhabit the one cut to pieces, that is, the soul wounded by many iniquities, or the Church, which is torn by schisms and heresies, and at each wound laments her slain sons. But when he says: Has fallen silent, or has been made like, all the people of Canaan, he signifies that their blasphemy will cease on the day of judgment, and that the mouth which they set on high, and the tongue reaching all the way down to the earth, will fall silent in eternal silence. And because Jerusalem has sinned a sin, and has therefore been turned into commotion, the people is called Canaan, which means commotion. For it cannot say: He has set my feet upon a rock, but, uncertain and wavering, it is always in motion. Hence also Noah, a holy man, after he awoke from sleep, gave Canaan a name out of a curse, saying: Cursed be Canaan the boy; a servant he will be to his brothers. Not only are sinners likened to Canaan, however, but according to the quality and diversity of the sin, one is likened to Pharaoh, another to Nimrod the giant. And on the contrary, through good works and virtues, since the paths of the virtues too are diverse, one takes up the spirit of Abraham, another that of Moses, another that of Elijah; for which reason it is said by the Apostle: Be zealous for the better gifts. But he who is perfect, yet according to that perfection which the human condition can receive, is marked with the likeness of God.

All who were wrapped in silver have perished. LXX: All who are lifted up by silver have perished. Those, he says, who trusted in riches and had such great wealth that they reckoned themselves as it were wrapped and fenced about by their riches, or certainly, according to the LXX, those who were lifted up into pride and despised the poor, were laid waste when wrath came. And consider at the same time that he did not say: They will perish who are lifted up by silver, but that already now, before the day of punishment comes upon them, in the very fact that they are proud, and always think about their treasures and guard them, they have perished and collapsed. Whoever has understood this, I think, will not much desire riches, in which those who are lifted up will not perish, but have perished. Nor indeed should it be thought that only those have perished who are lifted up by silver, but according to this definition he too will perish who boasts in the nobility of his birth. He will perish who is puffed up by dignities; he will perish who glories in the strength of his body; he will perish who, softened by a womanish languor, grows his hair, plucks out his hairs, polishes his skin, and adorns himself at the mirror, which is properly the passion and madness of women. But if anyone wishes to be lifted up and to be proud with a holy pride, let him be lifted up with the apostles, when he has been found worthy to suffer insult for the name of Jesus Christ; let him glory with the Apostle, who exulted in tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience, patience hope, and hope does not confound.

1:12

And it will be at that time, I will search Jerusalem with lamps, and I will visit upon the men settled on their lees, who say in their hearts: The Lord will not do good, and he will not do evil. LXX: And it will be on that day, I will search Jerusalem with a lamp, and I will take vengeance upon the men who despise their charges, and say in their hearts: The Lord will not do good, and he will not do evil. At the time and on the day of the captivity of Jerusalem, whether by the Babylonians or by the Romans — because she forsook the law of the Lord, and acted impiously against the Lord her Creator —, the Lord will search out with a lamp all the hidden places of Jerusalem, and will let no one escape unpunished. Let us read the histories of Josephus, and there we shall find it written that princes and kings and powerful men and priests, who had hidden themselves in them for fear of death, were dragged out of sewers too, and caves, and grottoes, and tombs. And I will visit, he says, upon those who trust in their bodies and in their strength, which he disparagingly (διασυρτικῶς) calls lees, or else their sins, in which they were deeply settled; who, doing away with providence, said that God is the author neither of good nor of evil, that is, that he would render neither good things to the good nor evil things to the evil, but that all things were governed by the will of fortune and borne along by uncertain chance. But at the consummation of the world, since that is understood as the day of the Lord itself, the Lord will search Jerusalem, that is, his Church, with a lamp; and he will take vengeance upon the men who are despisers, who would not keep their charges, that is, who despised the commandments of the Lord, and moreover, saying that they sinned with reason, blasphemed in their hearts, that it was of no profit to do good, nor of any harm to do evil, because God would render neither the reward of good works nor the punishment of evil ones. And rightly Jerusalem, that is, the Church — which was formerly called Jebus, which means trodden down —, was called Jebus when she was being trodden down by the nations and was the plaything of demons, and after the peace of the Lord began to dwell in her, and his place was made in peace, she obtained the name Jerusalem. Since, therefore, in the last time, as we have often said already, charity will grow cold because iniquity is multiplied, and the light of the sun will withdraw from Jerusalem, and there will be such great desolation that even the elect of God will scarcely be saved, then the Lord will search out all the vices in Jerusalem with the lamp of his word and reason, and will bring them out into the open, and judgment and vengeance will be passed even for an idle word, not upon sinners — for even sin could merit pardon —, but upon despisers, of whom it is said in Habakkuk: Behold, you despisers, and look, and in another place: Why do you not look upon the despisers?, and then: But he who is arrogant, and a despiser, a proud man, and so on. Vengeance will fall upon these, who did not keep the Lord's charges, and say in their hearts: The Lord will not do good, and he will not do evil; not that God does evil, but that punishment seems evil to him who suffers it. Otherwise the physician's scalpel too will be evil, because it cuts open wounds and amputates putrid flesh; and a father will be evil when he beats his son to correct him from his vices, and a teacher evil when he rebukes his pupil to instruct him: For all discipline for the present seems to be not of joy, but of sorrow; but afterwards it will yield the peaceful fruit to those who have been trained by it.

1:13-14a

And their strength will be for plunder, and their house for a desert. And they will build houses, and will not inhabit them, and they will plant vineyards, and will not drink their wine. Close is the great day of the Lord; it is close, and exceedingly swift. LXX: And their strength will be for pillage, and their houses will be scattered. And they will build houses, and will not dwell in them, and they will plant vineyards, and will not drink their wine, for close is the great day of the Lord; close and exceedingly swift. It is plain that in both captivities their whole army was cut down, and their houses torn down, and their fields and vineyards laid waste, and that the patience of God no longer put them off. But while they were saying to the prophets, this will be for a time, and for many days, the great day of the Lord came upon them, exceedingly swift. According to tropology, however, when the time of judgment comes, or of each one's death and departure from the world, then all their strength will be for plunder, so that what was strong for evil, and was lifted up against the Lord, may be made weak and broken and turned to something better. Just as, if someone plunders the strength of a robber and a pirate and a thief, and renders them weak, their weakness profits them; for with their limbs disabled, which they formerly did not use well, they will cease from evil work. As for what follows: And their house for a desert, there are many in the Church building Zion in blood and Jerusalem in iniquity, whom it profits that such houses be destroyed. Let us read Leviticus, where a leprous house is ordered to be destroyed. And because the leprosy is lasting and spreads, its stones and timbers and all its dust are ordered to be thrown outside the city into an unclean place. And in the beginning of Jeremiah too something of the kind is written: Behold, I have put my words into your mouth; behold, I have set you today over nations and kingdoms, to root up and to undermine, and to destroy, and to build, and to plant. A very bad building is destroyed so that afterwards a good building may be put up; a wicked planting is rooted up so that a planting may be set in its place. Hence we also read in Solomon: It is better to dwell under the open sky than in the plaster of strife with iniquity, and in a new house. As a good God, therefore, he destroys the houses of those who were settled on their lees, and had said in their heart: The Lord will not do good and he will not do evil; and he does not allow them to dwell in leprous and unclean houses, nor does he let them drink wine from the vineyards which they had planted. For if they had planted a vineyard of Sorek, and a chosen vineyard, wholly true, they would have drunk their wine, and would have been inebriated with the patriarch Noah, and with Joseph at noon; but because they said: The Lord will not do good and he will not do evil, and: Their vine was of the vine of Sodom, and their shoot of Gomorrah. Their grape is a grape of gall, and theirs is a cluster of bitterness; the fury of dragons is their wine, and the incurable fury of asps, therefore they have planted vineyards and will not drink their wine. And it is said mystically of Sodom and Gomorrah that all their planting perished. For if they had remained in what they had begun, so as to be like the paradise of God, and had not ended in evil, so as to be like the land of Egypt, their planting would surely have remained. Something of the kind is sounded also by that verse concerning the Egyptians in the psalms: He killed their vineyards with hail, and their mulberry trees with frost. For as a most merciful God he killed and overturned every Egyptian planting, and the shrubs which, rooted in Egypt, bear blood-red fruits, so that those who planted badly may not drink, and may not eat fruit from them. Near is the great day of the Lord and exceedingly swift, which no one can withstand; and it is near either because of eternity, since nothing is long to it, or because of the greatness of the punishment, since to one who suffers it, the torment that is to be inflicted never seems far off. Or else it is near, as we said above, when we depart from the world, and each one's death has brought about the consummation of the world; and not only near, but also exceedingly swift, so that the swiftness of its coming may be shown in what is added, exceedingly.

1:14b-16

The voice of the day of the Lord is bitter; there the strong man will be afflicted.

That day is a day of wrath, a day of tribulation and distress, a day of calamity and misery, a day of darkness and gloom, a day of cloud and whirlwind, a day of trumpet and clangor against the fortified cities and against the lofty corners. LXX: The voice of the day of the Lord is bitter and harsh; it has been set, strong. A day of wrath is that day, a day of tribulation and constraint, a day of misery and destruction, a day of darkness and whirlwind, a day of cloud and gloom, a day of trumpet and shouting against the fortified cities and against the lofty corners. In keeping with what precedes, take it either of the Babylonian captivity or of the last one, which they suffered at the hands of the Romans, over which the Lord also wept for Jerusalem, saying: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets, and stone those who are sent to you, and so on. For truly vengeance was exacted from the blood of Abel the just to the blood of Zechariah, whom they killed between the temple and the altar, and at the last for the Son of God; saying: His blood be upon us and upon our children, they experienced a bitter day, because they had provoked the Lord to bitterness; a day appointed by the Lord, on which not just any weak men, but the strongest men will be pressed down, and wrath will come upon them to the end. For they had often endured the wrath of the Lord before as well, but that wrath had not been one of consummation and of the end. What need is there now to describe how great the calamities were that they endured in both captivities, and how those who spurned the light of the Lord passed their lives in darkness and gloom, and those who would not hear the trumpets of the solemn days heard the shouting of men at war? As for the fortified cities and the lofty corners of Judea, which have been torn down to the very ground, I think it is a matter for the judgment of the eyes rather than of the ears, especially for us who are now in this province: we may see, we may test what is written. In what were once great cities we scarcely discern small traces of ruins. Of Shiloh, where the tabernacle and the ark of the covenant of the Lord were, scarcely the foundations of the altar are shown. Gibeah, that city of Saul, has been torn down to its foundations. Ramah and Beth-horon and the other noble cities built by Solomon are pointed out as small hamlets. Let us read Josephus and the prophecy of Zephaniah, and we shall see the history of the latter; and this is to be said not only of the captivity, but to the present day the faithless tenants, after the killing of the servants and at the last of the Son of God, are forbidden to enter Jerusalem except to lament; and to be permitted to weep over the ruin of their city, they pay a price, so that those who once bought the blood of Christ buy their own tears, and not even their weeping is free to them. You may see, on the day on which Jerusalem was captured and destroyed by the Romans, a mournful people coming, decrepit little women and old men covered with rags and years flocking together, showing in their bodies and in their dress the wrath of the Lord. A crowd of wretches gathers, and while the gibbet of the Lord gleams, and his ἀναστάσει (the Resurrection: the Church of the Anastasis in Jerusalem) shines, and the standard of the cross blazes from the Mount of Olives as well, you may see the wretched people lamenting the ruins of their temple, and yet not deserving pity; the tears are still on their cheeks, their arms are livid, and their hair dishevelled, and the soldier demands a fee so that they may be allowed to weep longer; and does anyone, seeing these things, doubt about the day of tribulation and distress, about the day of calamity and misery, about the day of darkness and gloom, about the day of cloud and whirlwind, about the day of trumpet and clangor? For they have trumpets even in their mourning, and, according to the prophecy, the voice of solemnity is turned into lamentation. They wail over the ashes of the sanctuary, and over the destroyed altar, and over the once fortified cities, and over the lofty corners of the temple, from which they once cast down James the brother of the Lord. Let this be said about the captivity of the Jews. But if, as above, we refer the day of the Lord to the consummation of the world, or to the end of each one's life, the interpretation will be plain: that the voice of the day of the Lord is bitter, and full of constraint and of wrath, and that the strong man is afflicted there, since even those who are holy are saved indeed, but yet so as through fire. That day is a day of tribulation, and distress, and calamity, and misery, on which they will say: Woe to us, for we have become wretched. A day of darkness: For everyone who does evil hates the light and does not come to the light, and it must be that darkness overtakes him who hates the light. It will be a day of cloud and whirlwind; for the tempest of the Lord will come upon him, and the clangor of the trumpet which the Apostle signifies, saying: At the last trumpet. For it will be a day of trumpet and clangor against the fortified cities, which they had built for themselves with Cain in the multitude of their sins. And against the lofty corners: perverse works, turning aside from the right way of the Lord. Hence the Pharisees, ὑποκρίται (hypocrites), are also rebuked by the Savior, because they worship at the corners of the streets. For the way that is straight and tends toward paradise is narrow, and strait is the way which leads to life. But the way that is broken, having corners, and crooked, is broad and spacious, the way which leads to death. And at the same time, amid the words of the Lord's severity, discern his clemency: that the day is bitter, and a day of wrath, and a day of tribulation, and a day of trumpet and clangor, for this reason, that the badly fortified cities and the perverse corners may be destroyed.

1:17-18

And I will afflict men, and they will walk like the blind, because they have sinned against the Lord; and their blood will be poured out like soil, and their bodies like dung; but even their silver and gold will not be able to deliver them on the day of the wrath of the Lord; in the fire of his zeal the whole earth will be devoured, for he will make a consummation with haste for all who inhabit the earth. LXX: And I will afflict men, and they will walk like the blind, because they have sinned against the Lord, and he will pour out their blood like dust, and their flesh like the dung of oxen, and their silver and their gold will not be able to rescue them on the day of the wrath of the Lord; and in the fire of his zeal the whole earth will be consumed, for he will make a consummation and haste upon all who inhabit the earth. It is not difficult to say, according to the former sense, that Jerusalem suffered these things, which she endured because of the cross of the Lord; for the visitation of the Lord departed from her, and all the men in the whole of Judea were afflicted, and because of the greatness of the oppression they walked like the blind, not knowing what to do. And they suffered these things because they had sinned against the Lord, that is, against the Son of God. For because they shed the blood of the prophets and the blood of Christ, their blood was poured out like soil throughout the whole region, and their bodies remained unburied, like dung upon the face of the earth. Their rich men too, who had gathered silver and gold by excessive iniquity, could not be delivered by their wealth from the day of the wrath of the Lord; for the fire of the Lord's zeal, kindled against them, devoured the whole province. Nor was the time in between long, for forty-two years after the Lord's cross Jerusalem was surrounded by an army, and her consummation was made with haste, and not hers alone, but that of all the inhabitants of the land of Judea. But at the consummation, whether of the world or of each one, all men will be afflicted who remained men and died as men. And they will walk like the blind, because they have lost the light of the virtues, and they will have no place for repentance; and they will suffer these things because they have sinned against the Lord. For if the Lord is justice, truth, holiness, and the other virtues, whoever has acted unjustly, and has lied, and has pursued harlots and vices, has sinned against the Lord. But what follows: And their blood will be poured out like soil, and their bodies like the dung of oxen, seems absurd, that we should say that in the resurrection of the dead, and at the consummation of the world and the judgment, blood is poured out and bodies lie like dung. Therefore what is said to Noah: And the blood of your souls I will require at the hand of all the beasts, and at the hand of man, and at the hand of his brother I will require the soul of man; whoever sheds the blood of man, for his blood shall his blood be poured out, is both ridiculous to believe of the resurrection and cannot hold in this life. For how many have shed blood, and their own blood has not been poured out? And others have killed a man by poison, or by hanging, and yet, though the man is dead, has blood been poured out? How then is the Lord going to pour out their blood in retaliation, when he who killed did not pour out blood? The blood of a man, therefore, must be understood as τὸ ζωτικὸν αἶτος, that is, the vital principle, by which he is quickened and sustained and lives; and whoever has poured this out, whether by a stumbling block or by perversity of doctrine, will have it poured out from him on the day of judgment, that is, he will be forced to lose what he seemed to himself to have as his vital principle. In keeping with blood of this kind, flesh too is understood, of which Isaiah also says: All flesh is grass, and the Lord in Genesis: My spirit shall not remain in these men, because they are flesh, and the Apostle, of both: Flesh and blood will not be able to possess the kingdom of God, nor will corruption inherit the incorruption of God. On the day of consummation, therefore, whether general or particular, all the blood that has been poured out will cry to the Lord and will appear in the midst, and the works of blood and the earthly works will lie like dust and dung, and silver and gold will not be able to deliver the rich from the day of wrath, while he who dies hears: Fool, this night your soul will be taken from you; and the things you have prepared, whose will they be? Not that we deny that gold and silver deliver the rich from death — For the ransom of a man's soul is his own riches —, but that they cannot deliver them at that time when they leave their riches behind of necessity. For the whole earth and all things that are earthly will be devoured by the zeal of the Lord. And when he says, by zeal, understand the Lord as still loving. For if he did not love the human soul, he would never be jealous over it, and, like a husband, take vengeance on the sin of his wife; for if he did not love her, he would not even be angry at her adultery. And the Lord will do this with haste for all who inhabit the earth, for those who have given themselves wholly to the earth, and were not strangers and sojourners, like the just man who speaks: I am a stranger on the earth, and a sojourner like all my fathers. And again elsewhere, not wishing to dwell any longer in the tabernacle of the flesh, he bears witness with a tearful voice, saying: Woe is me, that my sojourning has been prolonged. And indeed we who are in the tabernacle of this body groan, complaining: Wretched man that I am, who will deliver me from the body of this death?

2:1-2

Come together, gather yourselves, unlovable nation, before the command brings forth the day like passing dust; before the wrath of the fury of the Lord comes upon you, before the wrath of the indignation of the Lord comes upon you. LXX: Be gathered and be collected together, untaught nation, before you are like a flower passing away in a day, before the wrath of the Lord comes upon you, before the day of the wrath of the fury of the Lord rushes upon you. After the description of the evils which are to come on the day of the Lord — according to the twofold explanation of the captivity which we gave above —, the people is called to repentance, and it is said to it: Come together, and gather yourselves, or, as is written in the LXX: Be collected together, that is, according to the Apostle, be bound to one another by the bond of charity, unlovable nation, you who are unworthy of the love of God, or untaught nation, of which it is said in Deuteronomy: A foolish people, and not wise, and in Jeremiah: In vain have I struck your sons; you have not received discipline, before what has been prophesied comes to pass, before the effect follows the command, which will come as easily as passing dust, before the fury of the Lord is fulfilled upon you. And at the same time discern the clemency of God: for the prudent it would have been enough for him to have described the onset of the coming evils; but because he does not wish to inflict punishments, but only to frighten those who are about to suffer them, he himself calls them to repentance, so that he may not do what he has threatened. In a general sense, however, the whole multitude of believers, and of those who are called the people of God, is gathered into the Church, and it is said to it: Come together into the Church, join yourselves to one another in charity and peace, O untaught nation, you who will not receive the discipline of God, nor have knowledge of his commandments, but take delight in riches and in bodily health and in the beauty of this world, and in the pleasures of the flesh as well, which passes like a flower that withers in a single day. And for this reason I say to you: Come together, join yourselves, lest, when the time of judgment has come and all your glory has passed away, you should wish to repent only then, when there will be no place for repentance, but for punishments. Someone may ask how this same thing can be understood of each person departing from the world. It is said, then, to each one: O you who, occupied with the business of the world, run about in all directions, return to the Church of the saints, and join yourself to the life and company of those whom you see pleasing God, and draw the loosened limbs of your soul, which do not hold together, into the one framework of wisdom, and cling to her embrace, and hear in a mystical sense: Be strengthened, you slack hands; you feeble knees, be made firm; and do not glory in the goods of the flesh and in its flower, which passes away: For all flesh is grass, and all its glory like the flower of grass. The grass has withered, and the flower has fallen; but the word of the Lord abides for ever. We can use this passage as occasion requires, if ever we see someone occupied with the honors of the world and with gathering riches, coming to the Church either rarely or never, and say to him: gather yourself and join yourself to the people of God, you who do not hear the commandments of the Lord, before your glory passes away, before the day of the fury of the Lord comes upon you.

2:3-4

Seek the Lord, all you meek of the earth, who have wrought his judgment; seek the just one, seek the meek one, if somehow you may be hidden on the day of the fury of the Lord. For Gaza will be destroyed, and Ashkelon into a wilderness. Ashdod they will cast out at noon, and Ekron will be uprooted. LXX: Seek the Lord, all you humble of the earth; work judgment, and seek justice and answer them, that you may be protected on the day of the wrath of the Lord; for Gaza will be plundered, and Ashkelon into a wilderness; and Ashdod will be cast forth at noon, and Ekron will be uprooted. He is called humble of the earth who has been humbled not by humility, which signifies a virtue, but by sins, and cannot say with Christ: Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart. For everyone who humbles himself will be exalted. And in another place the word is directed to the holy man: The greater you are, the more humble yourself, and before God you will find grace. But he who has been humbled by sins and weighed down by the conscience of his offenses, and says: Like a heavy burden, they have grown heavy upon me, that man ought to hear: Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Let this be said at the beginning according to the LXX translators. But according to the Hebrew there is another sense. For it is said to the saints: O you who keep my precepts, who, set on the earth and knowing that everyone who humbles himself will be exalted, have imitated my meekness and have wrought judgment, seek the Lord in your meekness. And if you wish to know who this Lord is, seek the just one, seek the meek one: For the Father has given all judgment to the Son, who is going to judge justly. And because you are meek, therefore seek the meek one, so that whatever is lacking in your meekness may be filled up by him who is the fountain of meekness. And this I say to you: If somehow you may be hidden on the day of the fury of the Lord; that is, if perhaps, from the fact that you have sought the Lord and have wrought his justice, you may be able to avoid the wrath of the coming day and to escape the captivity that is to be brought upon the people of the Jews either by Nebuchadnezzar or by the Romans. But if he is in doubt about those who have done his judgments, saying: If somehow you may be hidden on the day of the fury of the Lord, what will become of sinners? For so great a devastation will come upon the land of Judea, and so lofty a conqueror, the Babylonian army, will come up hither, that the same captivity will seize even the most powerful cities of the Philistines, which always withstood you in equal combat. For Gaza will be destroyed, and Ashkelon will be reduced to desolation, and Ashdod they will drag into captivity not by stealth but by war, that is, in open daylight and by victory; and Ekron, which is interpreted uprooting, will undergo what its name signifies, that is, it will be uprooted. This is according to the letter and the Hebrew truth. But according to the Seventy, the humble of the earth, of whom we spoke above, are commanded to work judgment and to seek justice, which I do not think is anything other than Christ. And because everyone who seeks will find, what they have found let them answer to others, that is, let them teach others: For wisdom hidden and a treasure that does not appear, what profit is there in both? And this, he says, I command you, that you may be protected on the day of the wrath of the Lord, either at the consummation of the world or at the departure of each one from this world. For Gaza and Ashkelon and Ashdod and Ekron will undergo different punishments. For Gaza is interpreted its strength. All, therefore, who applaud themselves in strength of body and worldly power, and say with the devil, By strength I will do it, will be plundered on the day of the wrath of the Lord and brought to nothing. Ashkelon also, which is called weighed, or murderous fire, when the day of the wrath of the Lord comes, will feel the measure of its crime and will be pressed down by the same weight with which it acted. And because it burned to shed blood, and caused many souls to stumble, and in it was fulfilled: The man of blood and deceit the Lord will abhor, it will not be plundered like Gaza, but, reduced to desolation, it will be burned to dust by the fires of Gehenna. Ashdod too, which in Hebrew is called esdod and in our language means fire of generation, will be laid waste in clear light; for it burned with lust and raged in the fire of generation. And because all adulterers, their hearts are like an oven, and they have been wounded by burning arrows, not in darkness, not in a hidden judgment, but at noon, that is, when the saints will receive their full brightness, they will be cast forth into darkness, and they will not have the fellowship of the saints. But Ekron, which is interpreted barrenness, or uprooting, because it bore no fruits and by the perversity of its teaching uprooted very many, will itself also be uprooted. Understand all these varieties in the vices and sins of souls, and that on the day of judgment the fire will test of what sort each one's work is.

A dryness of thorns and heaps of salt, and a wilderness for ever: in its place the LXX, meaning I know not what, translated: Damascus torn up and left, deceived, unless I am mistaken, by the ambiguity of the word; for dryness, which in Hebrew is called mamasac, if its first letter mem is changed and daleth is taken, has the same remaining letters as Damascus too, and in place of the former word damasec can be read. But whoever has considered that apostolic saying in which it is said: In the last times very evil times will set in, and men will be lovers of themselves, greedy, arrogant, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, wicked, without affection, without peace, accusers, intemperate, cruel, hating the good, traitors, rash, puffed up, lovers of pleasures more than of God; having the form of piety, but denying its power. And also this, which is written in the Gospel, that as iniquity is multiplied the love of many grows cold, so much so that at that time this is fulfilled: Yet when the Son of man comes, do you think he will find faith on the earth? He will not wonder at the final devastation of the Church: that, while the Antichrist reigns, she is to be reduced to a wilderness, and handed over to beasts, and is to suffer whatever the prophet now describes. For if God, because of unbelief, did not spare the natural branches, but broke them off, and turned rivers into a desert, and springs of waters into thirst, a fruitful land into saltness, because of the wickedness of its inhabitants, why should he not, conversely, overthrow those of whom he had said: He turned a desert into pools of waters, and a land without water into springs of waters, and made the hungry dwell there, and so on, and those whom he grafted from the wild olive into the root of the good olive, if, unmindful of his kindness, they withdraw from their Creator and worship the Assyrian, and bring them back to the same thirst in which they had been before? Although this can be understood generally of the coming of the Antichrist, or of the end of the world, yet it can be taken daily of those who pretend to be of the Church of God and deny it by their works, and are hearers of the law and not doers, who vainly boast that they are beautiful, when flocks dwell in them, namely a multitude of vices, and brute animals serving the body; and all the beasts of the earth, which devour their hearts, and chameleons, which do not have one color, but are changed from moment to moment by different sins, now by avarice, now by luxury, now by cruelty, now by lust, now by sadness, now by exultation. And hedgehogs in their stalls, an animal spiny and full of thorns, and wounding whatever it touches. But beasts too will lie down in the holes, that is, in their hearts, and ravens, unclean birds, in their gates, either in the mouth or in the ears, so that they may always either speak or hear evil things. After this it is added and said: the Church will suffer these things, or has suffered them, for this reason, that she raised herself up in pride and, like a cedar, lifted up her top, given over to evil works, and nonetheless promising herself future blessedness, and despising the rest in her heart, and not thinking that anyone exists besides herself, and saying: I am, and there is no other besides me any more; how then has she been made in the wilderness a pasture of beasts? For where previously the Father dwelt, and the Son and the Holy Spirit, and his angels presided over its ministries, there beasts will then dwell, about which the prophet too laments, saying: Do not hand over to the beasts the soul that confesses to you. Everyone who passes through her will hiss and will move his hands. But if we understand this of the angels, we will interpret it thus: when the angels pass through her, and do not remain in her, as they had been accustomed to do before, they will be stunned and will marvel, and will not prop her up nor hold her up with their hand as she falls, but will lift up their hands and pass on through. Or else with a hiss they will raise their hands, and, as though lamenting in the manner of mourners, they will clap them together. If, however, we wish to take this of the devil and his angels, who also laid waste the vine that had been transplanted out of Egypt, we will say that through the soul from which Christ has withdrawn, and which before had been the temple of God and has ceased to be so, the serpent passes, and hisses in it, and vomits out the venoms of his malice, and not only does this, but also moves his works, which τροπικῶς (figuratively) are called hands. And lest you think, because we have named a serpent, that we forcibly understand the hands of a serpent as its work, take the testimony of Solomon: Death and life are in the hands of the tongue. We have said these things serving the allegorical interpretation as best we could. But if anyone finds things more probable and more reasonable than those set out by us, let the reader rather be led by his authority.

Woe, provoking and redeemed city, the dove. She did not hear the voice, and did not accept discipline; she did not trust in the Lord, she did not draw near to her Lord. Her princes in the midst of her, like roaring lions; her judges, wolves of the evening, they left nothing for the morning. Her prophets mad, faithless men. Her priests have polluted the holy, they have acted unjustly against the law. The just Lord in the midst of her will not do iniquity. In the morning, in the morning he will give his judgment in the light, and it will not be hidden; but the unjust one knew no confusion. I have destroyed the nations, and their corners have been scattered; I have made their ways deserted, because there is no one who passes; their cities have been made desolate, with no man remaining, nor any inhabitant. I said: Yet you will fear me, you will accept discipline, and her dwelling will not perish because of all the things in which I visited her. Nevertheless, rising at dawn, they corrupted all their thoughts. LXX: O illustrious and redeemed city, the dove, she did not hear the voice, she did not accept discipline; she did not trust in the Lord, and did not draw near to her Lord. Her princes in her like roaring lions, her judges like wolves of Arabia, they did not leave anything till the morning; her prophets bearing the spirit, men who are despisers. Her priests pollute the holy things, and act impiously against the law. But the just Lord is in the midst of her, and will not do iniquity. In the morning, in the morning he will give his judgment in the light, and he is not hidden, and he does not know iniquity in exaction, nor injustice for ever; I have pulled down the proud, their corners have been scattered, I have destroyed their ways, so that they may not pass through at all; their cities have failed, because no one remained, nor dwelt there. I said: Nevertheless you will fear me, and you will accept discipline, and you will not perish from his eyes in all the things in which I have taken vengeance upon her. Prepare yourself, rise up at dawn, all their clusters have perished. Many think that, because of the sequence of the discourse, this is said against Nineveh, of which it was said above: And he will destroy the Assyrian, and will make Nineveh a wilderness. But Scripture would never call Nineveh a dove, although some think that in Jeremiah the words from the face of the sword of the dove were said about Nebuchadnezzar. But it should be known that others, on the contrary, assert that in place of dove, Ἑλλάδα, that is, Greece, can be understood there, so that the sense is: From the face of the sword of Iona, that is, from the face of the sword of Greece. For Iona signifies both dove and Greece. Hence even to this day the Greeks are called Ionians, and the sea is called the Ionian Sea, and among the Hebrews their old name remains. So too the Roman princes, retaining an ancient name among the barbarian nations, are called Caesars. The whole discourse, then, is against Jerusalem: Woe, city once a dove, always sinning, and handed over to captivities, and redeemed again by the Lord. Woe, provoking city, which is said more expressively in Hebrew, mara, that is, παραπικραίνουσα, which we can render making God bitter, that is, by your fault turning the sweet and merciful Lord to bitterness, so that he who wishes to have mercy is compelled to punish. She did not hear the commandment of the Lord, and when rebuked she would not accept discipline; nor, when evils pressed upon her, did she ever trust in the Lord her God; nor did she walk behind him; nor, when he said: I am the Lord who draws near, and not from afar, was she herself willing to draw near to him. Her princes and judges and prophets and priests are also described, so that in the city we may understand the people, and in these titles of rank that I have mentioned, the leading men. Her princes, therefore, like lions, were always busy with plunder and with shedding the blood of their subjects; her judges were rapacious, leaving to others nothing they could seize. Her prophets, raving or astounded, which in Hebrew is said phoezim, and Aquila translated θαμβευταὶ (the astonished), spoke as if from the mouth of the Lord, and proclaimed everything against the Lord; the priests committed sacrilege in the place of the sanctuary, and while they acted against the law, they offered sacrifices according to the law. Therefore, because they acted unjustly, the just Lord will not do iniquity, but will render to the most wicked city what it deserves. In the morning, in the morning, that is, openly and without any ambiguity, he will pass judgment on her, and there will be nothing that can be hidden from him. And the Lord will do this so that the city, rebuked, may be converted to the better. But unjust Israel did not recognize its confusion, nor did it understand that the blows were inflicted on it for this purpose, that it might repent. I have avenged you, he says afterwards, on the nations, and I have destroyed their empires, so that you who had not perceived me through blows might at least know me through benefits. Or else it is to be understood thus: I have destroyed all your cities, O Judah, and all the villages and tribes subject to you, and the various borderlands, and so great was the desolation of men that there was no one to dwell in your cities; and after I did this, I sent my prophets, rising at dawn and calling to repentance, and I said: I have indeed done these things to you, O Jerusalem, but I did them that you might fear me, and accept discipline, and that your dwelling, that is, the temple, might not perish because of all the crimes you had committed. But on the contrary, the inhabitants of Jerusalem, while I was calling them to repentance, rose at dawn as if on purpose and out of contention, so that with the haste with which they ought to have returned to me they might fulfil all their thoughts, and show in deed what they had conceived in mind. This according to the Hebrew. But the illustrious city, redeemed by the blood of Christ, is clearly understood, in keeping with what was said above, as the Church, which is also called a dove because of the simplicity of the multitude of believers within her. She did not hear the voice of the Lord, nor was she willing to accept discipline, nor did she trust in the Lord, because she would not draw near to the Lord her God so as to merit pardon of sins. For in vain does anyone say that he hears the voice of the Lord his God and trusts in the Lord, when he destroys faith by his works, and is joined to mammon more than to the Lord his God, and approaches him with a double heart, and believes that he can serve as a soldier under two masters, the world and God. Her princes are like roaring lions. We have no doubt about the roaring and the ranging of lions when we see her princes thunder so against the peoples subject to them, and crush the common people with a tyrant's voice and rabid abuse, that you would think it not a shepherd among his flock, but a lion gnashing its teeth among the little sheep. Her judges too are like wolves of Arabia, killing in the evening and leaving nothing for the morning; not looking toward the rising of the sun, but lingering always in darkness, and turning the small possessions of the Church, and the things brought as offerings to God, to their own gain, so that the poor have nothing to eat in the morning; they lay everything waste as if in the night, with no one seeing; and when, after the manner of wolves, they plunder everything, they do not leave even small portions of food to the needy. Her prophets also, that is, the teachers who think that they teach the peoples and discourse on the Scriptures, πνευματοφόροι, that is, bearing the spirit, or spiritual — and this is to be read εἰρονικῶς (ironically) —, are men who are despisers; for what befits the Church is not to teach but to do, not to destroy words by deeds. But you, when you teach another and do not do it yourself, are to be called not so much a teacher as a despiser, of whom it is also written in Habakkuk: Look, you despisers, and behold, and wonder at the wonders, and perish. The priests too, who serve the Eucharist and distribute the blood of the Lord to his peoples, act impiously against the law of Christ, thinking that it is the words of the one who prays that make the εὐχαριστίαν (the Eucharist), not his life, and that only the solemn prayer is necessary, and not the merits of the priests, of whom it is said: And a priest in whom there is any blemish shall not approach to offer offerings to the Lord. Though the princes, judges, prophets, priests of Jerusalem do these things, the Lord is nonetheless merciful and just. Merciful, in that he does not withdraw from his Church; just, in that he renders to each one what he deserves. For after the morning has come, and the night of this age has passed, he will give his judgment in the light, and neither he himself nor his judgment will be hidden. And when he begins to demand from each one the money he had entrusted to them, he will not be unjust, nor will he make injustice prevail for ever, but he will pull down the proud princes, whom God resists, from their seats and from the height they held, and their corners will be scattered, that is, their perverse wills, turning aside from the straight road, corners in which the Pharisees too were always accustomed to pray, having despised the corner stone. But I think that it benefits the proud too to be pulled down from their arrogance, and to have their alleys and corners scattered, so that afterwards they may walk on the straight road. Then follows: And I will make their ways deserted, because there is no one who passes through, according to what is written in Psalm 1: And the way of the impious will perish. And also in Hosea, where it is said of Jerusalem committing fornication: Behold, I will close her ways with thorns, and I will block up her paths, and she will not find her track, and she will pursue her lovers, and will not overtake them, and she will seek them, and will not find them; and she will say: I will go and return to my former husband, for it was better with me then than now. Notice that unless the ways had been closed and the paths fenced off, and unless the Lord had destroyed her ways, the fornicating soul could never have said: I will go and return to my former husband. So the paths of the proud and their corners are scattered, that they may not walk in pride and in perversity, and their cities, which had been wickedly built in arrogance and pride, are destroyed, so that they may not stand and have most wicked inhabitants. And lest anyone think that we do violence to Scripture, let him learn from what follows. But I did these things, he says, that I might say to them: Behold, the paths of malice have been destroyed; from now on fear me, and learn discipline, lest my discipline also perish, finding in you no fruit of conversion, and all the things by which I wished to correct you be in vain, and that word which is written in Jeremiah may be applied to you: In vain have I struck your sons; you have not accepted discipline. Or else: Fear me, and receive discipline, so that all things may not perish from the sight of Jerusalem, nor she be brought utterly to desolation over these evils with which I threatened her. Nor let it disturb anyone, as I have often said already, that I interpret these things as spoken against the Church, since he knows that in the holy Scriptures Jerusalem always bears the type of the Church; whoever sins away from her is either led off into Babylon, or, if he chooses of his own accord to go down to Jericho, is wounded by robbers. For what is so illustrious as the Church, which has been founded in the whole world, so redeemed by the blood of Christ, and a dove because of the grace of the Holy Spirit, as the Church gathered from the nations? In her very many who say that they believe in Christ have neither heard his voice, nor received discipline, nor wished to be near him. As for the saying: Her princes in her like roaring lions, I know that I will offend very many, because I interpret these things of bishops and elders, though the wicked elders who desired to violate Susanna do not condemn the other elders who have lived well. And the wicked princes whom the prophetic word describes are no reproach to good princes: For when a fool is scourged, the senseless man will be wiser; but if the senseless man becomes wiser, how much more he who is wise? But her judges and princes, taking gifts and selling justice, are they not rightly called wolves of Arabia, or evening wolves, as Symmachus translated? For they do not deserve to be called wolves of Benjamin, which seize prey in the morning and at evening give food, but evening wolves, which eat by night and leave nothing for the morning. As for what follows: Her prophets πνευματοφόροι, that is, bearing the spirit, men who are despisers, let it disturb no one that, interpreting this of teachers, we call the same men both prophets and despisers, since the Apostle too commands: Do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, in whom you were sealed in the day of redemption. And David says in Psalm 50 (51): Do not take your Holy Spirit from me. For unless the Holy Spirit, when grieved, were accustomed to flee his former lodging and to abandon his dwelling, Paul would never have commanded what I said above, nor would David after his adultery have dreaded lest he lose what he had received; of which it is also written to the Hebrews: How much worse punishments do you think he deserves who has trampled on the Son of God, and has held unclean the blood of the covenant, in which he was sanctified, and has done insult to the spirit of grace? Moreover, it is written in the third book of Kingdoms (1 Kings) that the man of God, no doubt a prophet, who had said to the altar in Samaria: Altar, altar, thus says the Lord: Behold, a son of David will be born, and so on, because he despised the words of the Lord and ate with the false prophet — for so Josephus, expounding this passage, interpreted it —, was killed by a lion. And lest it be thought chance and not the judgment of the Lord, both the false prophet who had deceived him foretold that this would happen, and the lion itself, punishing the despiser, spared the ass. It is no wonder, then, if teachers who had been full of the Holy Spirit can become despisers, since among the careless, who do not keep their heart with all watchfulness, this very thing is frequently the cause of pride against the Lord and of contempt: that they have the knowledge of God, and know his great goodness, which he has hidden for those who fear him, and despise the riches of his goodness, storing up for themselves wrath in the day of wrath and of revelation. Let the priests too — who give baptism and at the Eucharist pray for the coming of the Lord, make the oil of chrism, lay on hands, instruct catechumens, appoint Levites and other priests — not so much be indignant with us for expounding these things, and with the prophets for prophesying them, as pray to the Lord, and take earnest care that they may not deserve to be among the priests who violate the holy things of the Lord. For it is not rank and the names of rank, but the work of the rank, that is accustomed to save princes, and judges, and prophets, and priests: He who desires the episcopate, he says, desires a good work. See what he said: He desires a good work, not a rank. But if, despising the work, one looks only at the rank, swiftly the tower in Siloam falls, and the heights of the cedar are struck by lightning, and the upraised neck is broken, and the swan, with neck outstretched and straining aloft, is reckoned among the unclean birds. 3:8-9

Therefore wait for me, says the Lord, on the day of my resurrection in the future; for my judgment is to gather the nations and to collect the kingdoms, to pour out upon them my indignation, all the wrath of my fury; for in the fire of my zeal all the earth will be devoured, because then I will restore to the peoples a chosen lip, that all may call upon the name of the Lord and serve him with one shoulder. LXX: Therefore wait for me, says the Lord, on the day of my resurrection for a testimony, for my judgment is upon the gatherings of the nations, that I may receive kings, that I may pour out upon them all my wrath, the wrath of my fury; for in the fire of my zeal all the earth will be consumed, for then I will turn among the peoples a tongue into its generations, that all may call upon the name of the Lord, that they may serve him under one yoke. The Jews interpret these things of the coming of Christ, whom they hope is yet to come, and they say that, when all the nations have been gathered and the fury of the Lord has been poured out upon them, the earth will be devoured in the fire of his zeal. And just as before the building of the tower there was a time when all peoples spoke one tongue, so, when all have been converted to the worship of the true God, they will speak Hebrew and the whole world will serve the Lord. But we, who follow not the letter that kills but the spirit that gives life, nor Jewish fables, hear from the Lord: Prepare yourself, rise at dawn: all their clusters have been scattered, and, prepared, we say: My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready. And we hear the command in Proverbs: Prepare your works for your going out. And that which is said in a sacred manner in Leviticus, where in the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month, Aaron offers for the people the scapegoat, and that a living one, and, laying his hands upon its head, calls down upon it all the sins of the people of Israel, and hands it over into the hands of a prepared man, and sends it into the desert, this we understand of ourselves, and, preparing ourselves at the command of the true priest, we remove evil from the midst of the Church. And when we have done these things, the night passes, the day draws near, and, walking honestly as in the day, we say: O God, my God, to you I keep watch from the dawn. And at once we add: In the morning you will hear my prayer, in the morning I will stand before you, and I will see. For unless we have been prepared, the sun of justice will not rise for us. But when the sun has risen, all the clusters from the vine of Sodom are scattered and perish, so that, as the lamp of Christ shines, not only the great bunches but even what seemed to be small in us perishes. And, promising us a reward for all these things, God says: Wait for me on the day of my resurrection for a testimony; for after vices and sins God will rise again in us. And according to what he commands in another place: Be my witnesses, and I am a witness, says the Lord God, and the servant whom I have chosen, the Father with the Son and the Holy Spirit is a witness for us, so that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may stand. And it seems to me that the sentence stands rather in this way, and that the truth is confirmed under these three witnesses, rather than through the letter. For there were two witnesses against Susanna, and against the Lord Savior himself, and yet the word did not stand in their mouth. Against Naboth, too, almost the whole city bore witness, and the agreement of most wicked witnesses had not the firmness of truth but the conspiracy of a crime. For my judgment, he says, is upon the gatherings of the nations, that I may receive kings, namely in the place of their punishments, that I may pour out upon them my wrath, all the wrath of my fury. He who is lesser quickly earns pardon and is very near to mercy: But the mighty will suffer torments mightily. Hence the nations and the multitude of the nations are gathered to judgment; but the kings, that is, the leaders of perverse doctrines, will be brought to punishment, so that all the wrath of the Lord's fury may be poured out upon them. And this is done not out of any cruelty, as the bloodthirsty Jews suppose, but out of mercy and the counsel of one who heals. For there follows: For in the fire of my zeal all the earth will be consumed. For when the nations have been gathered to judgment, and the kings to punishment, so that wrath may be poured out upon them, and not in part but whole, and with wrath and fury joined together, whatever is earthly in the whole world may be consumed, whatever pertains to the works of the earth, that is, of the flesh; laying waste all its brambles and thorny thickets, the blaze of my zeal will devour it. And then I will turn upon the peoples a tongue into its generation, so that each one, having laid aside error, may return to the ancient speech of confessing the Lord, and at the name of Jesus every knee shall bend, of those in heaven, on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess the Lord Jesus in the glory of God the Father. And casting away the bricks and the bitumen which we had in place of stones and clay, with which we were building up the pride of our error against the Lord, let us recover the tongue which we lost before, and let us be under the yoke of Christ, who says: My yoke is sweet, and my burden is light. It should be noted, however, that in the place where we translated: I will restore to the peoples a chosen lip, instead of chosen the Seventy said: into its generation, so that of the earth is to be understood. And the error arose from this, that the Hebrew word barura, which Aquila and Theodotion translated as chosen and Symmachus as pure, the Seventy read as badura, thinking the letter res (resh) to be daleth, because of the excessive similarity of the character, which is distinguished by a small stroke. And likewise, where we translated: On the day of my resurrection in the future, all the others too translated: for a testimony. The Hebrew who instructed me in the Scriptures maintained that laed in the present passage ought to be understood rather as εἰς ἔτι, that is, for the future, than as for a testimony. For Ed, which is written with the letters ain and daleth, is understood as ἔτι and μαρτύριον, that is, both the future and a testimony. We can also expound this passage of the first coming of Christ, when, all error having been removed, the demons trampled and earthly works destroyed, the apostles spoke in all tongues, and, the old error having been removed, the one lip of confession was restored. But the kings, too, who are destroyed and consumed by the divine burning, are to be understood as the leaders of perverse doctrines.

Beyond the rivers of Ethiopia, from there my suppliants, the sons of my dispersed ones, will bring a gift to me. On that day you will not be confounded over all your devices by which you have transgressed against me; for then I will take away from your midst the boastful ones of your pride, and you will no more be exalted on my holy mountain. And I will leave in your midst a poor and needy people, and they will hope in the name of the Lord. The remnant of Israel will not do iniquity, nor speak a lie; and a deceitful tongue will not be found in their mouth, for they will feed and lie down, and there will be none to terrify. LXX: From the ends of the rivers of Ethiopia I will receive my dispersed ones; they will bring sacrifices to me. On that day you will not be confounded by all your devices by which you have acted impiously against me, for then I will take away from you the disparagement of your insolence, and you will no more be magnified upon my holy mountain; and I will leave in you a meek and humble people, and those who are left of Israel will revere the name of the Lord, and they will not do iniquity; and they will not speak vain things, and a deceitful tongue will not be found in their mouth, for they will feed and lie down, and there will be none to terrify them. When the Lord has restored to the peoples of believers a chosen lip, and all have called upon the name of the Lord and have carried his yoke, then also from beyond the rivers of Ethiopia, whence the queen of Sheba came to hear the wisdom of Solomon, they will bring sacrifices to the Lord: And Ethiopia will hasten her hand to God; and the Ethiopian woman will be married to the true lawgiver, who struck Egypt with ten plagues, while the synagogue of the Hebrews envies. But what he says according to the Hebrew: From there my suppliants, the daughter of my dispersed ones, will bring a gift to me, is of this kind: O Israel, O synagogue, once a daughter, whom I dispersed through the whole world, though you envy, though you are tormented by jealousy, nevertheless sacrifices will be brought to me from Ethiopia, that is, from the people of the Gentiles. On that day, that is, when the multitude of the nations has believed, you too will not be utterly confounded over all your errors by which you transgressed against me, by choosing Barabbas and crucifying the Son of God. Then I will take away from your midst the scribes and priests and Pharisees, the boastful ones of your pride, and you will by no means boast any longer on my holy mountain, but you will have a poor people, unlettered men and fishermen, who will hope in the name of the Lord. The remnant of Israel, not the multitude that cried out: Crucify, crucify him, not the high priests and the nobles, but the remnant, will by no means do iniquity, nor speak a lie in Christ, since they believe the truth; nor will a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth, since they know that every lie is from the devil; for they will feed and will say: The Lord feeds me, and I shall lack nothing; in a place of pasture, there he has set me. Beside the water of refreshment he has brought me up; he has turned my soul; and there will be none to terrify, as the faith of believers overcomes the pride of persecutors. Let this be understood of the first coming of Christ, which the Jews promise themselves at the end; and they hope that they will dwell in Jerusalem, and, after the manner of cattle, will be filled with the bodily gifts and riches of Judah and fed on green grass, and that, when all the nations have been destroyed and made subject to them, no one can remain who might terrify them. But we, taking from this fable the occasion of a true history, will say that, once a chosen lip, or a pure and white one, as Symmachus translated, has been restored to us, we leave behind in the rivers of Ethiopia all the blackness of the soul, and the foul colour, and the venom of the dragon, with which we had been dyed by vices and sins, and the masters of perverse doctrines by whom we were formerly watered, and that we, with the once-dispersed Israel, will bring gifts to Christ. On that day, on which the light of Christ has risen for us, it will be said to each one of us: You will not be confounded by all your devices, namely the most wicked thoughts by which we acted impiously against the Lord; and all the pride and insolence will be taken away through which we were raised up against the Lord and against the mountain, his holy one, our Lord and Savior; and in place of proud and vain names a meek and humble people will be left in us, so that we may think nothing arrogant, nothing swollen, nothing that displeases God. And at the same time consider that on the day of judgment and in the consummation of the world all the names of dignities will be taken away, and one people will remain, and a flock under the good shepherd, which is meek and humble. Then also the people of Israel, when the fullness of the nations has come in, for God has shut up all under sin, that he may have mercy on all, will fear the name of the Lord. And the remnant of Israel will no longer do the iniquity through which they denied the Lord, nor speak vain things, promising themselves foolish fables; nor will a tongue of lying be found in their mouth, since Christ, who is the truth, speaks through them. For then they too will feed in one flock, and will lie down in the Church, and will not fear the onslaughts of the true Nebuchadnezzar. Seeing and reading these things, such great mysteries, let us cry out with the Apostle and say: O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God, how inscrutable are your judgments, and your ways unsearchable. The prophet, too, perceiving this and turning it over within himself, conjectures concerning the judgments of God: In the night I was exercised with my heart, and I swept my spirit, and I was saying: Will God cast off for ever, or will he not add to have mercy any more, or will he hold back his mercies in his wrath? And I said: Now I have begun; this is the change of the right hand of the Most High. And the sense is: This, that I thought the Lord was abandoning sinners for ever and, with wrath taking its place, restraining his mercies, I understood to have been done for this reason, that by the change of his right hand, which is the right hand of the Most High, he might change all things and have mercy on those whom he had previously cast away. Therefore let us also, both we and the remnant of Israel, knowing that we are to render an account for every idle word, and that the Lord will destroy all lying lips, not speak vanity. For vanity of vanities, and all is vanity, and: Every living man is utter vanity. Nor let us bring forth a lie with our mouth, but, having received the power to tread upon serpents and scorpions and upon all the power of the enemy, let us fear no terror, nor, with Christ as our guardian, dread the snares of wolves, but let us say: The Lord is my illumination and my savior; whom shall I fear? and the rest that is contained in Psalm 26 (27).

3:14-18

Praise, daughter of Zion, shout for joy, Israel; be glad and exult with all your heart, daughter of Jerusalem. The Lord has taken away your judgment, he has turned away your enemies; the King of Israel, the Lord, is in your midst; you will not fear evil any more. On that day it will be said: Jerusalem, do not fear, Zion; let your hands not be weakened. The Lord your God in your midst, the mighty one, he will save; he will rejoice over you with gladness, he will be silent in his love for you; he will exult over you with praise. Triflers (nugas) who withdrew from the law I will gather, because they were of you, so that you may no longer have reproach over them. LXX: Rejoice, daughter of Zion; proclaim, daughter of Jerusalem; exult and delight with all your heart, daughter of Jerusalem. The Lord has taken away your iniquities, he has redeemed you from the hands of your enemies; the King of Israel, the Lord, is in your midst; you will not see evils any more. At that time the Lord says to Jerusalem: Take courage, Zion, let your hands not be weakened. The Lord your God in you, the mighty one, will save you; he will bring gladness upon you, and will renew you in his love, and will rejoice in you with delight, as on a solemn day; I will gather your broken ones. Woe to him who has taken up reproach against her. Let it not seem strange, as we have often said, that the Hebrew chapters end in one way and those of the LXX, that is, the Greek and the Latin, in another. For where the translation differs in sense, there the beginnings or the ends must also differ. The Jews promise themselves all these things together with the Christ whom they think is yet to come, things which we, who have received Christ, have already obtained together with him. If, then, any of the Christians, and especially of the new wise men, whose names I pass over in silence lest I seem to wound anyone, thinks that the prophecy has not yet been fulfilled, let him know that he bears the name of Christ falsely, and has a Jewish soul, lacking only the circumcision of the body. For if these things have not yet happened but are still to come, we have believed in the coming of the Savior in vain; but, since our believing is not in vain, we understand that the mystery has been fulfilled in us which was kept silent through eternal ages and has now been made manifest through the prophetic scriptures and the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. Finally, let us consider the order of the reading, and we shall see that what is said pertains not to the Jews but to the Church of Christ. For after what had gone before: My judgment is upon the gatherings of the nations, that I may receive kings, up to the place where he says: That all may call upon the name of the Lord, and serve him under one yoke, and: Beyond the rivers of Ethiopia I will receive among my dispersed ones those who will bring me sacrifices; and after the remnant of the people of Israel, believing in Christ and saved, of whom it is said: And those who are left of Israel will fear the name of the Lord, and there will be none to terrify them. The Holy Spirit, preaching of the general consummation of the world, speaks: Rejoice, daughter of Zion, proclaim, daughter of Jerusalem, rejoice and delight with all your heart, daughter of Jerusalem. For every soul of the Church, which is set upon the watchtower and contemplates peace, is glad and rejoices that its iniquities have been taken away from it, and redeemed by him who redeemed all with his precious blood. For Christ has been made for us wisdom from God, and justice, and sanctification, and redemption, and the King of Israel has redeemed us, who dwells in our midst, saying: I and my Father will come, and we will make our abode with him. And: I will dwell and walk among them; and we will no longer see evils, thinking and doing virtues alone. On that day, says the Lord, as we see peace and are set on high, let your hands not be weakened, he who also said through Isaiah: Be strengthened, weakened hands, and let your works be strong. For the mighty Lord, whom no one can resist, your savior, will himself restore to you the gladness which you lost, and, the old man cast away, will make you walk in the new, and he will do these things in his love, not by your merit but by his mercy. And he will rejoice in you and delight, receiving your salvation as the richest victim of your solemnity; and he himself will say to you: I will gather your crushed ones. A heart, indeed, crushed and humbled God will not despise, and: A crushed reed he will not break. These things, meanwhile, if we wish to take them of the second coming of the Savior. Moreover, since the prophet Zechariah exhorts Zion and Jerusalem to a similar gladness, and Matthew says that this very prophecy was fulfilled in the first coming of Christ, we are compelled by necessity, or rather led by the very order of truth, to hope for what is said in Zephaniah not as future but as done. For it is written in Zechariah: Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion; proclaim, daughter of Jerusalem. Behold, your king comes to you, just and a savior, himself humble and mounted upon a beast of burden and a new colt. Let these things have been said according to the Seventy. But according to the Hebrew, the Church is commanded to praise, and Israel, seeing God with the mind, to shout for joy, and the place of peace to exult and be glad with all its heart, to which it was said: My peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you. For at the end and in the consummation of the world he has taken away her judgment, by no means judging and rebuking her, but saving her, and he has turned away her enemies, the hosts of demons. The King of Israel, the Lord, will be in her midst; she will not fear evil any more. On that day it will be said to Jerusalem the free, who is not in slavery with her children, but who is the mother of the saints: Do not fear, Zion, for she herself is also Jerusalem; your works will by no means be weakened, nor will you do things which you will mourn having done. The Lord your God, who will save you, mighty and powerful, will himself dwell in your midst; he will rejoice over you with exultation and gladness, and will keep silent about your sins in the love with which he loved you; and he will exult over you with praise, either because you are praiseworthy or because you sing with your praises. Triflers (nugas), or, as Aquila translated, those carried away, who withdrew from you, I will gather, because they were of you; that is: Those who through vices and sins had fled from your bosom and had come under the power of demons will come to you when the state of all things has been restored, and you will by no means suffer reproaches any longer over your lost sons. What we have rendered nugas, let us know that in the Hebrew it is the Latin word itself, and for that reason it was set down by us just as it stood in the Hebrew, so that we may know that the Hebrew tongue is the mother of all tongues, a matter which it is not the time to discuss now. But I wonder that Aquila and the Seventy, where we translated they were, namely in the place where we said: I will gather, because they were of you: instead of they were chose to translate woe, or ὄι, which Aquila always uses not for lamenting but for calling and crying out. For Hai, of the verb whose beginning is sum, signifies the past tense in the plural number: they were, or they had been. I know that these things are tiresome to the reader; but if he notices that I am writing not controversies and declamations, nor exulting in commonplaces, but commentaries, and commentaries on the prophets, he will rather blame me if anywhere I have chosen to play in the manner of the rhetoricians than censure me for lingering, as is fitting, amid such great obscurities.

3:19-20

Behold, I will kill all who have afflicted you at that time, and I will save the lame one, and her who had been cast out I will gather; and I will set them for praise and for a name in all the land of their confusion. At that time when I will bring you in, and at the time when I will gather you; for I will give you for a name and for praise to all the peoples of the earth, when I have turned back your captivity before your eyes, says the Lord. LXX: Behold, I will act in you, for your sake, at that time, and I will save the pressed one, and the rejected one I will receive, and I will set them in glory; and named in all the earth. And they will be confounded at that time when I have done well to you; and at the time when I have received you, for I will give you named and in glory among all the peoples of the earth, when I have turned back your captivity before you, says the Lord. And this the synagogue, which does not limp but is maimed in both feet, promises itself at the coming of its Christ, whom it hopes is yet to come; and it thinks that all the nations which afflicted Israel are to be killed by the Lord, and the synagogue saved, and she who had received a bill of divorce gathered, and that they are to be set for praise and for a name in all the land of their captivity, in which they had formerly been confounded; and that this happens at the time when the captivity has been brought back to Jerusalem, and the temple built, and the remaining order of ceremonies observed. These things it promises itself, and therefore it does not repent, and while it hopes for uncertain things, it loses certain salvation. Nor do I wonder that the synagogue says these things, which, because it does not receive Christ, suffers in its eyes, and is bleary-eyed with Leah, and is not loved by Jacob, and, with Rachel taking her place, is neglected. I wonder at Christians, or rather half-Jews, who seem to themselves to be of the Church, speaking thus and professing those things which, if they are true, we believe in Christ in vain, and our whole mystery is taken away, and we are more wretched than all men, believing that he has come who has not come. But since our hope is certain and the vows of the Jews are empty, let us now weave the present and last chapter according to the former understanding, and bring forward a testimony from Jesus son of Sirach: He who throws a stone on high throws it upon his own head. For since Zion and Jerusalem are set on high, whoever disparages Zion and Jerusalem and throws at her the stones of insults hurls them upon his own head, and his reproach will return upon his own head: And his pain and his iniquity will come down upon his crown. How many today reproach the soul that pursues the mysteries of God and wishes to see his peace, and say: He is mad, and drunk, and full of new wine; he flees the gatherings of men; he spurns delights, counts gold as mud, loves poverty alone. But those who are unbelievers reproach him even with the cross of Christ, and if ever they see him in distress and in temptation, they say: Where are your mercies, and the works of justice which you have performed? Why should I speak of unbelievers, when some leaders of the churches reproach men of this kind, and judge their life to be madness, and do not praise their present way of life but reproach them with their old sins? Nor do they hear that it is commanded: Do not upbraid a man who turns from his sins. Woe therefore to him who has taken up and undertaken reproach, and has bound himself over to such a task as to disparage Zion and the city of God, for the Lord is the avenger of this injury to his city, and he will say to Zion: Behold, I will act in you for your sake, that is, I will carry out your vengeance, and I will save the afflicted one, or, as it is said in the Greek, the pressed one, so that the sense is: I will save her who at present, like the grape and the olive, has been so pressed and afflicted by temptations and pressures, as by the winepress and the beam, that she might make wine and oil, and that Jesus might drink of the wine in the kingdom of his Father, and be anointed with the oil above his fellows. I think that it was for the pressing out of such wine and oil that Job suffered many things, and that, after he had made the wine and the oil, he heard from the Lord: Do you think that I answered you otherwise than that you might appear just? As if he were speaking to the grape and the olive: Do you suppose that I pressed and afflicted and crushed you for any other reason than to press wine and oil out of you? There follows: And her who had been rejected I will receive. God seems to reject us when he leaves us to temptation. Hence Job too: The visitation, he says, of the Lord has looked upon me. And not only do the just speak and say: But now you have rejected and confounded us, as is written in Psalm 43 (44), but the Lord and Savior himself, in the person of the man whom he had assumed: But you, he says, have rejected and despised, you have put off your Christ; you have overturned the covenant of your servant. That it is the person of the just in Psalm 43 (44) who say: But now you have rejected and confounded us, is proved from what follows: All these things have come upon us, yet we have not forgotten you, nor have we acted wickedly in your covenant; nor has our heart turned back. Her, therefore, who seemed cast away in temptations the Lord will receive at the end, and will set them in glory and named in all the earth, namely the sons of the pressed and rejected one, that is, of the Church. But what other sons of the Church can we understand as named, except the apostles? Look, I pray you, at Peter and Paul and Matthew and John, and consider that what was promised to Abraham: I will magnify your name, has been accomplished in deed in them. Daily it is named in the Church, daily their name is magnified, not because it profits them to be named by us in the Church, but so that we, magnifying their name and reading over what they wrote, may obtain salvation. At that time, he says, when the pressed and rejected one has been received, and her sons set in glory — for the Lord glories in his athletes when he sees them crowned, as he gloried over Job against the devil; hence the Apostle too, exulting in the progress of his disciples, says: Yes, by your glory — those who had been your adversaries and had brought reproach against you will be confounded; and then they will see that those whom they had thought wretched are happy, and that those whom they reckoned poor and cast down have become powerful and glorious; then they will see their captivity, through which in this age they had been subjugated under a harsh rule, brought back into the heavenly Jerusalem, and themselves rising again to reproach and to everlasting confusion. Grant to us, Jesus Christ, who are pressed and afflicted and rejected in this age, that you may receive us and set us in glory; let the water-snake (excetra) be confounded in time, let the hissings cease, let the venoms grow numb, and let its confusion profit for salvation. These things according to the Seventy. But since the Hebrew, except for the beginning, about which we spoke at the end of the preceding chapter, does not differ much from their translation, I do not think it needs exposition.
