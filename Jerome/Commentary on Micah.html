Commentary on Micah

Latin source: Jerome's commentary on Micah, in two books addressed to Paula and Eustochium (written at Bethlehem about 392–393; the preface to the second book answers those who accused him of pilfering from Origen), from the critical text of M. Adriaen, Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 76 (Turnhout, 1969), pp. 421–524, verified against the page images and collated with the text of Vallarsi and Migne, with the edition's evident misprints corrected. Each lemma gives Micah in Jerome's own version from the Hebrew and then, after "LXX:", in his Latin of the Septuagint; all Scripture is translated as Jerome quotes it, from his own Latin. Italics mark the lemmata and the other Scripture he quotes. Psalms keep the Latin (LXX) numbering, with the Hebrew number in parentheses. Greek words are kept as printed, with an English gloss, and Hebrew words keep Jerome's own transliteration; proper names take their English Bible forms. This English translation is released into the public domain.

Prologue

Micah, on whom I now desire to dictate commentaries, is third in the order of the twelve prophets according to the Seventy translators, sixth according to the Hebrew truth; and he follows the prophet Jonah, who succeeds Obadiah, and so it comes about that Amos is third, and Joel second after Hosea, who is first among all. Therefore, placed as it were in the heart of the volume, he must contain deep mysteries; and the word of God, which always descends to the prophets, descends to Micah as well, which means humility; to Micah of Moresheth, which to this day is a rather small village near Eleutheropolis, a city of Palestine. Moresheth, moreover, in our language means heir. Beautifully, therefore, humility, which is even the foremost among the virtues, is born of the hope of the Lord's inheritance. But humility not of the kind that comes from the consciousness of sins, but that which is placed among the virtues, according to which it is said: Humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the time of visitation, and: He who humbles himself shall be exalted, and: Before destruction the heart of a man is lifted up, and before glory it is humbled. Hence the Lord too: Learn, he says, from me, for I am meek and humble of heart. Just as, therefore, among us names of good wish, and as it were for an omen of virtue, are given, for example: Victor, Castus, Pius, Probus, and among the Greeks one is called σώφρων (temperate) and εὐσεβὴς (pious), and common nouns are turned into proper names, so among the Hebrews Micah too, and Obadiah and Zechariah and the others like them, are given to children by their parents from the name of a virtue.

Book One

1:1

The word of the Lord which came to Micah of Moresheth in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah: which he saw concerning Samaria and Jerusalem. Or according to the Seventy: Concerning the things which he saw concerning Samaria and Jerusalem. The word of the Lord, then, came to Micah after Hosea, Amos and Isaiah, who prophesied under Uzziah. From this we understand that Micah did not prophesy in the times of Uzziah, but in those of his son Jotham, after whom Ahaz reigned, Hezekiah succeeding to the rule of his father Ahaz, under whom the ten tribes were led into captivity by the Assyrians. As far, therefore, as concerns the history, according to the order of the captivity — because first Samaria, the metropolis of Israel, was taken, afterwards Jerusalem, the city of Judah —, the title of the prophecy is set down first concerning Samaria, second concerning Jerusalem. But as far as concerns the mystical understandings, since Samaria is always taken for heresies and Jerusalem for the Church, we say that the word of the Lord comes to Jerusalem and to the coheir of Christ, concerning perverse doctrines, and concerning the Church, if perhaps she has committed any sins, and that it weaves together the order of the whole volume. That Samaria, moreover, and the ten tribes, which were cut off from the stock of David under King Jeroboam, are taken in the person of the heretics, the whole of Scripture indeed bears witness, but above all the prophet Hosea, and this very book, which calls the heretics impious and the churchmen sinners. For it immediately follows: What is the impiety of Jacob? Is it not Samaria? And what is the sin of the house of Judah? Is it not Jerusalem? This, at least, according to the Seventy translators. How it is read in the Hebrew, however, we shall add a little later. But this too, that the heretics always trust in the supposed loftiness of their doctrines and despise the simplicity of the Church, is said in another place: Woe to those who despise Zion and trust in the mountain of Samaria; for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem. Moreover, Samaria fashioned for herself golden calves out of her own understanding, which had beauty indeed on the surface, but did not have the vital spirit, and she fashioned them in the house of God, which is the meaning of Bethel. For the people of Israel could not accept gods except those that were fashioned out of the Scriptures and in the house of God. But although they call their conventicles Bethel, yet after the fashioning of the idols it ceased to be Bethel, and is called Beth-aven, which means the house of the idol, for which the Seventy translated the house of Ὡν (On). So much for the title; now let us come to the opening of the prophecy, and, while the Lernaean beast rages, let us invoke the coming of the Holy Spirit. You, O Paula and Eustochium, pour out prayers to the Lord the Savior, that envy may not harm me, but that my mind, free, may think only of what it strives to explain, and may not feel the slaps of insults, which the Lord despised in his passion.

1:2

Hear, all you peoples, and let the earth attend, and its fullness, and let the Lord God be a witness to you, the Lord from his holy temple. LXX: Hear, all you peoples, and let the earth listen, and all who are in it, and let the Lord God be for a testimony to you: the Lord from his holy house. Since according to the history the sense is plain, I leave the understanding of it to the prudence of the reader. According to tropology, however, he calls together the peoples to hear, that is, the churches of the whole world, and the earth to listen, because the earthly doctrines of the heretics have been built up in it. That heresies, moreover, are numbered among the works of the flesh, which is always referred to the earth, the Apostle does not pass over in silence in writing to the Galatians, and the Lord in the Gospel shows to the prudent hearer: He who is of the earth speaks of the earth. And to distinguish them, he says again of the churchmen: But he who comes from heaven is above all, and: What he has seen and heard, to this he bears witness. If, however, what he says, Hear, you peoples, because of that saying of the Lord, He who has ears to hear, let him hear, sounds something more than what is brought in afterwards, Let the earth attend, we shall apply to the Church: Hear, all you peoples. But to the heretics, who have taken up an earthly doctrine: Let the earth attend, and all who are in it, so that, if these hear and those attend, they may not suffer the things which the word of the Lord afterwards threatens. And let the Lord be for a testimony to them, or, as it is read in the Hebrew, for a witness, or, as Symmachus more plainly translated it, testifying, and testifying from nowhere else than from his own house, which is the Church, or surely in the Son, that is, in our Lord Jesus Christ, who is truly the temple of God the Father, and from whose mouth the Father speaks, piercing the inward parts and the marrow of those who are willing to attend and to hear.

1:3-5

For behold, the Lord will go forth from his place, and he will come down and tread upon the high places of the earth, and the mountains will be consumed beneath him, and the valleys will be split like wax before the fire, like waters that run down headlong; for the crime of Jacob is all this, and for the sins of the house of Israel. What is the crime of Jacob? Is it not Samaria? And what are the high places of Judah? Are they not Jerusalem? LXX: For behold, the Lord will go forth from his place, and will come down, and will go up upon the high places of the earth, and the mountains will be shaken beneath him, and the valleys will melt like wax before the fire, and like water flowing down a slope; because of the impiety of Jacob are all these things, and because of the sin of the house of Israel. What is the impiety of the house of Jacob? Is it not Samaria? And what is the sin of the house of Judah? Is it not Jerusalem? O Samaria and Jerusalem, hear, and diligently attend to the Lord testifying to you from his temple and foretelling whatever he is going to do. Behold, the Lord will go forth from his place. For he who is meek and kind, and whose nature is clemency, is compelled for your sake to take on the character of cruelty, which he does not have. And he will come down and tread upon the high places of the earth. The descent of God, and the running down of his majesty to lower things, is to tread the earth and to crush all the powerful. The mountains, he says, will be consumed, or will melt, and the valleys beneath him, by which we are to understand princes and peoples. And just as wax does not endure the nearness of fire, and waters are borne headlong in a downward course, so all the pride of the impious, when the Lord comes, will be dissolved and will flow away. All this, moreover, will happen because of the crimes of the ten tribes, which he calls Jacob and Israel, and because of the transgression of Judah, since among the ten tribes Samaria was the metropolis, and in the kingdom of Judah Jerusalem fashioned the idols of the high places; this according to the letter. Tropologically, however, the Lord will go forth from his place, by which we can understand either the Son or all the saints. For the Son himself says: I am in the Father, and the Father in me, and of the saints: I will dwell and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people. From these, then, he will go forth, not that he abandons them; for the word of the Lord also went forth from the apostles to those who heard, and yet it did not leave them; and places of this kind, that is, those which deserve to have God as their guest, rising as it were with Christ and sitting with him in the heavenly places, are set on high. Hence too he is said to come down to those who cannot hear his teaching on the mountain. And when he has come down, he will not go up upon the lowly and upon those who are placed below, but upon those who are called the high places of the earth, and who, understanding the majesty of the Lord as he comes, will be shaken. And although they are mountains, yet they will tremble at the presence of such a charioteer or rider. But the valleys, that is, souls set into χοϊκοῖς (earthy) bodies and not rising with the heavenly man, will not be able to bear his presence, but whatever is hard in them will be dissolved, and so they will flow, as if waters had no level ground and were borne headlong into the depth. The Lord, therefore, will come, terrible for teaching, that is, for moving the mountains and for dissolving the lowness of the valleys, because Jacob committed impiety, and Israel sin. But the impiety of Jacob is the conventicles of the heretics, which are called Samaria. And the sin of Judah, that is, of him who confesses the Lord, is nothing other than Jerusalem, in which many crimes are found. That the house of Judah, moreover, is referred to Christ, whose Church it is, we have both often said, and let us set down this for the present: Judah, your brothers shall praise you; your hands shall be upon the back of your enemies. It can also be understood thus: that because of the impieties of Samaria and the crimes of the Jews the Lord went forth from what was once his place, and said to the Jews: Behold, your house shall be left to you desolate. And he came down from the heavens, and went up upon the high places of the earth, that is, upon those who, believing out of the lowliness of the Gentiles, deserved to be lofty. And the mountains were shaken, the doctrines of the philosophers and lofty kingdoms, and those who remained lowly were consumed and crushed by the coming of the Savior; and as the Church grew and the mountains rose on high, the idols fell down into the depth. The Lord, therefore, went forth from his place, and the Church was built from the Gentiles, so that the mountains might be moved beneath his feet and the depths of the valleys dissolved, because Jacob acted impiously, and Israel sinned, and all the tribes denied the Lord.

1:6-9

And I will make Samaria like a heap of stones in the field when a vineyard is planted, and I will pull down her stones into the valley, and I will lay bare her foundations, and all her carved images shall be cut to pieces, and all her wages shall be burned with fire, and all her idols I will lay waste; for they were gathered from the wages of a harlot, and to the wages of a harlot they shall return. For this I will lament and howl, I will go stripped and naked, I will make a lamentation like that of the dragons, and a mourning like that of the ostriches, for her wound is desperate, for it has come even to Judah, and has touched the gate of my people, even to Jerusalem. LXX: And I will make Samaria a watch-hut for fruit in the field, and a planting of a vineyard; and I will pull down her stones into the abyss, and I will lay bare her foundations, and all her carved images shall fall to pieces, and all her wages they shall burn with fire, and all her idols I will lay waste, for from the wages of fornication she gathered, and from the wages of fornication she destroyed. Because of this she shall lament and mourn, she shall go barefoot and naked, she shall make a lamentation like that of the dragons, and a mourning like that of the daughters of the Sirens; for her wound has prevailed, for it has come even to Judah, and has touched even to the gate of my people, even into Jerusalem. According to the order of the sins comes the order of the punishments. Samaria sinned first, and fashioned idols, and worshipped calves in place of the Lord; let her therefore also be the first to perish. I will destroy her when the Assyrians come, and I will make her like a heap of stones when a vineyard is planted, so that she is reduced to mounds. And I will pull down her stones into the valley. For she was situated in the mountains, where Sebaste now is, in which the bones of Saint John the Baptist are also laid to rest. And I will lay bare her foundations. For so great will the ruin be, and so great the overthrow of the city, that not only will the walls and buildings fall, but the foundations too will be laid bare down to the last stone. And all her carved images, and all the riches that various kings had brought together into her, will be cut to pieces and burned with fire and reduced to nothing. For the wealth and the manifold furnishings, which had been gathered, as was thought, from the fornication of idols, will be carried off to another harlot, that is, to Nineveh, so that, just as in their own land they committed fornication with the idols which they made, so they may go to another land of idols and of fornication, that is, to the Assyrians. Thus far concerning Samaria. And since the same wound is to reach Jerusalem — for she too sinned with a like error, forsaking her God and fashioning idols —, for that reason the prophet makes as it were a προσωποποιίαν (personification) of God, and under his own person brings in the feeling of one who laments, and says: For this I will lament and howl, I will go stripped and naked; for I have lost the ten tribes, and I will make a lamentation like that of the dragons, and a mourning like that of the ostriches. For just as dragons resound with a terrible hissing, according to the accounts of those who have written on natural things, at the time when they are overcome by elephants, and as ostriches are forgetful of their eggs and, as though they had not given birth, leave their young in the sand to be trampled by the feet of beasts, of which it is written more fully in Job, so I too shall go about without children, stripped and naked. And I will do this because her wound is desperate, that is, Samaria's. And the same sin, or rather the same punishment of sin, which had overthrown Samaria, will come even to Judah and even to the gate of my city Jerusalem. For as Samaria was overthrown by the Assyrians, so Judah and Jerusalem will be overthrown by the Chaldeans. But since we have once understood Samaria as the church of the heretics, which, separated from God, has become a conventicle of the people, the Lord himself threatens that he will make her a watch-hut for fruit, a field and a planting of a vineyard. For it is much better that a useless city be overthrown, and the stones with which it had been built be pulled down headlong, and that it be prepared for an orchard and a planting of vineyards, than that it remain in a most wicked building. For when her foundations have been destroyed and laid bare, — those by which she seemed to conceal her mysteries and to have firm doctrines on which she stood; and when all the idols which seemed to have outward show, and a beauty composed by artful speech, have been cut to pieces by the ministers of God, that is, by churchmen —, then in the place of the most wicked building the various fruits of the Church will spring up, and not only will they spring up, but they will also be guarded, and the vine of Sorek will be planted, from which may come the wine which the Lord promised that he would drink in the kingdom of the Father. And not only will her foundations, which before were pressed down by earth, be opened and brought out into the open, and the idols which she had fashioned for herself be cut to pieces, but the glory too and the riches which she seemed to have from her fornication and her error will be consumed by my fire, of which I said in the Gospel: I came to cast fire upon the earth, and how I wish that it may burn, and they will be burned up and reduced to nothing, because they were not acquired from the truth of doctrines, but from the fornication of the soul, and were collected from error here and there. For the heretics do not have riches coming from a paternal inheritance, but every day they find something to worship, and fashion idols for themselves with an artful hand and a curious mind. Because of this, when their field has been turned into a watch-hut for fruit and prepared for vineyards, and the stones with which the city had been built have been pulled down into the depth, and their foundations have been laid bare, and all the carved images cut to pieces and burned, and the wages which they promised themselves with vain hope, and whatever they seemed to worship as God, reduced to nothing, because they had procured every reward for themselves from the fornication of the soul, then, understanding their former error and returning to themselves, they will lament over the things at which they used to laugh, and they will mourn over the things in which, committing fornication, they formerly in some way rejoiced. And they will put off from their feet whatever deadly thing they had, and they will be barefoot, because the ground on which they are to stand is a holy place, and they will cast away all the garments of their fornication; and they will be naked, so that they may be clothed with the garment of Christ, and they will make a lamentation like that of the dragons. For the dragons too will one day lament, when they see the greatest dragon captured and hanging on the fisherman's hook, and the sea made desolate. And they will mourn like the daughters of the Sirens, for the songs of the heretics are sweet, and deceive the peoples with a pleasant voice. Nor can anyone pass by their songs unless he has stopped his ear and escaped as if deaf. And this Samaria of such a kind will lament and mourn for this reason: because, wounded by the arrow of the Lord and receiving the wound of the word, she will recognize her error. And not only did she herself sin, but she also wished to bring her iniquity and her error into the gates of Judah. Hence it is said of her: It has come even to Judah, and has touched even to the gates of my people, even to Jerusalem. She touched the gates, he says, by which we understand the ears. But she could not enter the middle of the city; for if she had entered, she would have made Jerusalem a Samaria. Whenever we see some in the Church scandalized at the discourses of the heretics, and asking how to answer their questions, and yet not withdrawing from the Church, let us say: Samaria, or the wound of Samaria, has come even to the confessing people, even to the ears of the people of God, and even to the ears of Jerusalem. For what is said, it has touched even to the gates of my people, is to be understood ἀπὸ κοινοῦ (in common), so that this is understood as well: it has touched even to the gates of Jerusalem. Thus far against Samaria and against Jerusalem; let us look also at the rest that follows.

1:10-15

In Gath do not announce it, do not weep with tears; in the house of dust sprinkle yourselves with dust; pass over for yourselves, dwelling of Shaphir, confounded with shame; she who dwells in Zaanan has not gone out. The house of Ezel will receive mourning from you, the house that stood by itself, for she who dwells in Maroth has grown weak unto good; for evil has come down from the Lord to the gate of Jerusalem. The tumult of the chariot of astonishment for the one dwelling in Lachish is the beginning of sin for the daughter of Zion, for in you were found the crimes of Israel. Therefore he will give emissaries upon the inheritance of Gath; the house of lying, for a deception to the kings of Israel. Still I will bring an heir to you who dwell in Mareshah; the glory of Israel will come as far as Adullam. LXX: You who are in Gath, do not be magnified; you who are in Bachim, do not build out of a house of mockeries; sprinkle earth on your derision, you who inhabit its cities well; the inhabitress of Zaanan has not gone out. Mourn for the house next to her; she will receive from you a blow of pain, who began unto good, she who dwells in pains? For evils have come down from the Lord upon the gates of Jerusalem. The sound of chariots and horsemen, the dwelling of Lachish, is the chief of sin to the daughter of Zion, for in you were found the iniquities of Israel. Therefore he will give emissaries as far as the inheritance of Gath; vain houses, they were in vain to the kings of Israel, until I bring heirs to you, you who dwell in Lachish; the inheritance will come as far as Adullam. The Hebrew disagrees greatly with the translation of the LXX, and both my translation and theirs are wrapped in such great difficulties that, if ever we have needed the Spirit of God, and indeed in expounding his holy Scriptures we always need his coming, now most of all we long for him to be present, and to unfold what he spoke in the prophets, so that what he himself deigns to promise elsewhere may be understood of us too: Open your mouth, and I will fill it. Gath, as the history of Kingdoms also testifies, is one of the five cities of Philistia, near the border of Judea, and for those going from Eleutheropolis to Gaza it is even now a very large village; from there too came that Goliath of Gath whom David killed in battle. Since, therefore, the prophet, or rather the Lord through the prophet, had said: I will mourn and howl, I will go despoiled and naked; I will make a lamentation like that of dragons, and mourning like that of ostriches, for the wound of Samaria is desperate and incurable, and it has come even to Judah and has touched the gate of my people, Jerusalem, for this reason I still command with the voice of one weeping: Do not announce it in Gath, lest the enemies hear and rejoice; do not weep with tears, that is, let not even your grief burst out into sobs; hide your weeping, lest your adversaries exult; let your faces have no tears, though your breast have grief; do not go outside, but in the house of dust sprinkle yourselves with the crumbling ash of the ruins. Pass over for yourselves, dwelling of Shaphir, which in the Syriac and Hebrew language means beautiful. For Samaria is shown even now, situated in the most beautiful and most fertile place of Judea. It is said to her, therefore: O you who dwell in a most fertile region, since you are confounded with shame, so pass over, so be led away into captivity, that because of the greatness of your evils not even your neighbor the Philistine may hear your voice. Moreover, what follows: She who dwells in Zaanan has not gone out, which means going out, or as Symmachus renders: The abundant dwelling has not gone out, is said of the same Samaria, which is at the very doors of the Assyrian captivity, and as soon as she is moved from her borders, enters hostile land. For the abundant dwelling is to be taken in the sense of what we have already said above, the beautiful dwelling. She, therefore, who dwelt in the going out, or in abundance, did not go out of her own will, but was led by force to the Assyrians. Hence the neighboring house, at the side, which is what Ezel means, namely the kingdom of Judah, will receive mourning from you, she who now for the time being, Samaria having been captured, stood firm and had God as her defender. And she received mourning, and was struck with fear and weakened in her good, she who dwells in Maroth, that is, in bitternesses; or as Symmachus renders: The dwelling provoking to bitterness, that is, ἡ κατοικία ἡ παραπικραίνουσα, which in Hebrew is called iosebeth maroth, on account of the captivity of the neighboring tribes, because evil came down from the Lord to the gate of Jerusalem; for the Assyrian, after laying waste Samaria, came also to Jerusalem, at the time when Rabshakeh was sent, hurling insults, about whom both the fourth book of Kingdoms (2 Kings) and Isaiah write more fully. There it is said that the Assyrian king sent from Lachish to Jerusalem, and afterwards, Lachish having been taken, passed on to attack Libnah. Therefore, O Lachish, city given over to idols, the chariot and cavalry of the Assyrians will come to you too, because in you also were found the crimes of Israel, and you were the beginning of idolatry in Judah; for through you, as through a gate, the impiety of the ten tribes migrated into Jerusalem. And not only upon Lachish will the tumult and the chariots come, but also upon Gath, the chief city of Philistia, of which I had said above: In Gath do not announce it; for the Assyrian will send his brigands, whom he calls emissaries, and will possess the house of idolatry and the city of lying, which was a stumbling to the kings of Israel. As for what follows: Still I will bring an heir to you who dwell in Mareshah, he has alluded beautifully to the name; for since Mareshah means inheritance, he called the coming of the enemies into her heirs; and Mareshah, that is, the inheritance, will come as far as Adullam, a city of Judah, which Adullam is glorious among the cities of Israel. In short, Symmachus too translated thus: Ἔτι κληρονόμον ἄξω καί σοι κατοικία Μαρέσα, ἕως Ὀδολλὰμ ἥξει τῆς δόξης Ισραὴλ, that is: Still I will bring an heir to you too, dwelling of Mareshah; as far as Adullam will come of the glory of Israel; that is, you who are glorious among the cities of Israel. And where gloriae is said, let it be the genitive case of the singular number, huius gloriae, of this glory, and not the nominative plural, hae gloriae, these glories. Or certainly let us understand it thus: The captivity of Israel which came to Lachish, and Gath, and Mareshah, will come as far as Adullam too. And of the glory of Israel should be read more pointedly, so that κατὰ ἀντίφρασιν (by antiphrasis) ignominy or devastation is meant. Moreover, as to our having interpreted above the city of the prophet, Moresheth, as inheritance, let the reader know that in the same little verse which we set down, Therefore he will give emissaries upon the inheritance of Gath, in the Hebrew, maraseth Geth is written for the inheritance of Gath. Thus far, according to the Hebrew, as best we could and as it seemed to us, certainly as we heard it from the Hebrews, we have steered our little boat as though among rocks and very sharp crags; whether it has entered port or still tosses on the open sea, it will be for the reader's prudence to judge. Now, by your prayers, let us go on to other waves, and, with shipwreck of the exposition threatening on this side and on that, let us escape if we can. Gath means winepress; those, therefore, who are in Gath, that is, in the winepress, thinking that they have gathered the vintage of the fruit of life and trodden the cluster from the vine of Sorek, are lifted up into pride, not knowing that the cluster of the land of Judea is not found in the territories of the Philistines. Do not, he says, be lifted up, you who are in the winepresses. For your vine is of the vine of Sodom, and your shoot of Gomorrah, and your grape is of bitterness, and you have a cluster of gall, and the fury of dragons is your wine, the incurable fury of asps. For even if you should bear fruit — since your vintage is not only of Sodom and Gomorrah, but also of Egypt and the other hostile nations —, the Lord will give your fruit to the blight, and your labors to the locust, and will kill your vineyards with hail, and your mulberry trees with frost. Let not the likeness of wine deceive you, and do not call the bitter sweet; taste your wine more carefully, and you will find, in place of the wine of Sorek, the fury of dragons and the venom of asps. Therefore do not be proud, but rather humble yourselves under the mighty hand of the Lord, and pass over to that winepress of which he who comes up from Edom, and ruddy from Bozrah, speaks in Isaiah the prophet: I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the nations there is no man with me. Again, since there are others too of foreign race, for the province of the Philistines has many regions and cities, who through evil works and thoughts contrary to God say: We have been destroyed, but let us return and build up what has been destroyed. Hence they are also called the borders of impiety, and the people with whom the Lord is angry. To them it is said: You who are in Bachim, do not build from a house of mockeries. Bachim in our language signifies mourning and weeping. In short, except for the Seventy, all translated κλαυθμὸν, that is, weeping. You, therefore, who are engaged in works and opinions of this kind, which are worthy of weeping, do not renew a most wicked building; and do not suppose your own understanding to be God's edifice, nor build upon sand, lest, when the storm comes and your house falls, the empty labor offer laughter to those who see it. Rather, understanding that your building is worthy of derision, sprinkle your heads with its ruins and dust, and repent, because you wished without counsel to build a house that would fall. There follows: She who dwells well in her cities has not gone out, dwelling in Zaanan. This seems to me to have this meaning: You who are proud in Gath and in Bachim, in vain do you try to build a house worthy of laughter; sprinkle yourselves with dust and repent, because you wished to press out a detestable wine and to construct a building contrary to God. But the Church of Christ, which dwells well, and, possessing churches in the whole world, is joined by the unity of the Spirit, and has the cities of the law, of the prophets and of the Gospel, and of the apostles, has not gone out of her borders, that is, out of the holy Scriptures, but keeps the possession she entered upon, because she dwells in Zaanan, which Symmachus, as we said above, rendered abundance; for she has the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, with whom are all spiritual graces and abundance of virtues. Hence it is said to her: Let peace be in your strength, and abundance in your towers. But you who dwell beside Zaanan, that is, beside the abundant Church, O you heretics, O contrary doctrines, mourn for yourselves, because you have built yourselves a house for derision, and have pressed the winepress into pride, and have built your house not in the Scriptures but in the neighborhood of the Scriptures, a house now worthy not of laughter but of mourning and tears. Hence it is also added: mourn for the house which is beside her, that is, beside Zaanan. But what is added, She will receive from you a blow of pain, is said to the same heretics who are commanded to mourn for the house neighboring the Church, because the enemy and avenger, the devil, to whom they are to be handed over for punishment, himself inflicts blows on them, and for their most wicked building wrings out of them the pain of the blows. These are inflicted for this reason, that, perceiving the sins they had committed, they may repent, and the dwelling of pains may be for them an occasion of good things. Or else the pain of the Church is indicated, because she mourns her one-time sons, and she herself is the cause of the heretics' salvation, if they are willing to return to their grieving mother. Moreover, as to what is brought in, For evils have come down from the Lord upon the gates of Jerusalem, the Marcionites and the Manichaeans too use this text, on the ground that the God of the law is the author of evils. Let us say that evils came down from the Lord in the way that the Savior also speaks in the Gospel: I saw Satan like lightning falling from heaven. For as there Lucifer, who rose in the morning, fell from heaven and was crushed upon the earth, he who used to send to the nations, so also these evils, which fell from the Lord and came to the gates of Jerusalem, were not evils before they fell, but because they fell headlong from the Lord, for that reason they became evils. And that we may know the snares of the evils: They came, he says, to the gates of Jerusalem, which, because they are strong and adamantine, are shut by the apostles, to whom the keys of Jerusalem have been entrusted; before them the enemies roam about, and whomever they see going out, they kill. If anyone, therefore, is of Jerusalem, which dwells well in her cities, he does not go out, since he dwells in abundance; but he remains always within, and does not go out of her gates, for whoever goes out of them is killed; and he is killed by those who lift themselves up in Gath, and dwell in Bachim, and build a house for derision. In short, the evils which came down from the Lord to the gates of Jerusalem have only the din of chariots and the tumult of horses, and a confused sound before the doors of Jerusalem, so that with the lance of their mouth they may kill whomever they see wandering. After this it is written: You who dwell in Lachish: she is the chief of sin for the daughter of Zion. Lachish is interpreted πορεία ἐστὶν, that is, walking. And so these too, who went up in their chariots, and had horsemen, and only din and empty sound, to whom she who dwells well in her cities said, These in chariots and these on horses, but we will call upon the name of the Lord our God; because they moved their feet, and were carried about by every wind of false doctrine, and wished to go out from the Church, which is interpreted watchtower, that is, Zion, were the chiefs of sin for the daughter of Zion. And in her, that is, in Lachish, were found the impieties of Israel, which separated the people of God from its former kingdom. She, then, who dwells in Lachish, that is, in a most evil walking, with a step ever wavering, is the chief of sin for the daughter of Zion; and the impiety of Israel is in those who always move their feet and are said to dwell in Lachish. Emissaries too will be given as far as the inheritance of Gath: Gath most wicked, and a winepress of poisons, which is built against the house of God, where there are vain houses, which were built for derision. And these vain houses were made in vain for the kings of Israel: as far as the history is concerned, for those kings whose sins are written in the books of Kingdoms and of Chronicles; but as far as anagogy is concerned, for the chiefs of the heretics and the leaders of perverse doctrines; for to them the houses are vain and built in vain. And they last only so long, until the heirs who are to be brought in by the Lord hold them. After this there follows: You who dwell in Lachish, the inheritance will come as far as Adullam. Adullam means testimony of the draught, or of their drawing, which in Greek is expressed more significantly, μαρτυρία ἀντλήσεως αὐτῶν. We read in Proverbs: If you are evil, you alone will draw up evils. She, therefore, who dwells in Lachish, that is, on a most evil road, will come to the testimony of her own draining, which she will drain and drink down according to the measure of her works. Or certainly it should be punctuated so that what is said, you who dwell in Lachish, is referred to what precedes, and the order and sense is: I will bring heirs to you from the Church, you who dwell in Lachish, because you too will be of the inheritance of the Lord, when you have drained and received what you deserve. At the end of this section I beseech the reader not to take necessity for choice, nor to judge a lengthy exposition to be verbosity, and to marvel rather that in places so rough I find anything at all, than that I leave out none of the things that have to be said.

And in order that Scripture might teach what it was that they devised at night and did by day, it sets it out part by part. They coveted, he says, fields and took them by violence, and houses, it is understood, they coveted, and those which they had coveted they plundered; and not only did they wrong men and their houses, but they also laid waste with rabid mouth their descendants, who deserved mercy because of their tender age. Then a parable will be spoken over you, and your miseries will be turned into a song: We have been laid waste with devastation, the portion of my people. For the glory of the daughter of Israel has been made bald and shorn over her once most delicate sons, and if any hair grew back afterwards, it was cut off in turn by another shaving and shearing. Then all their counsels were turned into toil, and what they devised, with mind and spirit asleep, brought them toils; and what they had done was thrown into confusion as soon as the light of Christ and vengeance appeared. For though they had read that Israel conquered when Moses lifted up his hands to the Lord, and was overcome by Amalek when Moses let his weary arms sink, they did not lift up their hands to the Lord, but, perpetrating every crime against the poor and the people of the Lord, they desired fields, and plundered the houses of orphans, and laid waste alike a man and his wife and his children and his substance. As for what follows, ἐξαίφνης, that is, suddenly, it is not found in the Hebrew scrolls, and yet it can fit the present passage, so that we say: Therefore thus says the Lord: Behold, I devise evils against this tribe suddenly, from which they cannot lift their necks, and for this reason they will not be able to lift them, because the time is evil. For just as they worked evils against the Lord Jesus, so they will endure the evils of perpetual captivity, and will come into such great distress that all their songs and psalms will be turned into mourning. For the land of promise, which had earlier been divided by lot to two and a half tribes beyond the Jordan, with Moses casting the cord, and afterwards apportioned by Joshua son of Nun to the remaining tribes, has been divided among the nations by a Roman surveyor, and there was no one to forbid; rather, though it holds all the nations, there is no one of the Jews who possesses the ancient soil in its former freedom. But if we wish to follow the third exposition, of which I spoke above, that the whole discourse is directed to the human soul, which, falling from paradise, has come into the captivity of this world, we shall see that all our thinking is toil and pain, and our beds are full of evils, and the very light that is seen is mixed with darkness, and what we plot at night we accomplish in gloom. For who of us lifts holy hands to God without anger and scheming? Who does not desire the estates of this world, forgetful of the possessions of paradise? You may see some joining field to field and boundary to boundary, and the lands of cities not sufficing for the small little body of a man. Therefore the Lord devises evils against us, from which we cannot lift our necks, nor walk upright, for it is a most evil time, according to the words of John, who says: The world is set in wickedness. The same thing is signified also by that daughter of Abraham in the Gospel, a noble soul, whom Satan had bound and bent over, and who could not be raised up on high before the coming of the Lord, nor look up at her Creator. Hence the Savior says: This woman, he says, a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan had bound, ought she not to have been loosed from this bond on the sabbath day? Are not my words good with him who walks uprightly? And on the contrary my people has risen up as an adversary; you have taken away the cloak from over the tunic, and those who were passing by in simplicity you have turned into war. LXX: In the Church of the Lord do not weep with tears, nor let them lament over these things? For he will not cast off the reproaches, which he speaks: The house of Jacob has provoked the Spirit of the Lord, if these are his devices; are not his words good with him, and have they walked upright? And formerly my people stood out with enmities against its own peace; they flayed off its skin, to take away hope, the crushing of war. Do not speak, he says, speaking, for which Aquila translated: Do not drip, dripping; at times by a Hebrew idiom calling eloquence a dripping, from the fact that it flows and reaches the ears of those who hear, in the likeness of descending rain. Do not deceive yourselves, O house of Jacob, and say to one another for mutual consolation: God is good, the captivity which we fear will not come. Will his generous mercy and his most clement Spirit, which has risen widely and abundantly upon all, be cut short and be severe in us alone? Or are his thoughts of such a kind as we see in men, so that he holds on to an old anger and is roused to vengeance by sudden fury? To this the Lord answered: I am indeed good, and my words sound clemency, but to those who walk on the right road. But he who, I do not say long ago, but yesterday, worshipped idols in insult to me, and, as far as lay in him, seized tyrannical arms against me; who stripped the unhappy people of God's help, and took away the cloak as if from over the tunic; who turned against me in war all the simple among the believers and those who rest in the authority of their elders, will not the Lord drip upon him, and will not confusion seize him? But as for what we have translated, And on the contrary my people has risen up as an adversary, since the word mul signifies both the contrary and yesterday, Symmachus rendered it more plainly, so as to say: One day before, my people stood out as an enemy, in order to resolve the question, namely that God was imputing to the people not past vices but recent ones, and such as seemed to have been committed as if yesterday. So much according to the Hebrew understanding; but the Seventy translators in this place do not cohere with themselves at all. For to what was set first, In the Church of the Lord do not weep, what consequence is there, that there should be added, for he will not cast off the reproaches which he speaks: The house of Jacob has provoked the Spirit of the Lord? And also what follows: If these are his devices, are not his words good with him, and have they walked upright? What bearing on the later part have the words that are said: And formerly my people stood out as an adversary against its own peace; they flayed off its skin, to take away the crushing of war? It seems to me, however, that in a place so very difficult the sense can be rendered or expressed thus, if only the prudent reader will acquiesce in our reasoning. The Church, then, is commanded not to have sadness and anxiety over the affairs of the world and the losses that are wont to happen in this world, and it is said to her inhabitants: O you who are in the Church of the Lord, rejoice always, and whatever befalls you from the judgments of God, give thanks for it. Nor do I say this because you ought not to weep — for Blessed indeed are those who weep, for they shall laugh —, but I warn you of this, that you do not lament over the things that are of the world. If one of your kin has died, if the treasury has seized your property, if gout or any other disease whatever has oppressed your body, do not weep, do not shed tears, and do not consider the present but the future, and be weighed down rather because you dwell longer in this tabernacle of death. Rather beware of this, that you do not insult those who fall, and think another's ruin your own edification; let each one measure himself by his own strength, not by the weakness of another. Otherwise what justice is this, for the branches of the wild olive to insult the branches of the olive, which were broken off because of their unbelief, and to say: The house of Jacob provoked the Spirit of the Lord to anger, killing the prophets, worshipping idols, crucifying the Son of God? He who does this will not be free from reproach, and with the measure with which he judged he will himself be judged, and just as he speaks of the sins of the one who falls, so another will insult his ruin. There follows: If these are his devices: are not his words good with him, and have they walked upright? That the Jewish people stumbled, he says, so that the fullness of the Gentiles might come in, belongs to the dispensation of God, so that afterwards, when Israel believes, all might be saved, and all might stand in need of the mercy of God. Hence the Apostle too, coming to the question of this place, proclaims that the depth of the wisdom and knowledge and of the judgments of God is an unsearchable abyss. If, then, it is God's devices and thoughts that the former branches of the olive should be broken off and others from the wild olive grafted in, you ought not to insult but to fear lest you fall, nor should you suppose that you please God in this, if you read his words, that is, his scriptures. The scriptures profit the reader only if what is read is fulfilled in deed. If, speaking of the scriptures, you can say: Or do you seek a proof of him who speaks in me, Christ? The Lord indeed will give the word to those who preach the gospel with great power, and upon a high mountain has he gone up who preaches the gospel to Zion, and he lifts up his voice in strength who preaches the gospel to Jerusalem. And in this way the words of God are good, if they are with him, that is, if God does not desert the preacher whose heart and lips agree. But he who confesses with his lips while his heart is far from God, and recounts his justices and takes up his covenant in his mouth, and is defiled with the filth of sins, with him the words of God are not good. This is to be said not only of the sinner, but also of him who does not have spiritual grace, that is, the grace of prophecy and of teaching and of interpretation, and of the greater charisms. This must be said, that if a man of this kind wishes to give the causes of the elements and the reason for his faith, and to explain why God, who is good and the Creator of all, came to the Jews alone and called the nations only in the last age, he does not have the good words of God with him, but out of his own unskillfulness defiles even those words of God which are good, words which walk upright and require upright ears. And these things indeed the Lord commands the people that succeeded, and the Church gathered from the Gentiles: not to insult the former generation, and so by insulting not be free from reproaches. And he himself, who is the true judge and speaks without perturbation, relates how in his passion Israel was contrary to him, and did this not because it could harm its Creator, but because it perpetrated everything against its own peace. Hence too the word to Jerusalem: If you knew the things that are for your peace. And with their peace lost, they flayed off their own skin, that is, they took away from themselves the help of God by which they were protected, and left their flesh as it were bare, without skin and covering, so that whatever seemed beautiful while the mercy of God covered it from above, this, when that mercy withdrew, displayed ugliness to those who saw it. And when peace and the help of God withdrew, because they had resisted the Lord, of whom it is said, The Lord who crushes wars, the Lord is his name, they could not resist their adversaries, but were defeated in every contest, nor was there anyone to crush the wars springing up against them, in respect of both persons of the enemies, either the men who led them captive, or the strength of the adversaries who daily slaughtered their souls by blasphemy.

The word is still against the people of God, to whom he had already said above: On the contrary my people has risen up as an adversary: from over the tunic you have taken away the cloak; namely that they had not only done this, but had also made the women, that is, the matrons once delicate, go into captivity, or, under a metaphor, the cities of Judea, which in Isaiah too he calls the daughters of Zion, because Zion was their metropolis. From their little ones too, he says, you have taken away my praise forever: no one remained among the people, all being either killed or captured, who sang my psalms; and even the few who survived in Babylon testify that they cannot sing. How, they say, shall we sing the song of the Lord in a foreign land? Arise, therefore, and go into captivity, for you have no rest in this land, which has been polluted because of your crimes and cannot be cleansed unless it first keeps a long sabbath. And the reason I say to you, you have no rest here, is that your land is polluted and will be corrupted with a most grievous rotting, namely by captivity, either the Babylonian or the Roman, because it drank the blood of the Lord; for it can be understood according to either truth of the history.

LXX: The leaders of my people will be cast out of the houses of their delights; because of their evil devices they have been cast out. This can be taken both generally of the princes of the Jewish people, the priests and Pharisees, who after the passion of the Lord were cast out of the city of their delights, in which they had formerly wantoned because of their evil devices; and specially of the stock of David, because as soon as the Lord was born, the prince failed from Judah, and the leader from his thighs, when he came for whom it was reserved, and the expectation of the nations appeared. But the prophetic word also describes the princes of the Church who abound in delights and believe that they keep their chastity amid feasts and wantonness: that they are to be cast out of their spacious houses and sumptuous banquets, and dishes sought out with great labor, and cast out because of their evil thoughts and works. And if you wish to know whither they are to be cast out, read the Gospel: Into the darkness namely without, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth. Is it not a confusion and a disgrace to preach Jesus crucified, the poor and hungry master, with stuffed bodies, and to set forth the doctrine of fasting with red cheeks and swollen faces? If we are in the place of the apostles, let us not only imitate their speech, but also embrace their way of life and their abstinence. Holy, certainly, is the ministry of the apostles also, to minister to widows and the poor. It is not right, they say, that we should leave the word of God and serve tables. But now the priest of Christ invites to dinner, I do not say the poor, I do not say the brethren and those who cannot invite in return, from whom, apart from the grace, episcopal hands could hope for nothing else, but soldiers and men girt with the sword, and judges, with centurions and squadrons of soldiers keeping watch before their doors. The clergy run about the whole city, seeking to provide for the judges what they either cannot find in their own praetoria or at least do not buy up once found. Nor indeed is it to be thought that this invective is directed generally at everyone, but rather that the prophetic word strikes those who are such, and threatens them with punishments and everlasting darkness, so that those who are not restrained by shame and modesty may repent at least at the threat of penalties.

LXX: Draw near to the eternal mountains. By the eternal mountains we can understand either angels or prophets, of whom it is also written in the psalm: Its foundations are on the holy mountains, and in another place: I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to me. And he draws near to the eternal mountains who is not separated by his sins from the fellowship of the blessed, just as Moses too drew near to God, not in place but in merit. And to those who drew near to the eternal mountains the Lord himself spoke: I am a God who draws near, and not a God from afar. The mountains are called eternal to distinguish them from those which are not eternal, namely the princes of this world, the dark mountains; and when these have been lifted up like the cedar of Lebanon, they pass away with the world, and their place cannot be found.

LXX: Arise and walk, for there is no rest for you here. We are commanded to think that there is rest for us in none of the things of the world, but, as if rising from the dead, to strive toward the heights, and to walk after the Lord our God, and to say: My soul has cleaved after you. But if we despise this, and refuse to hear him who says, Arise, you who sleep, and be lifted up, and Christ will enlighten you; we shall indeed slumber, but we shall be deceived, and we shall not find rest, for where Christ does not enlighten the one who rises, what seems to be rest is tribulation.

LXX: Because of uncleanness you have been consumed with corruption. For what we have said, you have been consumed, it can, according to the Greek understanding, sound in the Latin language as you have been corrupted, so that the order is: because of uncleanness you have been corrupted with corruption. This is said to those who, serving the pleasure of the body and lust, corrupt not only their soul but their body as well, lovers of pleasures rather than lovers of God. He could also have said, because of uncleanness you have been corrupted, and the sense would have been complete even without with corruption. But now, because he says, You have been corrupted with corruption, he seems to me to have said it to distinguish it from a salutary corruption, of which the Apostle also speaks: And though our man who is outside is corrupted, yet he who is inside is renewed from day to day. He who always carries the dying of Jesus in his body, and corrupts the outer man, and subjects the flesh to the rule of the soul, is indeed corrupted, but not with corruption, because his corruption is salutary.

LXX: You have fled with no one pursuing. This is said to those who because of uncleanness have been corrupted with corruption, that through the consciousness of their sins, even without punishments, they do not dare to resist the enemy and fight. Hence too those who are fearful in battle with the enemy, and would terrify the minds of their brothers, are cast out of the camp and driven from the battle line, and in the curses of Leviticus the word is directed to men of this kind: The sound of a flying leaf will pursue you, and you will flee, with no one pursuing. I know that I have read in the commentaries of a certain man expounding the beginning of John the Evangelist, All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing, that he referred what is said, nothing, to wickedness; and again that he interpreted wickedness itself as the devil, and by this step, as it were, understood the very nothing that was made without Christ to be the devil. If, then, wickedness, or the devil, is nothing, and those who have been corrupted with corruption fled with no one, that is, nothing, pursuing, the devil pursued them in the guise of nothing. But if this seems to anyone too forced, and argued against the simplicity of Scripture by an artifice of eloquence rather than by true interpretation, let him follow either the former exposition or one he has found himself.

Would that I were not a man who has the spirit, and that I rather spoke a lie. I will drip for you into wine and into drunkenness, and this people will be the one upon whom it drips. Gathering I will gather Jacob, all of you; I will bring together into one the remnant of Israel alike. I will set him like a flock in the sheepfold, like cattle in the midst of the folds. They will be in tumult from the multitude of men; for he will go up opening the way before them; they will divide and pass through the gate and go out by it; and their king will pass on before them, and the Lord at their head. In this section too the Seventy translators differ much from the Hebrew. Therefore let us first expound according to what has been handed down to us by the Hebrews, and afterwards, if God wills, we shall treat of their translation. O Jewish people, to whom belong the promises, and whose are the covenants and the law, and from whom is Christ according to the flesh, to whom, when the Babylonians or the Romans were threatening, I said: Arise and go into captivity, since there is no rest for you in this land, which because of its uncleanness will be corrupted by a final devastation, do not think that I speak willingly and proclaim with joy what I see coming; I would wish myself to be anathema for my brothers, who are Israelites. Would that I spoke from my own mind and did not have the Holy Spirit; and that, counted rather among the false prophets, I alone might perish, and the things I say might not be true; and that so great a multitude might believe in the Son of God, and not be handed over to perpetual captivity. But since I am a prophet, and speak by the Spirit of God, and, sent by the Godhead, proclaim the truth, for this reason I will drip my word for you into undiluted wine, which will make you drunk and cause you to fall. And while I drip, and sing of the evil of the captivity to come, this people will receive my rains, that is, willing or not, it must endure what I say. And lest you think that I am a prophet of evils only: now indeed the foretold captivity will come; but behold, in me speaks the word of God, which comes to all the prophets. When it is silent, the prophet does not speak; and now it says: I will come, and take a human body, I will be born of a virgin. Or thus: Because I came in the humility of the flesh and you did not believe me, I will come at the consummation of the world in my majesty with the angels and the other powers, and then I will gather all of you, Jacob; then I will bring together into one the remnant of Israel, and join them alike with the people of the Gentiles in my sheepfold; then I will hedge you about with a most firm wall, and so great will be the multitude of believers, and the tumult of the flock set among the folds, that the number of the cattle will be overcome by their abundance. And lest perhaps you think, because I said, I will set him like a flock in the sheepfold, and like cattle in the midst of the folds, that I am speaking of sheep, understand that these sheep are men. For there follows: They will be in tumult from the multitude of men. Tumult is the voice of very many, and the shout of an exceedingly great multitude uttered together, so that we may not suppose it to be the voice of one, but the common voice of all, praising the good shepherd, who has leveled every steep place and made it level with his own foot, so that he, the leader of their journey, is himself the door of paradise, and says, I am the gate; and as he divides the way and goes before, being both the way and the gate, the believing flock will pass through him. And this shepherd is king and Lord. Hence there follows: And their king will pass on before them, and the Lord at their head. But if we wish to take all these things of his first coming, and to refer the whole of Jacob and the remnant of Israel to the apostles and to that multitude from the Jews which was saved in the Acts of the Apostles, the expositor's explanation will not be far from the truth. For these the Lord truly gathered into his sheepfold, and set in the midst of the folds, and made a way before them, and led them into the Church, and he was their king before them, and is the Lord at their head forever.

LXX: The spirit has set a lie. It has dripped for you into wine and drunkenness, and it will be from the drop of this people; Jacob, to be gathered, will be gathered. It is not, as most suppose, the spirit stood false, but the spirit has set a lie that must be read, which in Greek is said πνεῦμα ἔστησε ψευδὲς, that is, τὸ ψεῦδος (the lie). For just as with putrid wounds, lest a cancer creep on and the dead body feed upon the living flesh, physicians arrest the wound and burn it with a cautery or with a καυστικῷ (caustic) powder, so the Spirit of God put an end to the lie, lest the people of God be any longer subverted by the voices of the false prophets. That spirit, in the New as well as in the Old Testament, wherever it is set down without an addition, is to be understood in a good sense, we have often said, and we shall now set it out in part, lest anyone be in doubt. But the fruit of the spirit is love, joy, peace, and: If we live by the spirit, let us also walk by the spirit, and elsewhere: But if by the spirit you put to death the works of the flesh, you will live, and in the Old Testament: Giving breath to the people which is upon it, and spirit to those who tread upon it; there is no doubt from what precedes that it signifies the earth. For those who tread on earthly works and put them under their feet surely deserve to receive not an evil but a good spirit. On the contrary, an evil spirit is always read with an addition, as there: But when the unclean spirit has gone out of a man, and in another place: He rebuked the unclean spirit, and: An evil spirit seized Saul, and other things like these. The Spirit of God, therefore, who put an end to the lie in the false prophets, will himself drip for you into wine and drunkenness: the wine that gladdens the heart of man, and the drunkenness with which Noah was made drunk, and of which it is said elsewhere: Eat, friends, and be drunk. Yet all this gladness and drunkenness, in comparison with the wisdom of God, which like a river waters the heavenly Jerusalem, is a drop and a very small droplet. Nor will those hesitate to speak of a drop of the wisdom of God in men who have read of the stone cut from the mountain without hands, and that the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and that the apostles themselves too knew in part and prophesied in part. From this wisdom, then, that is, from the drop of the Jewish people — for he came only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel —, Jacob was gathered: everyone who supplants Esau, and snatches away his birthright and blessing, and, before he is born, while still lying in the womb, seizes the heel of his hairy brother.

LXX: With all, receiving I will receive the remnant from Israel. When, he says, the people of the Gentiles has believed, and the whole world has been brought to my faith, and the fullness of the Gentiles has entered, then the remnant of Israel too will be saved; not that remnant of which it is written in the book of Kingdoms, I have left for myself seven thousand men who have not bowed the knee to Baal, and of which Paul speaks, So also at this present time a remnant according to the election of grace has been saved, and of which this prophet testifies above, And it will be from the drop of this people; Jacob, to be gathered, will be gathered, but that remnant which, after all have been received, will be received by God at the end, and of which it is now said, With all, receiving I will receive the remnant of Israel, so that, according to what is written, God, shutting up all under sin, may have mercy on all.

LXX: I will set their turning away like sheep in tribulation. The remnant from Israel, which I am to receive after I have received all, I will now in the meantime, because they have turned away from me, set in tribulation, and straiten, and make to sit without priest, without altar and without prophet, so that him whom they did not perceive through benefits they may understand through punishments.

LXX: Like a flock in the midst of their resting place; I will set is understood. Not only, he says, will their turning away, by which they turned away from me, be set like a flock in tribulation, but after they have been afflicted and have completed the time of distress, they will be set in rest, that is, in their resting place. And then they will migrate from men and transcend the state of the human condition, and will fulfill what follows: They will leap forth from men. And not only will they leap forth and depart, but all to whom the word of God comes, and who, leaving human vices behind, imitate the way of life of the Godhead, and hear: I said, you are gods, and all sons of the Most High, they will leap forth from men, and will be borne, as it were, to heaven.

LXX: Go up through the division. The present place has as it were its own exordium and an apostrophe of prophetic speech to him who desires to be saved, who is commanded to go up through the division. This indeed will become clearer if we take an example from Genesis, where twins are born of Tamar, with whom the patriarch Judah lay: It came to pass, it says, when she was giving birth, and had twins in her womb; in the very birth one put forth his hand, and the midwife tied scarlet on his hand, saying: This one will come out first. But when he had drawn back his hand, at once his brother came out, and she said: Why has the wall been divided for your sake? And she called his name Perez, which is interpreted division. And after this his brother came out, on whose hand was the scarlet, and she called his name Zerah, which in our language means seed, or rising. The elder people, then, in whom was the rising and the seed before the Church was born from the Gentiles, showed its hand in works, and afterwards drew it back, hearing through Isaiah: For your hands are full of blood. And when he had drawn back his hand and ceased from the works of justice, his brother, the people of the Gentiles, came out. And for his sake the hedge was divided, and the Lord and Savior destroyed, so that he might be able to advance, the partition which was in the middle and the wall separating the two peoples; and he made one flock, and created the two in himself into one new man, making peace. Hence too the midwife, speaking prophetically, says to Perez, the younger people: Why has the wall been divided for your sake? If you have understood the example from Genesis, go up, you who desire to be saved, by no means through the old people, which has its hand drawn back, but through the new, in which Christ is the way, in whom Jesus is the gate, through whom we advance to the Father; for he himself dissolved the middle partition and wall, that is, the obscurity of the ancient prophets, and opened all the mysteries of the old law, and, having scattered the difficulty of advancing, revealed the way to the eyes of all, so that whoever wishes to go on may be hindered by no obstacle nor be frightened by the fog of obscurity.

LXX: Before their face they divided and passed through the gate, and went out by it. And their king went out before their face. But the Lord is their leader. For this reason I said to you, Go up through the division, you who have risen with Christ and seek the things that are above: because the angels, or the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, divided what seemed to stand in the way, and made a way for those who wished to enter; and because, the road being open, they took to the road, they not only entered the gate, but also passed through it. And they entered because their king too entered the same gate and opened the way for them, so that they might walk without any difficulty. For the Lord himself is both king and shepherd, both the way and the gate, and he says: I am the gate: whoever enters through me will be saved; he will go in and go out, and will find pasture. Of this gate it is prophesied elsewhere too: This is the gate of the Lord, the just will enter by it. But whoever has entered ought not to remain in the state in which he entered; let him go out to the pastures, so that in entering there is the beginning, and in going out and finding pasture, the perfection of the virtues. He who enters is still in the world, and understands the Creator from the creatures. But he who goes out transcends the whole creation, and, counting as nothing all things that can be seen, will find pasture above the heavens, and will feed on the word of God, and will say: The Lord shepherds me, and I shall lack nothing. This is said so that we might understand the testimony of the Gospel, He will go in and go out, and will find pasture, and what is now said through the prophet, And they passed through the gate, and went out by it; yet this passing through and going out cannot be granted without Christ our king, who is king and Lord. For immediately there is joined: But the Lord will be the leader.

3:1-4

And I said: Hear, O princes of Jacob, and leaders of the house of Israel. Is it not yours to know judgment, you who hate the good and love evil, who violently tear their skins from off them, and their flesh from off their bones, who have eaten the flesh of my people, and flayed their skin from off them; and they have broken their bones, and chopped them up as in a cauldron, and like flesh in the midst of a pot. Then they will cry to the Lord, and he will not hear them, and he will hide his face from them at that time, as they have acted wickedly in their devices. The word is plainly directed against the princes of Israel, and under the metaphor of lions or of robbers their cruelty is described: that they despoiled the poor, and killed them, and crushed their flesh and bones; and that, as in the midst of a pot, so in the city of Jerusalem they afflicted the wretched common people; and that on account of these things punishment is afterwards brought upon them in the day of captivity, whether by Nebuchadnezzar or by Vespasian and Titus. And they cry to the Lord, and he does not hear them, and he hides his face from them, because they acted wickedly in their crimes.

LXX: And he will say: Hear these things, O princes of the house of Jacob, and the rest of the house of Israel. The sentence clings to what went before. For above he had said: But the Lord will be the leader, and he will say: Hear, O princes of the house of Jacob, and the rest of the house of Israel. For "the rest of the house of Israel", all except the Seventy translated "leaders of the house of Israel". The Lord, therefore, who made a way for his people, and went out before their face, and is himself the leader of the journey of the simpler people, whom he calls his flock, threatens those who are unwilling to follow him as he goes before, but are proud, and, as judges of the people, do not follow his footsteps, and he says: Hear, O princes of the house of Jacob, and leaders of the house of Israel. But what is it that compels them to hear? It is not yours, he says, to know judgment, you who hate the good, and seek evils; that is, you do not deserve to know the judgment of God, which is a great abyss, and the depth of his justice a perverse mind does not find. Or how can you know the judgment of God, you who hate the good and seek evil, who loathe the holy poor and honor rich sinners? And at the same time let us consider the force of the words: if not to love good things is a mark of sin, of how great a crime is it even to hate them? And on the contrary, if not to flee evil things is a fault, of how great an impiety is it even to seek them out diligently? After this the savagery of the judges is described, and their cruelty toward those subject to them.

LXX: Tearing away their skins from them, and their flesh from their bones. As they have devoured the flesh of my people, and flayed the skins from them, and broken their bones, and chopped them up like flesh in a cauldron, and like flesh in a pot; so will they cry to the Lord, and he will not hear them, and he will turn away his face from them at that time, because they have acted most wickedly in their devices. It had not been enough for them to despoil the flock subject to them, but they also afflicted their bodies with harsh rule, and crushed their bones, so as to break and grind whatever perhaps had been in them. As, therefore, they despoiled my people, and stripped them of all the beauty and comeliness of their skin, and put their flesh and bones into the boiling pot which the Assyrian king kindled, handing my flock over to the devil and his angels, so they too, when the day of vengeance has come, will cry to the Lord and will not be heard, because they themselves did not hear those who asked; and they will stretch out their hands to the Lord, and God will turn away his face from them, because they too turned away their countenance from those who entreated them. And they will suffer all these things because they acted most wickedly in their pursuits and in their pleasures; and they were not kings but tyrants, not overseers but lions, not teachers of disciples but wolves of the sheep; and they glutted themselves with flesh, and grew fat, and, like a most fat victim of slaughter, were also made ready for the punishments of the Lord. Thus far against the wicked princes; the word that follows is against the false prophets and the very wicked teachers, who trip up the people of God with flattery, promising knowledge of the Scriptures.

3:5-8

Thus says the Lord concerning the prophets who lead my people astray, who bite with their teeth and preach peace; and if anyone does not put something into their mouth, they sanctify war against him; therefore night shall be to you instead of vision, and darkness to you instead of divination, and the sun shall go down upon the prophets, and the day shall be darkened over them, and those who see visions shall be confounded; and the diviners shall be confounded, and they shall all cover their faces, because there is no answer of God. But I am filled with the strength of the spirit of the Lord, with judgment and truth, to declare to Jacob his crime, and to Israel his sin. LXX: Thus says the Lord concerning the prophets who lead my people astray, and bite with their teeth, and preach peace over him, and it was not given into their mouth, they sanctified war against him; therefore night shall be to you instead of vision, and darkness shall be to you from divination, and the sun shall go down upon the prophets, and the day shall grow dark over them; and those who see dreams shall be confounded, and the soothsayers shall be mocked, and all these shall speak against them, because there is no one to hear them, unless I fill up strength in the spirit of the Lord, and judgment and power, to declare to Jacob his impieties, and to Israel his sins. We read that there were false prophets in Israel who for gifts preached peace, which had not been given to them, and if anyone had not given them presents, however holy he might be, they would announce to him that the wrath of God was going to come. Hence it is now said to them that they speak lies, and that their word is not prophecy but divination, and that they have not light but darkness and error. And when the things they had promised have been turned to the contrary, then they are covered with confusion, because there will not be, he says, an answer of God.

Now not even the demons will receive power to deceive them by their fraud. The oracles will be mute, the unclean spirit will be silent and will not dare to mock. So much concerning the false prophets. Again the speech of the prophet speaking about himself is introduced: while they prophesy falsely and are covered with confusion and ignominy, I, what I speak, I speak moved by the Holy Spirit, and I speak the judgment of the Lord and the truth. And while the false prophets bite with their teeth and preach peace, I without any fear declare to Jacob his crime and to Israel his sin, because they either worshipped idols in place of God, or crucified the Son of God. But if we choose to take what is said according to the Seventy as concerning the heretics, who are truly false prophets, and say, thus says the Lord, when the Lord has not sent them, we shall not err. For they deceive the people that was once God's with error, and they do this in order to eat them, either simply in receiving presents, or mystically in the slaughter of their souls; and they promise them peace and the heavenly kingdoms, and say, there is no need for you to live continently and holily; hold the faith which we teach, and you will obtain all the promises of the Lord. Speaking such things, they rather stir up the wrath of the Lord against them, and sanctify war against them. Wherefore, O you heretics, who think that you have prophecy and imitate the Church of God, where you suppose there is vision, there will be night, and where you boast of prophetic utterance, there the unclean spirit will speak. For the sun of justice will set upon prophets of this kind, and they will see their own darkness and be confounded. And when they have been proved to be diviners rather than prophets, they will be laughed at over their dreams, and the peoples who had formerly been deceived by them will speak against them. Then the teachers themselves too will repent, and no one will hear them except I, whom they had offended. And because I am merciful, and do not wish the death of the sinner, but desire that he turn back and live, when I have heard them I will give them the power of my spirit, and I will fill them with my judgment and strength, so that those who formerly deceived the people with blandishments may afterwards, by declaring true things, deter them and call them back to the right way, and those who had been the cause of error may begin to heal the wounds which they had inflicted, and become an occasion of health. Notice in the present passage that someone can teach after sin, provided, however, that he has washed away his former vices with worthy repentance. Hence David too, after adultery and homicide, says in the psalm: You will sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed; you will wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow. Nor is he content with his own purity alone, but he adds: Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and strengthen me with a governing spirit. And when you have done this. I will teach, he says, the wicked your ways, and the impious will be turned to you.

3:9-12

Hear these things, O princes of the house of Jacob, and judges of the house of Israel, you who abhor judgment and pervert all that is right, who build Zion with bloodshed, and Jerusalem with iniquity. Her princes judged for gifts, and her priests taught for hire, and her prophets divined for money; and they leaned upon the Lord, saying: Is not the Lord in our midst? No evils will come upon us. Therefore, because of you, Zion will be plowed like a field, and Jerusalem will be like a heap of stones, and the mountain of the temple like the high places of the forests. LXX: Hear these things, O judges of the house of Jacob and the rest of the house of Israel, you who abhor judgment and pervert all that is right; who build Zion with bloodshed, and Jerusalem with iniquities. Her leaders judged for gifts, and her priests gave answers for hire, and her prophets divined for money; and they leaned upon the Lord, saying: Is not the Lord among us? No evils will come upon us. Therefore, on account of you, Zion will be plowed like a field, and Jerusalem will be like a watch-hut for fruit, and the mountain of the Lord like a grove of the forest. No one doubts that Jerusalem was overthrown on account of the crimes which are described in this section; and since earlier the threat had gone before against either the judges or the false prophets: Hear, O princes of the house of Jacob, and leaders of the house of Israel. And after a few little verses: Thus says the Lord concerning the prophets who lead my people astray, and the rest; now the prophetic word is woven generally, both against the judges and against the false prophets, and against the priests, and against those who promised themselves the knowledge of God, and it rebukes them because on account of their crimes Zion is to be plowed like a field, and Jerusalem is to collapse like a heap of stones, and the mountain of the temple is to become like the high places of the forests. We see fulfilled the things that were said; the eyes prove the utterance of the mouth; the sight is the witness of the prophecy. This very testimony is written also in the scroll of Jeremiah, where mention is made of the prophet Micah, and the devastation of Jerusalem is foretold. The judges and princes of the house of Jacob and of the house of Israel, that is, of both the two and the ten tribes, not only did not do judgment, but abhorred it, and perverted all that is right, so that not even a little justice might remain in the city, because they built Zion with bloodshed, and Jerusalem with iniquity, with the plundering of the poor, the slaughter of the innocent, the killing of the saints. And if any of the princes seemed to judge what was right, he rather sold his sentence, and judged for gifts. The priests also did not teach the people unless they had received a price; and whereas it is said to the saints: Freely you have received, freely give, they, bringing forth the answer of God when money had been received, sold the grace of the Lord for avaricious hire. And after these evils, not understanding their sin, as though they were buying back the love of God with crimes, they kept declaring that they were judges and priests and prophets of God, and in accordance with their impenitent heart they said: The Lord is in our midst, and no evils will come upon us. Since, therefore, they did not repent, and the whole people followed the vices of the princes and priests and prophets, Zion will be plowed like a field, and Jerusalem will be like a heap of stones, and the temple, which formerly shone with gold and silver, will collapse into utter ruins. These things are against the people of the Jews, upon whom the true captivity and the final ruins were imposed, on account indeed of their earlier crimes, but chiefly on account of the shedding of the Lord's blood. Hence too Zion has been plowed like a field, and Jerusalem turned into mounds of stones, and that temple, once noble and lofty, has been ground down into the last ashes. But if anyone transfers these things which are said of Jerusalem and of Zion to the Church (for she is the house of Jacob and the house of Israel, according to what is written in Isaiah: Jacob my servant, I will uphold him, and that word to Timothy: That you may know how you ought to conduct yourself in the house of God, which is the Church), he will see clearly that the princes of the house of Jacob, and the rest of the house of Israel, or, as is better written in the Hebrew: judges of the house of Israel, are none other than the bishops, presbyters and deacons, who, unless they have kept their heart with all watchfulness, abhor judgment and pervert all that is right; for when they judge according to the person, and in the hearing of a case it is not the merit of the cause that prevails but the power of the defendants, do they not abhor judgment and pervert all that is right? But it can also be taken in another way: that the princes of the house of Jacob and the judges of the house of Israel abhor and detest judgment, turning aside from the sentence of God the judge, and distorting every rule of the Scriptures with evil interpretations; these build Zion with bloodshed, and Jerusalem with iniquity. For whereas Scripture commands: Do not bring the ungodly man into the tabernacle of the just, and: The man of blood and deceit let the Lord abhor, these, ordaining as clergy any of their hangers-on whatever, and exposing their life as a scandal to the peoples, are guilty of the unbelief of those who are scandalized. Hence it is said that it is better for a man that a millstone turned by an ass be tied around his neck and that he be cast into the deep, than that he should scandalize one of the least of the Church. And although the prophet Malachi calls priests angels, and their mouth is the oracle of the Lord, they do not judge without the taking of gifts, though they read: Gifts blind the eyes even of the wise, and like a bridle in the mouth turn away reproof. And although it is said to the apostles: Do not possess gold and silver, nor let there be copper in your belts; and money, even when earned by labor, is to be shaken out of the hands of a holy man, they sell the words of the Lord and trade in doves in the temple. But the prophets of Jerusalem also divined for money, not knowing that prophecy is one thing and divination another; for divination is never taken in a good sense in the Scriptures. There shall be, he says, no augury in Jacob, nor divination in Israel. They seemed to themselves indeed to be prophets, but because they took money, their prophecy became divination. The apostle Peter: Silver, he says, and gold I have not; he certainly could have sold to Simon Magus what he was asking for, or rather, he could have pretended to be selling it, for the Holy Spirit can be neither sold nor bought; but he condemned the money offered, together with the one offering it. Now you may see that the prophets of Jerusalem do not have prophecy in their mouth, and rest upon the Lord, and say: No evils will come upon us, on whose account God's watchtower is cut by the enemy's plow; and the place once of peace becomes full of ruins, and the temple of the Lord is turned into briars and thorns, and is a dwelling of beasts. Nor let anyone be troubled by what we read in the first book of Kings (1 Samuel): that Saul, wishing to go to Samuel, said to his servant that he could not go to him because he did not have a price to offer for the prophecy, and that the servant answered: Behold, there is found in my hand the fourth part of a shekel of silver, and I will give it to the man of God, and he will tell us our way; for it is not written that Samuel accepted it, or that they offered it to him; on the contrary, they are fed by the prophet and invited to a meal. But suppose that he did accept it: it should be reckoned rather as offerings for the tabernacle than as payment for prophecy. For a shekel contains twenty obols, and the fourth part of a shekel is five obols. So our priests too, if they wish to sell prophecy, and to set out in their chairs the doves which the Lord overturned with a whip, let them accept five obols, not the prices of country estates. The same thing is heard also in the third book of Kingdoms (1 Kings): that the wife of Jeroboam, when her son was sick, goes to Ahijah the man of God and brings him loaves and raisins and a little jar of honey. For it is told what she brought with her, and yet it is not written that the prophet accepted it, since indeed he both rebuked her and foretold the mourning to come; although it may be that those who had been accustomed to go to soothsayers (for there were many diviners and soothsayers in Israel) thought, by a bad custom, the same thing also of the prophets, and wished to offer to holy men what they had been accustomed to offer to diviners, and that Scripture only related what they wished; yet it did not add either that they dared to offer it or that the prophets accepted it. The Apostle Paul: Those who serve the altar, he says, both partake of the altar and live. It is permitted to you, O priest, to live from the altar, not to live in luxury. The mouth of the ox that treads out the grain is not muzzled. We know these things, and yet the Apostle does not abuse this license; and having food and clothing he is content; night and day he labors with his own hands, so as not to be a burden to anyone. And he swears in his epistles that he has conducted himself holily and without avarice in the gospel of Christ, and he asserts this very thing not only of himself but also of his disciples, that he sent no one who either demanded anything from the churches or was willing to accept it. And if in some epistles he gives thanks, and calls the gifts of the senders the benevolence of God, he gathers them not so much for himself as for the poor among the saints who were in Jerusalem. Now the holy poor were those in Jerusalem who first believed in Christ from among the Jews and, cast out by their parents and kinsmen and relations by marriage, had lost both their possessions and all their household goods, which the priests of the temple and the people laid waste. If the poor are such, let them accept. But if, under the pretext of the poor, a few houses are enriched, and we eat gold in glass and earthenware vessels, then either let us change our clothes along with our treasures, or let the poor habit not seek the riches of senators. Little does it profit not to have a little linen cloth about the neck for wiping away sweat. What does it help to be μονοχίτωνας (wearing a single tunic), and to display poverty in our dress, when the whole crowd of the poor sighs for our purse? Therefore, because of us, who are such, who build Zion with blood, and Jerusalem with iniquity, who judge for gifts, who give answers for hire, who divine for money, and who, claiming besides a feigned sanctity for ourselves, say: No evils will come upon us. Now there came to this tower the first power, or the first principality, he who had said: I am alpha and Ω, the beginning and the end, the first and the last. And he who, in the person of the man he had assumed, says in Proverbs: The Lord created me in the beginning of his ways for his works, or, as it is written in the Hebrew: The Lord possessed me: for canani means not he created me, but he possessed me and held me. And though he is the living bread coming down from heaven, he granted to his disciples that they should be called bread. Hence the apostle Paul too says with confidence: For we all are one bread. In this way, then, the first power and the kingdom enters into Jerusalem, so as to make those who believe in her a power and a kingdom. Why now are you drawn together in grief, or why have you known evils? At once it is answered and said: Because there is no king for you, and your counselor has perished, because pain has seized you like a woman in labor. Or surely, though you have all these, by your own fault you do not deserve the help either of the king or of the counselor. As for his saying: Why have you known evils, it is to be taken in this sense: that everyone who deserves and suffers evils is said to know evils and to be ignorant of good things. But there are some who assert that in almost all the testimonies taken from the Old Testament there is an error of this kind, that either the order is changed or the words, and sometimes even the sense itself is different, since the apostles or the evangelists did not pick the testimonies out of a book but trusted to memory, which is sometimes deceived. Ephrathah can sound in our language καρποφόρον, rich and fruitful, and show in a mystery the very same thing as house of bread.

Again the prophet, as he had been commanded, speaks to the mountains, and not only to the mountains but also to the strong foundations of the earth, and says: Hear, O mountains, the judgment of the Lord, and you strong foundations of the earth. And he gives the reason why he urges them to hear: because the judgment of the Lord will be decided with his people, and with Israel. For the mountains to which the prophet speaks, and for the strong foundations of the earth, the LXX translated hills and valleys, understanding, as it seems to me, that the people had done nothing worthy of the hearing of mountains, but only of hills, which are lower than the loftiness of the mountains, or of valleys sunk into the depths. Arise, he says, contend in judgment before the mountains, and let the hills hear your voice. He is commanded to arise who either sits, or lies, or sleeps, or is dead, according to what the Apostle says: Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you. Arise from the dead, so that you may walk in newness of life, so that, leaving the earth, you may strive toward higher things. And contend in judgment against the mountains, by which I think nothing else is signified than the angels, to whom the care of human affairs has been entrusted, the Song in Deuteronomy agreeing on this very point: When the Most High divided the nations, when he scattered the sons of Adam, he set the bounds of the earth according to the number of the angels of God. These are ministering spirits, sent for service on account of those who are to possess the inheritance of salvation. And contend in judgment, so that, whether the mountains or the hills are found not to have cared for the peoples worthily, either it may be seen to be my fault, since I set such ones over them, or the blame may be taken from the people and referred to the princes. Let us read the Apocalypse of John the apostle, in which the angels of the churches are praised and accused for the virtues and vices of those over whom they are said to preside. For just as sometimes it is the bishop's fault, sometimes the people's, and often the teacher is answerable for the sin, often the pupil, and now it is the father's failing, now the son's, according as they are taught well or badly, so in the judgment of God the charge will be referred either to the angels, if they have not done all that belonged to their office, or to the people, if, while the angels did everything, they themselves scorned to hear. There are some who interpret the mountains and hills and strong foundations of the earth as Abraham, Isaac and Jacob and the rest of the patriarchs, before whom, as hearers called together to the judgment, the case of the people of Israel is to be argued. Others think the mountains, hills and valleys are angels, as we said above, who either serve God in the heavenly places, or preside over men upon this earth, or, being set in the underworld, are called the foundations of those who through their own fault became earthly; of these foundations of the earth we find it written elsewhere too: A fire is kindled from my fury, it will burn down to the underworld below; it will devour the earth and its foundations. The strong and ancient foundations of the earth — on account of which the earth has not passed away until now, and, hanging over the void, stands balanced — are the merits of the just, of whom the Apostle speaks: Built upon the foundations of the apostles and prophets. Just as, then, the apostles and the prophets and the whole choir of martyrs are the strong foundations of the earth, so, according to the LXX, the valleys and precipices, which in Greek are more expressively called φάραγγες, are the foundations of those who have received the image of the χοϊκοῦ (of the earthy one). The judgment of the Lord, therefore, will be decided with his people, and with Israel. He who, as God, could inflict punishments for the crimes of a sinful people does not wish to appear powerful, but just, and he summons the sinners to judgment, according to that prophetic word: Come, and let us judge, says the Lord; even now he presses the people of Israel, in the presence of the angels and of all creation, to answer if it has anything to answer, so that God may be justified in his words and may prevail when he is judged.

My people, what have I done to you, and in what have I been troublesome to you, answer me, for I brought you up out of the land of Egypt, and from the house of servants I freed you, and I sent before your face Moses, and Aaron and Miriam. My people, remember, I pray, what Balak king of Moab devised against you, and what Balaam son of Beor answered him, from Shittim to Gilgal, that you might know the justices of the Lord. LXX: My people, what have I done to you, or how have I grieved you, or in what have I been troublesome to you? Answer me: for I brought you up out of the land of Egypt, and from the house of bondage I redeemed you, and I sent before your face Moses, and Aaron and Miriam. My people, remember what Balak king of Moab devised against you, and what Balaam son of Beor answered him, from Schoenis to Gilgal, that the justice of the Lord might be known. For justices or justice Symmachus rendered mercies, and where the LXX has Schoenis, all rendered the Hebrew itself, Settim. Now it is a place in which Balak king of the Moabites gathered an army against Israel, ὁμώνυμος (of the same name) with the trees which even today grow throughout the desert of Mount Sinai. For where in the LXX the ark of the covenant, or the altar and the tabernacle, and the other things which are reported to have been made of incorruptible wood are mentioned, in the Hebrew Settim wood is set down, which has the likeness of the tree that we commonly call the white thorn. Hence I think that the LXX too rendered σχῖνον, that is, the mastic tree, but that gradually, through the error of copyists, it came about that σχοῖνοι, that is, ropes, was read instead of σχίνοις, that is, mastic trees. God, therefore, speaks to the people of Israel, and summons them to judgment, and grants them leave to dispute against him. My people, what have I done to you that I ought not to have done? Or how have I grieved you? although this is not found in the Hebrew. But a father grieves the son he has scourged, and the shepherd visits the iniquities of the sheep with the rod. In what have I been troublesome to you? Or, as is written more expressively in the Hebrew, with what toil have I oppressed you? Or will you interpret my benefits as an insult, and, longing for the melons and the meats of Egypt, will you grieve that you were brought out of the land of Egypt and freed from the house of bondage by my help, because I gave you as leaders Moses my friend, and Aaron my priest, and Miriam my prophetess? But if this seems little to you, remember that time when Balak king of Moab hired Balaam the diviner for a fee against you, and see how, against his own will, the soothsayer, wishing to curse you, blessed you; from Shittim to Gilgal, surveying with his eyes the whole army of Israel and changing places, as if I could not go with him as he went and pass over with him as he passed over; and this I did so that my mercies and justices might be known to you, for I love you so much that, while I am cursed daily by the mouth of blasphemers, I did not allow you to be cursed by the enemy. The Hebrews expound this passage, in which it is said: From Shittim to Gilgal, that you might know the justices of the Lord, thus: From the time when you committed fornication in Midian until the time when Saul was anointed king at Gilgal, recall to memory what evils you have done, and how great the good things I have done for you, and you will know my mercy toward you. Let this be what God spoke to carnal Israel according to the history. But we who long to contemplate the glory of the Lord with unveiled face, and truly have Abraham as our father, let us hear God, when we have sinned, disputing against us and reproving us by the greatness of his benefits. For we once served Pharaoh, and made mud and bricks for the Egyptian people, and he redeemed us who gave himself as a redemption for all, so that we who had been redeemed by the Lord, whom he redeemed from the hand of the enemies and whom he gathered from the regions, might say: For his mercy is forever. He also sent before our face Moses, the spiritual law, and Aaron, the great priest, not bearing the typical ephod and the truth, but having on his forehead the seal of holiness which God the Father sealed. And he sent Miriam, the prophecy of the prophets; and not only did he grant us this, but he also freed us from the hands of the enemies. For let us remember what he devised against us who wished to devour us and lick up our congregation: the true Balak, the devil. For Balak is interpreted ἐκλείχων, that is, licking up, king of the paternal water; since Moab, according to another etymology, is called paternal water. When, therefore, Balak was lying in wait for us, and lying in wait through his vain people, which is what Balaam is interpreted, God did not allow us to lie under his curses, but on the contrary there blessed us, compelled by the truth of the thing itself, the vain people of the nations, born of him who is in the skin — for Beor is interpreted in the skin — ever given over to the flesh and to the works of death; and the vain people, sprung from him who is wholly in the skin, answered on our behalf, always changing places, or standing upon thorns, or upon cords, so that we may follow the error of the common edition too. Now thorns, according to the Savior's words, are the cares of this world, and riches, and pleasures, in which the vain people is. And that it stands in ropes, namely in the bonds of sins — for with the ropes of his own sins each one is bound — Isaiah also is witness, saying: Woe to those who drag out their sins like a long rope, and iniquities like the strap of a heifer's yoke. If, then, it stands, it stands only on thorns and on ropes; but if it wishes to go around, it has no steady step, but, always wavering and tottering, it comes as far as Gilgal, which is interpreted κυλισμὸς, that is, rolling, or a wallowing-place. Whenever we see some rising up against us, and thirsting for our blood with greedy jaws, and, by an unforeseen dispensation of God, those who had come against us coming over to our side, let us say: Balaam came from the cords as far as Gilgal, that the justice of God may be known.

And afterwards answers: I will take the cup of salvation, and I will call upon the name of the Lord. Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints. Yet even this very blood we do not give, but give back. And what is there alike? When the Just One has died for sinners, the Son of God for men, shall we, sinners and men, die for the confession of his name?

LXX: It has been declared to you, man, what is good, or what the Lord requires of you, except that you do judgment, and love mercy, and be ready to walk with your God. Since you are in doubt, O people of Israel — or rather the whole human race, for I am by no means speaking to the people of the Jews, but my speech will run out generally to every man — how you can appease God for your sins, if you do not even have victims by which your impiety may be compensated, I will answer you what God requires; indeed, I have already shown it before in the law. The Lord requires of us, and, counting our salvation necessary, asks to receive what profits the giver: that we do judgment, that is, that we do nothing without reason and counsel, so that the mind first judges what it is going to do and afterwards completes it in deed; that we love mercy, and are merciful not as if compelled or out of necessity, since God loves a cheerful giver. The word esne, which the LXX translated 'to be ready', and we 'to walk carefully', Theodotion expressed more significantly: καὶ ἀσφαλίζου τοῦ πορεύεσθαι μετὰ Ἐλωαὶχ, that is: And take careful heed that you walk with your God, or, as the Fifth Edition translated: καὶ φροντίζειν, namely to act carefully, and to have this care, that you walk with your God. For he who says that he believes in Christ ought so to walk as he also walked. And the apostle Paul: Be imitators of me, he says, as I am of Christ.

In this section the LXX disagree greatly with the Hebrew truth, and most of all at the beginning: Hear, O tribe, and who will adorn the city? And the statutes of my people shall be scattered. In place of which we have put, for the sake of the coherence of the discourse: And you have kept the precepts of Omri, although in the Hebrew too it is written: And the precepts of Omri have been kept, and all the works of the house of Ahab. For if Ammi had been written in the Hebrew, the LXX would rightly have translated it 'of my people'; but as it is, since Amri is written, with the letter res added, it denotes not the name of the people but the father of Ahab, of whom the history of Kings relates, and no one is in doubt that it is an error. In short, after the father's name the son is placed, and it is said: And all the works of the house of Ahab. Let us therefore return to the beginning of the section. And, discussing it first according to the letter, let us, with the help of your prayers, strive to attain the spiritual sense. Hear, ten tribes of Samaria, what the Lord solemnly declares to you: Still there is fire, that is, iniquity, in the house of the ungodly Omri, and the treasures of ungodliness persist in the royal house. Do you wish to hear, one by one, the evils of which your city is full? Learn: a scant measure that provokes the wrath of God, a deceitful balance and differing weights, and men who sell their wares by one weight and buy by another. And if indeed the poor were doing this, their want could have defended the crime by necessity. But as it is, her rich men are full not so much of riches as of iniquity, because all riches, while they despoil others, are begotten by iniquity. Lying follows the amassing of riches, and the hand accustomed to storing up treasures possesses a deceitful tongue. Truth begets poverty, lying begets riches. When your princes were doing this, I did not wish to overthrow you at once, but little by little I began to strike you and to warn you with various blows. I sent famine upon you, I sent thirst, I sent diseases, and devastation by the enemy round about; the crop did not bring forth grain, the pressed olive did not yield oil, barren vintages denied the wine. Against the iniquities, and the deceitful measures, and the fraudulent weights, I brought these punishments. But because you kept all the ceremonies of idolatry which Omri, the ungodly king, had established, and observed every work of the house of Ahab and Jezebel in place of my law, I was driven by your crime to give you and your inhabitants up to hissing, and that you should bear the reproach of my people, while, taken captive by the Assyrians, you are conquered as if you were the people of God, and on your account my name is blasphemed among the nations. It should be noted in the present passage that where we read: And you shall bear the reproach of my people, or, as the LXX translated: You shall receive the reproaches of the peoples, Ammi is written in the Hebrew for 'of my people'. If, then, Ammi means 'of my people', no one doubts that above, for Amri, 'my people' was wrongly translated. Thus far we have set out what seemed right to us according to the Hebrew; now, returning to the translation of the LXX translators, let us discuss each point as best we can. The Samaritan tribe, which cut itself off from the people of God, is called to hear. And it is told: In vain you fashion idols, you put together golden calves with a craftsman's hand, and in imitation of Jerusalem you wish to build another metropolis; for who will be able to adorn the city? Can fire, which is kindled by the flaming darts of the devil, and the house of the wicked man, which according to its hardness and impenitent heart treasures up for itself wrath on the day of wrath, and of the revelation of the just judgment of God? And injury increases iniquity, so that it not only plunders from the house of God, which is the Church, but also lays waste what belongs to others with pride and haughtiness. Can he be justified who, without balance and weight, fraudulently gathers his riches from the testimonies of the Scriptures, riches which are treasures of iniquity? For although the Lord commands: There shall not be in your bag a great weight and a lesser, these men, for the sake of shameful gain, always respect persons in judgment, and in the same case judge rich and poor now one way, now another, not according to the matter but according to the difference of their means; and the inhabitants of their city, which they think they are adorning with depraved doctrines and perverse teaching, speak lies, and set their mouth on high, and despise the simplicity of the people of the Church. Hence the most merciful God does not strike them all at once, but strives little by little to admonish them through blows, saying: And I will begin to strike you with ruin on account of your sins; and the sense is: O city which the heretics wish to build, I will strike you so that you perish, not unto abolition, but in so far as you are a sinner. There follows: You shall eat, and not be satisfied. For they read and do not understand; and feeding on the words of the Scriptures, they suffer a want of truth. And I will cast out, he says, you; and you shall lay hold, and shall not save; and whoever shall be saved shall be handed over to the sword. I will abandon you, he says, to your own judgment; and after you have sought many things and found nothing, understanding your error, you will see that you cannot be saved by all your doctrines. But whoever think themselves satisfied, and have not been cast out from themselves, nor have grasped the truth, will be handed over to the sword, and will be instructed by punishments. You shall sow, therefore, O tribe, and O most wicked city, which the heretics build with fire, and iniquity, and insults, and a deceitful balance, and a fraudulent bag; you shall sow, and not reap; you shall press the olive, and not be anointed with oil; and the grapes, and not drink wine. Indeed it profits you, once your error is recognized, that you may not have disciples, that you may not anoint your head with the oil of sinners, that you may not make drunk with the wine of Sodom those who drink it. And the statutes of my people, or of Omri, shall be destroyed, and all the works of the house of Ahab, of those who stood forth as patriarchs and princes in the heresies. These we can refer either to the contrary powers or to the heresiarchs, such as were Marcion and Basilides, and recently Arius and Eunomius. And you walked in their wills, namely those of Omri and Ahab. And he says well, in their wills. For the teaching of wicked masters is not the teaching of God, but the inventions of their heart. And I will hand you over to ruin, so that you perish in so far as you are heretical. And your inhabitants to hissing, either so that you may follow the hissing of the good shepherd, according to Zechariah, who says: I will hiss for them and gather them, because I have redeemed them. Or surely to the hissing of the dragon, that is, to the destruction of the flesh, that the spirit may be saved, and that, being corrected, they may learn not to blaspheme. But how the name of Omri and Ahab, princes of Samaria, can be referred to Jerusalem and Judah, under whose names the Church is interpreted, I do not well understand.

This according to the Hebrew. Moreover, according to the Seventy, who disagree in certain points and at the end of the section translated altogether differently, the sense seems to me to be of this kind. The prophetic word, or the apostolic, laments over the human race in general, that the word has cast the seed of doctrines in vain, and that, instead of crops and grain, the late reaper has found scarcely empty stubble and hollow stalks, and has not been able to find even small clusters in the vineyard, and the rest up to the end of the section. For if he is blessed who speaks into the ears of one who listens, and the ear of the hearer is the desire of the wise man, and an understanding hearer is the joy of the speaker, on the contrary a bad pupil is the teacher's grief; and the words of Jeremiah also agree with this complaint: I have not profited, nor has anyone profited me. There are some who think this is said in the person of the Savior, complaining that in so great a multitude of believers, and in the whole world of the human race, he scarcely finds works worthy of his blood, he who also says in Psalm 29 (30): What profit is there in my blood, while I go down into corruption? Others, however, assert that it by no means befits his person to say: Woe is me, for I have become like one who gathers stubble in the harvest, he who said in the Gospel: Lift up your eyes and see the regions, for they are already white for reaping. And elsewhere: The harvest indeed is great, but the laborers are few. Those, then, who wish it to be understood in the person of the Savior say that it is no wonder if he says: Woe is me, he who both wept over Jerusalem and shed tears at the death of Lazarus. But that too, I have become like one who gathers stubble in the harvest, they refer to the consummation of the age, which they also meaningfully interpret as the harvest; and they say that this prophecy can be fulfilled at that time when, iniquity having multiplied, the love of many will have grown cold, and the Son of Man, when he comes, will find faith rare upon the earth; for then, like stubble after the harvest and like clusters after the vintage, scarcely will any be found among them who have kept the faith amid the devastation of all; and they think that this utterance, spoken in the person of the man who was assumed, is confirmed by what follows: Woe is me, O soul, the soul of which he was speaking: My soul is sorrowful, even unto death. The one returning has perished from the land, either because the Antichrist kills the saints, or because all fall away at the greatness of the stumbling-blocks. And there is none among men to correct; all are judged in blood, not in light and small sins, but in the greatest and in those that concern blood. Not neighborliness, not friendship, not kinship will hold back the crime; all will raise their hands to evil, so that even he who has not been able to do evil nevertheless sins by his will while he prepares his hands. The prince himself asks, and the judge speaks peaceful words; for he receives gifts, the desire of his soul. Since this is plain, and I am wary of the ill will of princes and judges, I leave it to the reader's understanding and will add only this: Gifts blind the eyes even of the wise; they also give life to a soul to which they ought not to have given life, and kill the soul that lives by its own merit and virtues, and they do this for the sake of gifts which they shamelessly demand and more shamefully receive. Theophrastus wrote three volumes on friendship, preferring it to every other love, and yet he testified that it is rare in human affairs. There is also Cicero's book on friendship, which he entitled Laelius, in which that precept of our own writers, That a friend should be to us like old wine, and that we should drink it in sweetness, is set down in almost the same words. Friendship either finds equals or makes them; where there is inequality, the eminence of one and the subjection of the other, there is not so much friendship as flattery. For it sometimes happens that one who persevered with us in one temptation is overcome by others and withdraws. Secondly it is commanded: Nor hope in leaders: Cursed indeed is the man who puts his hope in man. Hope in man is vain, and true hope is in God. Let us honor the bishop, defer to the presbyter, rise for the deacon, and yet let us not hope in them, for hope in man is vain, and sure hope is in the Lord. For man was not created for the sake of woman, but woman for the sake of man, and: The wife, he says, is to fear her husband. It belongs to the wife to fear, and to love her husband with fear. The Greek and Latin histories are full of how many men were deceived by their wives and had their lives betrayed. From the Scriptures, moreover, the examples both of Delilah, whom we mentioned above, and of another woman before Delilah bear witness, who wrung from Samson his secret of seven days by her tears, and by feigned love discovered what lay hidden. Do not believe friends, and leaders, and wives, who can change and are only for a time, since even son and daughter, forgetting their upbringing and their infancy, rise up against the authors of their life and of their bodies, and do insult to those whom it is a crime to hurt even by a look. These things having thus been set forth, if indeed we have been able to reach the sense of the Scriptures, the holy man, understanding that love has grown cold, and that at the consummation of the world men are not lovers of God but lovers of themselves, while others believe in friends and leaders and wives, and son and daughter and daughter-in-law rise up against father and mother and mother-in-law, and a man's enemies are his own household, himself believes in the Lord, and all his contemplation is in his God; and although he is pressed by the tribulations and pressures of the world, yet, having confidence in no one except him who says: Do not fear, I have overcome the world, he waits for God his Savior, and believing in him, and ever directing his eyes towards him, he hopes that he will be heard as often as he calls upon him.

It seems to me that every soul, a Jerusalem in which the temple of the Lord was built, and the vision of peace, and the knowledge of the Scriptures, and which was afterwards overcome by sins, led into captivity and handed over to torments, speaks against Babylon, that is, the confusion of this world, and against the opposing power that presides over this world: Do not insult me, my enemy, because I have fallen, and I shall rise again; The Lord indeed lifts up those who are dashed down, and he speaks through the prophet: Shall he who falls not rise again? Moreover, these peoples and these sheep will be struck, and will be shepherded with the rod, for this reason, that they had dwelt alone in the forest. This we can indeed take also of those who, separating themselves from the Church, give themselves up to the banquets and friendships of the Gentiles, and also of those who out of hatred of the human race seek a solitary life, such as we read that Timon was at Athens; not that the solitary and prophetic life which Elijah and John had is to be condemned, but because, if someone, despising the rest, is himself proud and dwells in the forest of vices, he must be corrected with the rod. He who dwells alone and does not dwell in the forest is in the praise of virtues; but he who is alone and does not do the works of justice, and enjoys only the pleasure of quiet, and does not toil in the work and labor of Christ, nor seek his food with his own hands, as the Apostle commands, and is lifted up into pride, he dwells in the forest and moves among the fruitless trees. But yet, because he is the good shepherd, and his rod strikes for this purpose, to amend, the prophetic word promises better things, and says: In the midst of Carmel they shall feed on Bashan and Gilead, according to the days of eternity, and according to the days of your going out from Egypt. Carmel means knowledge of circumcision, Bashan confusion, and Gilead transmigration of testimony. Therefore the people of God, and the sheep of his pasture, which before were fed apart from the Lord's flock and outside his Church, and moved about in the forest of errors, will afterwards be transferred to the knowledge of the true circumcision, and will serve God in the spirit, and will glory in the Lord, and will not trust in the flesh, and will be the true circumcision, and not the concision. And when they have been fed with the spiritual circumcision, understanding their former sins, they will be confounded in their vices, and will blush, and will be in the confusion that leads to life; for there is also another confusion that leads to death, in which Og, king of Bashan, once dwelt, since Bashan means confusion; and from this worst confusion the Lord also promises that he will free his people: The Lord said: From Bashan I will turn back, I will turn back from the depths of the sea. And when we have known the true circumcision, and have been confounded over our sins, then we shall be in Gilead, which means transmigration of testimony, in the Church of Christ, into which the testimonies of the law and the utterances of the prophets have passed over; and this will be done for us according to the days of old, according to the days when we went out from the land of Egypt, of which Moses says: Remember the days of eternity, not the days of this age, which are called evil, but the everlasting days. And he remembers the days of eternity who does not look at present things, and has risen with Christ, and sits with him in the heavenly places, already anticipating in mind that he has been freed from the days of the present age. The divine word also promises that he will show wonders to his people and to the sheep of his inheritance: Then, he says, the nations shall see, and shall be confounded in all their strength, because they had once laid waste and prevailed against the people of God; and their confusion will bring them progress, when they have understood their own evils. For they will put their hands upon their mouth, and their evil works will take from them all freedom of speaking. But just as the hands of the impious nations close their mouths, so the hands of the just will open their mouths, as they receive from good work the faculty of speaking with God. Their ears too will grow deaf, because malice has not only blinded the gaze of the eyes but has also made the ears grow deaf; for they would not hear the voice of the charmers, and of the sorcerer who charms wisely. And according to Isaiah: With their ears they heard heavily, although it is much less to hear heavily than not to hear at all, and to become deaf so as not to hear the word of truth. After such great evils it is said of them that they will lick the ground like serpents that drag the earth, walking on their belly and eating earth all the days of their life. And doing the works of the flesh, that is, earthly works, and dragging with them, up to the day of the vengeance and visitation of the Lord, not dust, not small traces of earth, but the whole ground. And when they have done this, and have come to the judgment of God, and have been shut in and troubled, they will be troubled and shut in for as long as the earth which they had dragged like serpents remains in them. But when that has failed, they will be astonished and will marvel, not at the Lord their God — for they will not yet deserve that he be called the Lord their God —, but at the Lord our God. And suddenly there is an ἀποστροφὴ (a turning of address) to Christ, and it is said to him: And they shall be in fear of you. For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord. And these things will happen so that the nations may see, and be confounded in all their evil strength, and put their hands upon their mouth, and grow deaf in their ears, and lick the earth like serpents that drag the ground, so that first they may be shut in, and, once shut in, be troubled, then, terrified, be astonished before the Lord God of the saints, and at last they too may fear him. This is according to the LXX. Moreover, since our edition, at least in the present passage, does not differ much from them, let us consider that what has been said on theirs has been said on this also.

7:18-20

Who is a God like you? You who take away iniquity, and pass over the sin of the remnant of your inheritance. He will not send his fury any more, because he is one who wills mercy. He will return and have mercy on us; he will put away our iniquities, and will cast all our sins into the depth of the sea. You will give truth in Jacob, mercy to Abraham, which you swore to our fathers from the days of old. LXX: Who, O God, is like you? Taking away iniquities, and passing over injustices for those who are left of your inheritance? He did not hold his anger as a testimony, because he is one who wills mercies. He will return, and will have mercy on us, he will sink our sins; and all our sins shall be cast into the depth of the sea. He will give truth to Jacob, mercy to Abraham, as you swore to our fathers according to the former days. The prophet, understanding that the multitude of the nations was troubled in its enclosure for this reason, that it might be astonished and fear God, and that the Lord rages for this reason, that he may take away sins and give salvation, praises the Lord and marvels, and says: Who is a God like you? Taking away iniquities, and passing over injustices, so that, just as the destroyer in Egypt passed over the people of Israel and did not lay it waste — and was it not from this passing-over that the phasa, that is, the pascha, received its name? — so you may spare the nations, in no way imputing to them their iniquities. Moreover, what follows, For those who are left of his inheritance, he did not hold his anger as a testimony, has this sense: if he spared the nations, which would not believe his law, and would not impute their iniquities to those who were left over and left remaining of the people, nor brought his anger as a testimony of their just punishment, what will he do with his own flock, which feeds in the midst of Carmel, and in Bashan, and in Gilead? For he is one who wills mercy, and returning he will have mercy on us; and our heavy sins, and the iniquities that sit upon the talent of lead, he himself will carry, and will sink in the sea, and will not let them appear. He will give truth to Jacob, and mercy to Abraham, so that to the people that is a supplanter, and new, and always set in wrestling, he may render his truth in Christ, and to the multitude of the nations — for Abraham is called the father of many nations — he may grant mercy, as he swore to those who were the fathers of our faith according to the days of old, that out of the whole multitude of men he would save some in truth, others in mercy. Moreover, where we translated, He will not send his fury any more, for "any more" Symmachus rendered "forever", Theodotion "to the end", the Seventy and the Fifth Edition "as a testimony", for which laed is set in the Hebrew; and it can be understood as "any more" as well as "forever" and "testimony". I too will say at the end of the volume, sealing the labor of my little work with an invocation of the Lord: God, who is like you? Take away the iniquity of your servant; pass over the sin of the remnant of my soul; do not send your fury upon me, nor rebuke me in your anger, since you are merciful, and your mercies are many. Return, and have mercy on me; hurl down my iniquities, and cast them into the depth of the sea, so that the saltiness and bitterness of vices may perish in a false region. Give the truth that you promised to your friend Jacob, and the mercy that you promised to your friend Abraham, and free my soul from the persecutors of your prophets, Ahab and Jezebel, as you swore to my fathers in the days of old, saying: As I live, says the Lord. I do not wish the death of the sinner, only that he turn back and live, and elsewhere: As soon as you have turned and groaned, you will be saved. Then my enemy will see, and will be covered with confusion, she who now says to me: Where is the Lord your God? I will see your vengeance on her, and she will be like the mud of the streets, and will be trodden down, so that she may no longer build Egyptian cities out of mud and straw.
