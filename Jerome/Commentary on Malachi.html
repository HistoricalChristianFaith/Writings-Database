Commentary on Malachi

Latin source: Jerome's commentary on Malachi, in one book addressed to Minervius and Alexander (written at Bethlehem about 406, the last of his commentaries on the twelve prophets), from the critical text of M. Adriaen, Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 76A (Turnhout, 1970), pp. 901–942, verified against the page images, with the edition's few evident misprints corrected. Each lemma gives Malachi in Jerome's own version from the Hebrew and, in most places, then after "LXX:" in his Latin of the Septuagint; all Scripture is translated as Jerome quotes it, from his own Latin. Italics mark the lemmata and the other Scripture he quotes. Headings follow the Vulgate chapter numbering, as in English Bibles (Hebrew Bibles count Malachi 4:1–6 as 3:19–24). Psalms keep the Latin (LXX) numbering, with the Hebrew number in parentheses. Greek words are kept as printed, with an English gloss in parentheses where Jerome gives none. This English translation is released into the public domain.

Prologue

We wish to interpret Malachi, the last of the twelve prophets, whose name the LXX translated "his angel", saying: The taking up of the word of the Lord upon Israel in the hand of his angel, in place of which we read in the Hebrew Malachi, which is more correctly and expressly rendered "angel", that is, "my messenger". Nor is it to be thought, according to the opinion of some, that an angel came from heaven and took up a human body, in order to speak to Israel the things that had been commanded by the Lord. For if names are to be interpreted, and if from names not a spiritual understanding but the order of the history is to be woven, then both Hosea, who is called savior, and Joel, who is interpreted the Lord God, or beginning, and the rest of the prophets will not be men, but either angels or the Lord and Savior, because this is what their names sound. In short, apart from the Seventy, the others, interpreting the name Malachi, translated it as it is read in the Hebrew. Now the Hebrews consider Malachi to be Ezra the priest, because all the things that are contained in his book this prophet also recalls, saying: The lips of the priest shall guard knowledge, and they shall seek the law from his mouth, because he is the angel of the Lord of hosts. The time also and the title agree, as we have said in the psalms too, that those which have no titles are to be believed to belong to those by whose names the preceding psalms are headed. Therefore Malachi too, that is, Ezra, must be believed to have lived after Haggai and Zechariah, who prophesied under Darius. And for this reason he has no title, because his book stands in place of a title; in it we learn that in the reign of Artaxerxes king of the Persians, Ezra son of Seraiah, and of the others, up to the place where it is said: son of Phinehas, son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the priest from the beginning, went up from Babylon, and the king gave him, according to the hand of the Lord his God, all his request; and there went up with him some of the sons of Israel, and of the sons of the priests, and of the sons of the Levites, and of the singers, and of the doorkeepers, and of the Nethinim to Jerusalem, in the seventh year of King Artaxerxes, and they came to Jerusalem in the fifth month; this is the seventh year of the king, because on the first day of the first month he began to go up from Babylon, and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem. These things, my Minervius and Alexander, you who are one not so much in blood as in religion, I have said briefly in the Preface, so as not to abandon the work on the prophets that I had undertaken and toil for you at an extraordinary labor, so that, setting out the words of Malachi first according to the Hebrew truth, then according to the LXX translators, and upheld by the help of your prayers, I may expound what follows. Origen wrote three volumes on this book, but he did not touch the history at all, and, after his custom, he was wholly occupied in the interpretation of allegory, making no mention of Ezra, but thinking that it was an angel who wrote, according to what we read of John: Behold, I send my angel before your face. This we do not accept at all, lest we be compelled to admit falls of souls from heaven. I am not aware of having read other commentaries on this prophet, except a short booklet of Apollinaris, of which one should speak not so much of an interpretation as of the bare points of an interpretation.

1:1

The burden of the word of the Lord to Israel, in the hand of Malachi. LXX: The taking up of the word of the Lord upon Israel, in the hand of his angel. What burden signifies, that is, weight, which in Hebrew is called massa and by Aquila ἄρμα, or what λῆμμα signifies, that is, taking up, which both the Seventy and the other translators rendered, we have said in other prophets. For Nahum too writes: The burden of Nineveh, the book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite, and Habakkuk: The burden which Habakkuk the prophet saw, and Zechariah: The burden of the word of the Lord in the land of Hadrach, and of Damascus his rest. And again in what follows: The burden of the word of the Lord upon Israel. Let us therefore be content with that explanation, and now say only this, that the weight of the word of the Lord to Israel, or, as the LXX say, upon Israel, is indeed heavy, because it is called a weight, but has something of consolation, because it is taken up not against Israel but to Israel. For it is one thing when, for example, we write to this man or that, another when we write against this man and that, because in the one there is a share of friendship, in the other an open avowal of enmities. And it must be known that once Israel, that is, the ten tribes, had been led away into captivity, the two tribes too, Judah and Benjamin, are called Israel indifferently by the former name. But as for what is said: In the hand of his angel, or of Malachi, take the hand for works. Hence the word of God came both in the hand of Haggai, and in the hand of Jeremiah, and in the hand of Moses. For in those in whose hands is iniquity, and whose right hand is filled with gifts, and whose hands are full of blood, the word of God does not come, but in those who wash their hands among the innocent. With these waters Pilate too tried to wash his hands, so as not to consent to the blasphemies of the Jews; over them the psalmist rejoices, saying: Upon the waters of refreshment he has brought me up. Of this water the Lord promises through the prophet: I will sprinkle you with most clean water. But he who is a sinner is made drunk with the Babylonian cup, and it is said of him: Thorns spring up in the hand of the drunkard.

LXX: Set it upon your hearts. This is not found in the Hebrew, but I think it was added from Haggai, in whom we read: And now set it upon your hearts from this day and upward. After the title of the prophet, then, or the preface, this is to be taken in two ways: Set it upon your hearts, that is, take note of and consider that which was said above: The taking up of the word of the Lord upon Israel in the hand of his angel. Or else: take careful note of the things that are to be said afterward, so that you may know them not with the ears of the body but with the understanding of the mind and heart, and may make for yourselves treasuries in which to receive the riches of the words of God, and wisdom may act with confidence when you have been enlarged, and, your heart filled with the words of God, you may drive out the most wicked thoughts, the murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts and the rest that come forth from the heart, and may fulfill what was said by the Savior: He who has ears to hear, let him hear.

1:2-5

I have loved you, says the Lord. And you have said: In what have you loved us? Was not Esau the brother of Jacob, says the Lord, and I have loved Jacob, but Esau I have hated? And I have made his mountains a wilderness, and his inheritance a place for the dragons of the desert. But if Idumea should say: We are destroyed, but returning we will build the things that are destroyed; thus says the Lord of hosts: They will build, and I will destroy; and they shall be called the borders of impiety, and the people with whom the Lord is angry for ever. And your eyes shall see, and you shall say: May the Lord be magnified upon the border of Israel. LXX: I have loved you, says the Lord, and you have said: In what have you loved us? Was not Esau the brother of Jacob, says the Lord; and I have loved Jacob, but Esau I have hated; and I have made his borders a desolation, and his inheritance the dwellings of the desert. For Idumea will say: It is destroyed; let us return and rebuild the deserted places. Thus says the Lord Almighty: They will build, and I will destroy, and they shall be called, for them, the borders of iniquity, and the people against whom the Lord has been prepared for ever. And your eyes shall see, and you shall say: The Lord has been magnified upon the borders of Israel. Israel, that is, Judah, to whom the word of God and the vision of the Lord used to come, is compelled to carry his burden and the heaviest weight of punishments, so that it may lay down its heavier sins and feel through torments what it did not feel through benefits. And lest the punishment of his own should seem unjust, the Lord adds: I have loved you. For whom the Lord loves he rebukes: and he chastises every son whom he receives. And by saying I have loved, he denies the present while he confesses the past. And they, forgetful of his benefits, answer with the same rashness with which they sin: In what have you loved us? To which the Lord says: To pass over everything else, and the fact that you have lately come from the Babylonian captivity, I will deal with your cradles: before you were born, nay rather before Rebekah poured forth Esau and Jacob from her womb, in Jacob I loved you, in Esau I hated the Idumeans. The apostle Paul, sifting this passage in a mystical disputation, writes to the Romans, joining together two testimonies, from Genesis and from Malachi: But Rebekah too, having conceived by one act of intercourse with Isaac our father. For when they were not yet born, nor had done anything good or evil, so that the purpose of God according to election might stand, not from works but from him who calls, it was said to her: The elder shall serve the younger; as it is written: Jacob I have loved, but Esau I have hated. For what is said: As it is written, refers both to the book of Genesis and to the prophet Malachi. Not only, he says, did I love Jacob before he was born, and hate Esau before he was poured forth from his mother's womb, but I have kept my love and my hatred toward their posterity: my hatred toward Esau, whose mountains, which are called Seir, I have reduced to a wilderness, and whose cities I have made to be deserted and to be held by serpents and beasts. But if Edom, that is, Esau, should say: we have indeed been destroyed, unto the wrath of God, but we will build the cities again, know this from the Lord's foretelling, that as they build, I will destroy, and their everlasting desolation will prove my wrath. So I have shown my hatred toward Esau in deeds, but my love toward you, that is, toward Jacob, I will prove by what follows. When they have been destroyed and reduced to a wilderness, your eyes will see, and you will say: May the Lord be magnified upon the border of Israel; and from the comparison with the evils your brother suffers, you will feel the benefits of God toward you. The Jews falsely flatter themselves that the Romans are prophesied as Edom, and they themselves as Israel, at the consummation of the world, on the ground that once the Roman empire, that is, the Idumean, has been destroyed, the kingdom of the world will come to the Jews. These things we have said as well as we could, laying the foundations of the history. Now let us come to the spiritual understanding. Israel, a man or a mind seeing God, or, as I think better, εὐθύτατος Θεοῦ, that is, most upright of God, is loved by the Lord, and wishes to know the reason for his love toward him. And the Lord answered that Esau and Jacob were begotten of one stock, that is, that vices and virtues proceed from the one fountain of the heart, since by the freedom of choice we incline to either side as we will; but the vices are born first, through infancy, childhood and youth, and afterward a firmer age reproves and supplants them. The elder brother is hairy and bloody with his hunting, and delights in woods and beasts. The younger is smooth and simple, and dwells innocently at home. God makes the borders of Idumea, that is, of the earthly and bloody, a wilderness, and allows nothing to grow from the earth and to endure. But if shameless malice should strive to build again the things that have been destroyed by the word of God, the Lord declares himself the adversary of the things that are restored by the vices. And after everything within the hostile borders has been overthrown, then we can see the eyes of Israel, and all the saints can say: May the Lord be magnified in the borders of those who behold God with the mind. Moreover, the love and the hatred of God arise either from foreknowledge of what is to come, or from works; otherwise we know that God loves all things, and hates nothing of what he has created, but properly claims for his love those who are enemies of the vices and rebels against them. And on the contrary he hates those who long to build up again what has been destroyed by God. Now God is said to hate ἀνθρωποπάθως (in human fashion), as he is said to weep, to grieve, to be angry, so that when we hear of his hatred toward the wicked, we may flee the things that we understand God to hate.

1:6-7

A son honors his father, and a servant his lord; if then I am a father, where is my honor? And if I am a lord, where is my fear? says the Lord of hosts. LXX: A son glorifies his father, and a servant will fear his lord. And if I am a father, where is my glory? And if I am a lord, where is my fear? says the Lord Almighty. Although before you were born I began to love you as sons in Jacob, still, choose by which name you will call me: either I am your father, or your lord. If a father, render the honor due to a father, and offer a devotion worthy of a parent. If a lord, why do you despise me? Why do you not fear your lord? Now he is speaking to those who returned from the Babylonian captivity under Zerubbabel and Joshua son of Jehozadak, and Ezra the priest, and Nehemiah, and who, though the altar had been built, had not yet built the temple nor raised the walls of the city, and nonetheless remained in their former sins, venerating God neither with love nor with fear. Now what we have called glory, or honor, is one word both among the Greeks, δόξα, and among the Hebrews, chabod, but we have put honor in keeping with the proper idiom of the Latin language. For in the Gospel too, where the Lord and Savior says: Father, the hour has come; make your Son illustrious, that your Son may make you illustrious, among the Greeks δόξασον is read, that is, glorify, a passage which most of the Latins have translated honor. And at the same time let us consider that in the holy Scriptures one becomes a son or a servant by will, not by necessity of nature. For whoever has received the spirit of adoption is turned into a son of God; but whoever has received the spirit of servitude unto fear is made a servant of God. So God wishes first that we be his sons, and do good by our will; if we wish to attain this, that he may at least have us as servants, and that we may withdraw from evil through dread of punishments. We read of sons in a bad sense: We were sons of wrath, and sons of gehenna, whom the Pharisees, having gone round sea and land, begot worthy of torments. And Judas the traitor is called the son of perdition, and in Psalm 88 (89) it is written of the Lord: The son of iniquity shall not go on to harm him. In Hosea too those are called sons of fornication who were begotten of a harlot mother, of whom it is written: Your mother has committed fornication. And in the Gospel the Jews are branded as sons of the devil: You are born of your father the devil, and you wish to do the desires of your father. We read of sons in a good sense, as sons of God: As many as received him, he gave them power to become sons of God. And sons of wisdom, as the Gospel proclaims: Wisdom has been justified by her sons. And sons of Abraham: Amen, amen, I say to you, God is able from these stones to raise up sons of Abraham. And sons of the Apostle: My little sons, with whom I am in labor again until Christ be formed in you; and many things of this kind. A son therefore honors, or makes illustrious, his father, according to what is written: So let your light shine before men, that they may see your works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven. A servant too honors his lord, not with the same love as a son, but it is understood ἀπὸ κοινοῦ (from the shared word): a son honors his father, a servant his lord; and yet God Almighty, knowing the difference between a son and a servant, seeks glory from the son and fear from the servant: For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, so that from the fear of servants we may pass over to the glory of sons.

To you, O priests, who despise my name, and you have said: In what have we despised your name?

1:7

You offer polluted bread upon my altar, and you say: In what have we polluted you? In that you say: The table of the Lord is despised. LXX: You, O priests, who despise my name, and have said: In what have we despised your name? Offering polluted loaves at my altar; and you have said: In what have we polluted them? In that you say: The table of the Lord is despised. But what follows: And the things set upon it you have despised, we have marked with an obelus, because it is not found in the Hebrew, and was added from what follows. To you, then, O priests, who despise my name, is this word directed, you who, having returned from Babylon, ought, from fear of your past servitude, to have turned to the Lord with your whole mind; and not only do you not do this, but, imitating Cain and answering God with proud words, you question him whom hidden things do not escape, and you say: In what have we despised your name? so that by the shamelessness of your pretence you may cover the wound of your conscience. Do you wish, then, to know in what you have despised my name? You offer polluted bread upon my altar, namely the showbread, which, according to the Hebrew traditions, you yourselves were bound to sow, yourselves to reap, yourselves to grind, yourselves to bake; and now you take any loaves at all from the common stock, and answer with rash voice and say: In what have we polluted them, or you? For when the mysteries are violated, he himself whose mysteries they are is violated. But what follows: In that you say: The table of the Lord is despised, we can also interpret thus: that having returned from Babylon, with the temple not yet built, and living in huts and in the ruins of the ancient city, they had built only an altar, not of the same glory as the former one had been, and they thought that the sanctity of religion was lacking because the grandeur of the building was lacking. We have drawn faint lines, on which the spiritual explanation is to be impressed. The divine word rebukes negligent bishops, and presbyters and deacons, or else, since we are a priestly and royal race, all who, baptized in Christ, are reckoned by the name of Christ, as to why they despise the name of God; and when they ask in what they have despised his name, it shows them the causes of the offence. You offer, he says, polluted bread upon my altar. We pollute the bread, that is, the body of Christ, when we approach the altar unworthy and, filthy as we are, drink the clean blood, and we say: The table of the Lord is despised; not that anyone would dare to say this, and to utter with criminal voice what he impiously thinks, but the works of sinners despise the table of God. We can also put it another way: a teacher of the Church who makes the spiritual bread and distributes it to the peoples, if, either for human glory or for the worldly gains that follow on glory, he speaks among the peoples and flatters the rich, and honors sinners, and, according to James, looks up to those who come to him with gold rings, and repels the poor saints, despises the name of God, and pollutes the bread of doctrines, and hurls insults at God himself, thinking the table of his Scriptures to be common with the tables of idols and of worldly doctrine.

1:8

If you offer the blind for immolation, is it not evil? And if you offer the lame and the sick, is it not evil? Offer it to your governor, whether it will please him, or whether he will accept your face, says the Lord of hosts. LXX: For if you offer the blind for a sacrifice, is it not evil? And if you offer the lame and the sick, is it not evil? Offer it to your governor, whether he will receive you, whether he will accept your face, says the Lord Almighty. About the diversity of victims, and which ought to be offered and which not, we learn more fully in Leviticus. So the people that had returned from Babylon, and the priests, the Levites, the doorkeepers and singers, and the Nethinim and Solomon's servants, of whom Ezra writes a κατάλογον (catalogue), were offering to God victims that were unlawful, namely blind and lame ones, and ones worn out by various kinds of debility; for this is what he means by sick, taking in everything with one word. If, he says, you were to offer gifts of this kind to your governor, would he not spurn them? Would he not think an injury had been done to him? And do you dare to offer to God what you do not dare to give to men? It would take long now to open the mysteries of all the victims; I shall speak only of those contained in the present passage. Blind is the victim of the soul that is not illumined by the light of Christ and has no eye that looks from the Gospel. Lame is the prayer of one who asks, which comes to pray with a double mind, and hears with the people of the Jews? How long will you limp on both feet? And sick, and covered with every infirmity, is the one that does not have Christ, the power of God and the wisdom of God. Prayers of this kind, which are without the light of truth, and do not have the firm footsteps of wisdom, and waste away with various debilities, if they are offered to the leader of churches, or to any learned and wise teacher, will they not be rejected, and fall back as an insult upon him who has dared to offer such things?

1:9-10

And now entreat the face of God that he may have mercy on you, for by your hand this has been done, if in any way he may accept your faces, says the Lord of hosts. Who is there among you that will shut the doors and kindle my altar gratuitously? LXX: And now entreat the face of your God, and ask him; by your hands these things have been done, if I shall accept your faces from you, says the Lord Almighty, because among you too the doors shall be shut, and my altar shall not be kindled for nothing. In this place the Seventy translators differ widely from the Hebrew truth; and it must be that, where the translation is different, the sense too is different. Since you have offered lame and blind and feeble victims, for you have done all these things that I have spoken of, do penance, if in any way God may have mercy on you. For there is no one among you, down to the lowest ministry, I do not say high priest, not priest, not Levite, not singer, but not even the doorkeeper, and he who puts the fire under the altar to burn the whole burnt offerings, who does not receive from me the wage of his labor. Now he says this signifying the tithes of all the produce which are offered by the peoples. From this it is shown that the service which does not demand a wage in the present is more acceptable to the Lord. Hence the Apostle too preaches the gospel freely, and labors night and day with his own hands so as not to be a burden to anyone, and testifies that this glory of his, of gratuitous preaching among the nations, must by no means be made void. Moreover, the LXX suggest a far different sense: O priests, who sacrifice feeble sacrificial victims, turn to repentance, and entreat the face of the Lord, and pray for the works of your hands. But as for what he adds: If I shall accept your faces from you, I do not know whether it fits an exhortation to repentance. For no one says: Pray to me, and I will not spare you. There follows: Among you the doors shall be shut, namely those of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Hence the Savior says: I am the door. Or otherwise: The doors of the Scriptures will not be opened to you, nor will you be able to look into the holy of holies, nor to know the mysteries of the Lord, nor to kindle his incense at the altar, because your prayers will not reach him. And this word which is said δωρεὰν, and which we have translated gratuitously, those who follow the understanding we are now setting out turn into grace, namely that they do not have the grace of serving the altar of the Lord.

I have no pleasure in you, says the Lord of hosts, and I will not accept a gift from your hand.

1:11-13

For from the rising of the sun even to its setting my name is great among the nations, and in every place sacrifice is made, and a clean oblation is offered to my name, because my name is great among the nations, says the Lord of hosts. And you have polluted it, in that you say: The table of the Lord is defiled, and what is placed upon it is contemptible, with the fire that devours it. And you have said: Behold, from toil, and you have blown upon it, says the Lord of hosts. And you have brought in from plunder the lame and the sick, and you have brought in a gift. Shall I accept it from your hand? says the Lord. LXX: My will is not in you, says the Lord Almighty, and I will not accept sacrifice from your hands, because from the rising of the sun even to its setting my name is glorious among the nations; and in every place incense is offered to my name, and a clean sacrifice, because my name is great among the nations, says the Lord Almighty. But you have defiled it, in that you say, the table of the Lord is polluted, and its foods that are placed upon it are despised, and you have said, behold, they are from affliction, and you have blown upon them, says the Lord Almighty. And you brought in plunder, and the lame and the feeble, and you offered sacrifice: shall I accept them from your hands, says the Lord Almighty? It is a rule of the Scriptures: where a most manifest prophecy of things to come is being woven, not to weaken what is written through the uncertainties of allegory. Therefore the word of the Lord is now addressed in its proper sense to the priests of the Jews, who offer the blind and the lame and the sick for sacrifice, so that they may know that spiritual victims will succeed carnal victims; and that it is by no means the blood of bulls and goats, but incense, that is, the prayers of the saints, that is to be offered to the Lord; and that not in one province of the world, Judea, nor in one city of Judea, Jerusalem, but in every place an oblation is offered, by no means unclean, as by the people of Israel, but clean, as in the ceremonies of the Christians. For from the rising of the sun even to its setting the name of the Lord is great among the nations, as the Savior says: Father, I have manifested your name to men. And although my name, he says, is going to be great among the nations, you, O leaders of the Jews, have polluted it and are polluting it. For he so weaves the prophecy of things to come as not to abandon the present time. And it is for this reason, O priests and leaders of the Jews, that in every place a clean oblation is offered to me, and my name is great among the nations: because you say the table of the Lord is defiled; and what is placed upon it is contemptible, with the fire that devours it. The people, having returned from Babylon, built, according to the book of Ezra, only an altar of chance and unhewn stones, without a temple, without the buildings of the city, without the building of the walls; and they thought the worship of religion was the less, because the adornment of the temple was lacking. To them the Lord says: You think the altar polluted, and the whole burnt offerings and victims that are placed upon it; and that the fire too which devours the victims is polluted. Nor do you understand that almighty God seeks not gold and gems and a multitude of sacrificial victims, but the will of those who offer. But as for those who judge that not the altar but the table on which the loaves were placed ought to be understood, how they can interpret what follows, with the fire that devours it, I do not see at all; for fire does not devour the showbread, which they always exchanged, new for old, and which, when taken away, went to the use of the priests. Or at any rate it is to be understood thus: You have polluted my name in that you say: What does it profit if we offer the best? Whatever the things offered may be, they are to be devoured by fire. Now the fruit of the altar is fire, and the food of the fire is the sacrificial victims or the whole burnt offerings. Nor is the impiety of your former speech enough, but you have also said this: Behold, from toil, and you have blown upon it, says the Lord of hosts. The sense of this speech is this: You have said: We have returned from captivity, we have been a prey to enemies, we have toiled much on a long journey, we are poor, whatever we were able to have has been consumed by the toil of the road, we offer such things as we have; and by saying these things you have blown upon your sacrifices, that is, you have made them worthy of my blowing upon them. Or, as can be read in the Hebrew: And you have blown upon me by saying these things: you have done injury not to the sacrifice but to me, to whom you were sacrificing. Wherefore I will by no means accept it from your hand, says the Lord Almighty. Some think that this, which is said specifically to the Jews, because their victims are unclean and polluted and the sacrifice is transferred to the nations, ought to be understood of the priests of the church, who offer victims to the Lord negligently. But if we accept this, then the victims must again be transferred from the Church to another religion. And as the gospel succeeded the law, so in turn things that will never be will succeed the gospel. They also refer the defiled table of the Lord to the holy Scriptures, if they are understood otherwise than as they were written.

1:14-2:2

Cursed is the deceitful man who has a male in his flock, and making a vow sacrifices a feeble one to the Lord. For I am a great king, says the Lord of hosts, and my name is dreadful among the nations. And now this commandment is for you, O priests: If you will not hear, and if you will not set it upon the heart, to give glory to my name, says the Lord of hosts, I will send want upon you, and I will curse your blessings, and I will curse them, since you have not set it upon the heart. LXX: And cursed is he who was powerful, and had a male in his flock, and his vow was upon it, and sacrifices a feeble one to the Lord, because I am a great king, says the Lord Almighty, and my name is illustrious among the nations. And now this commandment is for you, O priests: If you do not hear, and if you do not set it in your heart, to give glory to my name, says the Lord Almighty, I will send a curse upon you, and I will curse your blessing, and I will curse it, and I will scatter your blessing, and it shall not be in you, because you do not set it in your heart. What is written: And I will scatter your blessing, was added by the LXX, and is not found in the Hebrew. Whatever the human mind can find in excuse of its sin, to defend itself with a false satisfaction, the divine word foresees, and rebukes and condemns. And the sense is: Because you offer the blind and the lame and the sick, and this very thing not from what is yours, but from plunder and spoils and the tears of the wretched, and moreover you say, from our toil and poverty we offer what we have, therefore I speak in general terms, so that your own conscience may convict you. Certainly you plead poverty and the injury of captivity and the slenderness of your household means; hear what I add: Cursed is the deceitful man who has a male in his flock, and making a vow sacrifices a feeble one to the Lord. If you do not have a male, the curse does you no harm. Now by saying these things he shows that they have what is best, and offer what is bad. To you, therefore, O priests, this commandment is given: you have done what is impious, in contempt of me. But because I prefer the repentance of the sinner to his death, even now I say: If you will not hear me, nor understand, so as to give glory to my name, which is dreadful among the nations, I will send real penury upon you, so that, by no means lying, but forced by want of all things, you may say that you do not have the best things to offer. And I will curse, he says, your blessings, that is, the things which you now possess by my blessings; or else, whatever is blessed by you will be cursed by me. And I will curse them: your blessings is understood, since you would not understand what is said; for this is what is said: You have not set it upon the heart. We can take the cursed and deceitful one to be the people of the Jews, who, though they had a male in their flock, the Lord Savior, the spotless lamb who takes away the sins of the world, and though admonished by the prophecy of all the prophets to sacrifice and receive the ram that was caught by its horns in the thicket Sabec, would not do this, but sacrifices a feeble one to the Lord, crucifying the Savior and choosing Barabbas, the author of robbery and sedition, who according to the mystical senses is referred to the devil; and while they despise him and prefer the devil to the Savior, the name of Christ is dreadful among the nations, which have received the passion of the Lord and venerate him with awe-struck honor. It can also be said of us: if, created wise by God and having a nature suited to sacred disciplines, we neglect our talent and give ourselves up to vices and luxury, and, having a male, sacrifice a feeble one to the Lord. Now our prayer is feeble and blemished when it is corrupted by anger, envy, enmities, and other disturbance of the mind; if, offering a gift at the altar and remembering that our brother has something against us, we do not first go and make satisfaction to him. Hence the Apostle too commands: I will therefore that you pray in every place, lifting up holy hands without anger and disputing. And the shepherd of our thoughts and virtues is the understanding, of which it is said in Psalm 102 (103): Bless the Lord, my soul, and all that is within me, his holy name. If it rules its flock well, that blessing is fulfilled in it: Blessed the herds of your oxen, and the flocks of your sheep. But if, having by nature a male, that is, what is strong, firm and robust, it sacrifices what is light, or through excessive softness effeminate and wanton, or feeble and corrupted by various disturbances of the soul, it will feel fulfilled in itself what is written: The mighty shall mightily suffer torments, and: To whom they have given more, of him they will demand more. Hence it is said in its proper sense to the priests that, if they will not hear and keep it in their heart, so as to give glory to the name of the Lord by good conduct, but on the contrary his name is cursed among the nations because of them, he will send upon them want of all good things, and turn their blessings into a curse. Those who abuse health for lust, and turn riches into luxury, and disfigure a good reputation by sordid conduct, these change the blessings of God into a curse. Or else, since the commandment is given in its proper sense to the priests, their blessings are turned into a curse when they do not bless the saints from a true affection of the heart, as Isaac blessed Jacob, and Jacob the patriarchs, and Moses the twelve tribes, but through sweet words and blessings deceive the hearts of the innocent; and those who act wickedly are blessed by them, and they flatter sinners, provided they are rich, and fawn upon their vices; of whom it is said: My people, those who call you blessed lead you astray, and supplant the paths of your feet.

2:3-4

Behold, I will cast the arm at you, and I will scatter upon your face the dung of your solemnities, and it will take you with it. And you shall know that I have sent to you your commandment, that my covenant might be with Levi, says the Lord of hosts. LXX: Behold, I will separate the shoulder from you, and I will scatter the stomach upon your face, the stomach of your solemnities, and I will receive you together; and you shall know that I have sent to you this commandment, that my testament might be with the Levites, says the Lord Almighty. Since he is speaking to the priests: I will cast, he says, into your faces the things you think holier in the law, and granted to you by God from the sacrificial victims as a reward for virtues. The arm, for which the LXX translated shoulder, that is, the right shoulder of the animal. And I will scatter, he says, ἔνυστρον, that is, the stomach, or according to the Hebrews, dung, that is, phares, μετωνυμικῶς (by metonymy), naming that which is contained in place of that which contains it; for the shoulder and the breast and the tongue and the stomach, and the things described in Leviticus, the priests received from the sacrificial victims. All these, he declares, he casts away for their sins and throws into the faces of the priests, so that, namely, those who offer may be such as the things that are offered. And it will take you, he says, with it, that is, the dung of your solemnities, so that your stinking faces may be befouled by the putrid admixture of dung; and so that, from the fact that I cast you away, you may understand that I am the very one who once chose your father Levi, and through Levi Aaron, and granted you the glory of the priesthood, that my covenant or testament might be with Levi, and he might minister to me in an eternal priesthood. It is to be noted that the Hebrew word brith Aquila translates συνθήκην, that is, covenant; the LXX always διαθήκην, that is, testament; and that in very many places of the Scriptures testament means not the will of the dead, but the covenant of the living. Now God willed all men indeed, but especially priests, to have no blemish, and to adorn either the shoulder or the arm with good works. In the breast a good conscience is signified; in the tongue holy confession, so that what we believe with the heart unto justice we may confess with the mouth unto salvation. In the stomach too, to have nothing deadly, but what sustains and quickens our life; for unless food is digested in the stomach, and the body is watered by its juice, the body wastes away, lacks strength, and is borne toward death. But we turn everything to the contrary, so that the Lord casts the arm at us, or separates our works from the works of the saints; and we, who were commanded to anoint our head, and when fasting to wash our face from the filth of sins, and with unveiled face to contemplate the glory of the Lord, now say: The confusion of my face has covered me; and like Cain our face has fallen, nor can we fulfil what was commanded: Lift up your eyes and see, but we have faces smeared and polluted with the dung of vices, and we say: My scars have putrefied and are corrupted, from the face of my folly. Hence all these things have been turned into dung, as Scripture says: And the dung will take you with it, namely the dung with which our faces are smeared, we who ought to have come full and clean to the solemnities of God, and either to eat the flesh of the lamb with our shoes on, or, when seven weeks were completed, to offer God the fruits of our works, or to live as passers-through in the tabernacles of this world, and to say: I am a stranger and a sojourner, as all my fathers were. And because, for what we have translated: The dung will take you with it, the Seventy translated: And I will take you up together, an interpretation is supplied: that after the priests have been covered with shame and disgrace, and have understood their sin, and have done penance, they are then taken up by the Lord; and that what follows fits this sense: And you shall know that I have sent this commandment to you. But the former interpretation seems to me the true one, lest among words of threatening he should have placed the words of one who coaxes, especially since what follows also speaks of the indignation of God.

The office of the perfect priest is described in the person of God: what he ought to be, and what he wished him to be who commanded that he should be. For above he had said: That my covenant might be with Levi, says the Lord of hosts, and through the patriarch Levi it came down to his posterity, Aaron and Eleazar, Phinehas and the rest who were born of his stock. Now at the end he says: You have made void the covenant of Levi, says the Lord of hosts. From this it is clear that everything said through Levi pertains to the priests, and especially to the high priest. We read in Numbers of Phinehas, who struck Zimri with the Midianite harlot with a dagger: Phinehas son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the priest, has made my fury rest from the sons of Israel, because he was jealous with my zeal against them; and I did not destroy the sons of Israel in my zeal, saying thus: Behold, I will give him a testament of peace, and it shall be to him and to his seed after him a testament of an everlasting priesthood; because he was zealous for his God, and made entreaty for the sons of Israel. Now let us not think that the testament, or covenant, of life is of this life which we share with beasts and all living things, but of that life which says: I am the life. For indeed our life is hidden with Christ in God, of whom we can say that he himself is also our peace, as the Apostle confirms: For he himself is our peace. God therefore granted to Levi, and through him to his posterity, that they should fear him: For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord; and that they should be afraid before his face, or draw back and shrink together, showing the dread of the mind by the shuddering of the body, according to what is written: Upon whom shall I rest, but upon the humble and quiet and him who trembles at my words? The law of truth was in his mouth, that is, the teaching of the apostles, which in a priest ought to be disfigured by no poisoning, but should proceed wholly from the fountain of truth. And iniquity was not found on his lips, so that he may imitate his Lord, of whom it is said: Who did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth. In peace and in equity he walked with me, so that he may both have peace in himself and make peace for others, and accept no one's person in judgement, and therefore walk with God, as Enoch walked, who was translated to the Lord and was not found: And he turned many away from iniquity. He who is a priest, and does not rebuke those who offend, passes over the office of a priest. The lips of the priest shall keep knowledge; he did not say, shall bring forth, but, shall keep, so that they may speak in season, and give their fellow servants their food in due season. And they shall seek the law from his mouth. In Haggai the same is written: Ask the priests the law of the Lord. It belongs to the discipline of a priest, when asked, to answer concerning the law. If, diligent in other matters, he pleads an ignorance that is negligent in the holy Scriptures, he boasts in vain of a dignity whose works he does not display. This is what the apostle Paul writes to Titus: That he may be able to exhort in sound doctrine, and to refute those who contradict. And to Timothy: Since from infancy you have known the sacred letters, which can instruct you unto salvation, that you may rebuke sinners before all. There follows: Because he is the angel of the Lord of hosts. Ezra, the priest of God, sets forth his own name, that is, Malachi, which is interpreted angel of the Lord; and the priest of God is most truly called an angel, that is, a messenger, because he is the mediator between God and men and announces his will to the peoples; and for this reason the rational is on the breast of the priest, and on the rational doctrine and truth are placed, so that we may learn that a priest ought to be learned, and a herald of the Lord's truth. Some falsely think that what is said to Levi, and through Levi to the priests, is to be understood of Christ, not observing that what follows is contrary to the person of Christ.

But you have departed from the way, and have made very many stumble in the law; you have made void the covenant of Levi, says the Lord of hosts. Wherefore I too have made you contemptible and lowly to all peoples, as you have not kept my ways, and have accepted the face in the law. LXX: But you have turned aside from my way, and have made many weak in the law; you have corrupted the testament of Levi, says the Lord Almighty. And I have made you contemptible and dissolute among all the nations, because you have not guarded my ways, but accepted persons in the law. That these things do not pertain to the person of Christ, nor to those who are dedicated to his worship, the prudent reader understands even if I am silent. And the sense is: I wished you to do what is contained in the previous passage, and what I spoke of through Moses in Deuteronomy: Give to Levi his doctrine, and his truth to the just man, and the rest; but you have departed from the right way, or turned aside, though I said: Turn aside neither to the right nor to the left; and you have made many weak in the law, or made them stumble, as Aquila and Symmachus translated. To turn aside to the right is to abstain from foods which God created to be used, to condemn marriage, and to run into what is written in another place: Be not overmuch just. To turn off to the left is when someone gives himself over to luxury and lust, and makes many stumble in the law, someone for whom it is better that an ass's millstone be tied about his neck and he be cast headlong into the sea, than that he should make one of the least stumble. Now those make many weak who, when believers in Christ have received the strength of faith, make them grow weak by the negligence of their own conduct; to them it is most rightly said: You have made void the covenant of Levi, the covenant of life and peace, and the other things which we said pertain to the office of the priest. For which reason I too, he says, have made you contemptible and lowly to all peoples, so that they might despise you, and instead of honor and glory trample on you as cast down and lowly: As you have not kept my ways, and have accepted the face in the law. Among all the sins of Levi, or of those who are from Levi, the priests of God, this one is set down as both the last and the greatest: that they accept the face in the law, so that they consider not causes but persons, and, despising the just poor man, receive and honor the unjust rich. To them it is said in Psalm 81 (82): How long do you judge iniquity, and take up the faces of sinners? And Paul to the Galatians: God, he says, does not accept the person of man. And the apostle James rebukes and condemns this sin more fully. But whatever is said to the former people, let us think said to us also, so that we may more cautiously withdraw from vices and not accept the face in the law, nor, we who are worshippers of God, prefer falsehood to truth.

Is not one Father of us all? Has not one God created us? Why then does each one of us despise his brother, violating the covenant of our fathers? Has not one God created us? Why has each one forsaken his brother, so as to make abominable the testament of your fathers? Judah has been forsaken, and abomination has been done in Israel and in Jerusalem, because Judah has defiled the holy things of the Lord, in which he loved, and has devised strange gods. May the Lord destroy the man who does these things, until he is driven out of the tabernacles of Jacob, and from among those who offer sacrifice to the Lord Almighty. Before we discuss the present passage, the tradition of the Hebrews must be set down, or rather the truth of Scripture must be unfolded. We read in the volume that bears the title of Ezra, in the person of Ezra himself speaking: The princes came to me, saying: The people of Israel, and the priests and the Levites, have not separated themselves from the peoples of the lands and from their abominations, namely of the Canaanites, and the Hittites and the Perizzites, and the Jebusites, and of the Ammonites, and of the Moabites, and of the Egyptians, and of the Amorites. For they have taken of their daughters for themselves and for their sons, and have mingled the holy seed with the peoples of the lands. The hand also of the princes and magistrates has been first in this transgression. And when I had heard this word, I tore my cloak and my tunic, and plucked out the hair of my head and of my beard, and sat down mourning. And again in what follows: And there were found among the sons of the priests those who had married foreign wives: of the sons of Joshua, the son of Jehozadak, and his brothers: Maaseiah, and Eleazar, and Jarib, and Gedaliah; and they gave their hands that they would put away their wives, and offer a ram of the flock for their offence. At the end of the passage also, after the enumeration of those who had taken foreign wives, Scripture records: All these had taken foreign wives, and there were among them women who had borne sons. Having returned, then, from the Babylonian captivity, the princes and the priests and Levites, as well as the rest of the people, cast off their wives of the Israelite race, who, through poverty and the hardship of the long journey, and through the frailty of their sex unable to bear the toil, had been broken down, and had contracted infirmity and unsightliness of body; and they had joined themselves in marriage to foreign women, either in the flower of their age, or more beautiful in the adornment of their bodies, or the daughters of the powerful and rich. So Ezra the prophet rebukes them and calls them to the repudiation of their new spouses, that they may take back the wives whom they had let go: Is not, he says, one father Abraham of us all? Of whom it is written in Isaiah: Look to Abraham your father, and to Sarah, who bore you, for he was one, and I called him. Has not one God created us, who chose our race out of Abraham? Why then do we despise our old wives, and cast off the daughters of our brothers, so as to forsake the covenant of our fathers and not take wives according to the law? Judah has transgressed; for this tribe returned from Babylon with the priests and Levites; and abomination has been done in Israel and in Jerusalem. Not at all among the ten tribes, which were held by the Assyrians, but among those which returned from Babylon by command of King Cyrus under Zerubbabel, Ezra and Nehemiah. Because Judah has defiled the sanctification of the Lord, which he loved, and has had the daughter of a strange god, by mingling with foreigners the seed of Israel and of the Gentiles; that is, by taking as wives the daughters of the heathen, who serve idols. Since, therefore, they have done this, the word of the prophet is directed to them, and they are drawn back from sin by curses. May the Lord destroy the man who shall have done this. How beautifully he does not cut off pardon from error, nor did he say, May the Lord curse him who has done this, but, who shall have done, stretching the curse into the future, so as to call sinners to repentance. The teacher, he says, and the disciple, out of the tabernacles of Jacob, whether he be a priest or a layman, will be struck by one curse, and there will be no difference of punishment among those in whom the sins are equal. And him who offers a gift to the Lord of hosts: it is understood, may the Lord destroy him also who would wish to offer a gift at the altar for men of this kind, whose only remedy is not to do what they have done. Some, not understanding this place, nor knowing the truth of the history, understand what he says, Is not one father of us all?, of Abraham in such a way that they recall that Abraham is the father of the nations too, according to what is written: Is God able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham? Or at any rate they affirm one God the Father, according to what we read in Deuteronomy: Is not this the very Father who possessed you, and made you, and created you? And again: You have forsaken the God who created you. And elsewhere: I have begotten sons and exalted them: but they have forsaken me. And in the volume of the psalms: Strange sons have lied to me, strange sons have grown old and have gone lame from their paths. And those who sinned, departing from this one father, made for themselves many fathers of their vices: For everyone who commits sin is born of the devil. And what follows, Why does each one of us despise his brother, violating the covenant of our fathers, they have interpreted so as to say: that we all, begotten of one father, ought to be one and to have one lip of confession, but that afterwards, through pride and the building of the tower against God, we were divided into many tongues and opinions. They refer it also to almsgiving, so as to say: that we despise our brothers when we do not share with them what we have received from God for the sustaining of life. And they go on to this, that they assert that according to the Scriptures the Lord is called our brother, he who commanded Mary Magdalene to announce to his brothers that the Lord had risen; and he speaks in the psalm: I will declare your name to my brothers, in the midst of the Church I will sing to you. So the Jews deserted their brother, and defiled the covenant of their fathers, which God had made with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, that in their seed, which is interpreted as Christ, all the nations should be blessed. These things they conjecture in various ways, because they do not find a sure way. In short, what follows according to the LXX, Judah has been forsaken, and abomination has been done in Israel, and in Jerusalem, they expound thus: that the Jews have been forsaken because they blasphemed the Lord Savior, and have suffered what is written: According to the multitude of their impieties drive them out, for they have provoked you, O Lord, so that they were scattered as wanderers and fugitives through all the provinces. For they defiled the holy things of the Lord, loving, in place of the Son of God, and devising for themselves, strange gods: For he who does not receive the Son does not receive the Father, who sent him. And therefore, because they did this, they perish for so long, until they are humbled, either by the hardship of captivity or through the consciousness of their sins; and they are cast out of the tabernacles of Jacob, so that, the temple and the altar having been destroyed, sacrificial victims are no longer offered among them. Not content with this explanation — because Judah means confession of the Lord —, they transfer the interpretation to the penitent, who, after he has confessed the Lord, if he sins, does abomination in Israel and in Jerusalem, in the mind that sees God and in the vision of peace. For he pollutes the mysteries of Christ, receiving his body and blood unworthily, because he has loved vices and has devised for himself strange gods, having as many gods as he has sins. According to the apostle Paul: The belly is the god of the gluttonous; and according to James: By whatever one is overcome, to that he is made subject. And he who has done this is driven out of the Church, and out of the tabernacles of Jacob, who supplants vices and sins, until he is humbled for his own good, and a victim is offered for him to the Lord Almighty.

Let us interpret the history, and, fitting brief statements to the individual verses, let us set out what has been handed down to us by the Hebrews. The Israelite wives, forsaken, and seeing women of foreign nations in their husbands' beds, fled to the help of God alone; prostrate day and night before the altar of the Lord, with tears, groaning and wailing, they cast reproach upon his providence, that it did not look upon human affairs and did not help the wretched. Hence God says that he cannot accept sacrifice and sacrificial victims from the hands of the priests who committed these things, hindered as he is by the weeping and lamentations of the wives; and moreover that they ask for what reason he does not accept sacrifice from their hands; and at once he adds: Because the Lord has borne witness between you and the wife of your puberty, whom you have despised, saying: For this reason a man shall leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife, and the two shall be in one flesh. And therefore she is called partner, and wife of union and of compact, she who was made by God from the rib of the man. And the remnant of his spirit, whether of God, as some think, or of the husband, as others suppose, because through affection one soul seems in a certain manner to be in two, joined in spirit, united in mind. Since, then, one made both, both the man and the woman, the union of both was therefore made by the Lord, that children might be born. For the one God, what does he seek but the seed of God, that is, sons begotten of the Israelite stock? Since, then, you have fruitful wives and rejoice in children, why do you seek beauty in wives, which is fitting for harlots, not for wives? So God commands through the prophet, and says: Guard your spirit, that you be not led away by lust, that you be not overcome by love of foreign women. And do not despise the wife of your youth, so that she who was first joined to you in virginal marriage may continue until old age. But it could happen that the princes, priests, Levites and people would answer: God commanded through Moses that when we have hated our wives, we should put them away. And it is to be read: You tell me, it is written: Since you have hated, put away your wife, says the Lord God of Israel. And at once he answered: This was indeed commanded in the law, but because of the hardness of your heart. Which the Lord pursues more fully in the Gospel: But whoever, except for the cause of fornication, unjustly puts away his wife, iniquity shall cover his garment, that is, the body with which the soul is clothed, says the Lord of hosts, so that he may be punished in that very thing in which he sinned. Wherefore, this little question having been resolved, he presses home and repeats what he had said above: Guard your spirit, and do not despise, either the guarding of your spirit, or at any rate your wife, if she is poor or unsightly. What we have expounded specially, according to the book of Ezra, concerning the forsaken wives, others think is said generally against those who plunder what belongs to others and, gathering riches unjustly, dare to offer gifts to God, which he says he can by no means accept, prevented by the tears and weeping and groans of those who have been laid waste; and that this place agrees with that testimony: Honor God from your just labors. But they also insert this sense: that those who, because of the loss of their property, and the death of their children, and shipwreck, and the other things that belong to the loss of worldly goods, are turned to weeping and give themselves wholly to lamentation and groaning, and do not, by strength of mind and hope in God and the rewards of things to come, despise all things, are not received by him even if they direct prayer to God, because they are disfigured by lamentations unbecoming and unfitting for a man. Now what follows, Because the Lord has borne witness between you and the wife of your puberty, or of your youth, whom you have despised; and she is your partner and the wife of your covenant, or of your testament, ; and no other made her: and the remnant of your spirit, they interpret thus, so as to call the wife of youth the natural understanding and the law written in the heart, which is implanted in all men. Whence also the nations, not having the law of God, do the things that are of the law. Of this spouse, they say, it is proclaimed in Proverbs: A wife is joined to a man by God; and we are commanded to drink from our own waters and fountains, and that no one be partner in the drinking, and that we rejoice with the wife of our youth. This wife impels even the credulous to say: May God judge and may God see; and to him I leave all things between me and you to be judged; of her Ecclesiastes also speaks: And live life with the woman whom you have loved, all the days of your vanity which have been given you under the sun. This wife is the remnant of our spirit, because she is always joined to our sense; and if she withdraws from us, at once we offend God, and our impiety covers us. Hence again it is urged: Guard your spirit; not the flesh, for those who are in it cannot please God; not the soul: The natural for man does not receive the things that are of the spirit; but the spirit: Because the spirit intercedes for us with unutterable groanings.

This, though in other words, we read in Malachi. But what follows, The voice of one crying in the desert, prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight, is said by Isaiah the prophet; and at once, explaining of whom both testimonies are said: There was, he says, John in the desert, baptizing and preaching the baptism of repentance, for the remission of sins. But the evangelists too interpreted the prophecy of the prophets concerning John, and while they agree in sense, they differ in words. For whereas the LXX rendered: Make the paths of our God straight, Mark and Luke said: Make his paths straight. But John: Make straight, he says, the way of the Lord. From which it is clear that the apostles and evangelists, and the Lord Savior himself, do not follow the authority of the LXX translators, which, having knowledge of the Hebrew tongue, they do not need, but translate from the Hebrew what they had read, not caring about the syllables and points of the words, provided that the truth of the sense is carried over. That they did this in many places we have indeed shown, and especially in that book which we entitled 'On the Best Kind of Translating' it has been proved by many testimonies together. This, then, which is said, Behold, I send my angel and he will prepare the way before my face, is said in the person of Christ, because he sent John into the desert of Judea to preach the baptism of repentance, for the remission of sins. But what follows, And at once the Ruler whom you seek will come to his temple, and the angel of the testament whom you desire, he thus speaks of himself as of another, according to the custom of the Scriptures. And no one doubts that this Ruler is the Savior, who is the Creator of all things, and is called the angel of the testament and the angel of great counsel. Others, however, think that what is said, Behold, I send my angel and he will prepare the way before my face, is said in the person of the Father; and what follows, At once the Ruler whom you seek will come to his temple, and the angel of the testament whom you desire, they understand to have been said by him of the Lord Savior. Now John prepares the way and makes the paths of our God straight in the hearts of believers, in which, because of depravity and unbelief, God could not walk before. And the temple they interpret either as the Church or as each one of the believers in the Church: Be built up as spiritual houses, a holy priesthood; to offer spiritual victims, and acceptable to God through Christ Jesus. And those who have believed: Do you not know that you are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you? The Jews understand what is said, Behold, I send my angel, as said of Elijah the prophet, and what follows, At once the Ruler whom you seek will come to his temple, and the angel of the testament whom you desire, they refer to the ἠλειμμένον, that is, their Christ, who they say will come in the last time. But I wonder how the outcome of events does not teach them the truth. For what temple of his will the Ruler find, when it has been destroyed to its foundations? Or if it is to be built by another before Christ comes, what more will their Christ do, when all things have been restored by another? Our Lord in the Gospel, interpreting Elijah the prophet as John the Baptist, says: If you wish to know, he is Elijah who is to come, of whom also this same prophet whom we are now discussing writes at the end: Behold, I send you Elijah the prophet, before the great and terrible day of the Lord comes. And how John is Elijah, he has given us the way of understanding, recalling that he came in the power and spirit of Elijah.

Let us therefore by no means think them light sins, perjury, and not paying the laborer his wages, and wronging the widow and the orphan, and oppressing the foreigner and the stranger, which are compared to sorcery, and poisonings, and adultery. We also said at the beginning that by the prophet Malachi Ezra the priest ought to be understood, and that all the things that are written in his history are contained in this volume too; and now we say that in the times of him and of Nehemiah — who clearly lived at the same time — there was a most grievous famine, and because of the famine an uprising, and that the poor, compelled by the necessity of their circumstances, sold their sons and daughters, and every possession, and their whole substance. In short, they say: Our sons and our daughters are very many; let us take grain as their price, and let us eat and live. And there were those who said: Let us pledge our fields and vineyards and our houses, and let us take grain in the famine. And I was very angry, says Ezra, when I had heard their outcry according to these words, and my heart took counsel with me, and I rebuked the nobles and the magistrates, and the rest. So in the time of famine the price of grain was very dear, so much so that they were compelled to sell their own sons; and both those who had little and those who had gathered much produce into their storehouses, by necessity or by the greatness of the price, were unwilling to render the tithes to the Levites, who had no share in the inheritance of Judea, but the first fruits and the tithes were their inheritance. And lest this be thought to be our own, let us set down the testimony of Ezra: And I learned, he says, that the portions of the Levites had not been given, and each one of the Levites, and of the singers, and of those who ministered, had fled to his own country. And I pleaded the case against the magistrates, and said: Why do we forsake the house of God? And I gathered them together, and made them stand in their stations. And all Judah brought the tithes of grain, wine and oil into the storehouses. And we set over the storehouses Shelemiah the priest and Zadok the scribe, and so on. We have heard the history of Ezra; now let us take up again the words of the prophet, looking more carefully whether prophecy and history agree. When it is said: Return to me, and I will return to you, says the Lord Almighty, it is clear that those whom he exhorts to come back had departed from the Lord. And see the mercy of the Lord: he promises like for like, that with the measure they have measured, it may be measured back to them. And just as it is written in Leviticus: If you walk perversely towards me, I too will walk against you with perverse fury, so now too he exhorts the people to return, so that he himself also may return to them. They, not understanding that they have departed from the Lord, shamelessly ask: In what shall we return? And they say: For when did we depart, that we should rightly be compelled to come back? The Lord answered: Will a man fix God, since you fix me? The Hebrew word that is written haiecba the LXX translated 'if he supplants', in place of which Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion put 'if he defrauds', so that the sense is: Does a man defraud God, since you defraud me? And indeed, according to the order of the history, because the people were not rendering the tithes and the first fruits to the Levites, the Lord says that he himself suffers fraud, since his ministers, compelled by famine and want, had deserted the temple. For if it is he who is visited in prison through others, and taken in when sick, and receives food and is given drink when hungry and thirsty, why should he himself not receive tithes in his ministers; and, if they are not given, himself be deprived of his share? This haiecba of which we have spoken is translated in the language of the Syrians and Chaldeans as 'if he will fix'; whence we too translated it so many years ago, referring it more to the mystery of the Lord's passion, in which men crucified God, than to the tithes and first fruits that are written of. Let the prudent reader ask how our translation agrees with what follows: In tithes and in first fruits, and let him see whether we can say this: That you might fix me to the cross, that you might lay wicked hands on your God, you brought about by the premeditation of many things beforehand, by withholding the tithes and first fruits, I do not say from my priests and Levites, but from me, who commanded through Moses that they be given. Let this be said by us about a single word, as we leave the judgement of its interpretation to the reader's discretion; now let us follow the order of the prophecy. Because you have not rendered to me the tithes and first fruits, therefore you are cursed in famine and want, and you supplant me, or defraud and deprive me, the whole nation. For 'nation', which in the Hebrew is written aggoi, the Seventy translated 'year', ἔτος instead of ἔθνος. And the sense is: Behold, the year is completed, and you have carried nothing into my treasuries, but everything into your own storehouses. And for the sake of tithes and first fruits, which were small things if they had been given by you, you have lost the richness of your possessions and all abundance of produce. But so that you may know that this was brought about by my anger, because you defrauded me of my share, I exhort you and admonish you to bring the tithes into the storehouses, that is, into the treasuries of the temple, and let the priests and Levites who minister to me have food; and prove me, whether I do not pour out such great rains that the floodgates of heaven will be believed to have been opened. And I will pour out on you a blessing even to abundance. The word 'pouring out' shows the name of bounty. But it can happen that, with rains watering the fields, there is indeed fertility; but either the locust comes, or the cankerworm, or blight, or the caterpillar, and the labours of men perish; therefore he joins to it and says: And I will rebuke for your sake the devourer, the locust, that is, and the rest that we have mentioned: And he will not spoil the fruit of your land. The vine also will fill the winepresses, and all the nations round about will marvel at the fertility of your land, so much so that all will long to dwell in it, and you will be an example to all nations of abundance in all things. What we have said of the tithes and first fruits, which were once given by the people to the priests and Levites, understand also of the peoples of the Church, who have been commanded not only to give tithes and first fruits, but also to sell all that they have and give to the poor and follow the Lord and Savior. But if we are unwilling to do this, let us at least imitate the beginnings of the Jews, so that we give to the poor a part of the whole, and pay due honour to the priests and Levites. Hence the Apostle too says: Honour widows who are widows indeed; and that the presbyter is to be honoured with double honour, especially he who labours in the word and in the teaching of God. Whoever does not do this is convicted of defrauding and supplanting God, and he is cursed in the want of all things, so that he who has sown sparingly will also reap sparingly, and he who has sown in blessing will gather fruits abundantly in blessings. If ever famine and want and the lack of all things oppress the world, let us know that this comes down from the wrath of God, who says that he is defrauded in the poor, if they do not receive alms, and is deprived of his own possession. We can also interpret the tithes and first fruits thus: If someone learned and instructed in the law of God is able to instruct others, he ought not to attribute it to his own prudence and to the talent that he possesses; but let him give thanks first to God, who bestows all things, then to his priests and teachers, by whom he was taught. For if he does not give thanks, but claims knowledge for himself, he will be cursed in want. But if, understanding God to be the giver, and giving thanks to those through whom he was instructed by God, he humbles himself and brings food into the storehouse of God, that is, ministers the nourishment of holy Scripture to the peoples in the Church, at once the floodgates of heaven will be opened over him, and spiritual rain will be poured out; and God will command his clouds to rain a shower upon him, and he will enjoy abundance of all things; and he will also rebuke for his sake the devourer, that is, the opposing powers, and his labour will bring forth fruit, and he will attain that which is written: Blessed is he who speaks into the ears of those who hear. He will also lift up his eyes and see the regions, that they are already white for the harvest; and he will gather fruits unto eternal life. Nor will the vine be barren in his field, he who says in the Gospel: I am the vine. And he who speaks through the prophet: I planted you a fruitful vine, wholly true; and such — through the confession of humility, and the giving of thanks to God and to the teachers of his Church — blessedness will he attain that all nations will call him blessed, and those who have heard him discoursing in the Church will long to dwell in his land and his teaching.

LXX: For behold, the day comes burning like an oven, and it will burn them up; and all the foreigners, and all who work iniquity, will be stubble; the coming day will set them on fire, says the Lord Almighty, and neither root nor branch will be left in them. And for you who fear my name the Sun of justice will rise, and healing in his wings and you will go forth, and leap like calves loosed from their bonds, and you will trample down the unjust, and they will be ash among your feet, on the day that I make, says the Lord Almighty. Because the impious made me labour with their words and said: Everyone who does evil is good in the sight of the Lord, and such men please him; and vain is he who serves God; and: What profit is it that we have kept his commandments and walked sorrowful before the Lord of hosts? And those who feared God spoke against their rash words, and each one conferred with his neighbour about the things that pertain to the fear of God: the Lord attended and heard, and a book of remembrance was written for those who fear the Lord and think upon his name, and he promises that when the day comes he will have them for a special possession, and will spare them as a man spares his son who serves him, and he drives it home more fully and says: Behold, the day will come, that is, the day of judgement, which will be light to the saints and darkness to sinners; and that day will be kindled like a furnace, or an oven, so that all the impious are turned into stubble and burned up by the heat of the furnace. And when it has set them ablaze and burned them up, it will not leave in them any root and shoot of wickedness. This is about the impious, what they will suffer on the day of judgement. On the contrary, it is said what will befall those who fear the name of God: And for you who fear my name the Sun of justice will rise. He will judge all things truly, and will allow neither good things nor evil, neither virtues nor vices, to lie hidden. And healing will be in his wings, so that he may carry on his shoulders those healed through repentance, according to that which is written in Deuteronomy: Spreading out his wings he took them up, and carried them on his shoulders. Then those who are now shut up in the world as in a prison will go forth, and they will leap like calves from the herd, or like calves freed from their bonds. Hence the Apostle too says: I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ, so that he may go forth and leap like a calf freed from its bonds, and like a victim of the Lord. Nor is he content with this end of joy, but he will tread down the impious when they are ash. Hence also there is this prayer for the just: And may God crush Satan under your feet swiftly. Abraham spoke to the Lord, perceiving that in comparison with the divine majesty he was ash: I am earth and ash; and therefore he will see the Sun of justice and will rest in the midst of his lots, and, carried by him, will be lifted up to the heavenly places. But he who through pride said: I will set my seat above the stars, I will be like the Most High, will be brought down to the earth, and will be like ash under the feet of the saints, when the day of the Lord who judges has come.

4:4

Remember the law of Moses my servant, which I commanded him in Horeb for all Israel, precepts and judgements. LXX: likewise. In the future there will be a just retribution of deserts, when the devouring flame will consume and burn up the root and the shoot of the proud, and for those who fear the Lord the Sun of justice will rise, and healing in his wings. Therefore remember the law of Moses my servant, which I gave him on Mount Horeb, which is Sinai, for all Israel, precepts and judgements. But since the Apostle says: We know that the law is spiritual, and blessed David: Unveil my eyes, and I will consider the wonders of your law; and since all ate the heavenly manna spiritually, and the whole people of Israel drank from the same spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ; those who believe in Christ ought to keep spiritually the precepts of the law which he gave in Horeb, which is interpreted 'dryness', through which the moisture of all vices is boiled away, and at the rays of the Sun of justice the flux of lust is dried up. Now the Lord spoke to all Israel, which with the mind sees God, and of whom it is said in the Gospel: Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.

4:5-6

Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and terrible day of the Lord comes; and he will turn the heart of the fathers to the sons, and the heart of the sons to their fathers, lest perhaps I come and strike the earth with anathema. LXX: Behold, I will send you Elijah the Tishbite, before the great and illustrious day of the Lord comes, who will turn the heart of the father to the son, and the heart of a man to his neighbor, lest perhaps, coming, I strike the earth utterly. After Moses — whose commandments, as we have taught, are to be kept spiritually — he says that Elijah is to be sent, signifying by Moses the law and by Elijah prophecy, as Abraham says to a certain rich man clad in purple: They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them. And the Lord and Savior, transfigured on the mountain, had Moses and Elijah in white garments speaking with him, and they told him what he was to suffer in Jerusalem. For the law and the whole choir of the prophets proclaim the passion of Christ. Therefore, before the day of judgement comes and the Lord strikes the earth with anathema, or utterly, or suddenly, as the LXX translated — for this is what ἄρδην means — the Lord will send, in Elijah — who is interpreted 'my God', and is from the town of Tishbe, which means conversion and penitence — the whole choir of the prophets, to turn the heart of the fathers to the sons, namely of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and of all the patriarchs, so that their descendants may believe in the Lord Savior, in whom they too believed: For Abraham saw the day of the Lord and was glad; or the heart of the father to the son, that is, the heart of God to everyone who has received the spirit of adoption. And the heart of the sons to their fathers, so that both Jews and Christians, who now disagree among themselves, may agree in an equal religion in Christ. Hence it is said to the apostles, who brought forth the seedbed of the Gospel in the whole world: In place of your fathers, sons have been born to you. For if Elijah has not first turned the heart of the fathers to the sons, and the heart of the sons to their fathers, when the great and terrible day of the Lord comes — great for the saints, terrible for sinners — the true and just judge will strike, not heaven, nor those who dwell in heaven, but the earth with anathema: those who do earthly works. The Jews and the Judaizing heretics think that before their ἠλειμμένον (Anointed One) Elijah will come and restore all things. Hence the question is also put to Christ in the Gospel: What of the Pharisees saying that Elijah is to come? To whom he answered: Elijah indeed will come, and if you believe, he has already come, understanding John in Elijah.
