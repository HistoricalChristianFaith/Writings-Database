Commentary on Amos

Latin source: Jerome's commentary on Amos, in three books addressed to Pammachius, each with its own prologue (written at Bethlehem in 406), from the critical text of M. Adriaen, Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 76 (Turnhout, 1969), pp. 211–348, verified against the page images and collated with Vallarsi's edition (Venice, 1766), with the edition's evident misprints corrected; its critical apparatus is not translated. Each lemma gives Amos in Jerome's own version from the Hebrew and then, after "LXX:", in his Latin of the Septuagint; all Scripture is translated as Jerome quotes it, from his own Latin. Italics mark the lemmata and the other Scripture he quotes. Headings follow Jerome's own division of the text into lemmata. Psalms keep the Latin (LXX) numbering, with the Hebrew number in parentheses. Greek is kept as printed, with an English gloss in parentheses where Jerome gives none, and Hebrew words keep Jerome's own transliteration. This English translation is released into the public domain.

Prologue

The prophet Amos, who follows Joel and is the third of the twelve prophets, is not the same man whom we read of as the father of the prophet Isaiah. For that one is written with the first and last letters of his name, aleph and sade, and is interpreted strong and robust; this one, however, with ain and samech, and is interpreted a people torn away; the middle letters, mem and uau, are common to both. But among us, who do not have so great a difference of vowel letters, and of the letter s, which among the Hebrews is threefold, both these and other names seem to be the same, which among the Hebrews are distinguished by the diversity of their letters and by their own peculiarities. This prophet, then, whom we now have in hand, was from the town of Tekoa, which lies six miles toward the southern region from holy Bethlehem, which gave birth to the Savior of the world; and beyond it there is no village at all, not even rustic huts like ovens, which the Africans call mapalia. So great is the vastness of the desert, which stretches as far as the Red Sea and the borders of the Persians, the Ethiopians and the Indians. And because on the dry and sandy ground no crops whatever are produced, everything is full of shepherds, so that they may make up for the barrenness of the land by the multitude of their flocks. Of this number of shepherds was also the prophet Amos, unskilled in speech, but not in knowledge. For the same Holy Spirit who spoke through all the prophets was speaking in him. Hence we translate from the Hebrew.

Book One

1:1

The words of Amos, who was among the herdsmen of Tekoa, which he saw concerning Israel. But the Seventy, intending I know not what, translated: The words of Amos, which came to be in Accarim of Tekoa, which he saw on behalf of Jerusalem. And it should be known that this prophet prophesied, not on behalf of Jerusalem, which is not found at all in the Hebrew, but concerning Israel, that is, the ten tribes, which are called Israel and were in Samaria. And indeed Aquila and Symmachus and Theodotion translated this likewise. The first, then, the prophet Hosea, speaks to the ten tribes, which are called Ephraim, Samaria, the house of Joseph and Israel. The second, Joel, utters his prophecy to Jerusalem and the two tribes, which are called Judah and Jerusalem and Jacob, making no mention at all of Israel. The third, Amos, preaches by no means to Jerusalem, which was ruled by the tribe of Judah, but to Israel in Samaria. This can be proved from his whole volume, and above all from what is written: Amaziah the priest of Bethel sent to Jeroboam king of Israel, saying: Amos has rebelled against you in the midst of the house of Israel. And a little after, Amaziah said to Amos: You who see, go, flee into the land of Judah, and eat bread there; and there you shall prophesy, and in Bethel you shall not again prophesy any more, for it is the king's sanctuary and the house of the kingdom. To whom Amos answered: I was not a prophet, nor the son of a prophet, but a shepherd stripping the berries of brambles; and the Lord took me as I followed the flock, and said to me: Go, prophesy to my people Israel; not, surely, to Judah and Jerusalem, as is wrongly found among the Greeks and Latins, but to Israel, that is, the ten tribes, which, because of the multitude of the people, had kept the original name. But in the place where the Seventy translated in Accarim, Theodotion put the Hebrew word itself: in nocedim, which Aquila rendered ἐν ποιμνιοτρόφοις, that is, among the herdsmen, Symmachus and the Fifth edition ἐν τοῖς ποιμέσιν, that is, among the shepherds. And I think that here too, because of the likeness of the letters daleth and res, they were deceived, and read nocerim as though for nocedim, and from there put down the word Accarim, although the letter nun at the beginning of the name leaves no excuse for the error. But up to the present I have not read that Accarim is Hebrew. And those who interpret it barrenness, that is, στείρωσιν, express Ekron, the city of the Philistines, rather than Accarim, which is not read anywhere at all. The words, therefore, of Amos, who was from the town of Tekoa, of the number of the shepherds, since the region itself is also a pastoral one, are contained in this volume, which he saw concerning Israel, not with the eyes of the flesh but with the gaze of the mind; for the prophets were called seers; otherwise, according to the letter, words are not seen but heard. Hence the people also saw the voice of God, and Moses, keeping silent, spoke to the Lord when he said to him: Why do you cry to me? In the New Testament also we read: What we have seen and heard, what we have seen with our eyes, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life. For the Word of God, which in itself is invisible, the apostles see and handle with their hands. Because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. And he saw these words:

In the days of Uzziah king of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash king of Israel, two years before the earthquake. At the time when Sardanapalus reigned among the Assyrians and the cities of Cilicia, of whom the distinguished orator wrote: "Uglier, he says, in his vices than in his name"; and among the Latins Procas Silvius, whom Amulius succeeded in the kingdom after driving out his brother Numitor; and when Amulius had been killed, Romulus, having gathered a band of shepherds and robbers, founded the city of his own name. This, moreover, is Uzziah king of Judah, surnamed Azariah, who, having tried to claim for himself a priesthood not due to him, was struck with leprosy on his forehead, when not only the punishment of the man who had committed sacrilege but also the earthquake, which the Hebrews record happened at that time, showed the wrath of the Lord. Jeroboam, however, is not the son of Nebat, who made Israel sin, but the son of Joash, son of Joathan, son of Jehu, the great-grandson of that same Jehu, under whom Hosea and Joel and Amos prophesied. Uzziah, then, is interpreted κράτος Κυρίου, that is, the dominion or strength of the Lord; Jeroboam, δικασμὸς λαοῦ, that is, the judging or the cause of the people, although some, reading it with the Greek letter chi, suppose it to mean the division of the people; Joash, Κυρίου χρόνισμα, that is, the delay of the Lord, or temporality. The words of Amos, therefore, at the time when the people of Israel had been torn away from the Lord and were serving golden calves, or had been torn away from the kingdom of the stock of David, sang out with a clear voice like a blaring trumpet, which is what Tekoa is interpreted. And they sang concerning Israel, which was once the most upright of God, that is, εὐθύτατος Θεοῦ. In the days of Uzziah king of Judah, in which, because of the temple and the holy of holies, the strength of the Lord remained, and ruled by its own dominion the people who confessed him. But the name of Jeroboam, who had been the cause of the division of the people, signifies that Israel remained for a long time in the error of idolatry before the universal captivity shook it; and this, in the two years, shows the twofold distress of the ten tribes and of the two, so that, if they were willing to repent, they might not feel the earthquake thereafter.

These things, Pammachius, dearest to my soul, you who by the interpretation of your name, as by a kind of prophecy of things to come, show that you war down the devil and the opposing powers with every art of fighting, I have embraced in a long discourse as the argument and ὑπόθεσιν (subject) of the prophet Amos; briefly reminding you of this: that I promised an explanation both of this prophet and of the prophets Hosea and Zechariah to other holy men indeed, but above all to your mother-in-law Paula, of holy and venerable memory, while she was living; and that I do not greatly fail in keeping faith with my pledge if what I promised to the mother, I pay to the son. But now it is time that, setting forth the words of the prophet himself, I should explain in each point what seems good to me.

1:2

And he said: The Lord will roar from Zion, and from Jerusalem he will give his voice. And the beautiful places of the shepherds have mourned, and the top of Carmel has been dried up. LXX: And he said: The Lord has spoken out from Zion, and from Jerusalem he has given his voice; and the pastures of the shepherds have mourned, and the top of Carmel has withered. It is natural that all craftsmen speak in the examples of their own craft, and that each one should bring forward likenesses from the pursuit in which he has spent his life: for example, he who is a sailor and a helmsman compares his sadness to a storm, calls a loss a shipwreck, names his enemies contrary winds; and again he calls prosperity and gladness a most gentle breeze and favourable winds, calm seas and waters like plains. On the other side, whatever a soldier says sounds of shield, sword, breastplate, helmet, lance, bow and arrows, death, wound and victory. In the mouth of the philosophers Socrates and Plato, Xenophon and Theophrastus, Zeno and Aristotle, the Stoics and the Peripatetics are always turning up. The orators extol to the sky with praises Lysias and Hyperides, Pericles and Demosthenes, the Gracchi, the Catos, the Ciceros and the Hortensiuses. The poets, if they are epic, celebrate Homer and Vergil in frequent speech; if lyric, Pindar and Flaccus. Why have these things been said? Clearly so that we may show that the prophet Amos too, who was a shepherd from among shepherds, and a shepherd not in cultivated places planted with trees and vines, or at least among woods and green meadows, but in the broad vastness of the desert, in which the ferocity of lions and the slaughter of flocks are found, used in his discourses the terms of his own craft, so as to name the terrible and fearful voice of the Lord the roaring and growling of lions. Comparing the overthrow of the Israelite cities to the solitude of the shepherds and the dryness of the mountains, he says: The Lord will roar from Zion, and from Jerusalem he will give his voice. Will roar is said in Hebrew iesag. And it is fittingly said that he will roar from Zion and give his voice from Jerusalem, where the temple and the worship of God were, in order to teach that God dwells not in the cities of Israel, that is, in Bethel and in Dan, where the golden calves were, nor in the metropolis Samaria and in Jezreel, but in the true religion, which at that time was practised in Zion and Jerusalem. When he roars from Zion and gives his voice from Jerusalem — for the law shall go forth from Zion, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem —, all the beautiful things of the shepherds and of the kings of Israel have mourned, and the top of Carmel, which once abounded in rich pastures, has been dried up. Carmelus it is called in Greek and in Latin, but in Hebrew it is named Chermel; and there are two mountains which are called by this name. One, in which Nabal of Carmel, the husband of Abigail, lived, toward the southern region. The other near Ptolemais, which was formerly called Acco, overhanging the sea, on which the prophet Elijah, on bended knees, obtained the rains. If, then, he is speaking of the Chermel in which Nabal lived, it suits rather the flocks of the shepherds, because it is near the wilderness. But if of the one which is close to the shore, it pertains to the kings and to the pride of the kings of Israel, who, he says, when captivity is impending, are to be laid waste like deserted mountains. Another interpretation: when the Lord, from Zion and Jerusalem, from the watchtower of the Church, which, set upon a mountain, cannot be hidden, and in which there is the vision of peace, has given his voice through the Old and the New Testament and through the teachers of the Church, and has sounded as it were a clear trumpet, then all the beautiful things of the shepherds, that is, the doctrine of the heretics, which seemed composed in fine language, and the knowledge of circumcision — for this is what Chermel means —, by which they promise themselves the knowledge of the true circumcision, will mourn and be dried up, and the thousands of the peoples, who because of their simplicity are called flocks, will wither with sudden drought.

1:3

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn him back, because they threshed Gilead with iron carts. LXX: And the Lord said: For three impieties of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn away from them, because they sawed with iron saws the women with child in Gilead. For the threshing sledges, which in Hebrew are called arsoth, and were translated by Theodotion iron wheels, which we have rendered iron carts, the Seventy translated iron saws. It is, moreover, a kind of cart which rolls on iron, toothed wheels underneath, so that, when the grain has been shaken out, it may crush the stubble on the threshing floors and break up the chaff into fodder for the beasts of burden, because of the scarcity of hay. Again, where in the Hebrew there is Benadad, and it is a single name, the Seventy translated the sons of Ader, deceived by the likeness of the letters res and daleth. In that place too where we said, from the house of pleasure, which in Hebrew is mebbeth eden, and all translated it likewise, the Seventy translated from the men of Charran, taking daleth, the middle letter of the name, for res, and, according to their custom, putting the Greek chi before ain, the first Hebrew letter. Cyrene also, which in Hebrew is called cira, they translated ἐπήκλητον, that is, called or named, because of the likeness of the word, since carae can be said for calling, and they understood the proper name as a common noun. We have spoken briefly about the causes of the different translation; now let us pass on to the sense of what has been said. First the word of God is directed against Damascus, in which Hazael reigned, who, at the time when Amos was prophesying, was pressing hard upon Israel, that is, the ten tribes, so much so that, after Ahab king of Israel had been overcome at Ramoth-gilead, he slaughtered and crushed the whole region which was called Gilead, and which had fallen as an inheritance to the half-tribe of Manasseh, like threshing sledges threshing grain on the threshing floors. This the prophet Elisha speaks of more fully in the volume of Kings, where we read it written: And he stood with him, and was troubled until his face was flushed; and the man of God wept. Hazael said to him: Why does my lord weep? And he answered: Because I know what evils you are going to do to the sons of Israel: you will set their fortified cities on fire, and you will kill their young men with the sword, and you will dash the little ones, and you will rip up the women with child. And Hazael said: For what am I, your servant, a dog, that I should do this great thing? And Elisha said: The Lord has shown me that you will be king of Syria. But as for what follows in the prophet:

1:4-5

And I will send fire into the house of Hazael, and it will devour the houses of Ben-hadad. And I will break the bar of Damascus, and I will destroy the inhabitant from the plain of the idol. LXX: And I will send forth fire into the house of Hazael, and it will devour the foundations of the son of Ader. And I will break the bars of Damascus, and I will destroy the inhabitants from the plain of ὢν. He gives us to understand that Hazael, in the reigns of Uzziah and Jeroboam, had already died in Syria, and that his son Ben-hadad had succeeded him in the kingdom, from whom afterwards all the kings of Aram, that is, of Syria and Damascus, held the name Ben-hadad. Hence he fittingly did not say: I will send fire into Hazael, but into the house of Hazael, that is, into his royal house, over which his son Ben-hadad presided at that time. And I will break, he says, the bars of Damascus, and I will destroy the inhabitant from the plain of the idol: that is, all the strength of the Syrians, who, as with a kind of cart and with bars, kept off the onsets of their adversaries. But the plain of the idol, which in Hebrew is called auen, and which both the Seventy and Theodotion rendered ὢν, Symmachus and the Fifth edition translated iniquity; Aquila ἀνωφελοῦς, that is, useless, to show the vain help of idols, since the people of Damascus is taken captive when the Assyrian comes. For we read that Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, after killing Rezin king of Damascus, of whom Isaiah also speaks, carried away the whole people of Syria, which was called Aram, to Cyrene, and for this reason it is now said:

And him who holds the sceptre from the house of pleasure and luxury, and the people of Syria will be carried away to Cyrene, says the Lord. LXX: And I will cut down the tribe from the men of Charran, and the people of Syria, the named one, will be led captive, says the Lord. So that we understand: I will break and I will destroy; and the people of Syria, last of all, will be carried away into the borders of Egypt. And what is said at the beginning: For three crimes of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn him back, and according to the Seventy: Shall I not turn away from him?, that is, from the people of Damascus, or Aram, that is, the Syrian, can have this sense according to the history: If he had persecuted my people once, or a second time, I would grant pardon; but now, since he has shown himself cruel a third and a fourth time, so that he crushed the captive multitude with iron carts, ought I not to chastise him with blows? Ought I not to turn the face of my clemency away from him? But according to tropology we can say this: the first sin is to have thought what is evil; the second, to have consented to perverse thoughts; the third, to have carried out in deed what you have resolved in your mind; the fourth, not to repent after the sin, and to be pleased with oneself in one's own offence. This all the heretics do, who not only think and do evil, but by their doctrine deceive all the simple, and, after the manner of the people of Damascus, which is interpreted drinking blood, drink the blood of those whom they have deceived. For sins of this kind, says the Lord, is it not right that I should chastise them with blows and turn my face away from them? So that those who do not see the truth of my dogmas may be overwhelmed with darkness by the turning away of my face. For they have crushed, or cut asunder, with iron carts the women with child of Gilead. These are the women with child of whom the Lord speaks in the Gospel: Woe to those who are with child, and to those who give suck in those days. The women with child are the souls of believers, who at the beginning of faith can say: From fear of you, Lord, we have conceived and been in labour; and if they have not been cut up and pierced with iron by the carts and the iron and toothed wheels, of which the psalmist says: The sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, they can also say what follows: And we have brought forth; we have made the spirit of your salvation upon the earth. And consider at the same time that they cannot cut up men of mature age, but only those who are still in the womb, or who cannot take solid food and are still nourished with the milk of infancy. These women with child, moreover, are of Gilead, which is interpreted transfer or migration of the testimony. For they have migrated from the Church into heresy, and have transferred the testimonies of God which they had received before to foreign borders. But the Lord will send fire into the house of Hazael, which they themselves call Hazael, that is, vision of God; a fire to consume the wood and stubble in them, and to devour the foundations of the son of Ader. For we ought to speak according to the Seventy as well, lest we seem to have set them out to no purpose: Ader is interpreted εὐπρέπεια, which we can call comeliness, because all the discourse of the heretics, being elegant and well composed and having the beauty of worldly eloquence, deceives its hearers the more easily. And by the bars of Damascus, as it were very strong and very firm bolts, let those be understood who are equipped with the art of dialectic and with the arguments of the philosophers. For example: he is called the door who conceived and brought forth a false dogma, like Arius in Alexandria; its bars and its very firm bolts are Euticius and Eunomius, who try to strengthen it with syllogisms and enthymemes, or rather with sophisms and pseudomena and sorites, which were wickedly invented by others. And when the bars of Damascus have been crushed and broken, he will also destroy the inhabitants from the plain of ὢν, which is interpreted labour or pain. For all the heretics have tried with excessive labour and with the pain of seeking to find some order and consistency for their heresy. And I will cut down the tribe from the men of Charran, which in our tongue is rendered holes. For they have no windows through which the light of truth may enter in to them, but certain holes and hidden passages through which harmful and unclean animals come in. And Aram will be led captive, a people lofty and exalted, which has been lifted up into pride and promises itself heavenly things, so that it may be taken captive for its own salvation and, according to the Hebrew, be carried away to Cyrene, where the calling of God is.

1:6-8

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of Gaza, and for four, I will not turn him back, because he carried away a complete captivity, to shut it up in Idumea. And I will send fire into the wall of Gaza, and it will devour its houses; and I will destroy the inhabitant from Azotus, and him who holds the sceptre from Ashkelon, and I will turn my hand upon Ekron, and the remnant of the Philistines will perish, says the Lord. LXX: Thus says the Lord: For three impieties of Gaza, and for four, I will not turn away from them, because they led captive the captivity of Solomon, to shut it up in Idumea; and I will send forth fire upon the walls of Gaza, and it will devour its foundations, and I will destroy the inhabitants of Azotus, and the tribe will be taken away from Ashkelon, and I will bring my hand upon Ekron, and the remnant of the foreigners will perish, says the Lord. The Lord threatens in the law that he will requite the sins of the fathers upon the children to the third and fourth generation; not by an equality of judgment, so that some sin and others are punished, but by the greatness of his clemency, since he always awaits repentance; and what is committed in the first generation he does not rebuke and correct until the third and fourth generation has come. Something of this kind the apostle Paul also says to the Romans: Or do you despise the riches of his goodness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that the kindness of God leads you to repentance? But according to your hardness and impenitent heart you are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each one according to his works. What he says, then, is this: I waited a long time for them to repent, and for that reason I was unwilling to punish them when they sinned, so that at some time they might turn and receive healing. But because they do the same things a third and a fourth time, I am compelled to change my sentence and to correct the offenders with blows. Let us see, however, what the Philistines and their cities sinned, whom the Seventy always translated foreigners; for wherever in the Old Testament we read ἀλλοφύλους, that is, foreigners, we must take it not as a common name for all foreign nations, but properly as the Philistines, who are now called the people of Palestine. What did Gaza do to provoke the Lord to anger and vengeance? It carried away the complete captivity of the people of the Jews, and shut it up in Idumea, so as to sell to the Idumeans those whom it had taken from the Jews. For complete captivity the Seventy translated the captivity of Solomon, taking the Hebrew word salma, which means perfect and complete, for the name of Solomon. Accordingly Aquila translated ἀπηρτισμένην and ἀναπεπληρωμένην, Symmachus and Theodotion τελείαν, which signifies not Solomon but perfect and complete, so that none of the captives remained who was not handed over to the Idumeans. Hence he says that he will send fire upon the walls and houses, or foundations, of Gaza, to devour and swallow up everything. And because we read of five cities of the Philistines, which were struck with plagues on account of the wrong done to the ark, after Gaza he passes to Azotus, which is called Esdod, and again to Ashkelon, and with a terrible voice he threatens that he will destroy the inhabitants of Azotus, and him who holds the sceptre from Ashkelon, that is, the royal power, or the tribe of Ashkelon, so that no one may remain there, not even men of the last age, who support their trembling limbs with a guiding staff. And again he moves his hand to strike Ekron. And having enumerated four cities, he takes in the remaining cities of the Philistines in a single word: And the remnant of the Philistines will perish, by whom we must understand either the city of Gath, or all the villages which lie under the aforesaid cities. According to tropology it seems that it should be explained by us thus: Gaza, which in Hebrew is called Aza, means strength or dominion; Solomon, peaceful; the Idumean, bloodthirsty and earthly; Azotus, that is, Esdod, fire of the breast, or of the paternal uncle, although some, wrongly reading dor for dod, think it means fire of generation; Ashkelon is interpreted murderous fire, or hung up and weighed; Ekron, στείρωσις, that is, barrenness, and ἐκρίζωσις, which is interpreted uprooting. Gaza, therefore, which promises itself the strength and dominion of its own knowledge, is accused of having shut up in Idumea captives from the household of Solomon. These I take to be the teachers of the Jews, and all who follow the letter that kills and will not receive the spirit that gives life, but want whatever they interpret and understand to be earthly, and do not hear, with the disciples, the Lord expounding parables and saying to them: Those who have ears to hear, let them hear, especially since the Apostle understands a plain history as referring to the two testaments: one upon Mount Sinai, which is Hagar, and is in slavery with her children; the other upon Jerusalem the free, which is our mother. He says, then, that he will send fire upon the walls of Gaza and upon all its arguments, with which it strives to surround and defend its lies. And when he has sent fire upon the walls, its houses, or foundations, will be devoured, whatever it could have that was firm and built by the art of dialectic. From Azotus also, which itself too belongs to the foreigners, he will take away the inhabitant, whose breasts, because he follows the letter, are full not of milk but of fire, and who falsely claims to have the fire of the paternal uncle, or whose whole generation is to be consigned to the fires. And he will take away the sceptre, or the tribe, from Ashkelon, where the murderous fire is. For just as the fire of the Lord will save those whose hearts it has penetrated, so that fire which fell from heaven like lightning, which kindles the arrows of the devil, will kill whomever it breathes upon. He will move his hand also upon Ekron, where there is barrenness and uprooting. For whoever does not receive the spiritual understanding will hear through Hosea: Give them, Lord. What will you give them? Give them a barren womb and dry breasts. And so as to take in everything at once in a single word, he says that he will destroy all the remnant of the Philistines, either the city of Gath, in which is the winepress of the devil, or all the remaining Philistines, who are called falling by the cup. For when they have become drunk, they will fall down and wallow in their own vomit.

1:9-10

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of Tyre, and for four, I will not turn him back, because they shut up a perfect captivity in Idumea; and they did not remember the covenant of brothers, and I will send fire upon the wall of Tyre, and it shall devour its houses. LXX: Thus says the Lord: For three impieties of Tyre and for four I will not turn away from her, because they shut up the captivity of Solomon in Idumea, and they did not remember the testament of brothers. And I will send forth fire upon the walls of Tyre, and it shall devour its foundations. The same three and four impieties or crimes which he had imputed to Damascus, to Gaza, and to Azotus, and to Ashkelon, and to Ekron, and to the remaining cities of the Philistines, he heaps upon Tyre as well, and he reproaches it for having shut up a consummate and perfect captivity in Idumea, concerning which we spoke above: And they did not remember the covenant of their brothers. We ask how the Tyrians are brothers of the Jews? He here calls brothers those who are friends and joined by close ties, because Hiram, the prince of Tyre, had friendship with David and Solomon, to such a degree that he, for the building of the temple and of the palace, brought cedar wood on rafts as far as Joppa; and they bestowed grain and oil on the Tyrians, and gave them twenty cities in Bashan as a gift; which he refused to accept, because they were full of grass. Therefore he says that he will send fire upon its walls, and that it will devour its houses or foundations, which the prophet Ezekiel, in the vision of Tyre, records that Nebuchadnezzar king of the Chaldeans did, where he says: He will surround you with fortifications, and he will heap up a mound round about. And again: Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon made his army serve a great service against Tyre. Every head was made bald, and every shoulder stripped of hair, and wages were not paid to him, nor to his army, for Tyre, for the service with which he served me against her, because his army heaped up a mound for taking Tyre, which formerly was an island. And history relates that Alexander of Macedon also did this, so as to make out of the island a peninsula, which the Greeks call χεῤῥόνησον. Tyre means tribulation, or one who hems in; and every perverse doctrine strives to seize the truth, and to shut it up in earthly senses, and it did not remember the covenant of its brothers, that we ought to ascend from the letter to the spirit, from earthly things to heavenly ones; and that it is a precept for us to write out the sacred Scripture in our heart threefold. But God will send fire upon all the fortifications of Tyre, which will devour not only its walls but also its foundations. Concerning the captivity of Solomon, in place of which the Hebrew has consummate and perfect, we have spoken above.

1:11-12

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of Edom and for four I will not turn him back; because he pursued his brother with the sword, and violated his mercy, and held his fury beyond measure, and kept his indignation to the end; I will send fire into Teman, and it shall devour the houses of Bozrah. LXX: Thus says the Lord: For three impieties of Idumea and for four I will not turn away from them, because he pursued his brother with the sword, and violated the womb upon the earth, and seized his horror for a testimony, and kept his onset for ever; and I will send forth fire into Teman, and it shall devour the foundations of its walls. Edom is the same as Esau, so called because of the cooking of the red lentil, for the food of which he lost his birthright. He is called by another name Seir, hairy and shaggy. Hence the mountains of the Idumeans are called the mountains of Seir, and from Edom, in the Greek language, it is called Idumea. He pursued his brother with the sword, namely the sons of Jacob, concerning which it is written more fully in the blessings of Isaac, with which he blesses first Jacob and afterwards Esau; so that the hatred of the brothers was preserved in their descendants, and the Idumean nation persecuted its brothers to such a degree that it did not even grant them passage into the holy land when they were coming out of Egypt; and not only did he pursue his brother with the sword, but he violated his mercy, or his womb, or, as Symmachus translated more plainly, his own bowels; so that he forgot his brotherhood, and hardened the bowels of mercy, and did not know that he was a brother, and despised the womb of Rebekah, who in one birth had brought forth twin infants. And what follows: And he held his fury beyond measure, and kept his indignation to the end, shows an ancient hatred, because they were never joined to one another in peace. Hence he threatens punishment for their crimes, and says: I will send fire into Teman, which is a region of the Idumeans, and it lies toward the southern quarter, which is what Teman means; this province is called not only Teman but also Daron and Nageb, because it faces the east wind and the south wind and the south-west wind. And as for what he says: It shall devour the houses of Bozrah, it signifies, not another city, as some think, but Idumea, ὀχυρωμένην and fortified, according to that which we read in Isaiah: Who is this who comes from Edom, with garments dyed from Bozrah; this one beautiful in his robe, walking in the multitude of his strength? Whatever we have said about Esau and Jacob, let us refer to the Jews and the Christian people. For they, earthly and bloodthirsty, persecuted their brother Jacob, who supplanted them and took away their birthright, and they persecuted him with the sword, so that they confiscated the substance and possessions of the believers, as we read in the Acts of the Apostles; and they violated mercy and the rights of nature, and forgot their mother Rebekah, which means patience, who bore them together in Christ; and holding on to their ancient fury and wrath, to this day in their synagogues they blaspheme the Christian people under the name of Nazarenes, and provided only that they may kill us, they are willing to be burned with fire. But the Lord will send fire into Teman, into the deserted and dry places of Judea — which are by no means watered by the showers of the prophets —, and it will devour all their fortifications, or the foundations of its walls, so that, with every sense of the letter collapsing, the Church may be built up upon the foundations of Christ the Lord. In place of mercy the Seventy translated womb, and in place of fury, horror, and in place of indignation, onset, led by the ambiguity of the words, because rehem signifies both womb and mercy, aphpho both his fury and his horror; and ebrath is said both for indignation and for onset. Moreover armanoth, which we have translated houses, Aquila and Symmachus translated βάρεις, that is, houses, and Theodotion, inhabitants; the Seventy alone, both here and above, said foundations.

1:13-15

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of the sons of Ammon and for four I will not turn him back, because he ripped up the pregnant women of Gilead, to enlarge his border; and I will kindle fire on the wall of Rabbah, and it shall devour its houses with howling in the day of battle, and with a whirlwind in the day of commotion; and Melchom shall go into captivity, he and his princes together, says the Lord. LXX: Thus says the Lord: For three impieties of the sons of Ammon and for four I will not turn away from them, because he cut up the pregnant women of the Gileadites, that he might enlarge his borders; and I will kindle fire upon the walls of Rabbah, and it shall devour the foundations with shouting in the day of battle, and it shall be shaken in the day of its consummation; and the kings shall go into captivity, their priests and their princes together, says the Lord. The divine word rebukes the sons of Ammon, who were begotten of the stock of Lot, and dwell in Arabia, where Philadelphia now is, because under Saul, king of Judea, Nahash the Ammonite, their prince, laid waste Jabesh-gilead, and ripped up its pregnant women, that he might enlarge his borders and subject all Gilead to his rule. Hence he threatens that he will kindle fire on the walls of Rabbah, which was the metropolis of the Ammonites, so that its houses may be devoured amid the shouting and howling of the conquering army; in the day of commotion and of whirlwind, when, Nebuchadnezzar conquering, its captives will be led away, and the idol of the Ammonites, which is called Melchom, that is, their king, will be carried off to the Assyrians; and its princes, that is, its priests, will be led away together. Priests is not in the Hebrew, but princes. So the Seventy added priests, so that, if you wish to know who these princes are, you may hear: priests. Now between the cruelty of Damascus and that of the sons of Ammon there is this difference, that they crushed Gilead with iron carts, or the pregnant women of Gilead with iron saws; but these ripped up the pregnant women of Gilead, to enlarge their border. In the people of Damascus, who drink blood, are understood the heretics, who not only have broken the people of God away from the Church, but have also crushed them and ground them with iron teeth, of whom it is written: Who devour my people as a meal of bread? In the sons of Ammon, which means people of grief, or my people, we understand the schismatics, who do indeed separate a deceived multitude from the Church of God, and break off and divide untrained souls which have lately conceived the word of Christ; yet they do not do this with the cruelty with which the heretics slaughter all whom they have deceived, but, remaining in the same rule of faith, they desire to enlarge their borders and to send down to posterity a name of glory. Therefore the Lord threatens that he will set on fire the walls of Rabbah, that is, of the multitude, in the day of howling and of battle, of commotion and of whirlwind, and that their king, the author of the schism, will go captive, and that the princes will submit their necks to the yoke of the Church.

2:1-3

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of Moab and for four I will not turn him back, because he burned the bones of the king of Idumea even to ashes. And I will send fire into Moab, and it shall devour the houses of Kerioth, and Moab shall die in the noise, in the blast of the trumpet, and I will destroy the judge from the midst of it, and I will slay all its princes with him, says the Lord. LXX: Thus says the Lord: For three impieties of Moab and for four I will not turn away from them, because he burned the bones of the king of Idumea into ashes; and I will send forth fire into Moab, and it shall devour the foundations of its cities, and Moab shall die in weakness with shouting, and with the voice of the trumpet; and I will destroy the judge out of it, and I will slay all its princes, says the Lord. Not only the sons of Ammon, but Moab too was begotten of the seed of Lot, who was the son of Abraham's brother. And so that God may show himself to be the Lord of all, and that all souls belong to his dominion, who is their maker, he avenges the injury done even to the king of the Idumeans, or rather the crime which was committed against him by the Moabites, in that they burned his bones even to ashes, and did not end their savagery and rage even at his death. The Hebrews relate that the bones of the king of Idumea, who was already buried, and who had gone up with Joram king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah against Moab, were afterwards torn up and burned by the Moabites in revenge for their grief. For this reason, therefore, they say, God declares that he will send fire into Moab, either the metropolis of the Moabites, from which the whole province was named, or the whole province, so that it might devour the houses of Kerioth, which is the name of a city; although the Seventy translated of its cities, that is, of Moab; and that it will perish in the noise and in the howling of the conquering army, of which the one is called in Hebrew saon, the other therua; and in the blast of the trumpet or horn, for this is what sophar signifies. And when Moab has perished, the counsel of its princes and judges will be vain, its cities and its leaders being overthrown alike. But just as one ought not to carry off the perfect captivity, or that of Solomon, and shut it up in Idumea, so as to make lowly and earthly men out of lofty and heavenly ones, which Gaza and Tyre are recorded to have done, so we ought not to burn the bones of the king of Idumea and dissolve them into ashes and embers. The Jews transfer the spiritual understanding into Idumean flesh, and the royal sense, which is engaged in the letter, and is most solid and most firm, they enervate and break down with certain genealogies and superfluous traditions, and reduce to dust; and not only do they do this, but all the heretics, who would have God, after the manner of a human likeness, sit on a high and lofty throne and set his feet upon the earth, so that, of course, they may not dangle; and have a nose with which to smell the odor of a good fragrance, eyes with which to see, hands through which to work, feet by which to walk, ears with which to hear, a mouth with which to speak, teeth with which to grind his food. Whoever reads that Judah went in to Tamar the harlot and begot two sons of her, if he follows the shamefulness of the letter and does not ascend to the beauty of the spiritual understanding, burns the bones of the king of Idumea. Whoever thinks that Hosea took a wife of fornication, and perceives nothing more in the saying than is contained in the simple words, burns the bones of the king of Idumea. And for that reason the Lord sends fire into Moab, which means from the father, because he was indeed born of God, but has abandoned him. And it will devour its cities, or towns, which is what Kerioth means. Hence also Kiriath-jearim is turned into our language as village of the forests. And Moab will die in no other way than in shouting, and noise, and howling, and the blast of the horn, so that they may be overwhelmed by the lofty senses which in the holy books are compared to the blasts of the horn. Then the divine word destroys both the judges and the princes, and all who preside over earthly works, and it commands the teachers of the Church: Go up onto a high mountain, you who bring good tidings to Zion; lift up your voice, you who preach to Jerusalem.

2:4-5

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of Judah, and for four I will not turn him back, because he has cast away the law of the Lord, and has not kept his commandments; for their idols have deceived them, after which their fathers had gone, and I will send fire into Judah and it shall devour the houses of Jerusalem. LXX: Thus says the Lord: For three impieties of the sons of Judah, and for four I will not turn away from them, because they have rejected the law of the Lord, and have not kept his precepts; and they have been deceived in their vanities, which their fathers followed, and I will send forth fire into Judah, and it shall devour the foundations of Jerusalem. To the other cities and nations mentioned above, Damascus, Gaza and Azotus and Ashkelon and Ekron, and the remaining towns of the Philistines, to Tyre also and Idumea, and to the sons of Ammon and Moab, he does not reproach that they cast away the law of God and despised his commandments; for they did not have the written law, but the natural one. Hence he says that they violated their own bowels and the bowels of mercy, and crushed the pregnant women of Gilead with iron carts, and carried off the captivity of Solomon, or the perfect captivity, and shut it up in Idumea, and did not remember the covenant of their fathers, and pursued their brother with the sword; and that they burst forth into such, I will not say cruelty, but rage, that they burned the bones of the king of Idumea, and would not allow death to be the last of all evils. But Judah, in which, at the time when these things were being said, there were the religion of God and the temple and the ceremonies, and which had received the law, and the precepts, and the judgments, and the testimonies, and the commandments — on the difference among which there is a fuller discussion in the eighteenth (19th) psalm and in the hundred and eighteenth (119th) —, is rebuked and convicted by the Lord; and he is going to receive fitting punishments, because he cast away his law and did not keep his commandments. And because he cast these away and despised them, his idols deceived him in due order and course. For he could not have been deceived by idols before, unless he had rejected the law of the Lord and had not done his commandments. These are the idols after which their fathers went in Egypt, fashioning images of the Egyptian ox, and adoring Baal-peor, and worshipping Ashtaroth and the Baalim. Hence the Lord threatens that he will send fire into Judah also, to devour the houses and the foundations of Jerusalem, not of that Jerusalem in which the vision of peace abides, but of those which have arisen in various cities under the name of Jerusalem. Whatever we have said about Judah is referred to the Church, in which there is the true confession, and the peace of the Lord, and the vision of truth. And in this it is reproved, that it has despised the law of God and has not done his commandments, and that each one, adoring his own vices and sins, has begun to have as God that by which he has been overcome, as the apostle Peter says: For by what one is overcome, of this he is also the slave. The miser worships gold, the glutton his belly, the lustful man his member and Baal-peor; the wanton woman, who, living in pleasures, is dead, adores venereal pleasures. Hence the Lord will send fire into Judah, and it will devour the most wicked houses and the vicious foundations, which have destroyed the glory of Jerusalem; and at the same time we learn that the Creator of all has care not only of Judah and Israel but of all the nations; and that, according to the Apostle, those who have sinned without the law perish without the law, and those who have committed offences under the law are judged by the law of the Lord.

2:6-8

Thus says the Lord: For three crimes of Israel, and for four I will not turn him back, because he sold the righteous for silver and the poor for sandals, they who crush the heads of the poor upon the dust of the earth and turn aside the way of the humble; and a son and his father went in to the girl, to profane my holy name; and upon garments taken in pledge they reclined beside every altar, and they drank the wine of the condemned in the house of their God. LXX: Thus says the Lord: For three impieties of Israel, and for four I will not turn away from them, because he sold the righteous for silver, and the poor for the sake of sandals, which tread upon the dust of the earth; and they struck the heads of the poor with the fist, and turned aside the way of the humble, and a son and his father went in to the same girl, to defile the name of their God; and binding their garments with cords, they made awnings beside the altar, and they drank wine from false accusations in the house of their God. He puts Israel, that is, the ten tribes, last for this reason: because, as we said before, almost everything that follows was written to it, so that the prophetic discourse might keep the order of the book within one continuous text. Their first crime, then, or impiety, enumerated in three offences or four, is that for money they sold a man, and a righteous man, who is the more admirable in this, that he was not overcome even by poverty so as to do anything unjust; and if indeed, compelled by the necessity of hunger, they had sold the poor righteous man for a price, there would be some excuse for the crime; but as it is, for the cheapest of things, sandals, with which they tread dust and dung, they sold the precious soul of a man. These, according to the Seventy, strike the poor man on the head; according to the Hebrew, they crush the heads of the poor upon the dust of the earth, and they are lifted up into such pride that they are unwilling to walk with the humble. And lest this misdeed be thought small, a son and his father went in to one girl, to violate the holy name of God. Whatever, therefore, is done shamefully is referred to an injury against God, who says: Through you my name is blasphemed among the nations. This is what the Apostle also writes to the Corinthians: It is actually reported that there is fornication among you, and such fornication as is not even among the nations, so that someone has his father's wife. And you are puffed up, and have not rather mourned, so that he who has done this deed might be taken away from your midst. It often happens that a father defiles his son's wife, that is, a father-in-law his daughter-in-law; both of which are forbidden in the law: You shall not reveal the shame of your father, and you shall not uncover the shame of your daughter-in-law. And so the letter too has the greatest usefulness, if it is kept. And because every outrage, once it has passed the boundary of shame, adds crimes to crimes and always advances to worse things, the ten tribes even reclined upon garments taken in pledge beside every altar, which the Hebrews interpret thus: upon the garments of those who were unwilling to go to the idols, which they had extorted by force, they reclined, feasting in the idol's temple. But according to the Seventy, so great was their contempt for God that they stretched out with cords beside the altar their garments in which they sleep, or in which they commit fornication, and made παραπετάσματα, that is, coverings, so that no one could see them committing fornication in the temple; and, serving drunkenness and lust, they bought even the very wine that they drank, not from their own labor, but from false accusations; and they did this in the shrine of their god, so as to defile those whom they thought gods with shamefulness and debaucheries. So speak, and so expound, those who follow the simple history. But we, who have shown in Hosea that under the name of Israel, and of Samaria, and of Ephraim, and of the sons of Joseph — of which tribe was Jeroboam, who separated the people from the kingdom of David and from Jerusalem and from the temple of God — the heretics are signified, let us understand now too, after Judah and Jerusalem, which is interpreted as the Church, that the prophetic discourse is directed at the heretics, who sell a holy and righteous man, and a poor one too, for money. The poor and righteous man is the churchman who does not have knowledge of the Scriptures, but, content with simplicity, does the commandments that have been enjoined, of whom it is written: The poor man does not endure threatening. And to the Galatians: Only that we should be mindful of the poor. All these things the heretics do for riches, and for sandals, with which they tread the dust of the earth; for because they cannot stand with bare feet upon the holy ground — on which Moses stood, and Joshua the son of Nun; whence the apostles too are commanded to go with bare feet, so that they may have nothing of death and of skins, which are referred to the flesh —; therefore they strike the poor on the head. And the Savior commanded his disciples that if by chance, since they were still dwelling on the earth, anything mortal had clung to their works, they should shake the dust off their feet. And the heretics strike the heads of the poor, although this is not read in the Hebrew, because they have turned aside the way of the humble. The way of the humble is that which says: I am the way, and the life, and the truth, which calls us to walk along it, and says: Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. But all the chiefs of the heretics swell with pride, of whom the Apostle also speaks: Lest, being puffed up, he fall into the judgment of the devil; who says: I will ascend into heaven, above the stars of heaven I will set my nest, I will be like the Most High. Hence the proud heretics God resists, but to the humble churchmen he gives grace. The son and the father, too, went in to one girl, to violate and profane the holy name of God. That the people of the Jews is our father, we often read, as Paul says: For I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea. And in another place: Ask your fathers, and they will declare to you, your elders, and they will tell you. And again: In place of your fathers sons have been born to you. Hence also, in the Church of the nations, it says: The sons of my mother have fought against me. We, therefore, are the sons, and the father is the people of the Jews. We sin and commit a crime when we go in with our parent to the observance of the sabbath, the injury of circumcision, the ceremonies of the abolished law; to whom the Apostle says: Behold, I Paul say to you, that if you are circumcised, Christ will profit you nothing. And again: Those who are justified in the law have fallen from grace. Whoever enters the Church in such a way as to keep the law within the Gospel, he goes in with his father to the girl, and commits debauchery, and violates the name of God. Hence those who say that it does the Jews no harm, after the coming of Christ, if they so believe in God as to keep the precepts of the law as well, defile father and son in one fornication. But also, binding their garments with cords, they make coverings beside the altar, holding out the faith of Christ as a screen. For as many as have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ, and they set their garments, which they have bound with the cords of sins, before the eyes of those who look, so that their outrages may not be seen, and so that whoever looks at the altar may not suspect the debauchery. This all the heretics do, committing fornication under the Christian name, and spreading the testimonies of the Scriptures over their errors and lies as a screen. Hence the apostles too spread their garments on the foal of the ass, so that the Lord might sit more softly, and might mount the road paved from the law and the prophets. On the contrary, the heretics do not spread their garments beneath the footsteps of the Savior; but they bind them beside the altar and hold them out before their sins, so that they may drink the wine of false accusation, or of the condemned, who are condemned from the very fact that they have gone out of the Church. This is the wine of Sodom, of the fury of dragons and of asps; whoever drinks it cannot be healed. And they drink the wine, not in the house of God, where the temple is and Jerusalem, but in the house of their god, whom they have fashioned for themselves with artful speech.

But I destroyed the Amorite from before their face, whose height was the height of the cedars, and he was strong as an oak; and I crushed his fruit above, and his roots beneath. It is I who made you go up from the land of Egypt, and I led you out in the desert forty years, that you might possess the land of the Amorite; and I raised up of your sons for prophets, and of your young men Nazirites. LXX: But I took away the Amorite from before their face, whose loftiness was as the height of the cedar, and he was strong as an oak; and I dried up his fruit above, and his roots below. I led you out of the land of Egypt, and led you around in the desert forty years, that you might possess the land of the Amorites; and I took of your sons for prophets, and of your young men for sanctification. You indeed sold the just man for silver and the poor man for sandals, striking his head, or crushing the heads of the poor in the dust, and you turned aside the way of the humble, so far that father and son went in to one girl and violated, or profaned, my holy name; and your garments, polluted by brothels and fornication, you bound with the chains of sins, so that you committed adultery in the holy place and, joining injustice to drunkenness, drank another's wine, and did this in the house of your God. But I, on the contrary, in return for your evils, which you paid back to me, bestowed good things on you: so that, as you were coming out of Egypt, I destroyed before your face Sihon king of the Amorites, who was as lofty and as strong as a cedar and an oak; and I crushed his fruit above, and his roots below. I led you out of Egypt, and through forty years I made you come to the holy land by a long circuit, that you might possess the land of the Amorite, of which we spoke above, which Moses divided to the sons of Reuben and Gad and to the half-tribe of Manasseh; and after such great benefits I added this as well, that I took prophets to myself from your sons, and from your young men, or chosen ones, made Nazirites, whom the Seventy translated as sanctified. Nor indeed, where the praises of God are spoken of, must the order of the history be kept; but it often happens that what was done first is told last, and what was done last is set at the beginning. This the seventy-seventh (78th) psalm and the hundred and fourth (105th), where the power of the signs, not their order, is described, will be able to teach us, and so will the titles of the psalms, of which let us set down only two examples, those of the third psalm and of the fifty-first (52nd), where what was done earlier is narrated last, and what we read as last is placed at the beginning. For in the volume of Kings we read that what concerns Doeg the Edomite, which is the title of the fifty-first (52nd) psalm, was written before Absalom rose against his father, which is noted at the head of the third psalm in its title. So too the Amorite was destroyed, or blotted out, last, which is now told first; and his making them go up from Egypt, and afterwards leading them out into the wilderness for forty years, we read was done at the beginning, things which are told last here, the order being changed. Therefore, before God led us out of the land of Egypt, out of the iron furnace, he destroyed before our face the Amorite, who is called bitter, or λαλητὸς, that is, speaking, or famous and celebrated in frequent talk. Now this Amorite and king of the Amorites is called Sihon, an unfruitful and barren tree, not because it bears no fruit, but because it brings forth bad fruit, of which it is said: Every tree that does not bear good fruit will be cut down and cast into the fire. And of the false prophets we read: Who come in the clothing of sheep, and inwardly are ravening wolves; by their fruits you will know them, by their bad fruits, that is. Whether, then, it bears no fruit, or bears fruit but not good fruit, it is called an unfruitful tree. This Sihon, of whom it is written: Already the axe is laid to the roots of the trees, because he was an unfruitful tree, was cut down when the Lord struck him; his height is compared to the height of the cedars, of which we read: I saw the wicked man exalted on high and lifted up like the cedars of Lebanon; and I passed by, and behold, he was not; and I sought him, and his place was not found. And he fittingly says that he passed by, because from him who passes over from the world, and can say: Passing over, I will see this great vision, the cedar is taken away, and the place of pride cannot be found. His strength too, like that of the oak, a tree most hard and most strong. From its name Philo, the most eloquent man of the Hebrews, thinks that Esau was called δρύινον, that is, oaken and made of hard oak, although Esau can also be understood as ποίημα, that is, a thing made, so as to be referred to evil works. Of this strong and sturdy one he speaks in the Gospel: When a strong man armed guards his court, the things he possesses are in peace; but if one stronger than he comes upon him and overcomes him, he will take away all his armour, in which he trusted, and will distribute his spoils. And the Lord granted us that he should crush from above the fruits of this Amorite Sihon, whose name we said means an unfruitful tree, because they were bad, and take them away, lest anyone, thinking them good, should eat and perish. His roots too he cut down and broke in pieces, so that nothing of the evil tree might sprout afterwards; and the Lord himself made us go out from the world and, through forty years, a number which always belongs to affliction and fasting, to mourning and sorrow, come through tribulations and straits to the holy land, that we might first possess the land of the Amorite, and the region that was once his might become our possession; and that afterwards he might raise up prophets from our sons, all the holy men who received the prophetic spirit, of whom we read more fully in the first epistle to the Corinthians; and that from our young men, or chosen ones, he might take Nazirites and sanctified ones, who, offering their souls to God in sacrifice, do not touch wine, which can make men drunk and overturn the state of the mind, so that they may have the hair of Samson, in whose head, because the head of a man is Christ, his strength and victory resided.

While I was bestowing such great benefits on you, so as to kill your adversaries and hand their land over to you, and to choose prophets and Nazirites from your sons and young men and consecrate them to my worship. If they follow the letter in all things, and thrust Jewish fables upon the churches of Christ, then they ought also to let their hair grow, and not eat grapes, dried or fresh, and not go in over a dead father and mother; and if perhaps they have done these things, and have been overcome either by human frailty or by necessity, let them shave their heads, and let all the days of their consecration and their toil be void. But if they do not do these things, and cannot, after the manner of Jewish innkeepers, mix water with wine, shadows with the truth, the constraint of the law with the grace of the Gospel, let them understand that drunkenness to be the one by which the vigour of the soul is made drunk and overwhelmed with worldly cares, and understand that it is those who charge the prophets, saying: Do not prophesy, who, overcome by envy, forbid learned men to bring forth the word of doctrine. And while the Lord says: Go and say to this people, they on the contrary command that you not speak in the name of the Lord, above all if he who teaches looks not to shameful gain, glory and boasting, but to the profit of those who read and hear.

Behold, I will creak beneath you, as a cart loaded with hay creaks, and flight shall perish from the swift, and the mighty shall not hold his strength, and the strong shall not save his soul, and he who holds the bow shall not stand, and the swift of foot shall not be saved, and the rider of the horse shall not save his soul, and the strong of heart among the mighty shall flee naked in that day, says the Lord. LXX: Therefore behold, I will roll beneath you as a cart full of stubble is rolled, and flight shall perish from the runner, and the mighty shall not hold his might, and the fighter shall not save his soul, and the archer shall not endure, and the swift of foot shall not be able to be saved, and the horseman shall not save his soul, and his heart is found among the powerful, and he shall flee naked in that day, says the Lord. While I was bestowing benefits on you, and leading you out of the land of Egypt, and killing the Amorite before your face so that you might possess his land, and raising up prophets from your sons and Nazirites from your young men, you were making my Nazirites drunk with wine and saying to the prophets: Do not prophesy. Therefore, just as a cart weighed down with a load of stubble or hay howls far off with creaking and noise, so I, no longer bearing your sins, and handing them over to the fire like stubble, will cry out and say: Flight shall perish from the swift, whom the Hebrews understand as Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who had earlier fled into Egypt. Here, however, let us take not the princes themselves but their houses and their offspring. And the mighty shall not hold his strength — the mighty one they interpret as Baasha, who was most ready for wars —, and the strong shall not save his soul — this one here they take to be Omri —, and he who holds the bow shall not stand, or endure, they think was said of Jehu the son of Nimshi, who struck Joram king of Israel with an arrow. And the swift of foot shall not be saved: they understand Menahem, who, hastening in vain, sent gifts to the king of the Assyrians. And the rider of the horse shall not save his soul: this one they interpret as Pekah the son of Remaliah, who, joined with Aram, that is, the Syrian, laid waste much of Judea under Ahaz its king. And the strong of heart, among the mighty, shall flee naked in that day, says the Lord. Hoshea alone, who was the last king of the ten tribes and tried to call the straying people back to the worship of God, will come out of the fire as if naked. And he calls him naked because under him the ten tribes were taken captive. This is what the Hebrews hold, and as it was handed down to us by them, so we have faithfully set it out for our own people. But now let us return to our own. God threatens that he will roll a cart weighed down with hay or stubble beneath them, so that, since they have no grain to be stored up in barns, their hay and stubble may be burned up in the fire. This is the hay of which the prophet says: Let them become like the hay of the buildings, which withers before it is plucked up. And: All flesh is hay. And the stubble is that over whose burning the prophet laments so mournfully, saying: Woe is me. For I have become like one who gathers stubble in the harvest, and like grapes at the vintage, when there is no cluster to eat, the first-ripe. Woe is me. O soul, for the reverent man has perished from the earth, and there is none among men who does what is right: all are judged in blood, and the rest. This is the hay and stubble of which the Apostle also speaks: Wood, hay, stubble, each one's work will become manifest, because in the day of the Lord it will be revealed in fire. And the cart that is rolled is the one of which we read in Isaiah: The land of Moab shall be trampled down, as a threshing floor is trampled with carts. And in another place: I will make you, he says, like the new wheels of a cart with the teeth of saws, and you shall crush the mountains and grind the hills to pieces, so that, after you have crushed the mountains and hills, flight may perish from the swift, as Paul says: Do you not know that those who run in the stadium all run indeed, but one receives the prize. And in another place: You were running well; who hindered you, so that you should not obey the truth? Hence, fearing for himself as well, he says: Not that I have already received, or am perfect; but I run, if I may lay hold of that in which I also have been laid hold of by Christ. All these things he did, lest flight perish from the swift; and for this reason, when he had now reached the finishing mark and received the prize of victory, he said in security: I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; for the rest, there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me on that day; and not to me only, but also to all who love his coming. If we flee with a quick step, sin will not be able to catch us; but if malice binds our feet, we shall speak with the prophet to God: Where shall I go from your spirit, and where shall I flee from your face? Of bad runners the Apostle speaks: It is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy. There follows: And the mighty shall not hold his strength; not that he is mighty, but that he boasts of being mighty. Or he is the one who trusts in his own strength and not in the mercy of God, according to what is written: I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and the understanding of the prudent I will reject; not that true wisdom can perish and the understanding of the truth be rejected, but that the wisdom of those perishes who seem wise in their own eyes and trust in their own learning. The strong one too, or the fighter, who will not save his soul, is he who by no means possesses the armour of the Apostle: having a shield, but not of faith; girded about the loins, but not in truth; clothed with a breastplate, but not of righteousness; bearing a sword, but not of salvation. A fighter of this sort does not sanctify the battle, nor can he fight the wars of the Lord, since he struggles against the truth on behalf of falsehood. Such a fighter cannot say: Blessed be the Lord my God, who teaches my hands for battle and my fingers for war. My mercy and my refuge. The heretics too have archers, who, bending the bow in vain, will not be able to withstand the arrow of the Lord, who speaks through Isaiah: But I am like a chosen arrow in the quiver of the Lord. These are the archers of whom David also sings: Behold, sinners have bent the bow, they have made ready their arrows in the quiver, to shoot in the dark at the upright of heart. And the swift of foot, he says, shall not be saved: he who, because of the sharpness of his wit, runs about through the testimonies of the Scriptures and strives to crush the truth with the eloquence of orators or the sophisms of dialecticians; and in this he is tripped and falls, because he trusts not in the Lord but in his own feet. The rider of the horse, too, shall not save his soul, he who does not know what was said through the prophet: The horse is deceitful for salvation. And he does not know that it is written: All who mounted horses have slumbered. This man will not save his soul, but as he perishes he will hear: Some in chariots, and some on horses, but we will call upon the name of the Lord our God. They were hindered and have fallen, but we have risen and stand upright. And the strong also of heart among the mighty shall flee naked. This passage can be explained in two ways: either that he can escape for this reason, that he has stripped off the old man and the skin tunics of sins, and was weighed down by no burden; or, on the contrary, that because he is naked and has lost the garment of Christ, of which it is said in the Apostle too: Put on Christ Jesus, and in another place: If indeed, being clothed, we are not found naked, his strength will profit him nothing, but in the day of battle and contest he will flee from his pursuers and, unable to resist without the armour of the Apostle, will turn his back to the enemy.

Hear the word which the Lord has spoken concerning you, sons of Israel; concerning every kindred which I brought out of the land of Egypt, saying: Solely you have I known of all the kindreds of the earth; therefore I will visit upon you all your iniquities. LXX: Hear this word which the Lord has spoken concerning you, house of Israel, and against every tribe which I brought out of the land of Egypt, saying. Nevertheless, you have I known of all the tribes of the earth; therefore I will take vengeance upon you for all your wickednesses. The prophetic word is directed to the sons of Israel, and who these sons of Israel are the following verse shows: Concerning every kindred, he says, which I brought out of the land of Egypt, which we must take to refer not only to the ten tribes but to all twelve, adding Judah and Benjamin. For he brought them all out of the land of Egypt, and he says: Solely — or, as Symmachus translated, Only —, you have I known of all the kindreds of the earth. And because I, who am the Creator of all, have known you alone, and have held you as my peculiar people, therefore upon you alone I will repay all your sins: For the mighty will suffer torments mightily; but he who is least is worthy of mercy. And in Ezekiel we read: Begin from my saints. And the judgment of God is said to have its beginning from his own house. Let this, meanwhile, have been said according to the history. Moreover, since hearing in the holy Scriptures is taken to mean not only that which we receive with the ears of the flesh, but also that of which the Lord says: He who has ears to hear, let him hear, and since for this reason all the people also saw the voice of God, the word of the Lord is properly addressed to those who see the Lord with their understanding, whom he brought out of the land of Egypt and out of the power of Pharaoh — so that they should by no means serve with mud and straw, building the cities of Egypt —; and whom he has known out of all the tribes of the earth, as the Apostle says: But now knowing God, or rather being known by him. And in another place: He who is ignorant will be ignored. The Lord, then, does not know all, but those who are worthy of his knowing them, as he says in the Gospel: Depart from me, workers of iniquity, I do not know you. For in that they are workers of iniquity, they are unknown to the Lord. Therefore, he says, because I have known you alone and have held you as mine, I will visit upon you all your iniquities. Whom the Lord loves, he reproves, and he chastises every son whom he receives. And he said well, I will visit, and not, I will strike, because the stroke of God is a visitation and a healing. And all your iniquities, he says, or your sins, I will visit, so that nothing may remain unstruck, and nothing fail to receive healing.

3:3-8

Will two walk together unless they have agreed? Will a lion roar in the forest unless he has prey? Will the lion's whelp give voice from his den unless he has seized something? Will a bird fall into the snare of the earth without a fowler? Will the snare be taken up from the earth before it has caught something? If the trumpet sounds in the city, will the people not be terrified? Will there be evil in the city which the Lord has not done, for the Lord God will not do a thing unless he has revealed his secret to his servants the prophets. The lion will roar, who will not fear? The Lord God has spoken, who will not prophesy? LXX: Will two walk together at all unless they have known each other? Will a lion bellow from his forest, having no prey? Will the lion's whelp give his voice from his den at all unless he has snatched something? Will a bird fall upon the earth without a fowler? Will a snare be set down upon the earth unless it has caught something? If the trumpet sounds in the city, will the people not fear? Will there be evil in a city which the Lord has not done, for the Lord God will not do anything unless he has revealed his instruction to his servants the prophets. The lion will bellow, and who will not fear? The Lord God has spoken, and who will not prophesy? I have read in the commentaries of a certain man, who wished to win assent to a difficult thing, that to the eight crimes, or impieties and sins, first of Damascus, second of Gaza and the other cities of Palestine, third of Tyre, fourth of Idumea, that is, Edom, fifth of the sons of Ammon, sixth of Moab, seventh of Judah, that is, the two tribes, eighth, which is the last, of Israel, that is, the ten tribes, as many threats are now given in return, and that the first answers to the first, that is, Damascus, the second to the second, that is, Gaza, and the rest to the rest. Whether this is true, he who wrote it will know for himself. Another, however, thinks that six examples are set forth, of two walking together on the road, and of a lion roaring in the forest, and of a lion's whelp giving its voice when it has seized something, and of a bird caught by a fowler, and of a snare which is fixed in the ground to catch prey, and of a trumpet sounding in the city, and that to these examples a likeness is given in return: as the things above have a cause and do not happen unless that cause has gone before, so no evil befalls in a city which has not been done at the Lord's command. There are some who think that the first example contains a teaching: that two cannot walk together unless they have first agreed with each other in him who made both one, and the middle wall of the partition destroyed, and fashioned the two into one new man; and he made peace between them, and joined them with the bond of love, so that, when two are together, they may ask of the Father and obtain whatever they have asked. These are the two rods which are joined to each other in Ezekiel, and the two peoples, of the circumcision and of the Gentiles, of which the Lord speaks to Elijah: I have left for myself seven thousand men who have not bent the knee before Baal. And in another place God: I was found, he says, by those who did not seek me; I appeared to those who did not ask for me. But when the two have been brought together into one, they will cleave to the Lord, and one spirit will come to be: For he who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit. These two, then, who agree with each other and walk together in the way of Christ, can fear the assault of none of their adversaries. But if they are separated by discord, at once the roaring lion will attack them, of whom the apostle Peter also says: Your adversary the devil, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking whom he may devour. This roaring lion does not move about in sown fields, not in vineyards, not in olive groves, nor among the fruit-bearing trees, which praise God, but where there are forests and untilled ground and wild beasts dwell. And not only will they lie open to the lion's bites, but the lion's whelp will seize them and lead them to his den, fleeing the light and making for the darkness. For everyone who sins loves the darkness and hates the light; of these we read in the psalm too: You have set darkness, and it became night; in it all the beasts of the forest will pass through. The lions' whelps roaring to seize their prey, and to seek their food from God. For the lion's whelp seeks not the prey which he already possesses, which is in his own power, but to snatch from the Church of Christ; of him it is written in Habakkuk: His foods are choice. The third punishment of those whom discord has separated is that they lose the wings by which they were formerly borne on high, and fall headlong from heaven to earth, and are held by the fowler, who is better called ἰξευτὴς, because with clinging birdlime he catches free birds which of their own will come down to earthly things. Of these it is written in Proverbs: Not unjustly are nets spread for birds. For just is the ruin of sinners, who, though they had the wings of a dove and ought to have flown through the air, were pressed down to earthly things by the weight of their sins, and through their own vice stuck fast to the birdlime. Of this we read in the Apostle: He who cleaves to a harlot becomes one body with her. The fourth punishment of those in discord is that they are caught in the snare, which is set not in heaven but on the earth; the saint glories in having escaped it, and says: Our soul, like a sparrow, has been snatched from the snare of the hunters; the snare is broken, and we are set free. This is the broken snare of which the Apostle speaks to believers: God will crush Satan under your feet swiftly. And again we read in David: Beside the path they have set a stumbling block for me. For they cannot deceive any of the simple among believers unless they put forward the name of Christ, so that, while we think we are finding Christ, we go on to the Antichrist. The fifth punishment is that of those who have abandoned the peace which surpasses all understanding, and which the Lord, as he went to the Father, left to the apostles, saying: My peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you: that, though set in the city of the Lord, they are terrified by the sound of the trumpet. For whatever is said in the holy Scriptures is a threatening trumpet, piercing the ears of believers with a great voice. If we are righteous, we are summoned by the trumpet of Christ to blessedness; if sinners, we hear what torments we are going to suffer. But the evil which the Lord does in the city is not the contrary of virtue, but affliction and torment, of which we read: Sufficient to the day is its own evil, that is, tribulation and distress. And in the prophet we read: I am God, who made light and darkness, who make peace and create evils. For just as darkness is contrary to light and day, so war too is contrary to peace; it is not evil in itself, but seems to be evil to those who endure it. And that we may know that in the holy Scriptures evil is sometimes taken not as the contrary of virtue but as affliction, punishments and distress, let us take one more example, from Jonah: And God saw their works, that they had turned from their most wicked ways, and he repented over the evil which he had spoken of doing to them. The evil there is surely the overthrow of the Ninevites, which God had threatened through the prophet, and not the evil contrary to virtue, which belongs to sin, on account of which he was threatening torments to sinners. There follows: The Lord God will not do a thing unless he has revealed his secret, or his correction, to his servants the prophets; not that God reveals to the prophets everything that he does in heaven or has already done before, but what he is going to do on earth. He revealed to Noah his servant that he was going to bring on the flood. He revealed to Abraham and Lot his servants that Sodom and Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim were going to burn. He revealed to Joseph the interpretation of the dream of the seven years of famine in Egypt, and to the prophet Jonah the overthrow of Nineveh, so that those who heard of the punishments to come might either repent and avoid the torments, or, if they scorned them, be punished more justly. So now too the Lord reveals through Amos his servant and prophet what he is going to do to the ten tribes, so that, if they are turned to better things and abandon their idols, they may be freed from the danger that threatens. And note that God, merciful and kind, always foretells what is to come, so that he may not be forced to inflict punishments. And what the heretics slanderously charge against the Creator, as though he were severe and savage, cruel, and nothing but a judge, because there is no evil in a city which he does not do, this we refer to the greatness of his mercy, in that he does not inflict punishments unless he has foretold them beforehand. And he who foretells does not wish to punish sinners. The lion, he says, will roar, and who will not fear? The Lord God has spoken, who will not prophesy? The word who is taken in this passage not for the impossible, but for the rare. For he will not fear, and he will not prophesy, who because of his hardness has scorned to hear the Lord. And the sense according to the history is this: If at the voice of the lion all things tremble and the whole nature of living creatures is terrified, shall we, when God commands us to speak and announce to the people the punishments to come, not prophesy? Shall we not speak? I know that a certain man has left it written in his commentaries that the roaring lion is to be understood as the devil, roaring at those who are going to perish, but that God, who speaks through the prophets, is to be referred to the Lord the Savior; so that, as they hear the lion's roaring in the bad sense and are taken captive unto death, so the saints may hear the Lord commanding in the good sense, and be saved.

Make it heard in the houses of Azotus, and in the houses of the land of Egypt; and say: Gather yourselves upon the mountains of Samaria, and see the many madnesses in the midst of her, those who suffer calumnies in her inner chambers; and they did not know how to do what is right, says the Lord, treasuring up iniquity and plunder in their houses. LXX: Announce to the regions among the Assyrians, and to the regions of the land of Egypt; and say: Gather yourselves upon the mountain of Samaria, and see many wonders in the midst of her, and the oppression in her; and she did not know what things are to come before her, says the Lord, who treasure up iniquity and misery in their regions. We said above that the prophet Amos prophesies especially, indeed in the great part of the volume, to the ten tribes, which are called Israel and Ephraim, and Samaria. And so now too he is commanded to announce to Azotus and to the land of Egypt the things that follow. In place of Azotus the Seventy, intending I know not what, put the Assyrians, although Azotus in the language of the Hebrews is called Esdod, and the Assyrians, Assur. For the houses too, which are called armanoth, they translated regions, which are called sadoth, and the words have nothing of likeness. Say, he says, that all the nations which are in the neighbourhood should gather and see the crimes of Israel, lest they think the judgment of God unjust. As if he were saying: First see what they do, and then approve my sentence. And he fittingly added: Upon the mountains of Samaria, whether of the city, which is now called Sebaste and was then called Samaria, or of the whole province, which is situated in mountainous country: And see the many madnesses in the midst of her, that in place of God they worship calves and sacrifice their sons, and, not content with this crime, they bring calumnies against the poor in their inner chambers, and do not know at all how to do what is right, treasuring up for themselves not gold and money, which are sometimes gained by labour, but iniquities and plunder in their houses; so that, because they abandoned the worship of God and worshipped idols, he delivers them, God does, to a reprobate mind, to do what is not fitting. And since, once and for all, according to the laws of tropology, Samaria is referred to the heretics, who falsely claim to be the guardians of the commandments of God, the divine word commands that they preach among the nations which have no knowledge of God, in whose teaching there is a burning fire, and tribulation and anguish, that they may consider the false Samaria, and go up to the mountains of her pride, and see the many madnesses in the midst of the city, while each one fashions what he wishes and worships his own fiction: as Marcion a God who is good and idle; as Valentinus thirty αἰῶνας (aeons) and a Christ last of all, whom he calls ἔκτρωμα, that is, an abortion; as Basilides, who calls the almighty God by the monstrous name ἀβράξας (Abraxas), and says that this same name, according to the Greek letters, and the number of the yearly course, is contained in the circle of the sun, whom the heathen, under the same number in other letters, call Μείθραν (Mithras). And the Iberian follies marvel at Balsamus and Barbelo. Are these not madnesses, and many madnesses, when each one fashions whatever has fallen into his mind? They also suffer calumny in her inner chambers, that is, all the simple among the believers are oppressed, while under the name of truth they worship a lie; and they do not know at all how to do what is right, because they have lost the path of truth, nor do they believe in him who says: I am the truth; and they treasure up for themselves the dogmas of iniquity, and they seize and shut up in their houses those whom they have been able to deceive by false error. But if it please us to read Assyrians — which, however, is not found in the Hebrew —, let us say that these are the ones of whom it is written: I will bring it upon the great mind, the prince of the Assyrians, who said: By my strength I will do it, and so on, so that the heretics are taken captive by the Assyrian and held in the land of Egypt, from which, coming now to the Church, they have been set free.

Therefore thus says the Lord God: The land shall be afflicted and surrounded, and your strength shall be pulled down from you, and your houses shall be plundered. LXX: Therefore thus says the Lord God: Tyre, and your land round about, shall be deserted, and he will pull down your strength from you, and your regions shall be plundered. In place of Tyre, which in the Hebrew is written with two letters, sade and res, and is called Sor, as Aquila and the Seventy likewise translated it, the Hebrew who instructed me in the holy Scriptures interpreted tribulation; nor do we reject his opinion, because Symmachus too, who is not accustomed to follow the κακοζηλίαν (affectation) of words but the order of the understanding, says siege and encircling of the land. In place of siege, which he calls πολιορκία, strength was put by Theodotion, who thought that what should be read was not Sar and Sor, which means tribulation or Tyre, but sur, which properly refers to a very hard rock, which in Greek is called ἀκρότομος, and which we in Latin can call flint. Let us speak, then, of each in turn. The LXX said: Tyre, and your land all around. And the sentence seemed to hang unfinished; so they added on their own: Shall be deserted; nor is it an error of the translators, where, because of the ambiguity of the word, both tribulation and Tyre can be said. And the sense is: Upon the mountains of Samaria my people has committed many madnesses, practising calumny, and utterly ignorant of what is right, and treasuring up for itself iniquity and plunder; therefore it shall be afflicted, or crushed, and their land shall be surrounded by the Assyrian army, and there shall be pulled down from you, he says, O Samaria, O ten tribes, O once my people, your strength; and there shall be plundered in your houses what you gathered by calumny from the tears of the wretched. The heretics too will either be afflicted in the day of judgment, and all their strength will be worn down, and what they had gained from plunder will be plundered, so that the peoples whom they had deceived may be set free; or certainly they will be afflicted daily by churchmen, and surrounded by the testimonies of the Scriptures, and there will be pulled down from them the strength of the syllogisms and the subtleties of words by which they had confirmed their dogma. And their houses will be plundered, so that those who had been carried off from the Church may return to the Church.

Thus says the Lord: As if a shepherd should rescue from the mouth of the lion two legs, or the tip of an ear, so shall the sons of Israel be rescued who dwell in Samaria in the region of the couch and on the pallet of Damascus. LXX: Thus says the Lord: As if a shepherd should tear away from the mouth of the lion two legs, or the tip of an ear, so shall the sons of Israel be torn away who dwell in Samaria over against the tribe and in Damascus. At the beginning of Amos, where we discussed those little verses: The Lord will roar from Zion, and from Jerusalem he will utter his voice; and the beautiful places of the shepherds mourned, and the top of Carmel was dried up, we said that he used the language of his own craft, so that, because as a herdsman of flocks he had known nothing more terrible than the lion, he compared the wrath of the Lord to lions. According to this sense, then, now too he takes an example from what he had often seen, and so he tells that the small remnant of the ten tribes is to be rescued from the hands of the Assyrians: as if a shepherd, when all the other limbs have been devoured, should snatch two legs or the tip of an ear from the lion's jaws; and, having set down the example for the comparison, so, he says, shall the sons of Israel be rescued, namely the ten tribes that dwell in Samaria, in the region of the couch and on the pallet of Damascus. This seems to me to be explained thus: We read in Isaiah that Rezin king of Aram, that is, of the Syrians, who reigned in Damascus, and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, having made a league, came fighting against Judah; concerning whom the Lord commands Ahaz king of Judah and Jerusalem not to fear the two half-burnt firebrands, when, as a sign of full liberty, both the conception and the childbearing of a virgin are shown to him. The sons of Samaria, then, dwell in the region and quarter of the couch, resting on the help of the Syrians, and promising themselves victory on the pallet of Damascus, so that, as one who is weary is refreshed on a couch, so they might strengthen their broken forces by the help of a neighbouring nation. Hence also in the same Isaiah it is said, by a type of things to come, that a child is to be born who, before he knows how to call father and mother, will take the spoils of Samaria and the power of Damascus, which, that is, were fighting against Jerusalem with their forces joined. For the region of the couch and the pallet of Damascus the Seventy translated: Over against the tribe and in Damascus, so that according to tropology we may refer the tribe to Judah, and Damascus to the calling of the nations, out of whom one flock of the Lord has been made, whose sheep the savagery of lions often mangles, and from whose jaws scarcely two legs or the tip of an ear is carried off. In the leg is shown the way of teachings, in the ear the mysteries of the words. Hence also the apostles are commanded to walk with bare feet, without shoes and without any skin of a dead animal. And to believers it is said: He who has ears to hear, let him hear. In the Apocalypse of John too we read: He who has ears, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches. And more plainly through the prophet: Put, he says, your words in the ears of your heart. This path both the heretics and their masters the demons hasten to obstruct and to interweave with nets, so that we may fall in the way which is Christ. But if there comes a churchman and man of God, and a Samson, which means sun, he kills the lion, and going down into the pit of hell, which cools the waters, in the time of snow, of storm and of winter, he strangles the lion, so that with secure foot and safe ears we may be able to follow the Lord and to hear his words.

Hear and testify in the house of Jacob, says the Lord God of hosts, that in the day when I begin to visit the transgressions of Israel upon him, I will visit also upon the altars of Bethel; and the horns of the altar shall be cut off and shall fall to the ground; and I will strike the winter house with the summer house; and the houses of ivory shall perish; and many houses shall be scattered, says the Lord. LXX: Priests, hear: testify to the house of Jacob, says the Lord God Almighty, that in the day when I avenge the iniquities of Israel upon him, I will avenge also upon the altars of Bethel; and the horns of the altar shall be undermined and shall fall upon the ground. I will confound and strike the winged house upon the summer house, and the houses of ivory shall perish; and many other houses shall be added, says the Lord. What is put at the beginning of the section according to the Seventy, priests, is not found in the Hebrew, but in place of this word is read ares, which Aquila interpreted as pallet; and I think that the Seventy put the Hebrew word itself, which some, not understanding it, read in place of ares as ἱερεῖς, that is, priests. Now the prophetic word commands that they first hear, and then testify in the house of Jacob, that is, among the ten tribes, what the Lord has said. When I begin, he says, to visit the transgressions, or impieties, of the house of Israel, and to render to them what they deserve, and the time of captivity has come, then the altars of Bethel shall be destroyed, where the golden calves were, and the horns of the altar shall be cut off, the altar which was split apart when Jeroboam too stretched out his hand, and they shall crash to the ground, broken, as the Assyrian lays waste. And I will strike, he says, the winter house with the summer house, which we can take simply according to the letter: that they were of such great wealth that they had double houses, winter ones and summer ones, of which some faced the north and others the south, so that according to the variety of the seasons they might secure a temperate measure of cold and heat and of the sky. We can call the winter house the kingdom of Israel, in which there was coldness of religion and of the worship of God, and various whirlwinds of the winds, and savage storm; and the summer house, Judah and Jerusalem, in which was the temple, and burnt offerings were offered morning and evening, and the warmth of religion flourished. And the houses of ivory, he says, shall perish, which can also stand according to the history. For we read that Ahab king of Israel abounded in such great luxuries that he made himself a house of ivory. For 'of ivory' we find in the Hebrew houses of tooth, that is, of elephant. Of these it is also written in the forty-fourth (45th) psalm: From the houses of ivory, out of which the daughters of kings have delighted you in your honour. When the Lord begins to visit the transgressions of Israel, which formerly beheld God with the mind, he will visit also upon the altars of Bethel, not the one altar which the Church has, but the very many altars of the heretics. For they have as many altars as schisms. He will cut off also the horns of the altar which in their pride they boast that they possess as the one altar. And their horns, that is, their arrogance, will fall to the ground; and he will strike the winter house, those heretics who toil in continence and fasts, in dry fasts and sleeping on the ground, among whom are Tatian and Mani; and the summer house, such as the Nicolaitans and the Eunomians, and the new followers of an old heresy, who for the gluttony of the belly admit every pleasure. And the houses of ivory will perish, which are put together with an artful tooth and the splendour of rhetorical polish. And many houses will be scattered, which are dispersed through the whole world. For when the one truth rises, many lies will be destroyed. For the winter house the Seventy translated οἶκον τὸν περίπτερον, which we have interpreted winged, because it has little doors over the windows, and as it were wings for keeping off the severity of the cold.

Book Two

I have read in a certain declamation: "The weakness of the body drags down the strength of the mind as well." On the contrary the apostle Paul: When I am weak, he says, then I am strong, and: Power is made perfect in weakness. For the spirit is against the flesh, and the flesh against the spirit; these are opposed to one another, so that we do not do the things we wish. Hence also in the Gospel it is said: The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak. Old age brings with it many things, both good and bad. Good, because it frees us from those most shameless masters, the pleasures, sets a limit to gluttony, breaks the onsets of lust, increases wisdom, gives riper counsels; and, as the body grows cold, it sleeps with the Shunammite, ever a virgin, and, despising luxury together with Barzillai, it hands it over to the young man, his son Chimham, and does not wish to cross the Jordan and to go out of its own regions into foreign territory. But what are thought to be the evils of old age are these: frequent illnesses, most troublesome phlegm, which some of the Greeks call κόρυζαν and others φλέγμα, dimming eyes, food turning sour, a hand that sometimes trembles, teeth bared of their gums and falling out during meals. Besides this, it is often tormented by gripings and stings of the stomach, and by the pains of gout in the feet and in the hands, so that it cannot even hold a stylus or a pen, so that it cannot walk on its own feet, and seems to be cut off from a great part of life, and already dead in many of its limbs. Since this is how things stand, in a comparison of evils I shall bear diseases more tolerably, provided that I am free of the one and most grievous mistress, lust. Old age too does indeed sometimes suffer the incitements of vices, and no one, according to the holy martyr Cyprian, is safe for long when he is near to danger. But it is one thing to be tickled by pleasures, another to be overwhelmed by them. There youth, which knows the necessities of the flourishing body, speaks with the Apostle: For I do not do the good that I will, but the evil that I do not will, that I do and: Wretched man that I am, who will deliver me from the body of this death? Here, however, rarely, among the dead ashes a faintly glowing spark strives to revive, and yet it cannot stir up a blaze. Wherefore, Pammachius, with my head already grey, obtain for me from the Lord that I may deserve to have as my companion wisdom, of whom it is written: Love her, and she will keep you; honour her, and she will embrace you, so that, with her helping me and as my companion, I may complete the work begun on Amos, and the second book may take its beginning from the cows of Bashan, so that I, who in the prophet Hosea treated at full length of the cows or calves of Beth-aven, may now also discourse on the very fat cows.

4:1-3

Hear this word, you fat cows, who are on the mountain of Samaria; who do calumny to the needy, and crush the poor, who say to your masters: Bring, and we will drink. The Lord God has sworn by his holy one that behold, days shall come upon you, and they shall lift you up on poles, and your remnants in boiling pots; and you shall go out through the openings, one against another, and you shall be cast into Armon, says the Lord. LXX: Hear this word, you cows of Basanitis, who are on the mountain of Samaria, who oppress the poor and trample the needy, who say to your masters: Give us, that we may drink. The Lord swears by his holy ones that behold, days shall come upon you, and they shall take you up in arms; and those who are with you, in pots, pestilent traders; and you shall be cast out naked, one facing another, and you shall be cast upon mount Remmam, says the Lord. In place of fat cows, the Seventy put of Basanitis; Aquila and Theodotion the Hebrew word itself, Basan; we, following the interpretation of Symmachus, who says αἱ Βόες εὔτροφοι, that is, fattened kine, have interpreted fat cows. Now he speaks to the princes of Israel and to all the nobles of the ten tribes, who gave themselves up to luxuries and plunder, that they should hear the word of God, and know that they are not ploughing oxen but fat cows from the herd, or such as are fed in the pastures of Bashan, which are places most fertile in grass; and by this he signifies that they were made ready not for agriculture but for sacrifice and for eating. You are fat cows on the mountain of Samaria, and you crush all the lowly, and you say to your masters, that is, to the shepherds, by whom we understand the kings: Give us and we will drink, that is, only give the command, and we will lay everything waste. But from the fact that he put: Give us and we will drink, and did not say: Give and we will eat, he signifies their drunkenness in wine and luxury, which overturn the state of the mind. And so the Lord swore by his holy one, whether by himself, or by the Son, or by the temple, or by everyone who is holy and is called the temple of God, that a day is to come not far off, nor after many ages, but already imminent: the day of captivity and distress, in which the cows are lifted up on poles, and their remnants in boiling pots, for which both in the Hebrew and in Aquila is written in cauldrons of little fish. For the poles too, which in Hebrew are called sannoth, Aquila interpreted shields, Symmachus and the LXX arms, Theodotion alone δόρατα; following him, we have interpreted poles or spears. This signifies that they are taken in battle, and carried and borne away by right of victory, while he nevertheless keeps the metaphor of the cows that he had begun, so that, as he had said that they were fat cows, he tells that their flesh is to be carried on poles or shields. And as a boiling cauldron wraps up small fish all together, so also the cows of Bashan are to be overwhelmed, without any order, by the miseries of captivity. And what follows: And you shall go out through the openings, one against another, can be explained thus: The way of captivity lies open to you, and when your cauldrons have been burnt out, you shall go out one against another; according to the idiom of the Hebrews, who, for what we say mutually or in turn, say woman and woman, that is, one against another. And you shall be cast, he says, into the places of Armenia which are called Armona. Finally Symmachus interpreted: And you shall be cast into Armenia, for which the Seventy translated mount Remmam, Aquila mount Armona, Theodotion mount Mona, the Fifth edition the lofty mount. Now the word of the Lord which the cows of Bashan are commanded to hear is, according to the tropology we have begun, addressed to the heretics, who, serving the belly and gluttony, are rightly called very fat cows, or cows of ignominy; for this is what Bashan means, that is, αἰσχύνη; and if we wished to call it confusion, we should be interpreting Babylon rather than Bashan. These fat cows, or cows of ignominy and dry, for Bashan is understood in this sense too, are on the mountain of Samaria, to which it is also said in Hosea: Take away your calf, Samaria. And again in the same prophet: For your calf was a deceiver, Samaria; and they are on the mountain of Samaria for this reason, that they are always lifted up into pride and promise themselves lofty things. Samaria also means watch, not because they keep watch over the words of the Lord, but because they boast that they are the watchmen of his precepts. Who do calumny to the needy and crush the poor. By the needy and the poor understand the churchman, who, content with the simplicity of the truth, does not seek the furniture of the heretics, and the brilliance of eloquence, and the riches of arguments. These cows say to their masters: Bring and we will drink. Their masters we can call either the princes of perverse dogmas, such as Valentinus, and Marcion, and Arius, and Eunomius, or those who through many books strengthen what others have wickedly invented. To these the cows of Basanitis say: Bring us and we will drink. For unless those give, these have nothing to devour, or rather nothing to drink so as to be drunk. But that we may know that waters and cups signify teaching, the Lord speaks to the Samaritan woman: Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again; but whoever drinks of the water that I shall give him will not thirst for ever. Therefore whoever drinks of the waters of the Samaritan woman, that is, of the heretics, will always thirst and will not be able to cool the burning of a parched throat, as Isaiah cries aloud about this very thing: As one who is thirsty dreams that he drinks, and when he has risen he still thirsts, and his soul has hoped in vain; so shall all the nations be that fight against Jerusalem. For truly whoever drinks of the waters of the heretics, and fights against Jerusalem, the Church of God, drinks in his sleep, and his soul is mocked by empty images. And when he thinks himself satisfied, then he will have the beginning of thirst. Hence also it is said to the churchman: Drink waters from your own vessels, and your waters from the springs of the wells, and let them be for you alone. And the Lord your God swears by his holy one against the cows that are slaves to fattening and feasts, or he swears by his holy ones, that days shall come upon them, days of judgment and of punishments; so that, carrying them in their arms, they may put them, and those who are with them, into cauldrons, or carry them; and either the cows themselves are pestilent traders, or those who come to carry them; and they are cast out naked, seeing one another, and are cast upon mount Remmam, says the Lord God. For we must speak also according to the LXX, lest we seem to have set them out in vain. When the day of judgment and of vengeance comes against the heretics, then they will be carried away in arms, that is, with their own arms, with which they had fought against the Church, or, conquered by the armour of God and overcome by his fighters, they will be put into cauldrons, so that those who were formerly pestilent traders may be burnt and boiled through; for they traded to this end, that they might hand over to death those whom they had deceived. These are they of whom it is said: The sons of Eli, sons of pestilence, who sat in the chair of the pestilent. And when they have been boiled through and burnt, they will come out naked, having none of the things which they formerly presumed for themselves. And they will see their ignominy, and will be cast upon mount Remmam, which means loftiness, so that they may be crushed in their own pride. Some interpret Remmam as the vision of someone, that is, ὅρασίν τινὸς. For they were not able to see all things, but promised themselves the knowledge of some part, so that they are cast down in that which they believed they knew. According to the letter, we can understand that, when the days of captivity come upon the cows of Samaria, the enemies overcome them in battle and crush them with their arms, and drive them, overcome in the encounter, into fortified cities, which are compared to boiling pots; so that, as Jerusalem, holding its peoples shut in and besieged, is likened to a boiling pot full of flesh, so also the cities of Samaria are compared to boiling pots, which by famine and pestilence force the peoples shut in them to go out, and to go into captivity seeing one another, and to be transferred to Armon, the mountains of Armenia, which border on the Medes and the Persians.

4:4-6

Come to Bethel, and act impiously; to Gilgal, and multiply transgression; and bring your victims in the morning, your tithes on three days. And sacrifice praise from what is leavened, and call for voluntary offerings, and proclaim them. For so you have willed, sons of Israel, says the Lord God. Therefore I also have given you numbness of teeth in all your cities, and want of bread in all your places; and you have not returned to me, says the Lord. LXX: You entered into Bethel, and acted unjustly; in Gilgal you multiplied acting impiously, and you brought in your sacrifices in the morning, your tithes on the third day. And they read the law outside, and called upon confessions. Proclaim that the sons of Israel have loved these things, says the Lord God. And I will give you numbness of teeth in all your cities, and want of bread in all your places; and you have not returned to me, says the Lord. First let us lay the foundations of the history: O wretched Israel, captivity is already near you, already the Assyrian army is pressing upon you; do what you please, act impiously; commit fornication freely with idols, so that the more shameless you have been, the more just my sentence upon your torments may appear. Come, he says, into Bethel, in which you set up the golden calf, and act impiously against God. Come to Gilgal, the place of idolatry, of which I spoke through Hosea: All their wickedness is in Gilgal. And again in the same prophet: In vain were they sacrificing oxen in Gilgal. And when you have come to Gilgal: Multiply transgression. For whatever you do there is transgression against God, to whom you have preferred idols. And bring your victims in the morning, so that there may be no delay in crime. Your tithes on three days, or, as Symmachus translates, your tithes on the third day. The explanation of this passage seems to us to be this: in Leviticus it is commanded in the law that certain sacrifices are not to be kept over to the second day, and that others are not to remain to the third day, and that if they do remain they are unclean. The sense, therefore, is: Slaughter unclean victims every day, and offer defiled sacrifices, and sacrifice praise from what is leavened, which according to the precepts of Moses is not offered to God at all. For 'praise' Aquila translated εὐχαριστίαν, that is, thanksgiving, which in Hebrew is called thoda. And call, he says, for voluntary offerings, which the Hebrews call nadaboth, that is, spontaneous. A freewill sacrifice, moreover, belongs to rejoicing, and in Latin we can call it a banquet. And when you have done this, proclaim your impiety to all, so that you may be seen not only to have done it, but also to have taught others. These things I command, and I speak in the imperative mood, in order to satisfy your will, because so you have acted and so you have willed, sons of Israel, says the Lord God. Wherefore I too have given you numbness of teeth, as the LXX translated, whom we have followed in this place because of the simplicity of the word, or cleanness of teeth, as Aquila and Symmachus translated, so as to show by clean teeth the greatness of the famine. And I caused want of bread, not in one city, but in all your cities, nor in one place, but in all your places. And though I have done this, not punishing, but granting an occasion for repentance, not even so have you returned to me, says the Lord. This we have said according to the Hebrew; let us pass on to the LXX translators, and discuss briefly what seems to us according to anagogy in each point; for the size of the books does not allow it, if we should wish to say both things in each edition. You entered into Bethel, that is, into the house of God, which is understood as the Church, and you acted impiously against the Lord, trampling on his precepts. And in Gilgal, which is interpreted revelation or wallowing-place, you multiplied impieties, claiming for yourselves the knowledge of the holy Scriptures, and while you are lifted up into pride, you have rolled down into the lowest depths of the mire. You also brought in your sacrifices in the morning, your tithes on the third day, transformed into an angel of light, and compressing the threefold understanding of the Scriptures — concerning which we have been commanded to write it threefold in our hearts — into the meaning of a single day. For we ought to understand holy Scripture first according to the letter, doing in ethics whatever is commanded; secondly according to allegory, that is, the spiritual understanding; thirdly according to the blessedness of things to come. But you, he says, despising the first and the second day, compose for yourselves certain spiritual fictions without a foundation, setting a roof on top of walls. Nor are the heretics, of whom and to whom this is said, content with this limit of impiety, but they read the law of God outside, withdrawing from the Church, and tried to join confessions and testimonies to each of their dogmas, which they feigned with a perverse heart; or they sacrificed from what is leavened, concerning which it is said in the Gospel: Beware of the leaven, that is, the teaching of the Pharisees. And they did these things not by error, but by design; not by a chance will, but with the fullest affection for their forebears. Hence the Lord threatens vengeance upon them: I will give, he says, you numbness of teeth, which in Greek is called γομφιασμός. For if anyone, according to Ezekiel, eats a sour grape, his teeth will be numbed; so that those who make ill use of the testimonies of the holy Scriptures, and eat them unripe and without their sweetness, lose the strength of their teeth, so that they cannot grind hard food and pass into the belly what would profit the whole body. This numbness of teeth and want of food I will give both in all your cities and in all your places, so that you may suffer a famine of the word of God, and of that bread which came down from heaven, and of which it is written in the psalms: Man ate the bread of angels. All of which I did, not out of cruelty and savagery, as the heretics slanderously claim, the ferocity of a stern judge, but that you might be converted to me, according to that which is written: In vain have I struck your sons; you have not received discipline.

I also withheld the rain from you, when there were still three months left until the harvest. And I rained upon one city, and upon another city I did not rain. One part was rained upon, and the part upon which I did not rain withered. And two and three cities came to one city to drink water, and they were not satisfied; and you did not return to me, says the Lord. LXX: And I held back the rain from you three months before the vintage; and I will rain upon one city, and upon one city I will not rain. One part will be watered, and the part upon which I shall not rain will dry up. And two and three cities will gather into one city to drink water, and they will not be satisfied; and you have not returned to me, says the Lord. Not only did I give numbness of teeth in all your cities, and want of bread in all your places, but I withheld the rain from you when there were still three months left until the harvest, the rain which is called the latter rain and is most necessary of all to the thirsty fields and ploughlands of Palestine, so that, when the grass was swelling toward the harvest and the wheat was bringing forth its ear, it withered from excessive drought. He means the spring season at the end of the month of April, from which three months remain until the wheat harvest: May, June, July. For 'harvest' the LXX, after their manner, translated 'vintage'; and if we accept this, it is altogether unusual and impossible throughout all the regions of the East. For we have never seen rains at the end of the month of June, or in the month of July, in these provinces, and above all in Judea. Indeed, in the books of Kings, rains were stirred up at Samuel's prayer in the days of summer and harvest as a great sign and portent. And it would have been superfluous now to threaten a drought in the month of July, in which he had never given rains. He withheld the rain, moreover, so that they should endure not only want of bread, but also the burning of thirst and a scarcity of drink. For in these places in which we now live, apart from small springs, all the waters are those of cisterns, and if the divine wrath holds back the rain, the danger of thirst is greater than that of famine, which Scripture records also happened in the days of the prophet Elijah for three years and six months. And lest they should perhaps think that this had befallen cities and peoples by the law of nature, and the course of the stars, and the changes of the seasons, he says that he rains upon one city and its fields, and holds back the rain from another, so that two and three cities go to another city, and yet are not satisfied with draughts of water. And though he has done these things, not as punishment, but as medicine, he rebukes those who persist in crime: and not even so did you return to me, says the Lord. The Lord also withholds, or holds back, the spiritual rains from the heretics, and all the dew of divine wisdom, and commands his clouds not to rain rain upon them three months before the harvest, or the vintage, so that they cannot attain to the fruit of the mystery of the Trinity. And just as this sun, which we see, completes its yearly course, until it returns to its former goal, in twelve months, each of which rolls on for thirty days; and the moon, which in Hebrew is called iare, in Greek μήνη, has in both languages given its name to the months from its own name, and is lit by the rays of the sun on the side on which it is near to it, receiving more or less light according to the variation of the times; so too the Church, adorned with the splendour of the true sun, fills up the number of the twelve apostles. Hence also the tribes in Israel are called twelve, and as a testimony of eternal memory twelve stones are taken from the bed of the Jordan, to be set up in the place of the second circumcision. The Lord, then, rains upon one city, the Church of the true confession, and upon another he does not rain, the one which is in the conventicles of the heretics. And while the one receives an everlasting rain, the other is dried up by a continual drought, so that those who thirst, compelled by want, may come to the city of the Lord, from which goes forth a most abundant fountain that waters the torrent of thorns. This is the fountain which says through Jeremiah: They have forsaken me, the fountain of living water, and have dug for themselves broken cisterns, which cannot hold waters. This fountain, proceeding from one fountain, runs down in a threefold union; and for these fountains of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit the psalmist sighs after the manner of a thirsting stag, saying: As the stag longs for the fountains of waters, so my soul longs for you, O God. And when two and three cities have gone to the one city in which the waters of hope, faith and charity abound, they will not be satisfied, because they have come to seek the divine grace not by will, but by necessity.

By this saying let the heretics be confounded, who interpret as cruelty the discipline and, so to speak, the medicine of the Creator. 4:10

I sent death upon you in the way of Egypt, I struck your young men with the sword, even to the captivity of your horses. And I made the stench of your camps rise into your nostrils, and you did not return to me, says the Lord. LXX: I sent forth death upon you in the way of Egypt, and I killed your young men with the sword, together with the captivity of your horses. And I brought out your camps in fire in my wrath, and not even so did you return to me, says the Lord. Israel is instructed by every kind of scourge and torment; hence also, at the time when they were asking help from the Egyptians, death is sent upon them, and their young men are struck with the sword, and the horses which they had multiplied for themselves against the precept of God a savage captivity shuts in, so that the stench of the camps and the foulness of a dying army fill the nostrils of the living. And though he did these things too, in order to rebuke the offenders and amend the erring, not even so did they return to him, says the Lord. He sends death in the way of Egypt not only at that time, but daily, so that whoever has trodden the Egyptian path may, as he dies, hear the Apostle teaching: For in that I died, I died to sin; but in that I live, I live to God. And in another place: If we have died with Christ, we shall also live together with him. And again: I always carry about the mortification of Jesus in my body. And once more: But I live, now not I, but Christ lives in me. By this death the Lord wishes to give us life, so that, dying to sin, we may live to God. We read in Isaiah, at least according to the Seventy translators — otherwise the Hebrew has it differently —: I sent death upon Jacob, and it came upon Israel. Jacob is the name of the body being born; Israel, a name of blessing. Death, therefore, is first sent upon Jacob, so that we may mortify our members which are upon the earth, and through the mortification of the bodily members may come to the death of Israel, so that all the incitements of the passions in us may die. God strikes whatever is strong in evil and is lifted up in a perverse youth, lest it reach old age, and he hands the horses over to captivity, lest they slip down the precipices of hell; and he makes the stench of their camps rise into their nostrils, so that they may recognize their sins and perceive that they stink, and say with David: My scars have putrefied and are corrupted because of my folly. And though he did these things with the desire of one who heals, not even so did they return to him, says the Lord.

I overthrew you as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and you became like a brand snatched from the burning, and you did not return to me, says the Lord. LXX: I overthrew you as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and you became like a brand plucked out of the fire, and not even so were you converted to me, says the Lord. It is the last remedy, both for the ten tribes and for the heretics and for all sinners, that, after he has sent death in the way of Egypt, and struck their young men with the sword, and consumed their horses, and made the stench of their camps rise into their nostrils, and still they have not returned to him, he should overthrow them as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah; and that, when they have been overthrown because of the likeness of their crimes to those of Sodom and Gomorrah, and the divine fire has burned up the worst buildings in them, they themselves should be delivered like a brand snatched from the burning. And just as Lot, when Sodom perished, was saved, losing his substance and a part of his body, which we understand to be his wife, so these, all losing the riches of Sodom, may escape naked, according to what we read in the Apostle: If anyone's work which he has built upon it remains, he will receive a reward; but if anyone's work is burned, he will suffer loss, yet he himself will be saved, but only as through fire. He, then, who is saved through fire is snatched like a brand from the burning. And to men of this kind, whom the Savior addressed in the Gospel, rebuking them: If you were sons of Abraham, you would do the works of Abraham, John the Baptist says: Brood of vipers, who showed you to flee from the wrath to come? Bear, therefore, fruit worthy of repentance, and do not presume to say within yourselves, We have Abraham as our father. For I say to you that God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham? Therefore both Israel and all the heretics, because they had the works of Sodom and Gomorrah, are overthrown like Sodom and Gomorrah, so that they may be delivered like a brand snatched from the burning. And this is what we read in the prophet: Sodom shall be restored to its former state, so that he who by his own vice is among the Sodomites may, after the works of Sodom have burned up in him, be restored to his former state.

4:12-13

Therefore I will do these things to you, Israel; but after I have done these things to you, prepare to meet your God, Israel; for behold, he who forms the mountains, and creates the wind, and announces to man his speech, who makes the morning mist, and treads upon the heights of the earth, the Lord God of hosts is his name. LXX: Therefore thus will I do to you, Israel; nevertheless, because I will do thus to you, prepare to call upon your God, Israel. For behold, he who establishes the thunder, and creates the spirit, and announces to men his Christ, making the morning and the mist, and ascending upon the heights of the earth, the Lord God Almighty is his name. Where we translated 'after', in the Hebrew is written eceb, and Aquila translated ὕστερον, that is, afterwards, and Theodotion 'at the last', and the Seventy 'nevertheless'; in the Hebrew it can be read thus: Therefore I will do these things to you, Israel, afterwards, that is, a long time after, and in the last ages, so that the verse begins again from another beginning: And since I am about to do this to you, prepare to meet your God, Israel. And again, in the place where the Seventy translated: Prepare to call upon your God, and we, following Theodotion, have put: Prepare to meet your God, Symmachus and the Fifth edition translated: Prepare to oppose your God, which in Hebrew is said hechin lacerath Eloiah. For 'mountains' also, which in Hebrew are called arim, the Seventy alone rendered βροντήν, that is, thunder. And why they said 'spirit' and we 'wind', which in Hebrew is called rua, the reason is plain: because by this word both wind and spirit are named. And as for what follows: Announcing to man his speech, while all translate it as we have rendered it, the Seventy alone translated: Announcing to men his Christ, deceived by the likeness and ambiguity of the word. For if we read 'his Christ', which in Hebrew is said Messio, it is written with these letters, mem, sin, iod, heth, and uau, which is what the LXX supposed. But if it is as in the Hebrew, according to Aquila τήν ὁμιλίαν αὐτοῦ, according to Symmachus τὸ φώνημα αὐτοῦ, according to Theodotion τὸν λόγον αὐτοῦ, according to the Fifth edition τήν ἀδολεσχίαν αὐτοῦ, all of which they translate as meaning that it is 'his speech', it will be written with these letters, mem, he, which is read ma, that is, 'that' or 'what'. Then sin, iod, heth, which we read sia, that is, speech. And the O, which is written with the single letter uau, signifies αὐτοῦ, that is, 'his'; and all mixed together it is read masio, having, compared with the word above, one letter more, he, the second letter. We have spoken of the variety of interpretation, which will be tiresome to the careless and welcome to the studious; now let us pass on to the sense of what is written. I gave numbness to your teeth, and you did not return to me, says the Lord. I withheld the rain from you, and I rained upon one city, and upon another I did not rain, and two and three cities came to one to drink water, and they were not satisfied, and not even so did you return to me, says the Lord. I struck you with scorching wind, and with mildew your gardens and vineyards and olive groves and fig groves, and you did not return to me, says the Lord. I sent death upon you in the way of Egypt, and I struck your young men with the sword, and I made the stench of your camps rise into your nostrils, and you did not return to me, says the Lord. I overthrew you as the Lord overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and you became as a brand snatched from the burning, and you did not return to me, says the Lord. Therefore I will do these things to you, O Israel, so that you who have despised what is past may at least be corrected by what I am about to bring upon you. And when he has said, I will do these things to you, he is silent about what he is going to do, so that while Israel hangs uncertain over each kind of punishment — which are the more terrible for this reason, that men suspect them all —, he may repent, lest God bring on what he threatens. But after I have done to you what I solemnly declare I will do, prepare to call upon the Lord your God. Everyone, after all, who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved. Or: prepare to meet your God, so that you may receive with all eagerness the Lord who comes to you. He it is who establishes the thunder, or makes firm the mountains, at whose voice the hinges of the heavens and the foundations of the earth are shaken. He it is who creates the spirit, which in this place we understand not as the Holy Spirit, as the heretics suppose, but as the wind, or the spirit of man, because no one knows what is in a man except the spirit which is in him; and the Spirit himself intercedes for us with unspeakable groanings. Or certainly by 'spirit' we ought to understand the soul, according to that which is written: You will take away their spirit, and they will fail, and they will return to their dust. And: Father, into your hands I commend my spirit, and saying this he breathed his last. And when he has created the spirit, he announces to men his own speech, or announces to man that man's speech, he who knows the secrets of the thoughts, and understands what the hidden mind ponders in silent thought, according to what we read in Jeremiah according to the Hebrew: The heart of all is perverse and unsearchable; who shall know it? I the Lord search the heart and test the reins. Which Scripture also attests in the hundred and thirty-eighth (139th) psalm: Your eyes saw my imperfect being. And the sense is: Before I was made, before I was shaped into limbs, while I was still contained in the seed, your eyes saw me. And Jeremiah hears from the Lord: Before I formed you in the womb, I knew you; and before you came forth from the womb, I sanctified you, and I set you as a prophet among the nations. And the evangelist: But Jesus, seeing their thoughts. But if we read: Announcing to man his speech, it is to be taken according to what was said above, where he says: The Lord God will not do a thing unless he has revealed his secret to his servants the prophets. And he who announces the thoughts of man and his speech, or his own, is the one who makes the dawn and the daybreak, and fills all things with clouds, and treads upon the heights of the lands, whose name is the Lord God Almighty. In place of this one reads in the Seventy: And announcing to men his Christ; on this pretext the heretics want the Holy Spirit to be created, from the order that follows: Creating the spirit, and announcing to men his Christ, so that the one is created and the other is announced among the peoples. To them we shall reply according to their own sense and the common edition: He who is the creator of all things, and establishes the thunder, or fashions the mountains, consequently brings the winds out of his treasuries, and, as the founder of the universe, promises his Son Christ to men. But after Christ has been announced, then the light of truth is opened to us, not to perfection, because now we see in part and know in part, and contemplate the things that are to come through a mirror and an image. Hence there follows: Making the morning and the mist, and ascending upon the heights of the earth. For the Lord is high in the high places, and he who is high does not dwell in low things; but the creator of the mountains ascends upon the mountains, in those who have their citizenship in the heavens, and, walking in the flesh, do not live according to the flesh but according to the spirit. But if we read according to Symmachus and Aquila: These things I will do to you, Israel, afterwards, and when I have done these things to you, prepare to oppose your God Israel, it is to be understood thus: In order to correct you I did the things which the preceding discourse describes; and because you were unwilling to return to me, I will do to you the things that are contained in my secret counsel. You killed the servants whom I had sent to you; last of all I will send my Son; but you, according to your custom, by which you have always resisted the will of God, prepare yourself to contradict and oppose your God, according to that which is written: Behold, this one is set for the fall and for the rising again of many, and for a sign which shall be contradicted. And he says this not to command what Israel ought to do, but to foretell what he will do of his own accord, as if rebuking and reproving him, so that, corrected, he may at least not do what has been foretold.

5:1-2

Hear this word, which I raise over you as a lament. The house of Israel has fallen; it will not again rise. The virgin Israel is cast down into her land; there is no one to raise her up. LXX: Hear this word, which I will take up over you as a lament. The house of Israel has fallen; it will not any more rise again. The virgin Israel is cast down upon her land; there is no one to raise her up. As far as concerns the order of the letter and the truth of the history begun, the ten tribes, which are called Israel, once led into captivity, by no means returned afterwards to their own land. And the people of Israel is called a virgin, not because it remained in the purity of virginity, but because it was once joined to the Lord like a virgin. Hence the prophet is also commanded to take up a lament over it, because it will by no means be restored to its former rank. But as far as concerns the spiritual understanding, the prophet takes up a lament over every Israel which used to discern God with the mind and afterwards ceased to serve him, according to that which is commanded to Ezekiel, that he should devour the book in which there was written, both within and without, lamentation, and song, and woe. Understand by within the Song of Solomon that follows, who says: The king has brought me into his chamber. And the forty-fourth (45th) psalm, in which it is written: All the glory of the king's daughter is within. But without is whatever is read in the letter, and is seen in the bark, and is not held in the marrow of the spirit. And so, both according to the letter and according to tropology, in all the books of the prophets a lament is written over those who repent after sins; a song over those who, stained by no filth of sins, are worthy of canticle and praise; woe over those who do not repent, but according to the hardness of their heart treasure up for themselves wrath in the day of wrath. But if, as we have said, the lament is over those who repent, and repentance restores health to the wound, how is it said according to the Seventy: The house of Israel has fallen, it will rise no more. The virgin Israel has strayed in her land, there is no one to raise her up? This can be resolved thus: after the house of Israel has collapsed by its own will, it will by no means recover its former dignity; after the virgin Israel has strayed in her land, she will no longer be able to find one to raise her up. And consider the proper meanings of the words. He who is a house, and is counted in the crowd, is said to fall. But he who is of the number of the virgins, if he strays, even for a light sin, will not be able to be raised up; not that he is not raised up, but the virgin Israel is by no means raised up, and the Lord of Israel will by no means rise again. For the glory is not the same of him who has always followed the Lord and of him who has wandered from the flock and afterwards been carried back on the shoulders of the good shepherd. Hence the Lord also says through another prophet: I prefer the repentance of a sinner rather than his death. Repentance becomes better not when compared with the purest holiness and the Church of Christ — which has no wrinkle nor spot —, but when compared with death and the underworld. We say these things, not in order to take away, with Novatus, the hope of repentance, but in order to make more fearful, and therefore anxious, those who, with the door of repentance open, while they hope for the future, lose the present, and who, though they could have remained without a wound, carelessly receive a wound, so as afterwards to be tormented by pain. There are many mansions with my Father, and star differs from star in brightness; so also is the resurrection of the dead, the saints shining like the sun and the moon, the evening star and the morning star. But those who have repented after sin will be made equal to other stars, according to the diversity of their merits.

5:3

For thus says the Lord God: The city from which a thousand went out, there shall be left in it a hundred; and from which a hundred went out, there shall be left in it ten, in the house of Israel. LXX: likewise. The divine word gives the reasons why the house of Israel, falling, does not rise again, and the virgin Israel, straying, or cast down upon the land, has no one to raise her up. The city, he says, from which a thousand went out, there shall be left in it a hundred; and from which a hundred went out, there shall be left in it ten, in the house of Israel; so that where once there had been a multitude, because of the excessive devastation scarcely a tenth part remains. And lest, clinging wholly to the letter, we leave the mysteries of numbers untouched: that the number seven is holy the sabbath also proves, on which God rested from all his works. And he commands that no servile work be done on it, except only those things which pertain to the soul, and that we should not carry burdens on it. Hence also in the wilderness the man who had gathered wood on the sabbath day, things that are to burn, is condemned by the sentence of the Lord. And seven weeks complete the number of holy Pentecost, and the jubilee year of remission and of the sounding trumpets is woven from this number. In the seventh month also tabernacles are pitched, and the Hebrew, when he has served six years, is set free in the seventh year. Secular philosophy also knows this, and the books of the physicians, of whom Galen, most eloquent and most learned, wrote three books, κρίσεων καὶ τῶν κριτικῶν ἡμερῶν (On Crises and Critical Days), in which, showing the power of the number seven, he says that the most burning fevers are broken on the seventh day; or, if the quantity of noxious humour and phlegm has been so great that it has by no means been consumed by the heat of the first week, the last day of the second week is awaited, that is, the fourteenth. But if, to speak with Hippocrates, the νόσος (the disease) has overcome this one, they pass on to the twenty-first day, that is, to the end of the third week; the days having been so established from the beginning of the world that all labours and troubles come to rest at the seventh number. Finally, the captivity of the people of Israel and the overthrow of the temple were also completed in the seventieth year of desolation, and seven stars are said to be wandering, according to the number of the days. Of the mysteries of this number Cicero tells more fully in the Dream of Scipio; and the Timaeus of Plato is a most obscure book, which is not made plainer even by the golden mouth of Cicero. Just as, therefore, the number seven has its own mystery, so the number that is held in unity, and is enclosed by the majesty of the one God, is sanctified and perfect and, so to speak, the true number. Hence the Son says: I am in the Father, and the Father in me, and, wishing all to be one with the Father, he speaks to him: Father, grant that, as I and you are one, so these also may be one in us. The first blessedness, therefore, is to be in the first number, which is one and true; the second in the second, that is, in the decad; the third in the third, that is, in the hecatontad. For as the decad is completed by the tenth unit, so the hecatontad is built from ten decads. The fourth number, which is contained in the thousand, consists of ten hecatontads. When, therefore, someone has repented, from the thousand and the fourth number he scarcely returns to the hundred and the third number. Again, he who is in the hundred scarcely returns to the second number, that of the first decad; and so it comes about that the house of Israel which had fallen cannot rise again, and the virgin Israel which had strayed has no one in the land to raise her up, because he who has once departed from unity, and has lost that glory of the purest virginity — of which the Apostle says: For I am jealous for you with the jealousy of God, and I have resolved to present you as a chaste virgin to one husband, to Christ, in which there is no spot nor wrinkle —, will not be able to recover his former state and the blessedness of unity, and it will scarcely be granted him to return from a thousand to a hundred, and to go back from a hundred to ten. I have said these things briefly, lest I should seem in this section to have fled tropology altogether because of the difficulty of the numbers.

5:4-5

For thus says the Lord to the house of Israel: Seek me, and you shall live; and do not seek Bethel, and do not enter into Gilgal, and do not pass over to Beersheba; for Gilgal shall be led captive, and Bethel shall be useless. LXX: For thus says the Lord to the house of Israel: Seek me, and you shall live; and do not seek Bethel, and do not go into Gilgal, and do not pass over upon the well of the oath; for Gilgal shall be led captive, and Bethel shall be as though it were not. It is the custom of the Scriptures always to join glad things to adverse ones, and after God has threatened sad things, he calls to repentance those whom he has frightened, according to that which we read in Isaiah: Woe, sinful nation, a people full of offences, a most wicked seed, unjust sons. And when he had said: Your land is deserted, your cities consumed with fire, strangers will devour your regions before you, he speaks to them, promising better things: Wash yourselves, be clean; take away your wickednesses from your souls, learn to do good, seek judgment, judge for the orphan, justify the widow; and come, let us dispute, says the Lord. Just as, therefore, in Isaiah he supports with gentle speech those whom he had frightened with a severe voice, so also in this prophet, to those to whom he had said: The house of Israel has fallen, it will not again rise; the virgin Israel has strayed in the land, there is no one to raise her up. Now he speaks to them and says: To the house of Israel, that is, to the ten tribes, Seek me and you shall live, since in that you do not seek me, you are dead. And when you have sought, you will find; and when you have found, you will live. And do not seek Bethel, in which was the golden calf, and Gilgal, the place of idolatry, of which I have also said above: All their wickedness is in Gilgal. And you shall not pass over to Beersheba. Beautifully he says: you shall not pass over to Beersheba, that is, to the well of the oath, where the tribe of Judah, whenever it went astray, had been accustomed to worship idols. But Israel raged in the worship of images to such a degree that, by no means content with its own idols, it passed over to those of others. Finally, Gilgal, he says, shall be led captive, and Bethel shall be useless, or not subsisting at all, when the idols in it have been overthrown. But of Beersheba he was altogether silent, because when the ten tribes were conquered, the city by the name of Beersheba, which was in the tribe of Judah at that time, was neither taken nor destroyed. And at the same time it must be noticed that the LXX translators in the present place translated the name Beersheba, saying: The well of the oath, and in later passages they set down the name itself: Your God lives, O Dan, and the way of Beersheba lives. And they set down the way of Beersheba because from Israel they went by a long journey to the farthest borders of Judah, which were in Gerar and adjoined the Egyptian wilderness, in order to worship idols. It is, moreover, the place in which Abraham dwelt; and from the fact that he and Abimelech, after seven sheep had been given, swore to a mutual covenant, it was called the well of the oath, or the well of the seventh, because of the number of the seven sheep. For Sabe signifies both. And according to the laws of allegory he commands the house of Israel, that is, those who promise themselves the knowledge of God, not to seek Bethel, and not to enter into Gilgal, and not to pass over, or go up, to the well of the oath, but rather to seek God and live in him. And they seek Bethel, which is interpreted house of God, who say: The temple of the Lord, the temple of the Lord; and they trust in buildings, of which the Lord spoke to the disciples: Days will come in which there will not be left a stone upon a stone that will not be destroyed. And they enter into Gilgal who after the coming of Christ desire to be circumcised again. For in Gilgal the people was circumcised a second time. Hence the place itself also received its name, because the Lord took away from them the reproach of Egypt. And you shall not pass over, he says, to Beersheba, or to the well of the oath, lest you think those to be the borders of Judea which Scripture once, according to the letter, had promised, from Dan to Beersheba. Nor should you say any longer with the prophet: God is known in Judea, his name is great in Israel, but you should hear with the apostles: Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world, because Gilgal also, that is, the circumcision of the flesh, will be led captive by the true circumcision of the heart. And Bethel, which you think the house of God, will not subsist, or, as I think better, returns useless, that is, auen, so that it is by no means called the house of God, but is called the useless house, or the house of the idol. Another interpretation: he seeks Bethel who follows only the letter that kills, and does not seek in the words the sense, which is God; and he enters into Gilgal who strives after greater revelations, promising himself the knowledge of things above; and he passes over, or goes up, to the well from which the Samaritan woman, desiring to draw waters that could not satisfy thirst, did not know him from whose belly proceed rivers of water springing up into eternal life.

Seek the Lord and live, lest perhaps the house of Joseph be burned like fire; and it will devour, and there will be no one to quench Bethel. LXX: Seek the Lord and live, lest perhaps the house of Joseph be kindled like fire; and it devour it, and there be no one to quench the house of Israel. Just as it is said in the person of God: Seek me, and you shall live, so the prophet speaks about the Lord, that they should seek him and live. For in seeking the Lord they begin to live; but if they have not sought him, and for that reason have not lived, at once the house of Joseph will be kindled like fire. By it we ought to understand, because of Jeroboam, who was of the tribe of Ephraim and of the house of Joseph, the ten tribes, which are called Israel and, from the greater part of the people, possessed the former name. But the two tribes which were ruled by the stock of David, who was of the tribe of Judah, were called Judah, and possessed Jerusalem, in which was the temple of God. And when the house of Joseph has been kindled, it will devour and consume Bethel, of which I have said above: Do not seek Bethel, and there will be no one to quench it, since it will have been set on fire by its own kings. In place of Bethel, in the LXX is read the house of Israel, since they translated the sense rather than the word; so that, as King Jeroboam succeeded, and all the kings who thereafter succeeded him in the rule, the ten tribes, which are called Israel, burn. This is the fire which is kindled, or shines out, and inflames the house of Joseph, so that Bethel is consumed; of which it is said elsewhere: Walk in the light of your fire, and in the flame which you have kindled. And since we have often referred the house of Joseph — because of Jeroboam, who separated the people of God from the stock of David, and made golden cows in Dan and in Bethel, and said: We have no part in David, nor inheritance in the son of Jesse — to the person of the heretics, who with polished speech have fashioned comely and beautiful and, so to speak, golden images, and adore the works of their own hands, and under the appearance of cows promise themselves an image of agriculture, it is said to them: Seek the Lord, and live, him who says: I am the way, and the truth, and the life, so that, after they have walked in him and found the truth, those who before were dead may then begin to live. And unless they do this, they will be burned by the ardours of the devil, and none will be able to be found among their princes, who themselves also have been kindled by the heretical fire, who could put out the flame that devours all things, and especially Bethel, which assumes for itself the false name of the house of God.

5:7-9

You who turn judgment into wormwood and leave righteousness on the earth; him who makes Arcturus and Orion, and turns darkness into morning, and changes day into night. Who calls the waters of the sea and pours them out upon the face of the earth: the Lord is his name. Who smiles devastation upon the strong, and brings plundering upon the mighty. LXX: Who makes judgment on high and has set righteousness on the earth; who makes all things, and transforms them, and turns the shadow of death into morning, and darkens the day into night. Who calls the water of the sea and pours it out upon the face of the earth: the Lord God Almighty is his name. Who divides crushing upon strength, and brings misery upon the fortress. In this place the common edition disagrees greatly with the Hebrew truth, as the prudent reader at once understands, without our reminder, from what we have set forth. Let us therefore expound first according to the Hebrews, and afterwards, if we have deserved it, with Christ opening the way, let us say what seems to us in the translation of the LXX. The house of Joseph, that is, the house of Ephraim, and for that reason the royal house, and Bethel, or, as the LXX translated, the house of Israel, that is, both kings and peoples, both worshippers and idols alike, will be overthrown, who by unjust judgment provoked God to anger. And they turned the sweetness of judgment into the bitterness of wormwood, which is a kind of most bitter herb, taking up iniquity and leaving righteousness. But what this righteousness is, the following verse shows: Him who makes Arcturus and Orion, and turns darkness into morning, and changes day into night. He of whom he had said above: Forming the mountains, and creating the wind, and announcing to man his speech, making the morning mist, and stepping upon the high places of the earth: the Lord God of hosts is his name, he it is who is the Creator of Arcturus, which in Hebrew is called Chima, and by Symmachus and Theodotion is rendered εἰς πλειάδα (the Pleiades), which is commonly called Boötes. And as for what follows, Orion, which in Hebrew is called Chasil, Symmachus translated it absolutely as stars, Theodotion as the evening star. But the Hebrew who instructed us in the holy Scriptures thinks that Chasil means splendour, and signifies in general the shining stars. But when we hear Arcturus and Orion, we ought not to follow the fables of the poets and the ridiculous and monstrous lies with which they try to defame even heaven and to set the reward of debauchery among the stars, saying: "He scans Arcturus and the rainy Hyades and the twin Triones, and Orion armed with gold"; but we ought to know that the Hebrew names, which among them are called otherwise, have been carried over into our language by the terms of the Gentile fables, since we cannot understand what is said except through those terms which we have learned by use and drunk in with error. Hence also in the book of Kings the Greeks translated the Hebrew Raphaim as titans, which is a most celebrated fable among the heathen, from which they write γιγαντομαχίας (battles of the giants) in praise of the gods; and the Typhoean shafts, and Mount Etna placed upon Enceladus, at whose movement Trinacria trembles. But this God, the Creator of all things, who makes Arcturus and Orion, changes night into day and day into night, and filters the most bitter waters of the sea, suspended by the heat of the upper air, and distils them into the sweet taste of the rains, like a medicinal cupping-glass, which by the heat of its upper round draws moisture and blood upward; from which we learn whence the rains come. And as for what follows: Who smiles devastation upon the strong, he returns to the present time. And the order is: he who is the Creator of all things also threatens captivity upon Samaria, and brings plundering upon the mighty, since it turns judgment into wormwood and leaves righteousness on the earth. Where we have said, who smiles, Aquila translated ὁ μειδιῶν. And μειδίαμα is properly said of what we can call a smiling, when someone is angry and, with his lips parted a little, pretends to smile, so as to show the greatness of his anger. Let us speak also according to the LXX: God makes judgment on high, when he judges truth and renders to each according to his work; and everyone who is an imitator of God, and desires to be his son, and to be perfect, as his Father who dwells in heaven is perfect, makes judgment on high, and does not imitate that judge who did not fear God, and did not respect man, and by the perversity of his judgment did not lift his sentence up to heaven, but dragged it down to the depths. And as for what follows: And has set righteousness on the earth, we ought to take it according to this, that he gave us Christ, his righteousness, and did not cast it away but set it down on the earth, so that, all iniquity being overcome, he might make us heavenly from earthly. I think that the Gentile poet also stole from this place, who, discoursing on the simplicity and blessedness of country folk, added: "Justice, leaving the earth, made her last steps among them." But in saying: Making all things and transforming them, they comprehend Arcturus and Orion in one word, neglecting to carry over the proper sense of the names into the Greek language. And God transforms all things when he makes heavenly things from earthly, and bestows on men the likeness of angels; when the moon will glow with the brightness of the sun, and the sun will have sevenfold light; when man, animal and weak and corruptible, is transformed into one spiritual and strong and incorrupt, changing his glory, not his nature; when those who understand will shine like the splendour of the firmament, and what is written will be fulfilled: One is the glory of the sun, another the glory of the moon, another the glory of the stars. Whoever wishes to cross them will at once be swept headlong, and, his feet overturned, he will not be able to say: He set my feet upon a rock; for he treads upon sands, which have no foundation, and when he has been in peril he will say, according to the Hebrews: The torrent has passed over my soul. On the contrary, we read of the just, whose judgment does not roll like water and whose justice is not borne along like an impassable torrent: The thoughts of the just are judgments.

Pass over, all of you, to Calneh, which is now called Ctesiphon, and pass over, all of you. Who are these all? Surely those of whom he had spoken above: Nobles, heads of the peoples, and you who are wealthy in Zion, and trust in the mountain of Samaria. You therefore, pass over into the city of Persia, and observe carefully, and from there go on to Hamath the great, which is now named Antioch. And he calls it great to distinguish it from the lesser Hamath, which is called Epiphania. Hence even to this day, for those going to Mesopotamia, the first stopping-place is called Emmas, a corrupted word indeed, but one keeping traces of the former name; its region is called Riblah, in which, in the presence of Nebuchadnezzar, the sons of Zedekiah king of Judea were killed, and his eyes were blinded. And go down, he says, to Gath of the Philistines. You who dwell on mount Zion and on the mountain of Samaria, go down to the Philistines, who live in the plains, and to all the best kingdoms of these, which lie under different cities, Gaza and Ashkelon, Azotus, Ekron and Gath, and see whether their border is wider than your border, whether you or they hold the larger provinces; you, I say, you of the people of Israel, who are set apart for the evil day, namely for the day of captivity, and draw near to the throne of iniquity, going to the unjust judge, the Babylonian king. Though you are about to suffer this, you now sleep on beds of ivory and wanton on soft couches, so as to couple lust with sleep. You eat not to drive away hunger and to sustain the human body, but for delights and luxury, so that whatever is tender and fat in the flocks and in the herds you make ready for your gullet; for you the lust of the genitals and of the throat, and a paunch seething with food, are not enough unless you soothe your ears with the songs of pipes and psaltery and lyre, so that what David did for the worship of God, devising the orders of the Levites and the varieties of instruments, you turn to pleasure and luxury. And you drink wine in bowls, not to sate thirst but to overwhelm the mind. And you are anointed, not with the purest oil to soothe the labours of the body, but with the most costly ointments. And while you abound in these delights, if you see any of the people perishing, you are moved by no pity over their slaughter, but you despise them as brute animals and as beasts perishing in their own gore. This same sense the prophet Ezekiel also sets down under the example of the shepherds: Who eat the milk of the sheep, and are clothed with the wool, and devour whatever is choicest, and do not heal the wounded, do not bind up the broken, do not seek the perishing. All that we have said according to the truth of the history, let us now transfer, according to the LXX translators, to the cloud of allegory. House of Israel, you who have withdrawn from me, who trust in the mountain of Samaria, who have gathered the vintage of the firstfruits of the nations, pass over and see, and go on to the very many walls, for this is what Hamath Rabba means; and from there go gently down into the winepress of those who fall while drinking: for this is what Gath and the Philistines signify. And contemplate everything, and especially the best kingdoms, or the best cities out of all the kingdoms; and consider whether their borders are more than yours. For if we wish to treat it in the mind and to examine the philosophy of all the nations, of the Egyptians, the Indians and the Persians, we shall find their borders narrower than the borders of the holy Scriptures: Into all indeed the earth their sound has gone out, and their words to the ends of the world. Those to whom he had said above: House of Israel, pass over, all of you, and see, from what sort of vices they are bidden to pass over to better things, the following words describe: Who come to the evil day, preparing for themselves by their own vice an evil day; not that any day is evil, but that each one prepares evil for himself, according to what we read in Ecclesiastes: Do not say, The former days were better for me than these, for you do not ask about this in wisdom; for it is foolish to lay it on the times, since it is in our power to make for ourselves either a good day or an evil one. These draw near to and touch false sabbaths. For just as there is what is called circumcision of the flesh and circumcision of the heart, and there are Jews openly and in secret, of whom the one kind is rejected and the other approved, so there are sabbaths of the Lord that are holy and rest upon truth, and others that are not holy and are false, which follow the idleness of the letter that kills. And what follows, You who sleep upon beds of ivory, we can interpret thus: he who is an athlete of the Lord, and is trained for wrestling, and prepares himself against the adverse powers, sleeps on the bare ground, imitating Jacob; and he puts at his head the stone which the builders rejected, and which has become the head of the corner. But those who give themselves to delights and luxury, and do everything for the sake of the belly, sleep on beds made of the ivory of a dead animal, and cling to unclean bones; and because vices seem beautiful and delight with their present sheen, they rest on their couches and are weighed down by the heaviest sleep. These do not eat solid and athletic food, by which the strength of wrestlers is reinforced, but soft and tender food, and of the left-hand side, such as are kids from the flocks and fattened calves, very tender, indeed still fed on milk, for this is what μοσχάρια γαλαθηνὰ means. They also clap with their hands to the voice and sound of the instruments, so that all their works look for pleasure, and they do nothing except what they provide for the belly and for lust. Nor let the prudent reader at once object to us this, and how it is written: Let the rivers clap with their hands, and: All nations, clap your hands, and: Shout for joy to God our helper. For there they are said to clap not to the sound of instruments, but to have one accord in the praise of God. After the voice of the instruments he added: They thought them as things standing, and not as things fleeing. For the things of the world and all bodies, according to Epicurus, flow and pass away moment by moment, and nothing stands firm in its own state, but all things either grow or decrease, and, in the manner of the waters of torrents, slide headlong. Hence we also read in secular literature: "But meanwhile time flies, flies beyond recall." And in another place: "Alas, Postumus, Postumus, the fleeting years slip by." For nothing is more fleeting than the world and the things of the world. While we hold them we lose them, and through infancy, boyhood, youth, and manhood, and the age that grows heavy, and ripe age, and the last years of old age, the seven stages into which Philo divides human life, we are changed, and we run, and unawares we arrive at the boundaries of death. And what follows, Who drink strained wine, can properly be referred to the heretics, who approve some Scriptures and reject others, wishing to drink strained wines, although in the holy Scriptures nothing is muddy and miry, but all is most pure, from the water above. These are anointed with the first ointments who, not having the perfumer's art, without any knowledge of the Scriptures, and of the traditions and teaching of the apostles, claim for themselves the dignity of the priesthood, and say that they are the anointed of the Lord, and contaminate the purest oil with the dregs of their own understanding. And while they do these things to the slaughter of those whom they have deceived, they are tormented by no grief, but rejoice in the deaths of others and take delight in the blood of the wretched. This most beautiful sense, in which it is said: They thought them as things standing, and not as things fleeing, is not found in the Hebrew, but in its place is written: Like David they thought they had instruments of song. Hence it seems to me to have been added by the translators, who in the description of luxury brought forth a maxim of this kind against vices and pleasures, so that they did not translate what was written, but added from their own what seemed good to them, or rather changed it.

6:7-11

Therefore now they shall migrate at the head of those who migrate, and the faction of the wanton shall be taken away. The Lord God has sworn by his soul; says the Lord God of hosts: I detest the pride of Jacob, and I hate his houses, and I will deliver up the city with its inhabitants. And if ten men remain in one house, they too shall die; and his kinsman shall take him up, and shall burn him, to carry the bones out of the house, and he shall say to the one who is in the inner rooms of the house: Is anyone still with you? LXX: Therefore now they shall be captives from the beginning of the mighty, and the neighing of horses shall be taken away from Ephraim; for the Lord has sworn by himself, says the Lord God of powers: For I will abominate all the insolence of Jacob, and I hate his regions, and I will take away the city with all its inhabitants. For the reasons above which the prophetic word describes — of those who sleep on beds of ivory, and wanton on their couches, and eat lambs from the flock and calves from the midst of the herd, and sing to the sound of the psaltery, and drink in bowls, and are anointed with the best ointment, and moreover suffer nothing over the breaking of his people, which was begotten of the stock of Joseph — the Lord now threatens and says: Because they have done this and this, now they shall migrate at the head of those who migrate. And the sense is: The punishment is by no means put off into the future, nor is it prophesied of ages long after; what now presses upon them, what is now about to come, my word foretells: that they will go at the beginning of those who migrate, namely the princes and the powerful, to whom he had said above: Hear this word, you fat cows, who are on the mountain of Samaria. And again: Woe to you who are wealthy in Zion, and trust in the mountain of Samaria; you nobles, heads of the peoples, entering with pomp the house of Israel. You who are first in riches will be the first to bear the yoke of captivity, according to what is written in Ezekiel: Begin from my sanctuary. Not from the saints, as most people think, but from the overthrow of the temple, which was holy. The mighty indeed shall suffer torments mightily, and from him to whom more is entrusted, more will be required. And there shall be taken away, he says, the faction of the wanton, who had one accord in seizing pleasures, and practised revels and shared feasts; these will be taken away together, so that those whose luxury was one may have one punishment. For this the LXX translated, the neighing of the horse shall be taken away from Ephraim, which is not found in the Hebrew, and will be discussed at length by us when we begin to weave the tropology. The Lord has sworn by himself, or, as we read in the Hebrew, by his soul, according to what is written in Isaiah: Your new moons and sabbaths and feast days my soul hates, not that God has a soul, but that he speaks with human affections. Nor is it surprising if he is said to have a soul, since he testifies that he has the other members too, which are lower than the soul, feet and hands and belly, and the other limbs. But if those who deny that Christ had a human soul, and say instead that in the human body God was in place of the soul, should raise this against us, let them hear that in Christ the substance of a soul is shown, just as the members of his body also had substance. But in God the Father, just as the other things that are spoken of are not members, but by their names a diversity of operations is indicated, so also the soul is not substantial, but an affection of the inner mind and the seat of thoughts, through which God indicates his will. Has sworn, therefore, the Lord, the God of hosts, that is, Sabaoth — for which the Seventy translated, of powers — that he detests the pride of Jacob and hates his houses. Here, following the preceding section, where it is written: And they suffered nothing over the breaking of Joseph, take Jacob either as the ten tribes, or certainly as the whole house of the twelve tribes. And he has sworn that he will deliver up the city with its inhabitants, either Samaria, or certainly Jerusalem, or both in common. This we can refer to the time of the Lord Savior, after whose coming and passion God detested all the pride, or the insult, of Jacob, with which they cried out that he was the son of a carpenter, and a Samaritan, and had a demon. For this reason Jerusalem with its inhabitants was delivered up to the Roman armies. And the wrath of God raged against them to such a degree that, even if ten men remain in one house, they too die, and a kinsman, or a neighbour, burns the corpses of the dead to carry the bones out of his house, because, for the crowds of the dying, whole bodies cannot be carried out. And when the one who has grown weary of carrying them away asks the one who is in the inner rooms of the house whether any remain whom he should hand over to him, and he answers: It is the end, I no longer have anyone to hand over to you for burial; then, before he can swear that he has none, the one who had asked, who was outside and had learned that there were none, commands him and says: Be silent, and do not remember the name of the Lord. He records this in order to show that not even when driven by the weight and necessity of evils are they willing to confess the name of the Lord, and that the name of God had come into such oblivion among Israel that Israel does not deign to hear his name even in a simple oath. We have drawn the slender lines of the history; now let us set our hand to the allegory. The princes of the heretics, who for the sake of their delights devoured my people and suffered nothing over the breaking of Joseph, will be the first to be led to punishment, and the neighing of the horse will be taken away from Ephraim, which in the holy Scriptures is taken in two ways: either for the pride and power of those who neigh, or for the greatness of lust. For pride and the conspiracy of the wicked, as there: Some in chariots, and some in horses, and: A horse is deceitful for safety. And the kings of Israel are commanded not to multiply horses for themselves. And in the book of Job the voice of the horse is compared to the blast of the trumpet. In Zechariah too we read, what is confirmed by the testimony of the Gospel and is referred to the presence of the Savior: Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion, proclaim, daughter of Jerusalem. Behold, your king comes to you, just and a Savior, himself meek, and mounting upon a she-ass and the foal of a she-ass; and he will destroy the four-horse chariots from Ephraim, and the horses from Jerusalem. But for the greatness of lust and the unbridled desire for intercourse, as in Jeremiah, who, describing the luxurious and the adulterers, added: Each one neighed after his neighbour's wife. Such horses the coming of Christ and the wrath of God hamstring. And the Lord swears by himself, since he has no one greater by whom to swear, that he detests all the insolences of the heretics and hates all their regions. For whatever they say is an insult, and worthy of the hatred of God. And he swears that he will take away the city and their meeting-places with those who dwell in them, namely both the people and the teachers, and that, even if ten men remain — men who, had they been in Sodom and Gomorrah, the fire would by no means have come down upon those cities — all will die that death which leads to Tartarus, of which Ezekiel writes: The soul that sins, it shall die. Their bones are buried by their kinsmen and their household, of whom it is said: Let the dead bury their own dead. And he who is outside, and does not enter the house of the dead, but rather casts the dead out, commands the one who burns the dead, and dissolves them into ashes, and crushes their bones, to be silent and not to stain the most pure name of God with a dead mouth. For God says to the sinner: Why do you declare my justices, and take up my covenant through your mouth? Hence we too must take care that we do not, being dead, bury the dead, but rather that, living, we lead forth to life those who are dead. If we do not do this, we are commanded and told, Be silent, because we are judged unworthy of the name of God.

6:12-15

For behold, the Lord will command, and he will strike the greater house with ruins, and the lesser house with rents. Can horses run on rocks, or can there be ploughing with wild oxen? For you have turned judgment into bitterness, and the fruit of righteousness into wormwood. You who rejoice in nothing, who say: Did we not by our own strength take horns for ourselves? For behold, I will raise up against you, house of Israel, says the Lord God of hosts, a nation; and they will crush you from the entrance of Hamath to the torrent of the desert. LXX: Therefore behold, the Lord will command, and he will strike the great house with ruins, and the small house with rents. Will horses give chase on rocks, will they keep silent toward the females? For you have turned judgment into fury, and the fruit of righteousness into bitterness. You who rejoice over no good word, who says: Did we not have horns by our own strength? For behold, I will raise up against you, house of Israel, says the Lord God of powers, a nation; and they will crush you, so that you do not enter into Hamath, and as far as the torrent of the west. Because you have leapt into such madness that even in the time of death, with evils hanging over you, you were unwilling to name the name of the Lord, therefore the Lord will command, and he will strike the greater house with ruins, and the lesser house with rents. If he commands, how does he strike? If he strikes, how does he command? But in commanding and giving orders to his ministers, he himself is seen to strike. Just as, when the Father commands and the Son performs, he who commanded himself does it, and that little verse is fulfilled: He spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created. For all things indeed were made through him, and without him nothing was made that was made. And in Egypt the Lord testifies that he himself killed the firstborn, who are reported to have been slain by the destroyer. So too in the present place the Lord commands, and he himself, through his ministers, strikes the greater house with ruins, that is, the ten tribes, which were called Israel, and the lesser house with rents, the two tribes, which were ruled by the stock of the house of David. And note the particular character of each. Israel, because it had sinned more greatly, is struck with ruins and handed over to perpetual captivity. But the house of Judah, in which the temple was, and which had sinned in part, is held in a captivity of seventy years, and is not struck with ruins but with rents. For a house that is rent can be mended; ruins call not so much for restoration as for building anew. The ruins and rents of these two houses he compares to horses and wild oxen: the former cannot run on rocks; the latter are so untamed that they do not take the yoke on their necks, and, being forest cattle, because of their wildness they refuse to cleave the earth with the ploughshare. But you, though horses and wild oxen cannot change their nature, have changed the nature of God, so as to make the sweet one bitter and to turn the fruit of his righteousness into wormwood, which is a most bitter herb. You who rejoice in nothing, either in idols and golden calves, which are nothing, as Esther says to the Lord: Do not hand over your sceptre to those who are not, or in nothingness and falsehood. You who think that by your own strength you took horns, and kings and power, with which to toss your enemies. Because, therefore, you have done these things, behold, I will raise up against you, O great house and lesser house, which are to be struck with ruins and rents; that is, O house of Israel and all the twelve tribes, the most savage nation of the Assyrians and Chaldeans, to crush and overthrow you from beginning to end, from head to tail; from the borders of your land which look toward the sun, to the torrent of the desert, or of the west, as the LXX translated, that is, from Hamath to Rhinocorura, between which and Pelusium the stream of the Nile, or the torrent coming from the wilderness, enters the sea. We said above that Hamath is Epiphania, which took its name from Antiochus, whose surname was Ἐπιφανὴς (Epiphanes). But those who think that the house of Israel here signifies the ten tribes will not be able to explain how it can be said in a threat against the ten tribes that they are to be crushed from Epiphania to the borders of Egypt, within which borders not the ten tribes only, but all twelve are enclosed, among which were also Judah and Benjamin. Some, according to anagogy, think that the great house and the lesser house signify the Jewish people and the Church gathered from the Gentiles. For they are called great because of the fathers, and the law, and the prophets; we lesser, because we were without the covenant and the commandments of God, of whom we also read in the Song of Songs: My sister is little, and she has no breasts. This house, great and small, gathered into the one household of God, if it does not keep discipline and does not do the commandments of God, will be struck with ruins and rents. Moreover, what he says, plucking sycamines, which Aquila translated searching sycamores, and Symmachus having sycamores, some explain in this way, holding that sycamines is the name of a kind of tree that grows in the plains of Palestine and bears wild figs; and these, if they are not plucked, produce very bitter little figs and are spoiled by gnats. To us, however, since the wilderness in which Amos lived produces no tree of this kind, it seems rather that he means brambles, which bear blackberries and relieve the hunger and want of herders. However the reader may wish to take it, this must be said: that the Lord took up a lowly and rustic herder, and sent him to his people Israel, and commanded him to go out from his own land and proceed to Samaria, and there prophesy the things that are to come. Since, therefore, he says, when the Lord commands me to speak, you, Amaziah, say to me: You shall not prophesy over Israel, and you shall not drip upon the house of the idol, hear what the Lord threatens against you. For house of the idol, in the Hebrew is written beth isaac, that is, house of laughter, for Isaac means laughter; which the LXX translated house of Jacob, understanding one name in place of another, and not the thing. Again, where we have put: And you shall not drip, Symmachus translated: you shall not rebuke. Now that the prophets drip is an idiom of the Scriptures, because they do not bring on the whole wrath of God at once, but announce it in small drops by their threatening. Because, therefore, you said to me: You shall not prophesy, your wife shall commit fornication in the city; which Symmachus translated better, πορνευθήσεται, not that she herself will commit fornication, but that, in the passive voice, violated by others, she will suffer fornication. And it is a great grief and an incredible disgrace when a husband, in the middle of the city, with everyone present, cannot prevent the outrage done to his wife. Your sons also, he says, and your daughters shall fall by the sword. The grief over a violated daughter is not so great as over a defiled wife, nor the grief over a slain wife so great as over slaughtered children. For a husband would more willingly have his wife killed than defiled. Nor is this enough for the outrage, unless his land too is divided with the line by the enemy, and receives new tillers. Amaziah himself also, who now glories in the power of the priesthood, will by no means die in his own land, but, led into captivity, will die in a land polluted with idols; and he will not die before he sees the people he had deceived enslaved and captive. According to tropology, Amaziah the priest means strong and rigid, because, rebellious and fierce, he forbids the churchman and true prophet to bring forth the words and the teaching of God, and prohibits him from correcting the erring people. His wife, the false Church, will lie open to the fornication of all, and his sons and daughters, whom he wickedly begot in error, will be struck by the Lord's sword; his ground too and all his possession will be the portion of demons, and he himself will die in a polluted land, which does not have God as its inhabitant but is contaminated by the many errors of a depraved religion; and all the people who take to themselves the false name of Israel will go captive out of their land, so as not to serve God, but to submit their necks to the yoke of heretics and demons.

What Jeremiah sees under the figure of the rod of watchings, or of the nut, because God kept watch over the sins of his people, lest, as though with eyes shut and blinking, he should not see their offenses, this Amos now beholds against Judah and Israel alike under the appearance of a hook, which in Hebrew is called chelub and is turned by the LXX into a fowler's vessel; whereas a fowler is properly called moces, as we read above in this same prophet: If a bird will fall upon the earth without a fowler. And the sense is: As the branches of trees are drawn down with a hook to pick the fruit, so I have drawn near the time of captivity. And that we may know that this is what we say, God himself explains what the vision he had shown the prophet signifies: The end has come upon my people Israel. And what follows: I will not again pass him by any more, signifies this: that he will no longer pass over the iniquities of his people, nor neglect them, nor allow their crimes to pass unpunished. And lest he seem to speak only of the ten tribes, he added: The hinges of the temple shall creak, or the ceilings, in that day, says the Lord. This, however, is to be taken ὑπερβολικῶς (hyperbolically): so heavy a weight of evils will press down that even the very hinges of the gates and the lofty ceilings will howl and feel an incredible devastation, when many will die and, the inhabitants being captured or killed, silence will be cast in every place. But if we choose to read a fowler's vessel instead of a hook for fruit, this must be said: that as a fowler, with birdlime or nets, draws down to the earth birds that fly through the air and range on high, so the Lord, through Sennacherib or Nebuchadnezzar, whom we now understand as the fowlers, seized, bound, carried away and killed his people, who were formerly free and lofty through their observance of the law. For not unjustly are nets spread for birds. And this is to be referred not only to the time of the Babylonian captivity, but also to the coming of the Lord the Savior, when they said: Away with such a one from the earth; crucify him, crucify him; and they lost the wings of the dove. And the end came upon them, and the final captivity, and God spared them no longer. And the hinges of the temple howled, or the ceilings of the temple, collapsing upon the slain people. And in every place silence has been cast, the Jews having Moses and the prophets, but not having the Word of God; reading the letter and losing the spirit, when their table was made into a snare, and into a recompense, and into a ruin; and their eyes were darkened, so that they should not see; and their ears were stopped, so that they should not hear; and their back was bowed down, so that they should not look up to heaven, but, with that woman of the Gospel who had a spirit of infirmity, should always be bent toward the earth. And the wrath of God was poured out upon them, and the fury of his wrath seized them, so that their habitation became desolate, and there was no inhabitant in their tents; when this was fulfilled: Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and I will send a famine upon the land; not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the word of the Lord; about which, if we reach that point, we must speak more fully later on. We, then, seeing that God did not spare the natural branches, let us fear to suffer the same things, and let us avoid the fowler's snares, and say to the Lord: Do not be silent, nor hold yourself back, O God, and do not keep silence from me, and I shall become like those who go down into the pit. Over the whole world the silence of the Jews has been cast: wherever they are, they mutter rather than speak; and though their blasphemy against the Lord the Savior reaches even to heaven, all interpretation of the Scriptures is mute and silent, and does not set its ears to hearing.

Shall not the earth be moved over this, and every inhabitant of it mourn, and it rise up wholly like a river, and be cast out, and flow away like the brook of Egypt? LXX: The Lord has sworn against the pride of Jacob: If he will forget all your works to the end. And over these things shall not the earth be troubled, and every inhabitant of it mourn, and its consummation rise up like a stream, and go down like the river of Egypt? Scripture quite often records the Lord swearing. First, in Genesis, the angel of the Lord called Abraham a second time from heaven, saying: By myself I have sworn, says the Lord, because you have done this thing, and have not spared your dearest son, blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you like the stars of heaven, and like the sand that is on the shore of the sea. And in the hundred and ninth (110th) psalm: The Lord has sworn, and he will not repent; you are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek. Discussing this, whoever he is who wrote the epistle to the Hebrews says: Since he had no one greater by whom to swear, he swore by himself. And in this same prophet we read above: The Lord has sworn by his holiness, or by his holy ones, that behold, days will come upon you, and they will take you up with weapons, or with poles, and the rest. He, then, who had earlier sworn by his holiness, or by his holy ones, now swears against the pride of Jacob, so that if they do not believe him when he threatens, they may believe him when he swears, after the likeness of men, who take up an oath when their simple words are not believed. Nor is it surprising if God is said to swear, since he sleeps with those who sleep and keeps watch with those who keep watch, and is said to be angry with those who have stored up wrath for themselves in the day of wrath. And he swears that he will not forget their works even to the end; for the proud God resists, but gives grace to the humble; but that he holds in memory all the sins they have committed, and brings them even to the end and the time of judgment. Hence the penitent pray: Do not remember our former iniquities; especially since against the proud the earth itself is moved, or troubled, and mourns, not the tenant and the stranger, but its inhabitant; and its consummation rises up like a river, and goes down like the stream of Egypt, so that he who repents rises with the climbing river, but he who persists in his offenses goes down like the river of Egypt and, entering the sea, is swallowed up. And by this he shows that the pride of Jacob, against which the Lord swears, is to be devoured by eternal punishments.

This famine, on the day of the Lord's resurrection, overwhelmed the people of the Jews, and joined to the famine was a most burning thirst, since they did not have the bread which came down from heaven, nor those waters which flowed from the belly of Jesus. The law has been taken away from them, and the prophets have fallen silent in eternal silence; they are moved from sea to sea, and from the British Ocean to the Atlantic Ocean, that is, from the west to the south, and from the north to the east, wanderers through the whole world, and they are not able to find the word of God. At this place we shall ask the Jews what day they think is signified, in which they are to endure a famine of hearing the word of God, especially since they read the Scriptures and follow the lowliness of the letter? To them we press home that what was foretold is a famine of spiritual understanding, in which Christ is discerned, and the passion and resurrection of the Lord are found. They go around the world and seek the word of the Lord and do not find it, because they denied the Word of the Lord, which came to pass in the hands of all the prophets, which in the beginning was with the Father, which was made flesh and dwelt among us. He who had said above: There shall be no flight for them; he shall flee, and he who flees shall not be saved, now cuts flight itself into its parts, and testifies hyperbolically that even if they do this and that, they cannot escape. If they go down, he says, even to hell, from there my hand shall bring them out: not that anyone is drawn back from the lower world before the day of resurrection, but that even those placed in hell are in his power. We know that this was fulfilled upon Dathan and Abiram, whom the gaping of the earth swallowed alive. And if they go up, he says, even to heaven, from there I will drag them down: because both Enoch and Elijah, caught up with their bodies into heaven, are governed by the will of God. And observe the proper use of the words: one goes down to hell, one climbs up to heaven; we are brought out of hell, we are dragged down from the heavens. In the one there is extreme despair, in the other the greatness of pride. If they are hidden on the top of Carmel, either by the borders of Phoenicia in the northern region, or toward the south on account of the vastness of the desert, where Nabal of Carmel once lived, from there, he says, I will search them out and take them away; and if in the depth of the sea, and, to speak of the prophet, in Tarshish, they try to avoid the eyes of God, there I will command the serpent, by which in this place he signifies Leviathan, or the sea-monster, so as to keep the metaphor, and to come to the enemies by way of the serpent and the sea-monster. And it shall bite them, that is, it shall devour them with its jaws. And lest perhaps, from what he said above, we should understand anything other than captivity at the hands of the enemy, Scripture, after its custom, has disclosed what it had said before under riddles. And if they go into captivity before their enemies, there I will command the sword, and it shall slay them: so that, namely, they may not think servitude the extremity of their evils, but the enemy's blade may pierce them even as captives; and those who have been able to survive and escape death may not escape the gaze of God, but he will set his eyes upon them for evil and not for good, so that he may always visit them and drive them to repentance through torments. And Almighty God says that he will do these things, at whose touch and nod the foundations of the earth are shaken, or melt, and every inhabitant of it is taken up with lamentation and mourning. He says these things to show the greatness of the divine power, lest perhaps he should indeed wish to do what he threatens, but his strength should not fulfil his will. But if the earth melts, or is shaken, and even insensible nature feels its Creator, how much more man, a fragile creature, the treasure of whose soul is shut up in earthen and bodily vessels? And as the river of Egypt runs down into the sea and is swallowed up, so also the land of Israel, of which he had said above: Who touches the earth and it melts, will go into captivity and will be devoured by the enemies; μετωνυμικῶς (by metonymy), the divine word signifying by that which holds, that which is held. And these things, following the order of the history, we have interpreted as best we could, if indeed understanding has not failed us in some points. But David sets out the whole περιοχὴν (the whole tenor) of this passage, saying: Lord, you have proved me, and you have known me. And at once he adds: Where shall I go from your spirit, and from your face where shall I flee? If I ascend into heaven, you are there; if I descend into hell, you are there. If I take my wings at dawn, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea: even there your hand shall lead me, and your right hand shall hold me. In Deuteronomy also we read something similar: This commandment which I command you today is not above you, nor placed far off, nor set in heaven, so that you could say: Which of us is able to climb up to heaven, to bring it down to us, that we may hear it and fulfil it in deed? Nor is it placed beyond the sea, so that you should make excuses and say: Which of us will be able to cross the sea and bring it all the way to us, so that we may be able to hear and do what is commanded? But the word is near you, exceedingly, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may do it. For when the soul, loosed from the bodily chains, has, by reason of the thinness of its substance, the freedom to fly where it will, or where it is compelled to go, it will either be led to the lower regions, of which it is written: Let sinners be turned into hell, all the nations that forget God, and: In hell who will confess to you? And: Your glory has gone down into hell. Or else it will be lifted up to the heavenly places, where are the spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places; and if it wishes to claim for itself the knowledge of the true circumcision, for this is what Carmel is interpreted, and, scorning humility, to dwell on the mountains, even there it will not be able to escape the searching hand of God. But if, despairing of salvation, it tries to avoid the eyes of God and to reach the farthest limits of the salt waves, even there the Lord will command the crooked and ancient serpent, who is the enemy and the avenger, and it will bite it. Taken captive also by vices and sins, it will be punished with the sword of the Lord, and he will set his eyes upon it for evil and not for good, so that through tortures and punishments it may return to the Lord. And when Almighty God has touched their land and shaken it, and has made all earthly things melt, they, understanding their crimes, will be turned to mourning, and the Lord himself will ascend and descend in a twofold way. He will ascend upon the saints like the river of its consummation, that is, of the earth, so as to consume their earthly works. He will descend upon sinners like the flood of Egypt, so that by the Lord's onrush they may be thrust down into the bitterness of torments.

9:6

He who builds his ascent in heaven, and has founded his bundle upon the earth; who calls the waters of the sea, and pours them out upon the face of the earth: the Lord is his name. LXX: He who builds his ascent in heaven, and founds his promise upon the earth; who calls the water of the sea, and pours it out upon the face of the earth: the Lord Almighty is his name. The Lord God Almighty, who looks upon, or touches, the earth and shakes it, is himself the one who daily builds his ascent into heaven, and says in the Gospel: My Father works until now, and I work. And not only did he once build Eve from the rib of Adam as a type of the Church, but daily he builds believers and the members of his body, and lifts them from the earth to heaven, so that in them he himself may climb up. The Lord ascended into heaven with Enoch, he ascended with Elijah, he ascended with Moses, the place of whose burial could not be found on earth, because he had ascended into heaven. He ascended with Paul, who, a vessel of election, was changed from a persecutor into an apostle, and was caught up from lowly things to the heights, so that he ascended into the third heaven, and through the Holy Spirit and the Son came to the Father, and heard the unspeakable words of the mystery of the Trinity, which it is not lawful for men to hear. He, therefore, who daily ascends in the saints, has founded his bundle upon the earth, of which he speaks in the Gospel: Fear not, little flock, for it has pleased my Father to dwell in you. This bundle is bound tight by the one religion of the Lord. Hence religion itself has taken its name from binding fast, and from tying into the Lord's bundle. Moreover, according to the Seventy, he founds his promise upon the earth, so that all his promises, which the holy prophets sang with their mouths, may not have an empty sound and the hollow names of tropology alone, but may be founded on the earth. And when they have the foundations of history, then let them receive the summit of spiritual understanding: so that Christ was truly born of a virgin, truly raised the dead Lazarus, at his touch the αἱμοῤῤοῦσα (the woman with the issue of blood) was truly healed, at the coming of the Lord the blind truly saw, the lame ran, withered hands were stretched out, the leprosy was cleansed; although according to tropology the divine word is daily born of a virgin soul; daily those dead in sin and bound with the cords of vices are bidden to come out of the tomb of their crimes; daily the works of blood are stanched; the blind look upon the light in faithfulness to Christ; those who were lame before in faith run in the way of the Lord; and hands withered by avarice are stretched out to almsgiving; and the leprosy of Miriam, which defiles whatever it has touched, recovers its former purity. But this Lord also calls the most bitter waters of the sea, and has poured them out upon those who have turned their face to the Lord. And he calls the bitter waters for this reason: to make them sweet, and to bring forth the winds from his treasuries, and by his command to hang aloft the waters heavy with salt, and, straining them and boiling them with the heat of the upper air, to dispense them in rains and send them out upon the face of the earth, so that all dry places may be watered with showers, and where sin abounded, grace may abound the more.

9:7-8

Are you not like the sons of the Ethiopians to me, sons of Israel, says the Lord? Did I not make Israel go up from the land of Egypt, and the Philistines from Cappadocia, and the Syrians from Cyrene? Behold, the eyes of the Lord God are upon the sinning kingdom, and I will crush it from the face of the earth; nevertheless, crushing, I will not crush the house of Jacob, says the Lord. LXX: Are you not like the sons of the Ethiopians to me, sons of Israel, says the Lord? Did I not bring Israel out of the land of Egypt, and the foreigners from Cappadocia, and the Syrians from the pit? Behold, the eyes of the Lord God are upon the kingdom of sinners, and I will take it away from the face of the earth. Nevertheless I will not take away the house of Jacob to the end, says the Lord. For Cyrene, Aquila and the Fifth edition put the Hebrew word itself, cir; the Seventy 'pit', that is, βόθρον; Theodotion 'wall'; Symmachus 'Cyrene', whom we too have followed in this place. The whole understanding of this passage which I have set forth, and of everything contained in this section, I will sum up in a brief discourse, up to the place where it is written: Who say, Evil will not draw near, and will not come upon us, so that we may be able to know what is said: I, the Lord God Almighty, from whom nothing can be hidden, who touch the earth and it shall be moved; who build my ascent in heaven; who call the waters of the sea and pour them out upon the face of the earth; whose name is the Lord; I who am the creator of all, formed all the nations from the same clay and begot them with an equal lot. In short, I have joined together the Ethiopians, the Philistines, the Syrians and the Jews, far apart as they are in places and in bodies, by the law of mortality, and at my will I move my servants hither and thither, and transfer them into all the provinces. Do not be lifted up into pride because I brought you out of Egypt and, as my peculiar people, did not allow you to serve Pharaoh; this same thing I did also for the Philistines, whom the Seventy translated 'foreigners', who in Hebrew are called caphthorim: I transferred them from Cappadocia and settled them in the regions of Palestine; the Syrians also, that is, Aram, I transferred from Cyrene; and so those who were made in an equal condition will be punished by an equal sentence of my judgment, and I will overturn all impious kingdoms, without respect of persons. But you, O sons of Israel, of whom I said: Israel is my firstborn son, and: Out of Egypt I called my son: I will strike you with the rod and will visit your sins; but I will not crush you for ever, and I will not take my mercy away from you; and, as though you were shaken and tossed in a sieve, I will cleanse and choose you, so that whoever is a little stone, and strengthened through repentance, may not fall out of my sieve, but whoever falls to the ground like dust may be struck with the sword; and the sinners of my people shall die, not because they sinned before, but because they persevered in their sins until death. But everyone who is Israel, who once saw God with his mind and was brought out of Egypt, if, desiring the vices of Egypt and the pleasures of the world, he returns not only into Egypt but into Ethiopia, in which whoever is, according to Jeremiah, cannot change his skin, is saved by the coming of Christ, and there is fulfilled in him what is written: Ethiopia shall hasten its hands to God. And when he has worshipped him, it will be said of him: Before him the Ethiopians shall fall down. And when they have fallen down, and have brought victims to the Lord from there, from beyond the rivers of Ethiopia, they will be able to say: I am black and beautiful, because the sun has discolored me. For bodies that languish in shade and idleness cannot bear the temptations and the heats of the world; but those which are made ready for wrestling and for contests, dried by the sun, overcome the injuries of the world and obtain the blessing of the Holy Spirit, who says to the righteous man: The sun shall not burn you by day, nor the moon by night. The Ethiopians, therefore, are turned into sons of God if they repent, and the sons of God pass over into Ethiopians if they come into the depth of sins. For God himself, the Creator of all, turns Cappadocians into Philistines, and those who dwelt in the cold of faith and lay exposed to the North, the harshest wind, he made to fall drinking, and, laying aside their pride, to feel the judgment of the Lord. The Syrians also, that is, the lofty and sublime, who are called Aram, he transferred from Cyrene, the weak wall, or the place which overhung the most bitter sea and was near the Syrtes; and what, according to the LXX, was held in the pit, he changed for the good, so as to make them lofty. For the eyes of the Lord are upon all the sinning kingdoms, which the devil showed to the Lord, and of which the Apostle says: Let not sin reign in your mortal body, so that you obey its desires. Nevertheless, although he visits sinners with the rod, and crushes and strikes those who have remained in sin, he does not allow to perish for ever those souls which are called the house of Jacob, and supplant sin, and overcome in the contest, and seize the heel of the bloodstained brother.

9:9-10

For behold, I will command, and I will shake the house of Israel among all the nations, as it is shaken in a sieve, and not a pebble shall fall upon the earth. All the sinners of my people shall die by the sword, who say: It will not draw near, and evil will not come upon us. LXX: For behold, I give command, and I will winnow the house of Israel among all the nations, as it is winnowed with a winnowing fan, and no crushing shall fall upon the earth. All the sinners of my people shall die by the sword, who say: It will not draw near, nor will evils come upon us. God, who has measured the waters with his hand, and the heaven with his palm, and encloses the whole earth in his fist, he himself, as befits his greatness, holding the edges of the earth with both hands, will shake it hither and thither like a sieve; so that, while the chaff and the filth of sinners fall to the earth, the pure grain may remain, to be stored in the granaries; or, as the LXX translated, he will hold the winnowing fan in his hand, and will cleanse his threshing floor, and will store the wheat in the granaries, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire; of which he speaks through Jeremiah: What has the chaff to do with the grain, says the Lord? The same thing, under the figure of another parable, is shown by that net which is cast into the sea of this world and draws out fish of every kind, and, when the bad fish have been thrown away, only the good are kept. So too the Lord has scattered the wretched house of Israel through the whole world, and shaken it in a sieve, and winnowed it with a shovel; and while the pebbles and little stones do not fall upon the earth, those will die by the sword who, on account of their filth and dust, are called the sinners of the people. And they will suffer this because they do not believe the prophecies of the prophets, nor think that the things which the Lord threatens through them will come. And though they promise themselves prosperity, they will afterwards endure evils; while, on the other side, the saints fear and do not sin, and therefore by no means die by the sword, because they said: Evils will draw near to us, and the punishments which our sins have deserved will come upon us; of which God speaks more fully in Jeremiah: I will take it up and speak against a nation and a kingdom, to take it away and destroy it; if that nation turns from its evils, I too will repent of the things that I had thought to do to it. God, therefore, is not changed, who is always immutable; but we change him by our conversion. He rages, he is angry, he threatens, and says that he will inflict punishments: If we repent, he too will repent of his sentence. Again, according to the same Jeremiah, he promises prosperity: If we grow slack through negligence, he too will repent of his pledge, and will change what he promised. Of this we can have an example in the Ninevites and in Jerusalem, of whom the one were set free from the punishments that hung over them, the other lost what had been promised to their fathers.

9:11-12

In that day I will raise up the tabernacle of David that has fallen, and I will rebuild the openings of its walls, and what had collapsed I will restore, and I will rebuild it as in the days of old, that they may possess the remnant of Idumea, and all the nations, because my name has been invoked upon them, says the Lord who does these things. LXX: In that day I will raise up the tabernacle of David that had fallen, and I will rebuild what had collapsed, and what has been undermined I will raise up, and I will restore them as the days of old, that the rest of men may seek me, and all the Gentiles upon whom my name has been invoked, says the Lord who does all these things. Where the authority of the apostles goes before, above all of Peter and James, whom the vessel of election calls pillars of the Church, there every suspicion of a differing explanation must be removed, and what is expounded by such great men, that must be followed. In the Acts of the Apostles, when a question had been raised among the apostles as to why Paul and Barnabas had received men from the Gentiles without circumcision and the observance of the sabbath, Peter answered as he ought; and James, approving his judgment, spoke thus: Men, brothers, hear me: Simon has related how God first visited to take from the Gentiles a people, for his name, and with this the words of the prophets agree; as it is written: After these things I will return, and I will build the tabernacle of David that has fallen, and its ruins I will rebuild, and I will set it up; that the rest of men may seek the Lord, and all the Gentiles upon whom my name has been invoked, says the Lord who does these things; known to the Lord from eternity is his work. This tabernacle of David, then, which had fallen — and it had fallen in those who say: It will not draw near, and evil will not come upon us, whom the Lord had shaken in his sieve and tested, and whose threshing floor he had cleansed with the winnowing fan of his majesty, and whoever had been sinners he killed with the sword —, he now says, according to the custom of the Scriptures, promising prosperous and joyful things after torments, after punishments, that he will raise up, and that in the resurrection of the Lord he will restore all things, so that what had fallen in the synagogues may rise in the churches, and believers may possess the remnant of Idumea, and all the nations; so that whatever is left of the bloodthirsty and earthly kingdom may be changed into heavenly kingdoms, and all the Gentiles who have forgotten the Lord may be converted and return to him. But if we choose to read according to the LXX: That the rest of men may seek me, and all the Gentiles upon whom my name has been invoked, we ought to take as the rest of men those who believed from the Jewish people, and who, like little stones, did not fall through the sieve, nor were cast out with the dust and the chaff and the filth. For a remnant will be saved at the beginning of the faith, and at the end of the world, so that when the fullness of the Gentiles has come in, then all Israel may be saved. And the name of the Lord which is invoked upon the remnant and upon all the Gentiles is that of which the Savior says: Father, I have revealed your name to men. And, not to put off with a long discourse the rule for these promises: both in this prophet and in the others, whatever is proclaimed about the building of Jerusalem and of the temple, and about the blessedness of all things, the Jews promise themselves with a vain expectation for the end of time, and they speak of it as to be fulfilled carnally. But we, who follow not the letter that kills but the spirit that gives life, prove that these things are already accomplished in the Church, and are fulfilled daily in individuals, who, falling through sin, are rebuilt through repentance.

9:13-15

Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and the ploughman shall overtake the reaper, and the treader of the grape him who sows the seed. And the mountains shall drip sweetness and all the hills shall be tilled. And I will turn back the captivity of my people Israel, and they shall build the deserted cities, and they shall dwell in them, and they shall plant vineyards, and they shall drink their wine, and they shall make gardens, and they shall eat their fruits. And I will plant them upon their own soil, and I will not uproot them any more from their land which I have given them, says the Lord your God. LXX: Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and the threshing shall overtake the vintage, and the grape shall change its colour at the sowing. And the mountains shall drip sweetness, and all the hills shall be planted. And I will turn back the captivity of my people Israel, and they shall build the ruined cities, and they shall dwell in them, and they shall plant vineyards, and they shall drink their wine, and they shall make gardens, and they shall eat their fruit. And they shall be planted upon their own land, and they shall not be torn away any more from their land which I have given them, says the Lord God Almighty. When the tabernacle of David that had fallen has been raised up, and what had slipped and been overturned has been rebuilt, the abundance of all things is described: how those who before going went and wept, carrying their seeds, coming shall come with exultation, carrying their sheaves, so that the threshing of the sheaves overtakes the vintage, or the ploughman the reaper, and the grape changes its colour and takes on its hue at the time of sowing. Or, as is truer, the treader of the grape overtakes the sowing, and so all things follow one upon another, so that no day is without grain, wine and joy. In that time the grape will be trodden in full winepresses, and new wine reddened with the blood of Christ and of the martyrs will be poured out, and a treader of the grape of this kind will be the seedbed of the word of God, so that their blood cries out in the world more than the blood of righteous Abel cried out. And whoever by the merit of his virtues has climbed up to the mountain heights will sweat honey, or rather will drip the sweetness of the word of God, of which it is written: Taste and see how sweet the Lord is, and: How sweet are your words to my throat, above honey and the honeycomb to my mouth. And those who are below the mountains, or rather second to the mountains, whom the bridegroom leaps over in the Song of Songs and calls hills, will be planted and will imitate the paradise of God, so that every fruit of teachings hangs in them. Then, if anyone is a captive through unbelief, and has not yet believed in the name of the Lord, and is of the remnant of Israel, once his people, he will return to the faith of Christ, so that what he had heard in the prophets he may hold in the Gospel. And these mountains which will drip sweetness, and the hills which have been planted, after the Lord has turned back the captivity of his people Israel, will build cities that were before deserted, and will dwell in them, so that each city that has been built may say: I am a strong city, a city that is besieged, and the one of which the Lord speaks in the Gospel: A city set upon a mountain cannot be hidden. And in the psalms it is said: The rush of the river makes glad the city of God. They will also plant vineyards with Noah, and drink their wine, and be drunk, and they will hear from the Lord Savior: Drink, my friends, and be drunk. And they will drink the wine which he promised he would drink new with his apostles in the kingdom of the Father. This is the vineyard of Sorek, whose wine we drink daily in the mysteries: Not content with the happiness of these things, they will make gardens for their delight, and will water them, so that no kind of virtue may be lacking in them, they will eat their fruits; for he who has planted and watered will himself also eat. And when the mountains and hills have done all these things, dripping sweetness, and building cities, and dwelling in them, planting vineyards, and drinking their wine, making gardens, and eating their fruits, then the Lord will plant the planters themselves upon their own land, of which it is said: I believe that I shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living, and in the Gospel: Blessed are the meek, for they shall possess the land. And after he has planted them, and made them firm with a root driven deep, he will not uproot them any more from the land which he had given them. From this we understand that the Church, until the end of the world, is indeed shaken by persecutions, but can by no means be overthrown; tempted, not overcome. And this will come about because the Lord God Almighty, or the Lord her God, that is, the Church's, has promised that he will do it, he whose promise is a law of nature.
