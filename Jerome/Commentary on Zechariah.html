Prologue

Already at the very end of autumn our brother, your son the monk Sisinnius, delivered to me a letter from your Grace; on reading it I rejoiced that you are safe and well, and mindful of me and of all the brothers who serve the Lord in the holy places, in whose relief you make friends for yourself of the unrighteous mammon and prepare eternal tabernacles, so that you can say with David: How lovely are your tabernacles, O Lord of powers; my soul longs and faints for the courts of the Lord. For if the sparrow has found a house for itself, and the turtledove a nest for itself where it may lay its young, why should you, who are a high priest of the Lord, and tread the winepresses at the end of the world so as to give to thirsting peoples the wine of the blood of Christ, not freely cry out and say: My heart and my flesh have exulted in the living God; blessed are those who dwell in your house. Having heard that you, in the valley of tears, in the place which God has set for the contest so as to give a crown to those who conquer, are arranging ascents in your heart, and going from strength to strength, and imitating the poverty of the Lord, that with him you may become rich, and that he may lay his head upon you, and day by day be received, visited, fed and clothed; and that above all you are fervent in the reading of the holy Scriptures. And since I desired to offer you some little gift of my poor talent, and the exposition I had begun on the twelve prophets was coming to its close, I was unwilling to abandon the work I had undertaken, but what I would have dictated even without you I have consecrated above all to your name; and I am glad that the most obscure book of the prophet Zechariah, and the longest among the twelve, is to be expounded at a time when, because of the haste of him who is to return, the interpretation suffers no delay; but, willing or unwilling, I am compelled to dictate, at least in hours gained and stolen by night, what I am to send you. Origen wrote two volumes on this prophet, reaching from the beginning to the third part of the book. Hippolytus also published commentaries, and Didymus, at my request, dictated five books of exposition, which, with three others on Hosea, he also προσεφώνησεν (dedicated) to me; but their whole ἐξήγησις (exposition) was allegorical, and they barely touched a few points of the history. Therefore, desiring to imitate that householder who brings forth from his treasure things new and old, and the bride of the Song of Songs, who says: New with old, my kinsman, I have kept for you, I have mingled the tropology of our writers with the history of the Hebrews, so as to build upon the rock and not upon the sand, and to lay a firm foundation, which Paul the architect wrote that he had laid. It will be for your kindness to look up, not to our learning, which is either none or small, but to the will that leans toward you, so that you may spur us on to the rest and urge us to run in the long field of the Scriptures. But if there are any to whom I previously promised an interpretation both of these and of other books, let them pardon my incredible love for you, and let them consider whatever I have written to you as written to themselves: For love is kind, love is not envious, it does not seek its own. But now it is time that, setting down the words of Zechariah, we spread the sails of interpretation to the Holy Spirit.

Book One

1:1

In the eighth month, in the second year of Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo, the prophet, saying. LXX: In the eighth month of the second year under Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah of Berechiah, the son of Iddo, the prophet, saying. That in the second year of Darius son of Hystaspes the seventieth year of the desolation of the temple, which had been foretold by Jeremiah, was completed, Zechariah himself is witness, saying: O Lord of hosts, how long will you not have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, with which you are angry? Behold, this is the seventieth year; and no one doubts that Cyrus, king of the Persians, who destroyed the empire of the Chaldeans, in the first year of his reign sent back from Babylon into Judea about fifty thousand captives; he also returned the vessels which Nebuchadnezzar had carried off, and ordered the temple to be rebuilt in Jerusalem, and he appointed as leaders of the people that returned Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest. So when Cyrus, who reigned thirty years among the Persians, had been killed among the Massagetae by Tomyris, queen of the Massagetae, his son Cambyses succeeded to the kingdom; and when he had completed eight years, he had as successors two brothers, magi; and when these had been killed by the people, Darius son of Hystaspes was made king of the Persians by the decision of the seven families. In the second year of his reign the word of the Lord came to the prophet Haggai, and to Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo. On Haggai we have already spoken; on the title of Zechariah a few things must be said. Since he is the son of Berechiah, the question is asked why he is called the son of Iddo. Nor is it doubted, according to the book of Chronicles, that he is that Iddo who was sent to Jeroboam son of Nebat, under whom the altar was torn down and the king's hand withered, and at his prayers was restored again. Therefore from the first year of King Cyrus to the second year of Darius son of Hystaspes forty years are reckoned, in which only the altar had been built and the foundations of the temple laid, since the neighboring nations round about were hindering the work, and so was Cambyses, king of the Persians, who had sent letters on this matter to the governors of Syria Κοίλης (Hollow), and of Phoenicia and Samaria. Ezra wrote the history of this more fully. But Haggai began to prophesy in the sixth month, on the first day of that month; Zechariah, for his part, in the same year indeed, but not in the same month, for two months later, in the eighth month of the year, he poured out his prophecy. Hence, rightly, according to the order of the twelve prophets, the former is placed tenth and the latter eleventh. And it is a mark of great confidence, nay rather of hope and faith in God, that what had been left undone for so long a time, whether through the king's prohibition or the nations', they should begin to do at the stirring of two men. These things, according to the history, we have set down first in our usual manner. Now we must discuss them briefly according to ἀναγωγὴν (anagogy). The eighth month among the Hebrews, which among them is called Marcheshvan, among the Egyptians Athir, and among us November, is the beginning of winter; in it, the heat of summer being spent, all the earth is stripped of its green, and the bodies of mortals are contracted, and there is no solemnity from the Law. For it is written that three times in the year every male must appear before the Lord our God. The first festival is that of unleavened bread, the Phase, which among us is called Pascha in Greek and Latin speech. The second is that of Pentecost, after seven weeks, in which the loaves of proposition are first offered from the new crops. The third, in the seventh month, is that of trumpets, of propitiation and of tabernacles, that is, σκηνοπηγίας, whose last day is called the day of departure and of the end. So the people, placed in captivity and still under the king of the Persians, with the temple not yet built and the ramparts and walls of the city not yet raised, sees prophecy not in a time of gladness, but after gladness. And yet it had the remembrance of the Lord, and because of the remembrance, blessing, and on account of the blessing, testimony. For this reason Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo, is sent to it. Zechariah is interpreted remembrance of the Lord; Berechiah, blessing of the Lord; Iddo, his testimony. Let us see, then, what the word of the Lord, which was in the beginning with God the Father, spoke to the people in the prophet Zechariah.

1:2-4

The Lord was angry with your fathers in anger, and you shall say to them: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Turn to me, says the Lord of hosts, and I will turn to you, says the Lord of hosts; be not like your fathers, to whom the former prophets cried, saying. LXX: The Lord was angry with your fathers with great wrath, and you shall say to them: Thus says the Lord Almighty: Turn to me, says the Lord of powers, and I will turn to you, says the Lord of powers; and do not become like your fathers, whom the prophets who were before rebuked, saying. The wrath of the Lord does not signify a disturbance in him who is angry, but the deserts and sins of those against whom the wrath of God rages; for the rest, the Lord never takes vengeance in anger, but rebukes in order to amend. Hence the prophet also prays in the psalms: O Lord, do not reprove me in your fury, nor rebuke me in your wrath, and in another place we read: Rebuke us, O Lord, yet not in your fury, but in mercy. So the Lord was angry with the fathers of those who are now being rebuked, not, as the Seventy translators have it, with great wrath, but simply with wrath. For if he had been angry with great wrath, he would never have called the captives to repentance through Daniel and Ezekiel. And although he was angry with them, it is to you that the Lord of hosts speaks, for which Sabaoth is set down in the Hebrew, which the Seventy sometimes translated 'Almighty', sometimes 'of powers'. And he says: Turn to me, and I will turn to you, according to what we read: Draw near to the Lord, and he will draw near to you. To which this is the contrary: You have withdrawn from me, and I have withdrawn from you. Now he commands those who have returned from Babylon to return to the Lord, so that the Lord too may return to them, lest the sons begin to suffer what their fathers once suffered, to whom the prophets of that time likewise spoke. It is to be noted that in a few short verses and brief sentences, in Haggai and Zechariah, says the Lord of hosts is always added, so that they may know that it is God who commands, both against the command of the king and against the enemies raging round about; and that by this confidence they may be roused to the building of the temple.

Thus says the Lord of hosts: Turn from your evil ways, and from your most wicked thoughts; and they did not hear, nor did they attend to me, says the Lord. LXX: Thus says the Lord Almighty: Turn from your most wicked ways, and from your evil devices, and they did not attend so as to hear me, says the Lord. The sentence hangs on what precedes, and what follows must be fitted to what came before. For Zechariah is not speaking to the people, but is showing what the former prophets said to their fathers, to whom they cried, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Turn from your evil ways and from your most wicked thoughts. This is what Isaiah cried, this Hosea and Joel, Amos and Jeremiah: that they should forsake their evil ways and abandon their most wicked thoughts, by which they were sinning both in deed and in mind; and nevertheless they did not hear the Lord warning them through the prophets, nor did they attend, not at all to the prophets who were speaking to them, but to me, says the Lord: for in them it was I who was speaking and being despised. Hence the Lord also says in the Gospel: He who receives you, he says, receives me.

1:5-6

Your fathers, where are they, and the prophets? Will they live forever? But my words and my ordinances, which I commanded my servants the prophets, did they not overtake your fathers? And they turned and said: As the Lord of hosts purposed to do to us according to our ways and according to our devices, he has done to us. LXX: Your fathers, where are they, and the prophets? Will they live forever? But receive my words and my ordinances, which I command in my spirit to my servants the prophets, who overtook your fathers. And they answered and said: As the Lord Almighty was prepared to do to us according to our ways and according to our devices, so he has done to us. Where, he says, are your fathers, who did not hear me nor attend to me? Where are your prophets? For it is to be understood Ἀπὸ κοινοῦ (from the shared word): Your fathers, where are they, and your prophets, where are they? that is, the false prophets; for he would never say of the holy prophets: Will they live forever? So, when both those who sinned and refused to return to me, and those who deceived the sinners with false promises, have been taken away by death, my words, which I spoke through my prophets, remain forever; they overtook your fathers and showed that what I said was true. And your fathers, proving upon themselves by the outcome of events the prophecies of my prophets, turned to repentance and said: As the Lord of hosts foretold that he would do to us, according to our works and sins he has repaid us. Let us read Daniel confessing to the Lord, in his own person and in that of the people, that they had refused to hear him, and that whatever they had suffered had befallen them justly.

1:7

On the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, Shebat, in the second year of Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo, the prophet, saying. LXX: On the twenty-fourth, in the eleventh month, which is the month Shebat, in the second year under Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah of Berechiah, the son of Iddo, the prophet, saying. Some put the twelfth month in place of the eleventh, and, interpreting the twenty-fourth day of that month by arithmetical reckonings, suppose it to be a square, firm and stable number, wishing the things that are written to be certain and stable. But let us say that in the same second year of King Darius, in the third month after the first vision, that is, in the eleventh after the eighth, which among the Hebrews is called Shebat, the word of the Lord came again to Zechariah. That the second number is unclean, and pertains to the union of the flesh, and to the things of the world and of matter, we have often taught. Now the eleventh month, which is called Shebat, and in our language is rendered 'rod', sounds of severity and rebuke, according to that saying of the Apostle: What do you wish? Shall I come to you with a rod? And of the psalmist, who says: Your rod and your staff, they have comforted me. And it falls in the very bitter season of winter, and is called mechir by the Egyptians, περίτϊος by the Macedonians, and February by the Romans. The twenty-fourth day of the month, too, signifies a conspicuous shadow of night, as the moon wanes into darkness and the blind and continual dread of night increases. Hence, fittingly, those who were still in captivity, and for the greater part of them were in exile among the Medes and Chaldeans and Assyrians, in the second year, and in the most severe cold of the eleventh month, and on the twenty-fourth day of that month, see for the Jewish people the things that follow.

1:8-13

I saw by night, and behold, a man mounting a red horse, and he was standing among the myrtle groves that were in the deep, and behind him horses red, dappled and white. And I said: What are these, my lord? And the angel who was speaking in me said to me: I will show you what these are. And the man who was standing among the myrtle groves answered and said: These are they whom the Lord has sent to walk through the earth. And they answered the angel of the Lord, who was standing among the myrtle groves, and said: We have walked through the earth, and behold, all the earth is inhabited and at rest. And the angel of the Lord answered and said: O Lord of hosts, how long will you not have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, with which you are angry? This is the seventieth year. And the Lord answered the angel who was speaking in me good words, words of consolation. LXX: I saw in the night, and behold, a man mounting a red horse, and he was standing between two shady mountains, and behind him horses red, and dappled, and white. And I said: What are these, lord? And the angel who was speaking in me said to me: I will show you what these are. And the man who was standing between the mountains answered and said to me: These are they whom the Lord has sent to go round the earth. And they answered the angel of the Lord, who was standing between the mountains, and said: We have gone round all the earth, and behold, all the earth is inhabited and at rest. And the angel of the Lord answered and said: O Lord Almighty, how long will you not have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, with which you are angry? This is the seventieth year. And the Lord answered the angel who was speaking in me good words and consoling speeches. Let us first discuss the history: I saw, he says, by night, not by day, like Moses, who saw God face to face, and Isaiah, who said: I saw the Lord upon a throne high and lifted up, before the people was led into captivity, before Jerusalem and the temple were destroyed; but now, with the people still set in the evils of servitude, he discerns in darkness the things he sees on their behalf. And behold, a man mounting a red horse. This the Hebrews think is the angel Michael, who is the avenger of the iniquities and sins of Israel. And as to his having stood among the myrtle groves that were in the deep, they want the myrtles to be understood as the prophets and saints who were living in the midst of the captive people and were in the deep; and the man, that is, Michael, was followed by horses red and dappled and white, the order, as they suppose, being changed, so that the white signify the Medes and Persians, because under them the captivity was released and the temple restored; the dappled, the Macedonians, some of whom were friends, others persecutors — the last vision of Daniel speaks most fully on this —; and the red, the kingdom of the Romans, bloody and murderous, which slew the people and overthrew the temple. Others, however, keeping the order as it is written, interpret the man who mounted the red horse, and the red, dappled and white horses, as four kingdoms. The first and second, the bloody ones, are the Assyrians and Chaldeans, the first of whom led the ten tribes captive under Shalmaneser, the second Judah and Benjamin, when the city of Jerusalem had been burned and the temple overthrown. The third and fourth, the dappled and white, they think signify the Medes and Persians, some of whom were merciful, like Cyrus, and Darius son of Hystaspes, and Ahasuerus, whom the Greeks call Artaxerxes, under whom the story of Esther is fulfilled, and others cruel, like Cambyses and the rest. So when the prophet saw a man mounting a red horse, and behind him red, dappled and white horses, and did not know what he had seen, and desired to know the meaning of his vision, and said: What are these, my lord? the angel who was speaking in him promises that he will tell; and while he keeps silent, the man who was standing among the myrtle groves relates it. These are the kingdoms which the Lord commanded to walk through the earth and to subject it to their power. In short, the angels who presided over the kingdoms and nations come not to another, but to the man himself and angel of the Lord who was standing among the myrtle groves, and say: We have walked through the earth, and behold, all the earth is inhabited and at rest. And the sense is: All the kingdoms under us are safe and pacified, and are not oppressed by any distress. From this answer the angel who interceded for Israel, taking the occasion to pray for the people, also says to the Lord: Since the whole world is quiet and at peace, why, O Lord, will you not have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, especially since, according to your promises, the time of the captivity has been fulfilled? And the Lord answered the angel who was speaking in the prophet good words, words of consolation: good concerning the promise of things to come, consoling concerning the need of things present. These things we have given according to the letter, lest we should seem to hide from our own people what we have learned from teachers of a foreign tongue. But the teachers of the churches understand the night in which the vision is seen as the obscurity of a mystical vision. For God made darkness his hiding place. And according to the Seventy translators it is said of him: The abyss like a garment is his clothing. Now the man who was mounting the red horse is the Lord and Savior, who, taking it up for the dispensation of our flesh, hears in Isaiah: Why are your garments red? He who is now shown as red to the captive people is described in the Apocalypse of John as sitting in white garments and on a white horse. And as to his being said to stand between two shady mountains — although 'two' is not found in the Hebrew — they take them as the New and the Old Testament, which is said to be covered with very dense foliage, and grove, and shady woods, because it is wrapped in many obscurities. And the myrtle groves that were in the deep, among which he is described as standing, they understand as the angelic powers, which ministered to him even when he was set in the flesh. Or else, removing what is not written, 'two', we can take the mountains absolutely and without number as very many saints, and the apostles and apostolic men, who are recorded as having traveled through the whole world, red, and dappled, and white; of whom some are crowned with martyrdom and are called red; others, for their works and teaching and the variety of their signs, are named dappled; others have received the rewards of virginity and of uncorrupted doctrines, and of the pure heart, which sees God. Then the prophet questions the same man and angel who was standing among the myrtle groves, and says: What are these, my lord? And the angel who was speaking in him, namely the prophetic sense, promises that he will show what he sees. And when he has promised, it is not he himself who answered, but another who was standing among the myrtle groves, and he says: These are they whom the Lord has sent to walk through the earth. For the apostles were sent by the Lord to fill the whole world with the preaching of the Gospel; and after they had fulfilled the work, they returned to the angel of great counsel who was standing among them (they who said with the Apostle: We are the good odor of Christ among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing) and among the peoples of both callings. And they said to him: We have walked through the earth, we have fulfilled the work enjoined on us, and behold, all the earth is inhabited and at rest, which before was deserted by God; now it is his dwelling, it rests from wars, from the battles of the vices and from sins, because it has received the inheritance of the Lord as he returned to the Father and said: My peace I give you, my peace I leave you. And as to the plea, full of mercy, that, with the seventieth year at hand, God should have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, they refer it to this understanding: that until true peace comes, and the sabbath rest, and the number of seven decades, that is, the seventieth year, is fulfilled, neither does Jerusalem, which is interpreted vision of peace and is the Church, receive full liberty, nor do the cities of Judah, the souls that confess the Lord. In short, the Lord answered good words and words of consolation, which now we see in part and prophesy in part. But when that which is perfect has come, may the things that are in part be done away, and, set free from the Babylon of this world, may we cry out: When the Lord turned back the captivity of Zion, we became as those consoled.

1:14-16

And the angel who spoke in me said to me: Cry out, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts: I am jealous for Jerusalem and Zion with a great jealousy, and with great anger I am angry with the wealthy nations; for I was angry a little, but they helped toward evil. Therefore thus says the Lord: I will return to Jerusalem in mercies, and my house shall be built in it, says the Lord of hosts; and the plumb line shall be stretched out over Jerusalem. LXX: And the angel who spoke in me said to me: Cry out, saying: Thus says the Lord Almighty: I am jealous for Jerusalem and Zion with a great jealousy, and with great anger I am angry with the nations that have been set on top, because I indeed was angry a little, but they have set themselves on top unto evils. Therefore thus says the Lord: I will turn to Jerusalem in mercy, and my house shall be restored in it, says the Lord Almighty; and the measure shall be stretched out over Jerusalem. These are still good words, words of consolation, which the prophet is now commanded to cry out: that the Lord has been jealous for Jerusalem and Zion with a great jealousy. But he who is jealous shows that he loves her for whom he is jealous, by no means saying through Jeremiah: As a woman despises her husband, so the house of Israel has despised me. To her the Lord used to speak through Ezekiel: Being angry, I will no longer be angry with you, and my jealousy has departed from you. He, then, who was jealous for Jerusalem and Zion, which are, moreover, one and the same city, is now angry with the wealthy nations, which he named above as the red, white and speckled horses: because he himself handed them over to be chastened, but they raged against those handed over; he wished them corrected as a son by a tutor, they tried to kill and punish them as an enemy. Like this is that passage in Isaiah: I gave them into your hands, but you showed them no mercy. On the old man you made the yoke very heavy, and you said: I shall be mistress forever. Now jealousy is taken ἀνθρωποπάθως (in human fashion), in the same way as anger. By this passage the heretics who disparage the Old Testament are refuted, since God, when he is angry, desires not to destroy those with whom he is angry, but to amend them. Because, therefore, he says, I am jealous for Jerusalem and Zion with a great jealousy, which the adversaries have pressed down more than I wished, therefore thus says the Lord: I will return to Jerusalem not in one mercy but in many mercies, and my house, that is, the temple, shall be built in it under Zerubbabel and Joshua son of Jehozadak; and the plumb line of the masons shall be stretched out over Jerusalem. Jerusalem and Zion, the vision of peace and the watchtower, which looks not on the wars of this world, nor on things lowly and earthly, but on peace and concord and the heights of heaven, can be understood as the Church. Angry with her because of vices and sins and charity growing colder every day, the Lord will hand her over to persecutions, so that, as in a smelting furnace, pure gold and silver may appear. But the adversaries to whom she has been handed over, striving to blot her out, fill Jerusalem with the blood of the slain from gate to gate. Hence the Lord, promising peace and mercy, says that he will build her again, and that the plumb line, or the cord, is to be stretched out in her according to the measures and ranks of each one. What the cord is, is shown in the following passage, where the prophet says: I lifted up my eyes and saw: and behold, a man, and in his hand a measurers' cord. And the temple of the Lord which the adversaries destroyed can also be understood as the venerable body of the Lord, of which he himself said: Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. Destroyed in the passion, it was raised in the resurrection, and it was the seedbed and the beginning of all temples, of which the Apostle too speaks: You are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you.

1:17

Cry out yet, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Yet my cities shall overflow with good things; and the Lord shall yet comfort Zion, and shall yet choose Jerusalem. LXX: And the angel who spoke in me said to me: Cry out yet, saying: Thus says the Lord Almighty: Yet the cities shall be poured out in good things, and the Lord shall yet have mercy on Zion, and shall yet choose Jerusalem. The angel who had said to the prophet above: Cry out; thus says the Lord of hosts, now too urges him to cry out, not with the straining of the voice but of the mind; and this is what he commands him to cry out: Yet my cities shall overflow with good things; those which you now see laid waste by the Babylonian fire shall again abound in all things, and with present good things the Lord shall console their past misery, and shall choose Jerusalem, which a little before he had cast off. But if we refer it to the Church, to which he promises true and everlasting good things, those good things must be believed to be the ones of which we read it written: May you see the good things of Jerusalem, and in another place: If you are willing and listen to me, you shall eat the good things of the land, and that saying: You shall trust in the Lord, and he shall lift you up above the good things of the earth. These a wise man, to whom the Lord had revealed the uncertain and hidden things of his wisdom, promises himself in the hope of things to come, and says: I believe that I shall see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living. With these good things, now that peace has been restored after the fires of the most savage persecution which the churches of the Lord endured both from the Gentiles and from the Arian heretics, we see the churches of the Lord overflow, and Zion consoled, and Jerusalem chosen, which he had formerly cast away. This very thing we can also understand both of the temple of the Lord and of each one of the believers. Some refer the consoled Zion and the chosen Jerusalem, and the other things that are proclaimed in this manner by all the prophets, to the heavenly Jerusalem, which, destroyed through a fall, is to be built up through virtues. All of which we shall more rightly interpret of the Church.

1:18-21

And I lifted up my eyes and saw. And behold, four horns; and I said to the angel who spoke in me: What are these? And he said to me: These are the horns that have winnowed Judah, and Israel and Jerusalem. And the Lord showed me four smiths, and I said: What are these coming to do? He spoke, saying: These are the horns that have winnowed Judah man by man, and none of them lifted up his head, and these came to terrify them, to cast down the horns of the nations which have lifted up the horn over the land of Judah, to scatter it. LXX: And I lifted up my eyes and saw. And behold, four horns, and I said to the angel who spoke in me: What are these, Lord? And he said to me: These are the horns that have scattered Judah, and Israel, and Jerusalem. And the Lord showed me four smiths, and I said: What are these coming to do? And he said: These are the horns that have scattered Judah, and have broken Israel, and none of them lifted up his head, and these have gone out to sharpen them in their hands. The four horns are nations that have lifted up the horn over the land of the Lord, to scatter it. That the four horns which winnowed and scattered Judah, and Israel, and Jerusalem are four nations, the Babylonians, the Medes and Persians, and the Macedonians and the Romans, both the Lord now explains when questioned by the prophet, and Daniel unfolds most fully, who in the vision of the image that had a golden head, and a silver breast, and a bronze belly, and legs and feet of iron and of clay, interpreted these four nations. And again in another vision, by four beasts, a lioness and a bear, and a leopard, and another horrible beast whose name he did not set down, he showed the same nations under another figure. That the Medes and Persians were one kingdom after the victory of Cyrus no one will doubt who has read both secular and sacred literature. And most rightly he has put horns for kingdoms, since holy Scripture has this custom, that it always signifies a kingdom by horns, as in that verse: And he exalted the horn of his Christ. And in another place: And he has raised up a horn of salvation for us in the house of David his servant. And the same prophet Daniel testifies to ten horns that are to reign at the last. At the time, then, when this vision was seen, the kingdom of the Babylonians had already passed away, that of the Persians and Medes was at hand, and that of the Greeks and Macedonians, and of the Romans, was yet to come. What the Babylonians, what the Medes and Persians, what the Greeks, that is, the Macedonians, did to Judah and Israel and Jerusalem, a learned man knows, above all under Antiochus, surnamed Ἐπιφανεῖ (Epiphanes), under whom the history of the Maccabees is woven. After the coming of the Lord and Savior as well, when Jerusalem was besieged, what the Israelites suffered both the Gospel foretells and Josephus, the native writer of the Jewish history, teaches most fully. These horns winnowed and scattered Judah almost man by man, so that none of them, pressed down by the heavy weight of evils, lifted up his head. The four smiths too, or craftsmen, whom the Greeks call τέκτονας, the prophet does not behold by himself, but the Lord shows them to him, and explains who the smiths and craftsmen are, whom we understand as angels obedient to the Lord's power, so that what the nations had destroyed, these may build. Now this, for which we translated from the Hebrew: These came to terrify them, namely the kingdoms, the Seventy translated: These came to sharpen them, in their hands. But sharpening them is to be taken for terrifying them. Or else they terrify, so that all may submit their necks under the yoke of Christ, and not, as someone has interpreted: when they have been sharpened, let them become worse. For if the angels come for this, to make the wicked worse, they are not to be called smiths, that is, destroyers of evils and builders of good things; but they too are to be taken as wicked and as destroyers. And this is to be noted, that these four horns of the nations lifted up one horn against the people of God; for they did not reign together and oppress Israel at the same time, but succeeded one another: the Medes and Persians the Babylonians, the kingdom of the Macedonians the kingdom of the Persians, the empire of the Romans the kingdom of the Macedonians. These things we have said following the order of the history. But that the prophet says he lifted up his eyes is to be fitted to the spiritual sense: I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where my help shall come, and: Lift up your eyes, and see the regions, for they are already white for harvest, and what we read in Isaiah: The Holy One said: Lift up your eyes, and see who has shown all these things. There is need, therefore, of the lifting up of the eyes and of spiritual understanding, that we may see the opposing powers which have lifted up their horn against us, and of which the Apostle speaks: Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of this world, of this darkness, against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places. These four adversaries, who were always fighting against the saints, Christ crushed at his coming. And of these the psalmist says over again: You shall walk upon the asp and the basilisk, and you shall trample the lion and the dragon. We can also take the four horns, which reigned against the people of God, as the four πάθη (passions), which learned men, not rendering them word for word κακοζήλως (with misplaced zeal) as passions, translate as perturbations: distress and gladness of mind, two present, and two future, fear and desire, of which the illustrious poet also gives indication:

Hence they fear and desire, grieve and rejoice.

Against these, four smiths and, so to speak, physicians and good craftsmen are not seen by the prophet, for he could not yet see them by himself without God's showing them, but they are shown to him by the Lord: namely the four virtues, prudence, justice, fortitude, temperance, of which Tully disputes most fully in the books On Duties, writing also a book of its own on the four virtues. Those, then, who destroy vices and build virtues, and all the saints who, possessing these remedies, are ever restoring the Church, are to be called smiths. Hence the Apostle too said: As a wise architect I have laid the foundation. And the Lord in his anger says that he will take away from Jerusalem the architect and the wise man. And the Lord himself, the Son of God Almighty and of the Creator of all things, is called the son of a smith. We have read in someone's commentaries that the four smiths are understood as the four evangelists, who restore to its former seat the oppressed Israel, that is, the mind that sees God, and Judah, who confesses the Lord, and Jerusalem, the vision of peace; and those whom the nations of the vices had scattered from the Church, they have drawn back to salvation through the preaching of the gospel.

He who before, with eyes lifted up, had seen sad things in the four horns, now again lifts up his eyes to see the man of whom it is written: Behold a man, the Rising is his name, of whom we also read above: And behold a man mounting upon a red horse, and he was standing among the myrtles that were in the deep, or, among the shady mountains. And of whom the Father speaks: He has built my city, whose craftsman and founder is God. He is also seen by Ezekiel, holding a cord in his hand, and a reed for the measure of a man's cubit, to measure Jerusalem, whose description, that is, its breadth and length, the same prophet pursues in mystical speech. Some think this was fulfilled in part under Zerubbabel, and Joshua, and Ezra, and Nehemiah and the other leaders or kings who ruled the people of Israel up to the coming of the Lord and Savior. But others, and above all the Jews, interpret it of the kingdom of a thousand years, bodily men seeking bodily things. Most of our writers, however, refer it to the heavenly Jerusalem, and say that it is to be built up again, having been left desolate by the fall of sinful souls. Let heretics suspect this. But let us interpret Mount Zion, and the mother of the firstborn and of the saints, as the Church, which was built in the passion and resurrection of the Lord, and is built every day by him who knows the measures and the merits of each one. What we have said of the Church can also be taken of the souls of believers, which are built every day by the Lord, and behold his peace, which was left to the apostles.

2:3-5

And behold, the angel who spoke in me was going out, and another angel was going out to meet him, and he said to him: Run, speak to this boy, saying: Jerusalem shall be inhabited without a wall, because of the multitude of men and cattle in its midst. And I will be to it, says the Lord, a wall of fire round about, and I will be in glory in its midst. LXX: And behold, the angel who spoke in me was standing, and another angel was going out to meet him, and he said to him, saying: Run and speak to that youth, saying: Jerusalem shall be inhabited fruitful from the multitude of men and cattle in its midst. And I will be to it, says the Lord, a wall of fire round about, and I will be for glory in its midst. The angel who spoke in the prophet, and was standing, or, as is read in the Hebrew, was going out, for that is what iasa means, the Hebrews think to be Michael. And the other angel, who was going out and coming to meet him, they suppose to be Gabriel, who speaks to Michael so that he may tell the prophet how great an abundance of all things, and multitude of men and cattle, and strength of walls Jerusalem shall have, which at present seems destroyed down to embers and ashes. But we, according to the spiritual sense, interpret all these things of the Church, which is inhabited without a wall, or, as the Seventy translated, κατάκαρπος, that is, with an abundance of all fruits, and has a multitude of men and cattle, and the Lord is fire round about her, and he himself dwells in glory in her midst. This is the city of which we read elsewhere: Glorious things are spoken of you, city of God, and again: Great is the Lord and exceedingly to be praised, in the city of our God, in his holy mountain. Whoever dwells in her can say: But I am like a fruitful olive tree in the house of God, and: The Lord rules me, and I shall lack nothing; in a place of pasture, there he has set me. By the water of refreshment he has brought me up. And Jerusalem, he says, shall be inhabited without a wall because of the multitude of men and cattle, or, with an abundance of all fruits. Some interpret men and cattle as two peoples, of the Jews and of the Gentiles: that those who were versed in the law and came to the faith of Christ are called men, while we, who after idolatry were as it were in the desert of the law and the solitude of the prophets, and have received his passion, ought to be called cattle. But others want all rational men, learned in the knowledge of the Scriptures, to be understood as men, and as cattle all the simple among the believers, and that of these it is said: Men and cattle you will save, O Lord. These cattle and these living creatures hear the voice of the good shepherd, and know it, and follow him, because he is both the shepherd, who also rules us, and the door, because through him we enter into the Church and to the Father, and a wall of fire round about, so that he may warm those who believe and dwell in the midst, who before had grown lukewarm from the coldness of charity, and that they may be fervent in spirit. But the wolves and the most savage beasts — of which it is written: Do not hand over to the beasts the soul that confesses you, and whose fruit is hay, wood, stubble, and they bring forth thistles and thorns —, let him consume with his fire; and he who is fire to the adversaries, let him be glory in the midst of the believers. That the Lord is the wall of Jerusalem we read in another place too: Mountains round about it, and the Lord round about his people. All these things the Jews think will come carnally under the ἠλειμμένῳ (anointed one), whom they promise themselves with the vainest hope: that Jerusalem will have such great blessedness that, because of the multitude of men and of all cattle, it cannot have a wall; but the defense of the Lord himself will be its wall, and it will enjoy the glory of him who dwells in its midst.

2:6-9

O, O, flee from the land of the north, says the Lord; for I have scattered you into the four winds of heaven, says the Lord. O Zion, arise, you who dwell with the daughter of Babylon. For thus says the Lord of hosts: After glory he has sent me to the nations that despoiled you; for he who touches you touches the pupil of his eye. For behold, I lift up my hand over them, and they shall be spoil for those who served them, and you shall know that the Lord of hosts has sent me. LXX: O, O, flee from the land of the north, says the Lord, for from the four winds of heaven I will gather you, says the Lord. In Zion be saved, you who inhabit the daughter of Babylon. For thus says the Lord Almighty: After glory he has sent me to the nations that despoiled you, for he who touches you, as it were touches the pupil of his eye. Behold, I will bring my hand upon them, and they shall be spoil for those who had served them, and you shall know that the Lord Almighty has sent me. The regions of the world, which the Greeks call κλίματα, are understood according to the situation of Jerusalem and of the temple. The Assyrians, therefore, and the Babylonians, who laid waste the people of God, dwell in the land of the north. In short, in Jeremiah the pot which according to Ezekiel is full of flesh — and by it the city of Jerusalem is understood —, is kindled from the face of the north. Since, therefore, the angel who had come to meet the angel who prophesied in Zechariah had commanded him to speak to the boy — for in comparison with the dignity of angels all human nature is called boyhood, because it is not angels who advance into us, but we who advance into angels —, and to say to him: Jerusalem shall be inhabited without a wall, and the rest. Now the same word of the Lord is addressed to those who dwell in the north, that they may be gathered from the four winds into which they had been scattered over the whole world, and that those who dwelt in Babylon may return to Zion. Or else thus: You who have been scattered into the four regions of heaven, flee from the land of the north; and O Zion, you who now dwell in Babylon, flee and return to your former seat. But that O is said a third time, in the vocative case, is an exhortation to flight, so that they may know that they have been warned to flee not once, but more often. It must also be said another way: the north wind is a very harsh wind, which makes very hard the hearts of those who dwell in its land, and from which evils are kindled upon all who dwell on the earth, and toward which, according to this very prophet Zechariah, the black horses go out, to take their stand in its region. He too who in Isaiah had said in his boasting: I will ascend into heaven, above the stars of heaven I will set my throne, I will sit on a lofty mountain, above the high mountains in the north, vaunts that he holds his kingdom in the coldest place of the earth. We read also in another place: Behold, smoke comes from the north; and of those who had departed far from the holy city it is said: Behold, these come from afar, from the north and the sea. And through Jeremiah the Holy Spirit speaks, calling those who are in captivity to return to Jerusalem: Go and read these my words toward the north, and you shall say: Return to me, dwelling of Israel, says the Lord. It is commanded, therefore, to us who have dwelt in the north and have lost the warmth of the Lord's fervor, who have been scattered over the world, and of whom the Gospel proclaims that the Lord sends his angels into the four winds and gathers us, and who are caught up in the vices and confusion of this world, that we flee to Zion, the Church of the Lord, and, leaving lowly things behind, take our stand on her watchtower and on the sublimity of her doctrines. But what follows: Thus says the Lord of hosts, after glory he has sent me, and the rest, introduces the voice of the Savior speaking, who, being almighty God, says that he was sent by the almighty Father, not according to that by which he is almighty, but according to that by which he was sent after glory. Who, though he was in the form of God, did not think it robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, and became obedient to the Father unto death, the death of the cross. Nor is it a wonder that Christ is called almighty, in whose person we read in the Apocalypse of John: Thus says the faithful witness, the beginning of the creation of God, who is, and who was, and who is to come, the Lord God Almighty. That too which we read in Psalm 23 (24): Lift up your gates, O princes, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the king of glory shall enter, and which again is said by other angels who did not know the mystery of the flesh assumed: Who is this king of glory? The Lord of powers, he is the king of glory, is referred to Christ. For where we read the Lord of powers, in the Hebrew Sabaoth is written, which is rendered almighty by the LXX translators. From this we understand that wherever the Lord of powers is said of Christ, he must be understood as almighty. Nor is it a wonder if Christ is called almighty: To whom has been given all power in heaven and on earth, and who says: All things that are the Father's are mine. But if all things, that is, God from God, Lord from Lord, light from light, then also almighty from almighty; for it cannot be that those whose nature is one should have a different glory. He was sent, therefore, after the glory of the divine majesty, to the nations that despoiled the people of God, so that those who had formerly plundered them might be spoil for those who served them, and the whole crowd of those who once served might know that the Lord Almighty has sent him. But as for what he says: He who touches you touches the pupil of his eye, take the touching as harassment and injury, according to what we read: Do not touch my anointed ones, and do no evil to my prophets. For whoever touches the saints of the Lord is as one who desires to harm the pupil of his eye, and strives to deprive him of the clear light, of which he speaks in the Gospel: You are the light of the world. And God lifts up his hand to strike the hostile nations, and to lead his people back either into Jerusalem or into the Church. By the hostile nations, according to tropology, understand the opposing powers, which every day subject sinners to their power and compel them to serve them.

And many nations shall be joined to the Lord on that day, and they shall be my people, and I will dwell in the midst of you, and you shall know that the Lord of hosts has sent me to you. And the Lord shall possess Judah as his portion in the sanctified land, and he shall yet choose Jerusalem. LXX: Rejoice and be glad, daughter of Zion, for behold, I will come, and I will dwell in the midst of you, says the Lord. And many nations shall flee for refuge to the Lord on that day, and they shall be his people, and they shall dwell in the midst of you, and you shall know that the Lord Almighty has sent me to you, and the Lord shall possess Judah as his portion in the holy land, and he shall yet choose Jerusalem. These words too are to be understood as spoken in the person of the Lord: that he exhorts his people, restored from captivity to its former seat, to be glad and rejoice because the Lord himself is coming and will dwell in its midst; and that many nations are to believe in him, of whom it is said: Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession; and that he dwells in its midst, saying to the disciples: Behold, I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the age; and that the Lord possesses Judah as his portion, Judah that confesses and believes in his name, and possesses it nowhere else than in the holy land, which is interpreted as the Church; and that he yet chooses Jerusalem, which he had abandoned to temptation in the persecutions. Some of the Jews think that these things were in part fulfilled under Zerubbabel and Joshua, Ezra and Nehemiah, especially since Jerusalem is chosen and Judah is possessed; that is to say, the two tribes which returned from the Babylonian captivity and were called Judah, and not Israel, which lives among the Medes to this day. Others, however, defer them to the future: that then the nations are to believe in him who is sent by the Lord, and Jerusalem is to be chosen; although surely all the nations have already believed in the Lord Savior, and a city that has been utterly destroyed cannot be chosen. But rightly, after the captivity, is the daughter of Zion called forth to joy, of whom it is also said in the psalm: When the Lord turns back the captivity of his people, Jacob shall exult, and Israel shall be glad.

2:13

Let all flesh be silent before the face of the Lord, for he has risen up from his holy dwelling. LXX: Let all flesh fear before the face of the Lord, for he has risen from his holy clouds. Since these things are so, and the Lord is going to possess Judah as his portion and to choose Jerusalem, let the whole race of men dread the coming of the Lord, for he has risen up from his holy dwelling. Now the Lord is said to rise, and as it were to awaken from sleep, when he rises up to avenge his people, according to what is written: Arise, why do you sleep, O Lord? Or else, according to the LXX, who said: He has risen from his holy clouds, understand the patriarchs, prophets and apostles, whom the Lord commanded not to rain rain upon Israel. These are the clouds of which it is written: And let the clouds sprinkle justice; for these bodily clouds, into which the denser air is gathered, cannot rain justice. In these clouds and with these clouds the Lord says in the Gospel that he will come. And in Isaiah we read that the Lord came into Egypt on a light cloud. Egypt is taken as the world, into which the Lord is recorded to have descended in the flesh, which, brought forth from a virginal womb, was weighed down by no weight of human seed and of sins. But that all flesh is to be taken not absolutely, of men and of beasts and of birds and of fish, but specifically of men, is shown by that scripture: Hear my prayer; to you all flesh shall come. For it is not all the flesh of irrational living creatures that is to come to the Lord, but that flesh which is to receive the gift of the Holy Spirit, and of which it is said: I will pour out of my spirit upon all flesh, and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy.

LXX: And the Lord showed me Joshua the high priest, standing before the face of the angel of the Lord, and the devil was standing at his right hand, to oppose him. And the Lord said to the devil: The Lord rebuke you, devil, and the Lord who has chosen Jerusalem rebuke you. Is not this like a brand plucked out of the fire? And Joshua was clothed in filthy garments, and was standing before the face of the angel, and he answered and said to those standing before his face, saying: Take away the filthy garments from him. Before we come to the spiritual understanding, we must say succinctly and briefly how the Hebrews explain this passage. Joshua (Iesus, the same name as Jesus) the high priest, whom the Greeks call ἀρχιερέα, the Latins pontiff, they take to be the son of Jehozadak, who presided over the people together with Zerubbabel. At his right hand the adversary was standing (for this is what satan means), to oppose him. And rightly was he standing at his right hand, not at his left, because the accusation was true, since he too, along with the rest, had taken a foreign wife, as is written most fully in Ezra and in Malachi, who follows this prophet. And the Lord said to Satan, to the accuser and his adversary, for he is the enemy and avenger, the accuser of his brothers: The Lord rebuke you, Satan, as the Lord rained from the Lord, and the Lord rebuke you, he who has chosen Jerusalem. Since, then, out of all the cities of Judah Jerusalem has now been chosen, the Lord in no way imputing to her the sins she committed, why do you try to overwhelm Joshua like a brand, which is commonly called a firebrand, Joshua who escaped as it were half-burnt from the Babylonian captivity? As for what follows: Joshua was clothed in filthy garments, they interpret it in three ways: either on account of the unlawful marriage, or on account of the sins of the people, or because of the squalor of the captivity. Now the angel before whose face Joshua was standing commanded the other angels, in the person of the Lord, to take away from him the filthy garments of which we spoke above. When they had fulfilled the command in deed, the same angel speaks again to Joshua: Behold, I have taken away your iniquity from you; these are the filthy garments; and I have clothed you with changes of raiment; that is, I have joined an Israelite wife to you; for which the Seventy translated ποδήρη, which we can call an ankle-length tunic, because it flows down to the ankles and feet. And as for what follows: Put a clean tiara upon his head, for tiara we read in the Hebrew Saniph, which most people call a mitre; and by this they want the dignity of the priesthood to be understood, because, once the filth of his sins had been taken away, he held a clean priesthood. This is what the Jews say. Our writers, however, explain it thus: that he is the high priest to whom it is said: You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek. Since he cannot be seen of himself, he is shown to the prophet by the Lord, standing before the angel of the Lord, whom they take to be the angel of great counsel: not that he is one and another, so that we should admit two persons in the Son, but that he is one and the same, and is both shown as a man in soiled clothing and said to appear as an angel, the mediator of men and God. And that he is not Joshua son of Jehozadak they try to show from this, that the words son of Jehozadak are not added in the present place, whereas in other places, where it is truly Joshua son of Jehozadak who is spoken of, he is always registered by his father's name. So Jesus is seen standing, set firm with a steady step: And Satan standing at his right hand, to oppose him. For he was tempted in every way, without sin. And in the Gospel the tempter approaches him, seeking always to oppose his right-hand works and his virtues. As for what follows: The Lord rebuke you, Satan, and the Lord rebuke you, he who has chosen Jerusalem, they explain it thus: that the Father and the Son is Lord, and in Psalm 109 (110) we read: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand; he speaks of the other Lord, not because the Lord himself who speaks cannot rebuke, but because, from the unity of their nature, when the other has rebuked, he himself who speaks rebukes: For he who sees the Son sees the Father, and he it is who has chosen Jerusalem, the Church, which contemplates the peace of the Lord. Now the brand plucked out of the fire can most rightly be understood as him who, though he was in Babylon, was not consumed by the Babylonian fire, nor touched by the flame of this world. Hence Moses too, in the wilderness, sees a great vision, in which the bush was burning and was not consumed. This Jesus was clothed in filthy garments, who, though he had committed no sin, was made sin for us. And he bears our infirmities, and grieves for us, and we reckoned him to be in sorrow, and under a blow, and in anguish. But he was wounded for our iniquities, and was made weak for our sins. And in the apostle Paul we read: Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us. It is he who speaks in Psalm 21 (22): Far from my salvation are the words of my offences. And in Psalm 68 (69): O God, you know my foolishness, and my offences are not hidden from you. All these are called filthy garments, and they will be taken away from him when he has blotted out our sins, so that, because he was clothed in filthy garments, we, rising again, may hear in him after baptism: Let your garments be always white; and the whole Church of believers hears through Isaiah: Wash yourselves, be clean. And of her it is prophesied in the Song of Songs: Who is this who comes up made white? Ποδήρην, moreover, take to be his incarnation, which is from the earth and is signified in the feet. By the clean tiara upon his head understand the splendor of the divine majesty, so that one and the same may be seen to be adorned, according to the man, with the ποδήρη, and according to God, with the tiara.

3:6-7

And the angel of the Lord solemnly charged Joshua, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts: If you walk in my ways and keep my charge, you also shall judge my house, and shall keep my courts, and I will give you those who walk, from among these who now stand by here. LXX: And the angel of the Lord was standing. And the angel of the Lord solemnly charged Joshua, saying: Thus says the Lord Almighty: If you walk in my ways and keep my precepts, you too shall judge my house; and if you keep my court, I will give you those who shall move about in the midst of these who stand here. The Hebrews, following the course of the interpretation they have begun, understand these things to have been said by the angel of the Lord to Joshua (Iesus, the same name as Jesus) son of Jehozadak: that after the removal of the filthy garments and the restoration of the dignity of a clean priesthood, he is given a command according to what is written in the Gospel: Behold, you have been made well; sin no more, lest something worse befall you; and a reward is promised him, if he walks in the ways of the Lord and keeps his precepts: that he himself should be the judge of his house, that is, should continue as high priest in the temple, and should keep his courts and porches; and that the Lord should give him some from the number of the angels who at that time stood in his sight, so that he might be walled about by their help and be secure from every treachery of his enemies. According to our writers, who refer all these things to the Lord Savior, this seems to be difficult: that it is said to Jesus by the angel that if he walks in the ways of the Lord and keeps his precepts, he too shall judge his house and keep his courts, and the Lord shall give him those who walk, from among those who stand by him. This is easily resolved if we consider him who deigned to take the form of a servant. And though he was rich, he became poor for us. Whatever, then, is said of the members is referred to the body; our progress is the Lord's victory. And when we have come to the perfect man, to the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ, he will judge the house of God, according to what the Apostle said: But Christ as a son over his house, whose house we are, and to Timothy: If I delay, that you may know how you ought to conduct yourself in the house of God, which is the Church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth. And not only will he judge his house, but he will also keep the courts of the Lord, of which it is written: Worship the Lord in his holy court. And he will judge, or discern, his house, and according to the merits of each, giving to one ten cities and to another five, and appointing some in the Church as prophets, some as apostles, some as teachers, some as workers of signs, holding some as eyes, some as hands, some as feet, according to what we read: God has stood in the assembly of the gods, and in the midst he discerns the gods. He also gave him ministers from the number of the angels, who, though set in the flesh, are like the angels, and of whom the Apostle spoke: Our citizenship is in the heavens. For if the angels neither marry nor are given in marriage, and those who persevere in virginal continence are like the angels, why should we not think that the apostles and all the saints, being of angelic dignity, were given to Jesus, to stand by him in the Church and never to have wavering feet, but to stand with the Lord who stands?

3:8-9

Hear, O Joshua, high priest, you and your friends who dwell before you, for they are men who portend. For behold, I will bring my servant the Rising, for behold the stone which I have given before Joshua: upon one stone are seven eyes. LXX: Hear then, O Joshua, high priest, and your neighbours who sit before your face, for the men τερατοσκόποι, that is, of portents, are beholders, for behold, I will bring my servant the Rising, for the stone which I have given before the face of Joshua, upon one stone are seven eyes. Our writers are hard pressed in this place by the Jews, on the ground that, following the sequence and texture of the discourse, they ought rather to understand Joshua (Iesus, the same name as Jesus) the priest, son of Jehozadak, than the Lord Savior. For if the word is addressed to the Lord, and to Christ it is said: Hear, O Joshua, high priest, who is the one of whom it is added: Behold, I will bring my servant the Rising, who by another name is called the stone, and has been given before Joshua, and upon this stone are seven eyes? Our writers, on the contrary, strive to maintain that Christ is called, according to different understandings, both Jesus the high priest, and the Rising, and the stone. But how they could explain that this is said of him, to him, as though of another, is most difficult. Those, then, who want the high priest to be Joshua son of Jehozadak interpret his friends who dwell, or sit, before him, and who are men who portend, as his disciples and the prophets. For the prophets were set as a sign of things to come. What is it, then, that Joshua and his friends are made to hear: I will bring my servant the Rising, and the rest? Because above God had promised Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest, that if he walked in his ways and kept his precepts, he himself would judge his house and keep his courts, and that he would give him ministers of angelic dignity, now he tells him and his friends that full happiness and perfect blessedness will come then, when the Rising comes, of whom it is written: Behold a man, the Rising is his name. And in Malachi: For you who fear my name the sun of justice shall rise, and healing in its wings. And in Numbers: A star shall rise out of Jacob, and a man out of Israel. In the Gospel too we read most plainly of Christ: In which the Rising from on high will visit us. To give light to those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace. He himself is called the Rising and the cornerstone, because he joins both peoples together and unites two walls into one house. To those who do not believe he is a stone of stumbling, of whom it is also said in the psalms: The stone which the builders rejected, this has become the head of the corner. This was done by the Lord. Upon this stone are seven eyes, of which Isaiah speaks: A rod shall come forth from the root of Jesse, and a flower shall rise up from his root, and the spirit of God shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and fortitude, the spirit of knowledge and piety; and the spirit of the fear of God shall fill him. Those who want the high priest and his friends to be understood as the Lord Savior and his disciples take the men τερατοσκόπους, men who portend, in this way: they refer them to the apostles, who perceived his mystical signs and recognized the future from the present; while in the man who was blind from birth they interpret the restored eyes as concerning the people of the Gentiles, and in the woman with the flow of blood they explain the Church as set free from the works of blood. As for what follows: For behold the stone which I have given before Joshua, the lovers of the history understand it of Christ in this way, saying that Christ was to come after Joshua son of Jehozadak. For this, they say, is what before Joshua means, that is, in his sight and before his face, so that it signifies things to come; and he is called a stone for his strength and hardness, with which he crushed all kingdoms, the stone which we also read in Daniel was cut from the mountain without hands.

3:10

On that day, says the Lord of hosts, a man shall call his friend under the vineyard and under the fig tree. LXX: Behold, I will dig a pit, says the Lord Almighty, and I will handle all the iniquity of that land in one day. On that day, says the Lord Almighty, each one shall call his neighbour under the vine and under the fig tree. Above he had said: Behold the stone which I have given before Joshua, upon one stone are seven eyes. Now, consistently, he keeps the μεταφορὰν (metaphor) taken from the stone, and says: I will engrave its carving, or I will carve its engraving. For where it is written in Hebrew maphate phethee, Aquila translated διαγλύψω ἄνοιγμα αὐτῆς, that is, I will carve its opening; Theodotion and Symmachus, I will carve its carving. And the sense is: I will cause this stone to be wounded by the nails of the cross and the soldier's lance, and in his passion I will take away the iniquity of the land in one day, the day of which it is written: This is the day which the Lord has made; let us exult and rejoice in it. On that day of the passion of Christ, the man who is perfect in Christ, and who with the apostles ascends to the summit of the Lord's goodwill, shall call his neighbour, either the believers from among the Jews or at any rate the people of the Gentiles, under the vineyard which is called Sorek, and of which he says in the Gospel: I am the vine, and whose fruit gladdens the heart of man; and under the fig tree, the sweetest fruits of the Holy Spirit, so that they may rest in eternal peace and, the sedition of the world and the gore of warriors having been suppressed, may know that they are under a king whose name, mystically in Solomon, is that of peace. Of this same sitting under the vineyard and under the fig tree the prophet Micah also makes mention, saying: On that day each one shall call his neighbour and his brother under the fig tree and his vineyard, and there shall be none to terrify them. As for what is written according to the Seventy: Behold, I will dig a pit, we can understand the ditch or pit in place of the carving of the stone: for everything that is carved is also dug. Yet if someone, more contentiously, will not refer this to the stone, but would have it hold, as it were, its own beginning and its own meaning, let us say that the Lord Almighty, on the day of his Son's passion, or else — as the Jews think, at the final end of the world, when he comes to judge — digs out and brings into the open the works of each person, and handles the whole earth on the day of judgment; for this is the one day, and on that day each of the saints calls his companions and friends, who are themselves also saints, to rest, and rejoices in the virtue of his works.

And the angel who was speaking in me returned, and roused me like a man who is roused from his sleep. LXX: And the angel who was speaking in me returned, and roused me as a man is wont to be roused from his sleep. For where had he gone, that he should return, especially he who was speaking in the prophet, and who could not be apart from him in whom he was speaking? But whenever human frailty is left to its own weakness, we must believe that God, and the help of his angels, departs from us. So the prophet too, stunned with amazement at the great vision, was numb in his whole mind and could not see the clear light of truth; hence he is roused from the past vision to another vision, as though waking from sleep, so that he may see what with closed eyes he did not see. Let us also say it another way. There is a testimony in the Proverbs of Solomon: If you sit down, you will be without fear; but if you sleep, you will sleep sweetly, and you will not fear the terror coming upon you, nor the onslaughts of the ungodly bearing down on you. Whoever sleeps this sleep, and hears said of himself what we read written of Lazarus: If he sleeps, he will be well, will be able to sing with the bride: I sleep, and my heart keeps watch. Let us see, then, for the contemplation of what things the prophet rises as if from sleep.

The angel who roused Zechariah, like a man rising from sleep, asks him what he has seen, so that, when he answers and does not know, and inquires what it is that he sees, and says: What are these, my lord?, we may hear from him: This is the word of the Lord to Zerubbabel: Not by an army, nor by strength, but by my spirit, says the Lord. Let us therefore go through the details one by one and say first what the Hebrews think, by whom we have been taught in the Old Testament; then by these, as by steps, let us climb to the heights of the Church. The lampstand of solid gold they interpret as the law, that is, νόμον. The lamp, that is, the flame shining and gleaming on the top of the lampstand, they take as Christ, who is the head of the law and gives light to the whole world. The seven lamps upon the lampstand they take as the seven graces of the Holy Spirit, of which we spoke above, saying that on one stone there are seven eyes. For no one doubts that the law was written at the dictation of the Holy Spirit. And by the seven pourers, in which is the oil that is sent into the lamps that shine upon the lampstand, they would have it understood that these seven graces come down from heaven to men through the law. And the two olive trees upon the lampstand, on the right side and on the left, between which the lamp shines in the middle, they interpret as the law and prophecy. And when the prophet had told his vision, not knowing what he was seeing, he asks the angel who was speaking in him, that is, his mind enlightened by God. For this is our angel, who, understanding wisely, shows us the will of God, and he says: What are these, my lord? But the angel does not explain the vision to the prophet, as he had been asked by him, but questions in turn the one who questions him: Do you not know what these are? And when he answers, I do not know, he himself also answers: This is the word of the Lord to Zerubbabel, saying: Not by an army, nor by strength; but by my spirit, says the Lord. The word of the Lord to Zerubbabel, then, which spoke not by an army, nor by strength, but by my spirit, is itself the interpretation of the vision: that not by an army nor by a multitude of warriors, but by the spirit of God, the people had been brought back and would be brought back more fully, and the adversaries would be laid waste. And what follows: Who are you, O great mountain, before Zerubbabel? Into a plain!; they understand it as said to the devil, who stood at the right hand of Joshua to oppose him, and lifted himself up against Zerubbabel and the people of the Jews. But he has been turned into a plain and humbled, and he lies under the feet of Israel, because God shall bring out the chief stone, Christ his Son, who was always a help to the people of Israel. And his grace, that is, the grace of the stone, toward those whom he brought back from captivity, shall make equal the grace which he always exercised toward their fathers. This we have found said by the Hebrews. Now let us discuss what has been written by churchmen in their commentaries. They understand the golden lampstand of purest gold as the Church, which in the holy Scriptures searches out the sense and the mind rather than the words. And that gold is taken to mean the sense is shown by the back and shoulders of the dove in Psalm 67 (68), which are said to shine with the green or the gleam of gold. The lamp they too understand as Christ himself, because he shines in the Church, he who said of himself: No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel, that is, under the measure of the law, but upon a lampstand, that is, the freedom of the Gospel, so that it may give light to all who are in the house. The seven lamps and their pourers they too understand as the graces of the Holy Spirit, through which the Church receives the oil of the mercy of God and of all the virtues. The two olive trees upon the lampstand they too understand as Moses and Elijah, who were talking with the Lord on the mountain and signifying what he was to suffer in Jerusalem. For the whole law and the prophets proclaim the passion of Christ. Others interpret the two olive trees on the right and on the left as the law and the Gospel, so that the Gospel is on the right and the law on the left; of which right and left it is said in the Song of Songs: His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. But most of our writers interpret the mountain as the devil, and the Antichrist, who dares to stand before Zerubbabel, from whom Christ was to be born, and to raise himself up, and to say in the Gospel: All these things have been delivered to me, and I will give them to you, if you will fall down and worship me. And the Lord, laying him low under his feet, and making him humble instead of proud, and cast down, says: Get behind, Satan; for it is written: You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve. And they give as an example in the Gospel the lunatic, whom the apostles had not been able to heal; they ask why they could not heal him, and they hear: Amen I say to you, if you have faith like a grain of mustard seed, you will say to this mountain: Pass from here, and it will pass; and nothing will be impossible to you. But this kind is not cast out except by prayer and fasting. There the mountain is plainly taken to mean the devil. But others, with no small rashness, refer what is plainly said of the devil to Christ, who in the holy Scriptures is quite often called a mountain. Nor is there any need to give examples, of which there is a great abundance. They are led into this error because it is written in the Seventy: Who are you, O great mountain, before the face of Zerubbabel, that you should set right? Namely, that this mountain which is before the face of Zerubbabel, that is, which descends from the stock of Zerubbabel, would himself set the world right, and that what follows is about him: I will bring out the stone of inheritance, of which it is written: It is you who will restore my inheritance to me. And in another place: He chose for us his inheritance, the beauty of Jacob whom he loved. And again: Jacob became the portion of the Lord, Israel the cord of his inheritance. And in Psalm 2: The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance. But God shall bring out the chief stone, of which we read: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God, and: All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. And what he says: He shall make equal the grace of its grace, signifies this: Of his fullness we have all received, and grace for grace, that is, for the grace of the law, the grace of the Gospel, so that both those who believe from Israel and the people of the heathen may receive an equal grace and a like gift. Hence Gabriel too says to Mary: You have found grace with the Lord. And the apostle Paul writes: For by grace you have been saved; and John the evangelist: The law, he says, was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

And the word of the Lord came to me, saying: The hands of Zerubbabel have founded this house, and his hands shall finish it, and you shall know that the Lord of hosts has sent me to you. For who has despised the small days? And they shall rejoice, and shall see the stone of tin in the hand of Zerubbabel. These seven are the eyes of the Lord, which run to and fro through the whole earth. LXX: And the word of the Lord came to me, saying: The hands of Zerubbabel have founded this house, and his hands shall complete it. And you shall know that the Lord Almighty has sent me to you; for who has despised in the small days? And they shall rejoice, and shall see the stone of tin in the hand of Zerubbabel; these seven are the eyes of the Lord, which look upon all the earth. Much is said both by the Hebrews and by our writers; following most of it and rejecting some, let us set out what we think, keeping the truth of the history, so that from it we may be able to recognize him who is prophesied through the history. The hands of Zerubbabel, the prince of the Jews who returned from Babylon, laid the foundations of the temple; and his hands shall reach to the top of the temple, completing what they had begun, and building up all that had been begun. We read in Ezra that the temple was begun and finished by Zerubbabel. And when, says the prophet Zechariah, you see the roof set by you upon the temple which you are now building, then you will understand that I was sent by the Lord, and that what I have spoken I said at his command: For who has despised the small days? Here 'who' is taken for 'rare', according to that saying: Who, do you think, is the faithful and wise steward? and: Lord, who dwells in your tabernacle, and who shall go up into your holy mountain? Rare, then, is the one who despises the small days of this world and thinks royal power worth nothing. When we see the powerful of the world gleaming with gold, glowing with purple and gems, surrounded by an army, let us say within ourselves: Who, do you think, despises the small days? Hence Jacob too, understanding that the life of men is short: Small, he says, and very evil are the days of my life. Those, then, who have despised the small days — and he refers this to royal power, to admonish Zerubbabel and Joshua and the people building the temple of God, by no means to fear the adversaries who were hindering them, but to listen to the Lord exhorting them — those, from the fact that they despise royal grandeur, shall also rejoice and see the help of the Savior, who is promised from the stock of Zerubbabel, and because of his strength is called a stone, and a stone of tin. For this in the Hebrew is written abdil, because he is the wall and the strength and the might of those who believe. For just as tin defends other metals from fire, and though bronze and iron are by nature very hard, yet if it is without tin it is burned and consumed, so all the strength of angels and of men, if the help of the Savior is not there, is proved weak and frail. Now this stone, that is, this mass, which among the Hebrews is written abdil, that is, of tin, ἐτυμολογεῖται (is etymologized as) ἀποχωρίζων, that is, separating and setting apart, so that, as tin through fire parts metals that are mixed and adulterated, so the Lord, the true assayer and χωνευτὴς (refiner), may set apart the bronze and the lead of the vices from the gold and silver of good works, so that pure gold and silver may remain. In different words this ἀποχωρίζων and separator is described in the Gospel: Whose winnowing fan is in his hand, and he will cleanse his threshing floor, and will separate the chaff from the wheat, he who cries through Jeremiah: What has the chaff to do with the wheat? says the Lord. Many of our writers interpret the hands of Zerubbabel, who founded the house and himself finished it, as Christ. But if we accept this, we shall be compelled to explain who the stone of tin in the hand of Zerubbabel is. For surely another Christ is not to be shown in the hand of Christ? Although some have taken the stone of tin as the body of the Lord, which was soiled by no stains of sins, and is not called lead, but purest tin. As for the seven eyes, which run to and fro through the whole earth and judge all things, we explained above that they are the seven spirits; and that nothing is hidden from the Lord, who is aware of things past and present and to come, and renders to each according to his works, above all when he comes in the person of the one who separates the good and the wicked, and of the refiner.

When the prophet asked what the two olive trees signified, one of which stood on the right of the lampstand, the other on the left, the Lord, or the angel of the Lord, would not answer. The prophet, understanding this, inquires a second time, and said: What are the two ears of the olive trees, or the two branches? He asks about lesser things, since he did not deserve to hear the greater. Now these two branches are in the hand of the two nostrils, or upon the two golden beaks, which in Hebrew are called sinthoroth, in Greek μυξωτῆρες; and these two μυξωτῆρες themselves, upon which are the two ears, or the two branches of the olive trees, are of purest gold. And when the prophet asked also about the two branches, and was asked again by the angel whether he knew what the two branches signified, and he had said: No, lord, the angel of the Lord answered: These are the two sons of oil, as Symmachus would have it, or of στιλπνότητος, as Aquila translated, that is, of splendor; or of πιότητος, that is, of fatness, as the Seventy rendered; or of λαμπρότητος, that is, of brightness, as Theodotion translated, who stand by the Ruler of the whole earth. Of the two olive trees which were on the right of the lamp and on the left we have read above. And so the prophet, now asking about them, does not deserve to hear, because he does not retain the former things; or else because what was said there more obscurely he desires to hear here more plainly; or at any rate his obstinacy is refuted by the angel's silence, because he insists that he knows greater things; although the Hebrews maintain that he heard nothing when he asked about the olive trees for this reason, that he did not ask well, and did not inquire into the whole of what he ought to have known. In short, afterwards he inquires more fully, adding the ears or branches of the olive trees, about which he had been silent above. For there he said: What are these two olive trees? Here he asks: What are the two ears of the olive trees? Μεταφορικῶς (metaphorically), because the trees are upright in the manner of ears of grain, and as ears are covered with their beards, so these are covered with a kind of palisade of branches and leaves, and rise up on high. Some of our writers interpret the two olive trees as the Son and the Holy Spirit, and the lamp in the middle as God the Father. But I do not know how, without blasphemy, they can take one as on the right and the other as on the left. The branches, too, or ears of the olive trees, they explain as the incarnation of the Savior and the likeness of a dove of the Holy Spirit, because we cannot see the olive trees whole, but a certain part, and, so to speak, the little branches of the incarnation of Christ and of the manifestation of the Holy Spirit have been shown to us. Others understand the two testaments, the Gospel on the right, the law on the left, because in the one there is the spiritual sense, in the other the bodily; and because we can explain neither the whole Gospel nor the whole law: And now we know in part, and we prophesy in part, and we cannot yet understand what is perfect. There are some who interpret the two branches of the olive trees, or the two ears, and the sons of fatness or of splendor, as the priesthood and the law, which afford joy to the whole earth. Others take them as Enoch and Elijah, of whom one pleased God in uncircumcision, the other in circumcision, and was caught up into heaven with his body. For splendor and oil, and fatness and brightness, we read in the Hebrew Isaar, according to what is written in the psalms of the joy and happiness of the saints: They shall be inebriated with the plenty, or with the fatness of your house: for πιότης means fatness rather than plenty. These things we have spoken as best we could, and as far as the powers of our small talent could bear, touching briefly on the various opinions both of the Hebrews and of our writers; if anyone says something better, or rather truer, we too gladly yield to what is better.

LXX: And I turned, and lifted up my eyes, and saw a flying sickle. And he said to me: What do you see? And I said: I see a flying sickle, of twenty cubits in length, and ten cubits in breadth. And he said to me: This is the curse that goes forth over the face of the whole earth, for every thief shall by it be punished unto death, and every perjurer shall by it be tormented unto death. And I will bring it forth, says the Lord Almighty, and it shall enter into the house of the thief, and into the house of him who swears falsely by my name, and it shall rest in the midst of his house, and shall consume it, and its timbers, and its stones. Having turned, says the prophet, to another vision, and lifting up my eyes from joyful and happy things to sadder ones, I see a flying scroll, which in Hebrew is called megella, and was rendered by Aquila and Theodotion διφθέρα (hide), by Symmachus κεφαλὶς, that is, a heading, according to what we read in the psalm: At the head of the book it is written of me; or, according to the Seventy, Δρέπανον πετόμενον, that is, a flying sickle. For all that he had seen above had announced the building of the temple, the coming of the Lord Savior, the liberation of the people from Babylon. And so, lest his heart be lifted up, as with the Apostle — to whom was given an angel of Satan to buffet him, lest he exalt himself —, he too sees things that are sad, so that whatever arrogance had grown from the revelation of good things might shrink from the threat of sad ones. And a flying scroll is shown, in which the sins of all are written down, so that each may receive according to his works, whether good or evil, as Daniel says: Thrones were set, and the books were opened. But if we take it as a sickle, as the LXX translated, let us take an example from the Apocalypse of John, in which it is written: And the angel answered, and said to him who sat upon the horse: Put in your sharpest sickle, and reap your vineyard, for its grapes are ripe. Instead of a sickle we read in Deuteronomy of arrows and a sword: I will make my arrows drunk with blood, and my sword shall devour flesh. And because God did not spare the angels who sinned, who by their own fault lost their heavenly dwelling, therefore he himself says through Isaiah: My sword has been made drunk in heaven. For all the sinners of the people shall perish by the sword, not, surely, by a bodily sword — since there are many and various ways to death besides the sword —, but by a spiritual sword, with which those are to be struck who have not repented, and whom the psalmist threatens, crying out: Unless you are converted, he will brandish his sword; he has bent his bow and made it ready, and in it he has prepared the vessels of death. And in Jeremiah we read: How long will you cut, O point, or sword, of the Lord? How long will you not be still? Return into your sheath. This sword is called not only a sickle, which cuts down hay, stubble and thorns, but it is also called the axe of the trees, which will cut down those who have not brought forth fruits worthy of repentance; and of whom John the Baptist cried out: Behold, the axe is laid to the roots of the trees; every tree that does not bear good fruit will be cut down and thrown into the fire. This scroll, in which the sins of all are written down, or this sickle, which cuts down the offences of all, is sent into the vineyard of Sodom, of which it is written: Our enemies are without understanding, for their vine is of the vineyard of Sodom, and their shoot from Gomorrah. Their grape is a grape of gall, and theirs a cluster of bitterness. Their wine is the fury of dragons, and the incurable fury of asps. This sickle has twenty cubits of length and ten of breadth, in which number joyful things are joined with sad. For the Lord rebukes in order to amend; and in the twenty, which is made up of two decades, harsh and sad things are announced; in the ten, that is, one decade, better and prosperous things; for Israel is instructed through all scourges and punishments. And at the same time let us warn those who think theft and perjury small crimes, that the curse which is described in the scroll and the sickle is brought into the house of the thief and of the perjurer, and remains in it, and consumes all its timbers and stones. But if in these things which are thought lesser — theft, I mean, and perjury — there is so great a threat of punishments, what shall we say of fornication, adultery, homicide, sacrilege, and all the crimes which are numbered by the Apostle among the works of the flesh? I have read that the twenty cubits of length and the ten of breadth refer to the age of the Lord Savior, that is, they make the number thirty, because the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, and by him the world is to be judged.

We pass from obscure things to obscure things, and with Moses we enter into the cloud and the darkness. Deep calls to deep, at the voice of the cataracts of God; and the wind goes circling round and round, and returns to its circles. We suffer the wanderings of a labyrinth, and guide our blind steps by the thread of Christ. Into this difficulty the courier of the book, in his traveller's hat, urges me on:

Who has begun well has half the deed done.

How much more ought we, who have already completed a third part of the way, to sweat with the same labor over what remains, so as not to lose what is past, and so that an unfinished work may not heighten the reader's longing. Therefore, my venerable Pope Exsuperius, be present by your prayers, you who are absent in body, and obtain from the Lord that the veil of Zechariah be taken away from my face, the veil that was drawn before the eyes of Moses because the ignoble crowd could not bear the brightness of his countenance, so that I too may be able to say with David: The Lord will give the word to those who preach the good news with great power. For the vine shall give its fruit, and all the winepresses shall be filled, the earth shall be clothed with glad crops, and with the rains and the night dew watering it, all things shall sprout; all that I have said, I will cause the remnant of my people to possess; the foundations of the house of the Lord have been laid, and the temple has been raised. The prophet Haggai too speaks these same things at the same time, who had said above: I struck you with a burning wind and with blight and with hail all the works of your hands. After the foundations of the temple were laid: Set, he says, in your hearts from this day and into the future, from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, from the day on which the foundations of the temple were laid, set it upon your heart. Is the seed already in the shoot, and has the vine and the fig and the pomegranate and the olive tree yet flowered? From this day I will bless, and again I will move heaven and earth alike. Let us also say it another way, following the explanation begun concerning the Church. Before anyone receives the faith of Christ, and the foundations of the Holy Spirit are laid in him, no one will be able to hear: There is a wage for your work. Whether he be a Jew, or a heretic, or a Gentile, whatever good work he has done, unless he has done it in the name of Christ, he will not have the wage of his good work. We see the virgins of the heretics, the rigor of the philosophers, the observance of the Jews in the variety of foods, and yet we say, according to Haggai, that they eat and are not satisfied; they drink and are not made drunk; they are covered and are not warmed; and he who gathers wages puts them into a bag with holes. But after they have received the faith of Christ, both those and these who had been sinners in the Church, and for the greatness of their offenses had been handed over to the captivity of this world and burned by the Babylonian fire, and have heard the Lord preaching: The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me; he has sent me to preach the gospel to the poor, to proclaim release to the captives, and sight to the blind, to heal those who are of a contrite heart, and when that which is said through Amos has been fulfilled in them: I will turn back the captivity of my people Israel, and they shall build the ruined cities, and I will plant them, then in those days justice shall rise, and abundance of peace. The vine shall give its fruit, the vine that says in the Gospel: I am the vine, you are the branches. Every branch that abides in me, the Father cleanses, that it may bear greater fruit. And when its branches have been cleansed, that is, its shoots and layers, and with budding eyes have begun to promise the hope of fruits to come, so that what is written may be fulfilled: The flowering vines have given forth their fragrance, then the sun of justice has colored the hanging clusters, so that, sent into the winepresses of Psalms 8 and 83 (84), which are entitled for the winepresses, they may be trodden by the foot of the Lord, who comes up from Bozrah, that he may pour out the wine that gladdens the heart of man. The earth too shall give its shoot, not the dry and stony earth full of thorns, but the good earth which yields fruit a hundredfold and sixtyfold and thirtyfold, so that those who have sown in tears may reap in joy. But the heavens too shall give their dew, the heavens of which it is written in Psalm 18 (19): The heavens tell the glory of God, and the firmament proclaims the works of his hands. Of these heavens it is said in the song of Deuteronomy: Rejoice, O heavens, with him, that is, with the Lord and Savior, whom he foreknew and predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son; it is he who speaks with Moses: Let my speech be awaited like the rain, and let my words come down like the dew. For those who were dead in sins shall rise again, and those who lay in whitewashed sepulchres, which are full of dead men's bones, shall be raised up, and those who dwell in the earth shall rejoice. And what the cause of the joy is, the following words show: For the dew which is from you is their healing. And all these things, namely peace and the fruit of the vines, and the abundance of the lands, which has grown up from the dew of the heavens, the remnant of my people shall possess, of whom Isaiah speaks: Unless the Lord Sabaoth had left us a seed, we should have been as Sodom, and we should have been like Gomorrah. For the remnant according to the election of grace has been saved, not by works, otherwise grace would not be grace.

8:13-15

And it shall be: as you were a curse among the nations, O house of Judah and house of Israel, so I will save you and you shall be a blessing: do not fear, let your hands be strengthened. For thus says the Lord of hosts: As I purposed to afflict you, when your fathers had provoked me to anger, says the Lord, and I did not have mercy, so, having turned, I have purposed in these days to do good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah: do not fear. LXX: And it shall be: as you were in a curse among the nations, house of Judah and house of Israel, so I will save you, and you shall be in a blessing; take courage and be strengthened in your hearts, for thus says the Lord Almighty. As I purposed to afflict you in that your fathers had provoked me to anger, says the Lord Almighty, and I did not repent, so I am ready and have purposed in these days to do good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah: take courage. He promises these things as to come after the building of the temple: that as the house of Judah and the house of Israel, namely the two and the ten tribes, were among all the nations a curse and a hissing and an example, so, when they have been saved and have returned to Judea, they may be a blessing to all. Do not fear, he says, the adversaries who rebel; be confident that what the Lord promises through me is true, let your hands be strengthened, finish the works which you have begun. The cause of the strengthening is the Lord's promise: For thus says the Lord Almighty, to whom nothing is impossible, who is able to fulfil what he promises: As I devised to afflict you and to hand you over to captivity, because your fathers provoked me to anger, and I did not have mercy, which the LXX translated οὐ μετενόησα, that is, I did not repent, which in Hebrew is written ulo naamathi; but I did not have mercy, so that I might correct you by captivity and train you through every torment and scourge; so now, in the present time, I have purposed to do good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah. And it is to be noted that when he is angry, the house of Judah and the house of Israel, that is, all twelve tribes which were handed over to captivity, are a curse among the nations. But after he has purposed to do good, he does not do it to Judah and Israel, that is, to the two and the ten tribes, namely to Jerusalem and Samaria, Oholah and Oholibah; but, leaving Israel in captivity, he does good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah. And he adds at the end: Do not fear, or take courage, in the sense which we have explained above. But concerning the Church and each one of the believers it can be understood thus: that in the time of persecution the Christians were a curse and an example to all the nations round about, because they had offended their Lord; and afterwards they are in blessing, when peace has been restored; and all this comes about because the Lord, who had at first been angry, afterwards has mercy on Jerusalem, the vision of peace, and on Judah, which confesses the faith of the Lord. Each of the believers, too, who are cast out of the Church for their vices and are handed over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, that they may learn not to blaspheme, when they have done repentance, will return to their former state and will see the peace of the Lord, and will possess the glory of their confession. What is written, I did not have mercy, or I did not repent, the heretics slander, as though the Lord were either cruel or changeable, if he either does not repent or repents. For if he has repented, they say that he is changeable; if he has not repented, they assert that he is cruel. Yet God repented that he had anointed Saul as king. And among the Ninevites, to whom he had announced through the prophet: Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown, because they did repentance, he himself too is said to have changed his sentence, not through the fault of an improvident mind, but from the difference in what they do, badly or well. For if they do evil, he threatens; if they have blotted out their former sins, he has mercy; God is not changed, who is one and the same and cannot be changed, but when they have changed from evil to good works, he himself also changes his own decree. He it is who also speaks in Genesis: The cry of Sodom and Gomorrah has been multiplied, and their sins are exceedingly great; I will go down, therefore, and see whether they are fulfilling it according to their cry which has come to me; and if not, that I may know. What he said is this: If they persist in their fury, punishment will not be lacking to the sinners; if they cease from their madness, they will become most worthy of my knowledge. For the Lord knows those who are his. And the Apostle writes to the Galatians: But then, not knowing God. And although God knows all, and nothing past, nor present, nor future is hidden from him, in the Gospel he says that he does not know the wicked: Depart from me, workers of iniquity, I do not know you. Therefore let us take both the knowledge and the repentance, and the wrath and indignation, and all the affections of God, not according to the defect of human speech, but according to the sense of the divine majesty.

8:16-17

These, then, are the words which you shall do: Speak the truth, each one with his neighbor; judge truth and the judgment of peace in your gates; and let none of you devise evil in your hearts against his friend, and do not love a lying oath; for all these are things that I hate, says the Lord. LXX: These are the words which you shall do: Speak the truth, each one with his neighbor, and a peaceable judgment, judge in your gates, and let each one not devise evil against his neighbor in your hearts, and do not love a lying oath, for all these things I hate, says the Lord Almighty. I have promised that I will not deal with the remnant of the people of the captives as in the former days. And as I purposed to afflict them, when their fathers had provoked me to anger, and I did not have mercy, so now, having turned, I have purposed in these days to do good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah. So that my sentence, then, may stand, and the promise may not be made void, do these things which I command: Speak the truth with your neighbors. Let us take neighbor to mean the whole race of men, since we have been begotten of one parent; otherwise, if neighbor is taken to mean kinsman, we must lie to strangers and foreigners. The Apostle says this same thing: Putting away lying, speak the truth, each one with his neighbor. Truth, he says, and the judgment of peace judge in your gates. In judgment let truth and justice come first; then mercy follows. For this is the judgment of peace: that the judge should have as his purpose to make peace between those at variance, according to that saying of the Gospel: Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God. And what follows, In your gates, agrees with that prophetic saying: They hated him who reproves in the gates, and they abhorred the holy word. And in another place: They shall not be put to shame when they speak with their enemies in the gate. David too used to judge in the gates, when Absalom, promising the truth of judgment, was laying a trap for his father. And it is asked why among the Jews the place of judging was in the gates. So that the farmers should not be forced to enter the cities and undergo some loss, the judges sat in the gates, so that they might hear townsmen and countrymen alike at the going out and coming in of the city, and when the business was finished, each might at once return to his own dwelling. And do not devise evil, he says, against your friends in your hearts; raath, which all with one voice have translated κακίαν, that is, evil, we can take in two ways, both for affliction and for wickedness. For affliction: If there is evil in a city which the Lord has not done, and: Sufficient for the day is its own evil. For wickedness, God speaks in the prophet Jonah: The cry of their evil has gone up to me, and in the Apostle we read: Filled with all iniquity and evil. In both ways, therefore, he who is holy neither afflicts his friend nor devises evil against him in his heart. And do not love, he says, a lying oath; for the Lord commands in the Gospel: But I say to you, do not swear at all, but let your speech be, yes yes, no no; for he who does not swear will never be able to swear falsely. Let him who swears hear what is written: You shall not take up the name of the Lord your God in a vain thing. All these are things that I hate, says the Lord, according to the words of Malachi, who says: And all the things that I hated, you did. In precepts which pertain to life and are plain, we ought not to look for allegory, lest, as the Comic poet says, we seek a knot in a bulrush.

8:18-19

And the word of the Lord of hosts came to me, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts: The fast of the fourth, and the fast of the fifth, and the fast of the seventh, and the fast of the tenth shall be to the house of Judah for joy and for gladness, and for glorious solemn feasts; only love truth and peace. LXX: And the word of the Lord Almighty came to me, saying: Thus says the Lord Almighty: The fourth fast, and the fifth fast, and the seventh fast, and the tenth fast shall be to the house of Judah for joy and for gladness, and for good festivals; — for in Hebrew this is said tobim, that is, ἀγαθάς —, and rejoice, and love truth and peace. To what Sharezer and Regem-melech had asked above through envoys, whether they ought to fast and lament in the fifth month and in the seventh month, as it stands in the LXX, or after the building of the temple to end the fast and lay aside their mourning, the prophet, after many things have been set out in between which they were to do and which they were to hope for, answered in the person of the Lord: The fast of the fourth, and the fast of the fifth, and the fast of the seventh, and the fast of the tenth — for month is understood ἀπὸ κοινοῦ (from the shared word) —, to the house of Judah, and to Jerusalem, shall be turned into festal days and joy. God seeks only truth and peace. On this passage many of our writers have said many things, and things at variance with one another. Some, confessing the obscurity by their silence, have passed over it in their commentaries as though it were a very deep pit, judging it more right to say nothing at all than to say too little. We are therefore compelled to have recourse to the Hebrews, and to seek the truth of knowledge from the spring rather than from the streams, especially since this is not some prophecy of Christ, where they are wont to dodge and to hide the truth with a lie, but the order of a history woven from what precedes and what follows. The fast of the fourth month, which among the Latins is called July, on the seventeenth day of that same month, they think was the day when Moses, coming down from Mount Sinai, threw down and broke the tablets of the law; and, according to Jeremiah, the walls of the city were first breached. In the fifth month, which among the Latins is called August, when because of the spies of the holy land a sedition had arisen among the people, they were ordered not to go up the mountain but for forty years to go round toward the holy land by long and wasteful wanderings, so that, except for two, Caleb and Joshua, all fell in the wilderness. In this month the temple at Jerusalem was burned and destroyed, both by Nebuchadnezzar and, many ages later, by Titus and Vespasian; the city of Bether was taken, to which many thousands of the Jews had fled; the temple was ploughed up by Turannius Rufus to the disgrace of the oppressed nation. And in the seventh, which among us is called October, as we said above, Gedaliah was slain, and the remnant of the tribe of Judah and of Jerusalem was scattered. Let us read Jeremiah. In the tenth month, which among us is called January, because it is the door of the year and its beginning, Ezekiel, set in captivity, heard, and all the people of the captives, that in the fifth month the temple had been overthrown, as we learn most fully in that same prophet. This, then, is the whole of what is said: The days of lamentation and of fasts which until now you have kept in mourning, know that, since I have purposed to do good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah, they are to be turned for you into gladness and joy and solemn feasts; provided only that you love truth and peace. According to ἀναγωγὴν (anagogy): since we fast when the bridegroom is taken away from us and we do not deserve to have his presence, when the Lord has returned to us and has purposed to do good to us, all sadness shall be turned into joy; and the former famine of the word of God shall be made up for by the presence of his teachings and the fullness of the heavenly bread.

8:20-22

Thus says the Lord of hosts: Until peoples come, and dwell in many cities, and the inhabitants go one to another, saying: Let us go and entreat the face of the Lord, and let us seek the Lord of hosts. I too will go, and many peoples and strong nations shall come to seek the Lord of hosts in Jerusalem, and to entreat the face of the Lord. LXX: Thus says the Lord Almighty: Many peoples shall yet come, and the inhabitants of many cities; and those who dwell in the cities shall be gathered into one city, saying: Let us go to entreat the face of the Lord, and let us seek the countenance of the Lord Almighty. I too will go, and many peoples and very many nations shall come, to seek the face of the Lord Almighty in Jerusalem, and to entreat the face of the Lord. The fast of the fourth, and of the fifth, and of the seventh, and of the tenth month shall be changed into excellent solemn feasts, so much so that the cities of Judea which before were deserted shall be thronged with many inhabitants; and one city shall go to another, and they shall exhort one another and say: Because for these seventy years the ways of Zion mourned, since there was no one to go to the solemn feast, all her gates deserted, and her priests groaning, now that peace has been restored, let us go to Jerusalem, where it is commanded in the law that we should sacrifice victims, and that three times in the year every male of ours should appear in the sight of the Lord; and when one city says to another: Let us go and entreat the face of the Lord, and let us seek the Lord Almighty, the other city will answer: I too will go: at which time many peoples and countless and strong nations shall come, to offer sacrifices in Jerusalem to the Lord of hosts, and to entreat his face. For the Lord draws near to those who do not tempt him, and shows his face to those who are not unbelieving. He who has seen the Son sees the Father also; and the Lord and Savior is the image of the invisible God, not that the Son is visible and the Father invisible, but that when the Son is named, the Father is perceived. For he is by no means Father if he has no Son. Hence he himself also says in the Gospel: Father, I have manifested your name to men. What we said of Jerusalem and Zerubbabel, or of the time after Zerubbabel, is more rightly and more fully referred to Christ and to Jerusalem, which is understood as the Church; and that then from the whole world both peoples and nations will flock together to offer sacrifices in the temple of the Lord. In a time of persecution also, as we touched on briefly before, let the teachers and priests of the Church boldly promise the captives and the believers that the παροικίαι (sojourning communities) are to be built again, and the tranquillity of peace restored, and the face of the Lord entreated in the churches. We run quickly over what is plain, so as to linger over what is more obscure.

8:23

Thus says the Lord of hosts: In those days, in which ten men of all the tongues of the nations shall take hold, and they shall take hold of the hem of a man who is a Jew, saying: We will go with you, for we have heard that God is with you. LXX: Thus says the Lord Almighty: In those days, if ten men of all the tongues of the nations take hold, and grasp the hem of a man who is a Jew, saying: We will go with you, because we have heard that God is with you. Some of the Jews say that these things were fulfilled under Zerubbabel and after Zerubbabel. Others put them off to a future time, when they hope that the Christ will come. But we understand them both more rightly and more truly of the coming of the Lord and Savior, when he was born of the virgin Mary. In short, it is written: Until peoples come? When it says until, it signifies not the present time, in which Zerubbabel and Joshua were, but the future, when many peoples and strong nations shall come, to seek the Lord of hosts in Jerusalem, and to entreat the face of the Lord. At that time, therefore, and in those days, ten men of all the tongues of the nations shall take hold of the hem of a man who is a Jew, saying: We will go with you, for we have heard that God is with you. And in Isaiah we read: Seven women shall take hold of one man, saying: We will eat our own bread and be clothed with our own garments; only let your name be called upon us, take away our reproach. Those, then, who there are called seven women, that is, Churches, whose number is contained also in the apostle Paul — for he writes to seven churches, to the Romans, to the Corinthians, to the Galatians, to the Ephesians, to the Philippians, to the Colossians, to the Thessalonians. And in the Apocalypse of John the Lord, girded with variety and with purest gold, walks in the midst of the seven lampstands, that is, of the churches of the Ephesians, the Smyrnaeans, the Pergamenes, the Thyatirans, the Sardians, the Philadelphians, the Laodiceans —, are now named ten in the prophet Zechariah, whom the Lord also sought, so that, if he had found them in Sodom and Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim, he might deliver them from destruction. For the letter iota, with which the name of the Savior takes its beginning, signifies the number ten not only among the Greeks but also among the Hebrews. And by this mystical language it is shown that all who are counted under the name of Christian, whom the Lord also says he left, seven thousand, in the time of the persecution of Jezebel and the flight of Elijah, who did not bow the knee before Baal, and who have come to the measure of the perfect man out of all tongues and nations, shall take hold of the hem of a man who is a Jew, that is, of the Lord and Savior, of whom it is also said in the psalm: Judah is my king, and: Judah, your brothers shall praise you, and again: A prince shall not fail from Judah, nor a leader from his thighs, until he comes for whom it is laid up; and he shall be the expectation of the nations. For there shall be the root of Jesse, and he who shall rise up to rule the nations, in him the nations shall hope. And when they have taken hold of him, they will long to cling to his footsteps, since God is with him. Or else, whoever out of all tongues and nations have believed shall take hold of a man who is a Jew, the apostles who are of the Jews, and shall say: Let us go with you; for we have heard through the prophets, and have learned by the voice of all the Scriptures, that the Son of God, Christ, God and Lord, is with you. Where the prophecy is most manifest, and the coming of Christ and of his apostles and the faith of all the nations are proclaimed, let us seek nothing further. As for our having said that the number seven thousand pertains to the name of Christians, reckon in Greek ἑπτάκεις χιλίους (seven thousand) and χριστειανούς (Christians), and you will find the same number and sum, that is, one thousand nine hundred and forty-one. But they also refer to the mystery of this number the parable of the ten virgins in the Gospel, whom we interpret of the senses of the flesh and of the spirit, if they have prepared the oil of good works for their lamps, and have doubled the number five, so as to be holy, according to the Apostle, in body and in spirit. So too the ten cities, which he who has governed the senses of the body excellently shall receive in the future, the number doubled.

9:1

The burden of the word of the Lord in the land of Hadrach, and of Damascus his rest, for the eye of man is the Lord's, and of all the tribes of Israel. LXX: The taking up of the word of the Lord in the land of Sedrach and of Damascus his sacrifice, for the Lord looks upon men, and all the tribes of Israel. This whole vision, or weight and burden of the word of the Lord, as Aquila translated it, pertains to the calling of the nations and the building of the Church. And the order of the words is: The taking up of the word of the Lord, sharp toward sinners, soft toward the just. For Hadrach, a name compounded of two whole words, sounds this: ad meaning sharp, rach soft and tender, and not, as it is wrongly read by some, Sedrach. Some refer Hadrach to the people of the Jews, Damascus to the calling of the nations. Hence the people, or the taking up of the word of the Lord, comes about in the land of Hadrach, over which the Lord exercised both his severity and his clemency: severity toward those who would not believe, clemency toward those who returned with the apostles; but Damascus is properly the rest of the Lord, according to what is written in Isaiah: And his rest shall be honor. For before the child knew how to choose good or evil and to call father and mother, he received the strength of Damascus and the spoils of Samaria. Hence Damascus too is rendered in our language drinking blood, or blood of sackcloth, so that the first interpretation signifies a bloodthirsty people, the second repentance joined to its cruelty. And what follows: For the eye of man is the Lord's and of all the tribes of Israel, they expound thus: For this reason the temple of God is to be built from both peoples, that is, both from the land of Hadrach and from Damascus his rest, because whoever looks to God and hopes in him, both from the nations and from all the tribes of Israel, is the Lord's; or, according to the LXX, because the Lord looks equally upon all men, and upon all the tribes of Israel. Let us also say it another way: In the land of Hadrach is the burden of the word of the Lord, and a most heavy weight. But in Damascus are his victims and sacrifice, for the Lord looks upon both the faith of the nations and the faithlessness of the circumcision without any respect of persons, and is equally the God of all, laying the heavy weight of his sentence upon some, and resting in the seat of others. The Jews say that all these things are to be fulfilled at the coming of the Christ, whom they promise themselves in the last time, so that both the land of Hadrach and Damascus, and Hamath and Tyre, and Sidon and Ashkelon and Gaza, Ekron also and Ashdod and the Philistines, shall bow their necks to a most mighty king and serve him as he dwells in Jerusalem, and that no one shall dare to raise a hand against Israel, all around being at peace. All of which we show to have been accomplished spiritually at the coming of the Lord and in his Churches.

9:2-4

Hamath also on its borders, and Tyre and Sidon, for they have taken to themselves exceeding wisdom. And Tyre built her fortification, and heaped up silver like dust, and gold like the mud of the streets. Behold, the Lord shall possess her, and shall strike her strength in the sea, and she shall be devoured by fire. LXX: And Hamath on its borders, Tyre and Sidon, since they were exceedingly wise, and Tyre built her fortification, and gathered silver like dust, and gold like the mud of the roads. Therefore the Lord shall possess her, and shall strike her strength in the sea, and she shall be burned up in fire. Hamath, which is interpreted χόλος, that is, wrath, is the same city that received the name Epiphania from Antiochus Ἐπιφανεῖ (Epiphanes), and is now a city of Coele Syria. This one, therefore, and Tyre and Sidon shall be within the borders of Damascus, or within the borders of the land of Hadrach, so as to believe in the Lord and Savior, to whom it was said by the Father: Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession. The daughters of this Tyre, that is, the souls that believe in Christ, shall bring gifts to the king, that what is written may be fulfilled: The daughters of Tyre shall worship him with gifts. And when the city of God, the Church, has been built, of which the psalmist sings: Glorious things are spoken of you, city of God, at once the rest will follow: Behold the foreigners, and Tyre, and the people of the Ethiopians, these were born there. Of the foreigners it is said in two psalms: Foreigners serve me, or are subject. Of the Ethiopians we read in the prophet Zephaniah: From beyond the rivers of Ethiopia they shall bring gifts to me, and in the psalm: Ethiopia shall stretch out her hands first to God. And of Tyre the Gospel speaks: Woe to you, Chorazin, woe to you, Bethsaida; for if the mighty works that were done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, perhaps they would long ago have repented in sackcloth and ashes. From the borders of Tyre and Sidon that Canaanite woman, the Syrophoenician, whose daughter was badly afflicted by a demon, came to meet the Lord and Savior; and she dedicated the first fruits of the faith of the Tyrians and Sidonians, so that those who had not believed in the time of Christ, because they had not yet seen the signs and mighty works, should afterwards see them through the apostles and believe, and should share in that blessedness which the Lord promises, saying: More blessed are those who have not seen and have believed. But Tyre and Sidon — of which the one is interpreted συνοχὴ, that is, distress, the other θήρευμα, that is, hunting —, took to themselves wisdom to such a degree that, with its sophisms and the nets of dialectic and the webs of the sophists, which Scripture calls fortifications, Tyre put riddles to Solomon, that is, to the peaceful king; and gathered its silver, namely the brilliance of eloquence, and its gold, the cunning of its thoughts, like the dust and mud that lie in the streets and roads, so as to possess the kingdom of idolatry that it had fortified. But against these fortifications the Apostle speaks: For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty to God for the destruction of fortifications, destroying reasonings and every height that exalts itself against the knowledge of God. And under the figure of Nineveh, which is interpreted adorned, or beautiful, for nothing is more adorned than the world, the word of God is directed through the prophet against the men of the world: And you shall be made drunk, and shall be despised, and you shall seek help from the enemy; all your fortifications are like a fig tree with its early figs: if they are shaken, they shall fall into the mouth of the eater. And so the Lord shall possess both Tyre and Sidon, when he has first struck their strength in the most salt and most bitter sea of this world; and, lest anything of their former wisdom remain in them, that is, of the swollen folly through which they procured for themselves feeble fortifications, and silver and gold like mud, he will smelt them with fire and cleanse them to purity; of which the Savior speaks in the Gospel: I came to cast fire upon the earth, and how I wish that it were kindled, according to what is written in Mark: Everyone shall be salted with fire.

9:5-8

We have set out the etymologies of the names in order to run briefly through the sense. Ashkelon and Gaza and Ekron, seeing that Hamath was within the borders of Damascus, and that Tyre and Sidon, after they were struck round about, and all their hay, wood and stubble burned up in the conflagration, were possessed by the Lord; they too, terrified by fear and grief and confusion, began to hope for better things. In short, Ashkelon, in which there was before the ignoble fire of the devil, and which had come to the full measure and weight of sins, trembled with dread because she ceased to have inhabitants. Ekron too, barren because without the law and the knowledge of God she had no sons, was uprooted, so that she might hear that prophetic word: Rejoice, barren one who do not bear; break forth and cry, you who are not in labor, for many more are the sons of the desolate than of her who has a husband. LXX: And you, by the blood of the testament, have sent forth your prisoners from the pit that has no water; you shall sit in the stronghold, prisoners of the congregation, and for one day of your sojourning I will render double to you. After the word of the prophet, or rather God the Father Almighty himself, had announced to Zion and Jerusalem that their king was coming to them, meek and mounted upon a she-ass, a beast of burden, and upon the foal of an ass, and that his power would be from sea to sea, and from the rivers to the ends of the earth, he makes an apostrophe to Christ himself, of whom the prophecy speaks, and says: You also, by the blood of your testament, or covenant, have sent forth your prisoners from the pit in which there is no water. This is understood as follows: By the blood of your passion you have, in your clemency, set free those who were held bound in the prison of hell, in which there is no mercy. In short, after the Lord rose again, those who were held by the bonds of Adam's sins, or, as some would have it, of ingrained error, and of death, rose with him and appeared in the holy city. Of this blood of the testament he himself also spoke to the disciples, pointing to his future passion: Take and drink of this, all of you; for this is the cup of the new testament in my blood. In prefiguration of this pit which has no waters, Joseph was sent into one by his brothers, and Daniel and Jeremiah by the Chaldeans and by the people of the Jews. Benaiah too, in the time of snow and cold, went down into a pit to kill a lion there. Jeremiah, however, is plunged not into the water of the pit but into the mud and mire of the pit, which could suffocate a thirsty man rather than refresh him. Hence it is also written in the psalm: I am stuck fast in the mire of the deep, and there is no substance. In this pit of hell there abode also that rich man once clothed in purple, whose boastful tongue was burned by the fires of punishment, and who was so far from having any refreshment of waters that he begged for the refreshment of the tip of the poor man's finger dipped in water. And again the word is directed to those very ones who were prisoners and who were to be set free by the mercy of Christ: Turn to the stronghold, you prisoners of hope. And the sense is: You who are now prisoners and are held in a harsh and terrible hell, you who hope for the loosing of your bonds at the coming of Christ, turn to the stronghold, or, you shall sit in the stronghold, of which it is written: The fear of the Lord is the bulwark of the holy one, so that you may say: Be to me a God, a protector, and a fortified place, that you may save me; and so that the prophet may also say of you: Behold, a strong city, and our salvation shall set a wall and an outer wall. But this stronghold, to which God exhorts the prisoners of hope, or the hoping prisoners of the Church, we ought to take as nothing other than the dwelling of paradise, into which the thief was the first to enter with the Lord; and for this reason they are called forth to the stronghold through Zechariah, because already then, and from that time, the Lord was promising that for a brief tribulation they would receive eternal rewards. Or, as is read in the LXX: For one day of your sojourning, I will render double to you. For in comparison with eternity, all that we suffer in the world must be called one day, a day not of dwelling but of sojourning, because we are strangers and sojourners, as all our fathers were. For what is at present momentary and light in our tribulation works in us, beyond measure, in sublimity, a weight of eternal glory, while we look not at the things that are seen, but at those that are not seen. For the things that are seen are temporal, but those that are not seen are eternal.

LXX: Ask rain of the Lord in due season, early and late. But since it is now the last time of the prophets, and the world is declining toward its end, and all the things that have been foretold await their fulfilment, ask of the Lord that he give you the late rain, that the Christ who has been promised may come and grant you dews and snows, for which azizim is written in the Hebrew. This whole place is obscure and doubtful, and the reader ought to pardon us if, in things that are ambiguous, we too walk with a hesitant step. These things he says of the heretics, who under the Christian name understand neither what they are saying nor what they are affirming, and who give heed to erring spirits and to teachers of demons who speak lies in hypocrisy, and have their conscience seared, so that, being by no means believers, they are led away by the consolations of those who promise vain things; and for this reason they have been handed over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, and led away into the captivity of the king of Babylon, and they shall be afflicted, because they do not have Christ as their shepherd, whom they promise themselves under a false name.

There is a threefold interpretation from the place in which we read: Behold, I will make Jerusalem a lintel of drunkenness for all the peoples round about, as far as the place where it is written: Sword, awake against my shepherd, and against the man who is my companion, says the Lord of hosts. Strike the shepherd and the sheep shall be scattered. For some of the Jews think that these things have already been partly fulfilled, from Zerubbabel down to Gaius Pompey, who was the first of the Romans to take Judea and the temple, a history which Josephus writes. Others, however, think that they are to be fulfilled at the end of the world, when Jerusalem shall have been restored, which the wretched Jewish nation promises itself with its ἠλειμμένῳ (anointed one), whom we read of above as the foolish shepherd. But others, that is, we who are reckoned by the name of Christ, declare that they are daily being fulfilled, and are to be fulfilled, in the Church until the end of the world. And so as not to stretch the measure of the volume by going through every point, setting it out thus: Those men say this, others suppose so, we think thus, we have set down three opinions, so that from the variety of interpretation the prudent reader may understand what is to be fitted to what. The Lord, then, who stretched out the heaven like a skin, and made the earth firm with a lofty mass, and formed the spirit of man within him, is likewise the Creator of all souls, so that out of the two substances of soul and body he might put together one living being. For spirit is frequently taken for soul, as there: Father, into your hands I commend my spirit, and: You will take away their spirit and they will fail, and they will return to their dust. This Creator, then, and Lord of the universe testifies that he will make Jerusalem a lintel of drunkenness for all the peoples round about, so that whoever touches its threshold may be made drunk and fall, or else the lintel itself may fall upon the one by whom it is touched. But Judah too, when Jerusalem is besieged, is captured by the nations, and passing into their fellowship he will be compelled to besiege his own mother city. And not only will the Lord make Jerusalem like a lintel of drunkenness for all the peoples; he will also make her like a stone of burden for all the peoples, and whoever tries to lift it will be torn with gashing. For all the kingdoms of the earth will be gathered against Jerusalem. It is the custom in the cities of Palestine, and to this day the old practice is kept throughout all Judea, that in villages, towns and forts round stones of very great weight are set, on which young men are accustomed to exercise themselves; and they lift them according to the variety of their strength, some up to the knees, some up to the navel, others to the shoulders and the head, some above the crown of the head, with arms held straight and hands joined, raising the weight and showing the greatness of their strength. On the citadel of the Athenians, beside the statue of Minerva, I saw a bronze sphere of very great weight, which I, because of the weakness of my poor body, could scarcely move. And when I asked what it meant, the answer was given by the inhabitants of that city that the strength of athletes was proved by that mass, and that no one went down to a contest until it was known, from his lifting of the weight, with whom he ought to be matched. So the sense is this: I will make Jerusalem for all the nations like a very heavy stone to be lifted. They will indeed lift her, and lay her waste according to the variety of their strength; but it must be that, while she is being lifted, in the very straining and raising of the weight, the very heavy stone leaves some gash or scrape on the bodies of those who lift it. Concerning the Church it can be interpreted thus: that all the persecutors who have fought against the house of the Lord are made drunk with that cup which Jeremiah gives to all the nations to drink, so that they drink and are drunk, and fall, and vomit, and go mad. I know that in the time of persecution many of our own were compelled to fight against the Church; but whoever wishes to lift this weight will indeed lift it, and will bear it up in his hands for the wrath of the Lord by which he chastises sinners; yet he himself will not go unpunished, since the sword of God fights against him. For the stone of burden, which we have rendered as best we could, the Seventy translated: A stone trodden down by all the nations. Everyone who treads it down — or her, that is, Jerusalem —, mocking shall mock. Its sense is plain: that in both ways Jerusalem is to be trodden down both by hostile nations and by persecutors, and is to be mocked at the pleasure of those who mock. But the former sense is better and truer. We expound each point briefly, so that we may at last come to the end, lest, if we write at greater length, the reader's mind be confused both by the obscurity of the matters and by the length of the discourse.

All, then, who are on the right way, to whom it is said: Do not be overly just, and on the left, who hear: The ways that are on the left are perverse, the devouring flame shall consume; and when the adversaries have been destroyed and removed, Jerusalem, that is, the Church, will again receive her former glory; and she will be in her own state, and the tents of Judah will be saved, the gatherings of Christians scattered throughout the whole world, from which, as from tents and tabernacles, we long to go to the house that is not made with hands, and to the heavenly Jerusalem. And it is for this reason that the tents of the common people, and of all who are reckoned by the Christian name and are counted among the crowd, receive peace first, while the princes of the churches are besieged and put to flight, so that the masters and teachers may in no way think that peace was restored to the Churches by their own teaching and wisdom, but by the help of the Lord.

According to anagogy: the Lord shall shield the inhabitants of the Church, when the peace of the churches has been restored after a most grievous persecution, when she shall also be adorned by the interpretation of her own name. 12:9-10

And it shall be on that day: I will seek to crush all the nations that come against Jerusalem. And I will pour out upon the house of David and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem the spirit of grace and of prayers. LXX: And it shall be on that day: I will seek to take away all the nations that come against Jerusalem; and I will pour out upon the house of David and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem the spirit of grace and of mercy. On that day when the Lord shall shield the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the Lord will seek to crush all the nations that come against Jerusalem. But he will crush them not unto perdition but unto amendment, so that they may cease to wage war against Jerusalem and begin to be of Jerusalem. For if he created all things out of nothing, he did not make them in order to destroy what he had created, but so that the things that were created might be saved by his mercy. Hence also in the Wisdom that bears the name of Solomon — if, however, anyone is pleased to accept the book — we find it written: He created all things that they might be, and the generations of the world are healthful; and there shall be no deadly poison in them. For just as the Lord came to seek what had perished, and saved the human race, so too he destroyed the nations in this respect, that they were hostile nations. In short, there follows: I will pour out upon the house of David and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem the spirit of grace and of mercy. Of this grace the apostle Paul also writes: The charity of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us. And in the above-mentioned volume it is contained: The things that are in heaven, who has searched out? Except that you gave wisdom and sent your Holy Spirit from on high; and so the paths of those who dwell on earth were corrected; and men were taught the things that please you. And in Isaiah the Lord says: I have given my spirit upon you. And again Scripture records of the same: I have given my Spirit upon him. But the word of pouring out shows the sense of largess, as is plain in what we have said: The charity of God has been poured out in your hearts. And in another place, in the person of God: I will pour out of my spirit upon all flesh. And what the spirit of grace is, the same Apostle tells the Hebrews: How much worse punishments do you think he deserves who has trampled upon the Son of God, and has counted polluted the blood of the covenant in which he was sanctified, and has done outrage to the spirit of grace. Hence also the graces of the Holy Spirit are said to be diverse. And the greeting of the Apostle: Grace, he says, and peace be multiplied to you, so that after he has forgiven us our sins, then peace may follow through mercy. The Jews record these things as already done in part, and to come more fully at the consummation of the world. But we understand and prove that they are fulfilled daily since the coming of Christ.

And they shall look upon me whom they pierced; and they shall mourn him with a mourning as over an only-begotten, and they shall grieve over him, as one is wont to grieve at the death of a firstborn. LXX: And they shall look upon me, because they mocked; and they shall mourn over him a mourning, as over one most dear. And they shall grieve with grief, as over a firstborn. The Hebrew letters daleth and res, that is, d and r, are alike and are distinguished only by a small stroke. From this it comes about that those who read the same word differently translate it now one way, now another. For the sake of understanding let us give one example: And Samuel, it says, was clothed in an ephod bad, that is, in a linen garment; for linen is called bad; hence also linens are called baddim. In place of this Hebrew and Latin expression some wrongly read ephod bar; for bar means either a son, or a sheaf of grain, or chosen, or οὖλος, that is, curly. What happened there by an error of interpretation we detect has happened here too. For if dacaru is read, it is taken as ἐξεκέντησαν, that is, they pricked or they pierced; but if, in the reverse order, with the letters exchanged, racadu, it is understood as ὠρχήσαντο, that is, they danced. And the error was born from the likeness of the letters. But John the evangelist, who drew wisdom from the breast of the Lord, a Hebrew of the Hebrews whom the Savior loved most, did not greatly care what the Greek letters contained, but rendered word from word, as he had read it in the Hebrew, and said that it was fulfilled at the time of the Lord's passion. But if anyone does not accept this, let him bring evidence from what passage of the holy Scriptures John brought forth these words, and when he has not found it, he will be forced against his will to accept the truth. And the Jews shall mourn as over an only-begotten and a firstborn, the same thing being signified in the Lord Savior, both only-begotten and firstborn. He is called Only-begotten on account of the property of his nature; Firstborn, according to the Apostle, of those who rise from the dead. For only-begotten the Seventy translated most dear, of whom we read in the Gospel: This is my most dear Son, in whom I am well pleased. Then they shall grieve that he was crucified by them, when they see him reigning in glory. The word κατωρχήσαντο (they danced over), among the Greeks, is compounded not from mockery but from dancing, namely because they danced against the Lord as if in sport, when, mocking and laughing, they said: Vah, you who destroy the temple and in three days build it, save yourself, coming down from the cross. These and other things they spoke as they mocked, dancing in a kind of frenzied leaping.

And the land shall mourn, families and families apart; the families of the house of David apart, and the women — or wives —, of them apart — for the Hebrew word nese, that is, γυναῖκες, signifies both —, the families of the house of Nathan apart, and their women apart, the families of the house of Levi apart and their women apart, the families of Shimei apart and their women apart. All the remaining families, families and families apart and their women apart. LXX: On that day there shall be a great mourning in Jerusalem, like the mourning for the pomegranate that is cut down in the plain, and the land shall mourn by tribes and tribes; the tribe of David apart and their women apart, the tribe of the house of Judah apart and their women apart, the tribe of the house of Nathan apart and their women apart, the tribe of the house of Levi apart and their women apart, the tribe of Simeon apart and their women apart; all the remaining tribes apart and their women apart. Adadremmon, for which the LXX translated ῥοῶνος (of the pomegranate grove), is a city near Jezreel which was once called by this name, and today is called Maximianopolis, in the plain of Megiddo, in which Josiah, the just king, was wounded by the Pharaoh surnamed Necho; over him Jeremiah wrote Lamentations, which are read in the Church, and that he wrote them the book of Chronicles testifies. Just as, therefore, at that time, after the sinful kings, all the hope of the people was in Josiah, and when he was slain a great mourning was stirred up in the city, as we read in the Hebrew: The spirit of our mouth, Christ the Lord, was taken in our sins, to whom we said: In your shadow we shall live among the nations. Although others, according to the spiritual understanding, refer this to the Lord Jesus; so, when the Savior has been crucified, the mourning shall be renewed in Jerusalem, as once it was in the city of Adadremmon, in the plain of Megiddo. And what follows: The families and families shall mourn, or tribes and tribes, apart: the families of the house of David apart, and the wives or the women of them apart; this signifies that in a time of tribulation and grief we ought not to be given over to marriage and the work of wedlock. Hence also in Joel, when the captivity was near, it is said to the Jews: Let the bridegroom go forth from his chamber, and the bride from her bridal room. And when the flood was impending, Noah is commanded: Enter into the ark, you and your sons, and your wife, and the wives of your sons. And afterwards, when the flood was over, it is said to him: Go out, you and your wife, and your sons, and their wives, so that those who had been separated in the ark while the peril hung over them, once restored to the world, might devote themselves to begetting and to children. And this is done not only in a time of distress but also in a time of prayer, when we wish to entreat the Lord, as the Apostle says to the Corinthians: Do not defraud one another, except perhaps by consent for a time, that you may give yourselves to prayer. Therefore now too the tribe of the house of David, and the tribe of the house of Nathan, and the tribe of the house of Levi, and the tribe of the house of Shimei are separated from their wives, so that they may mourn the only-begotten and firstborn Lord Jesus, of whom it had been said: His blood be upon us and upon our children. In David the royal tribe is understood, that is, Judah. In Nathan the prophetic order is described. Levi refers to the priests, from whom the priesthood arose. In Shimei the teachers are understood, for from this tribe the hosts of the masters sprang up. He was silent about the remaining tribes, which have no privilege of dignity. But in saying: All the remaining tribes, tribes and tribes apart, and their wives apart, he includes them all without a name. Let us say also that according to the LXX it is called ῥοῶν, not a single tree of pomegranates, that is, of the Punic apple, but a place planted with these trees, of which, according to the spiritual understanding, the bridegroom says in the Song of Songs: I went down to see in the produce of the torrent whether the vine had flowered, whether the pomegranates had flowered. For the Savior went down to the torrent of this world and its turbid waters, from which Elijah too, in a type of him, is described as having drunk, so that after the flowers of the vine and of the pomegranate he might receive the fruit of both, and, making his Church drunk, might hear from her: You shall give me to drink of the perfumer's wine, of the wine of my pomegranates. A drink of this kind not only drives away the heats of the stomach, but is also said to heal a corrupted belly and to benefit the other inner organs. For nothing is more beautiful than this fruit; in its redness it signifies the modesty of the Church, in the order of its seeds the ranks and members of the whole body distributed through their several offices. When the Savior has not found fruit in a vine and pomegranates of this kind, he will say: Every branch that does not bear fruit the Father will take away; and every one that bears fruit he will prune, that it may bring more fruit. And in another place John the Baptist cries out: Already the axe is laid to the roots of the trees. Every tree that does not make good fruit shall be cut down and cast into the fire. At the cutting down of the vines or of the pomegranates, when on the day of judgment all titles of dignity shall be laid down and that which is written shall have been fulfilled: Behold the man and his works; and when the chaff has been separated from the wheat, there shall be a great mourning not in another place but in Jerusalem. For the blow and the judgment shall begin from the saints, and kings and priests and prophets and teachers shall beat their breasts with their hands when they see that the most beautiful pomegranates have been cut down, and him whom they had pierced reigning in the majesty of the Father and his own.

LXX: And it shall be, if a man prophesies any more, his father and his mother, who begot him, shall say to him: You shall not live, because you have spoken a lie in the name of the Lord; and his father and his mother who begot him shall fetter him when he prophesies. And it shall be on that day, the prophets shall be confounded, each one by his vision when he has prophesied, nor shall they be covered with a cloak of sackcloth in order to lie. But he shall say: I am not a prophet, I am a man, a tiller of the ground, for Adam has been my example from my youth. And it shall be said to him: What are these wounds in the middle of your hands? And he shall say: With these I was wounded in the house of those who loved me. LXX: And it shall be on that day, the prophets shall be confounded, each one by his vision when he has prophesied, and they shall put on a skin of haircloth because they have lied; and he shall say: I am not a prophet, for a man begot me from my youth. And I will say to him: What are these wounds in the middle of your hands? And he shall say: Those with which I was struck in the house of the one who loved me. Whoever has attempted to prophesy, and has been condemned by the judgment of his parents, and whose prophecy has been proved false by the contrary outcome of events, shall be confounded by his vision, and shall no longer be covered with haircloth in order to lie. For this was the habit of the prophets: that when they were calling the people to repentance, they put on haircloth. Hence Isaiah too is commanded to take the sackcloth from his loins and to walk naked; for it was by no means a time of repentance, but of impending captivity. Therefore this false prophet too will by no means take up the prophetic habit, lest under another's clothing and covering he deceive all the simple, but rather he will cleave the earth with the ploughshare and, given over to farming, will prove himself a man, and subject to the sentence of God, who spoke to Adam: Cursed is the ground in your work; in toil you shall eat of it all the days of your life; thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you, and you shall eat the plants of the earth; and in the sweat of your face you shall eat bread. And when he has shown that he was born for this, to eat his bread in the sweat of his face, another will question him and say: What do these wounds mean, and these gashes that are in the middle of your hands? And the sense is: Why do you cling to the gibbet? Why are your hands pierced with nails? What have you committed, that you should be subjected to this penalty and torment? And he will answer and say: These gashes and these wounds I received, condemned by the judgment of my parents and of those who did not hate me but loved me. And truth will prevail so far, once falsehood has been put to flight, that even he who has been punished for his own fault confesses that he has suffered justly. What is written, Nor shall they be covered with a cloak of sackcloth in order to lie, the Hebrews expound thus: and they shall not be filled with the demonic spirit, those whom we read of in Isaiah as τριχιῶντας, that is, hairy ones, lest on this occasion of a feigned habit they belie the utterances of God within them, or deny God. For Chaesu can be interpreted both 'they shall lie' and 'they shall deny'.

13:7-9

O sword, be roused against my shepherd and against the man who cleaves to me, says the Lord of hosts. Strike the shepherd and the sheep shall be scattered, and I will turn my hand to the little ones. And there shall be in all the land, says the Lord: two parts in it shall be destroyed and shall fail, and the third part shall be left in it. And I will lead the third part through fire, and I will burn them as silver is burned, and I will test them as gold is tested. He shall call on my name and I will hear him; I will say: You are my people; and he shall say: The Lord is my God. LXX: O sword, arise against my shepherd and against the man his fellow citizen, says the Lord Almighty. Strike the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered, and I will bring my hand upon the shepherds. And it shall be on that day, says the Lord, two parts shall perish and shall fail, and the third part shall be left in it; and I will lead the third part across through fire, and I will burn them as silver is burned and I will test them as gold is tested. He shall invoke my name, and I will hear him and will say: This is my people; and he shall say: You are the Lord my God. For sword (framea), which the LXX translated ῥομφαίαν, we find in the Hebrew areb, and in Aquila and Symmachus μάχαιραν, that is, sword or point. And for what we have rendered, Against the man who cleaves to me, that is, προσκεκολλημένον μοι, Aquila translated: Against the man of my tribe, that is, σύμφυλόν μοι. Symmachus: Against the man of my people, which in Hebrew is said amithi. The LXX: Against the man his fellow citizen. Theodotion: Against the man his neighbor; they read vav at the end of the word instead of yod, letters which differ from each other only in size; and if yod is read it means 'my', if vav, 'his'. But I am amazed that some wish to thin out with allegorical interpretations this prophecy — which the evangelist Matthew referred to the Lord and Savior after the disciples fled at his passion, and says that it was then fulfilled —, and, while they want to seem to know more than the rest, do not hold to the rule of truth. For the evangelist Matthew relates: Then Jesus says to them: You will all take offense at me this night. For it is written: I will strike the shepherd and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered. And again: But all this was done that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled. Then all the disciples, leaving him, fled. Nor should it be thought that the testimony was taken from another place because in the Gospel God says that the shepherd was struck by himself, while in the present place we read that the sword and the point are commanded: Strike the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered. This is the sword, and this is the broadsword and the sword (framea), of which the Lord also speaks to the Father in Psalm 21 (22): Deliver my soul from the sword and my only one from the hand of the dog. Of this broadsword it is also written in Amos the prophet: All the sinners of the earth shall die by the sword. This cannot stand at all according to the letter, for many sinners perish by shipwreck, others by poison; these are choked by water, those a fire consumes. But by this sword and this broadsword all sinners perish, and the Ethiopians of black color too, of whom Zephaniah testifies with his sacred mouth, saying: But you also, Ethiopians, shall be slain by my sword. After the good shepherd, who laid down his life for his sheep, who spoke to the Father: Him whom you struck they have persecuted, and they have added to the pain of my wounds, was struck by the Father's will, and the man who cleaves to God, who says: I am in the Father, and the Father in me, hung on the gibbet and said: Father, into your hands I commend my spirit, at once the sheep were scattered, the whole multitude of believers in Christ. And the Lord turned his hand, as is read in the LXX, to the shepherds, whom many wrongly interpret as the princes of the Jews. But as it is written in the Hebrew, to the little ones, to whom the Lord had said in the Gospel: Fear not, little flock, and in Isaiah: Behold I and the children, or the little ones whom the Lord has given me. And two parts were made in all the land, of the Jews and of the Gentiles, who perished alike and failed. For at his passion the voice of the psalmist was fulfilled, saying: Save me, O Lord, for the holy one has failed, and: All have turned aside, together they have become useless; there is none who does good, there is not even one. As the Gentiles and the Jews were perishing, a third part in the world, that is, the third people, of Christians, suddenly sprang up. And he says beautifully: The third part shall be left in it, that is, in the land, because of the Jews and the Gentiles those alone who confessed the Lord were kept for life and for dwelling in the land. And that this third part too might not be soft, and its confession careless, it is led through fire and tested like silver and gold, the fire which the Lord too longs to blaze in believers and Paul desires to be fervent. Hence the apostles too, baptized in the Spirit and in the fire of the Lord, speak in the psalm: For you have tested us, O God; you have tried us with fire as silver is tried. And a little after: We passed through fire and water, and you brought us out into refreshment. And in another place it is said to the believer: If you pass through fire, the flame shall not burn you, because I am with you. And the reason the fire does not consume and the flame does not devour is this: For the voice of the Lord cuts through the flame of fire. When the little ones, over whom the Lord turned his hand, have been tested in this way, and through their calling the whole throng of the nations has believed, then the people of believers will call Christ by his name, and when he says: You are my people, the people will answer: You are the Lord my God. The Jews refer these things to Christ and contend that they will come to pass in the last time, but the difference between us and them is this: we say that they have already been fulfilled, they speak of them as yet to be fulfilled.

Behold, the days of the Lord shall come, and your spoils shall be divided in the midst of you. And I will gather all the nations to Jerusalem for battle, and the city shall be taken, and the houses shall be laid waste, and the women shall be violated, and half of the city shall go forth into captivity, and the rest of the people shall not be taken away from the city. LXX: Behold, the days of the Lord come, and your spoils shall be divided in you, and I will gather all the nations against Jerusalem, to make war; and the city shall be taken, and the houses shall be plundered and the women shall be defiled, and half of the city shall go forth into captivity; but the rest of my people shall not perish from the city. The days which the Lord threatens shall come, so that the spoils of Jerusalem are divided in the midst of it, and the other things that the prophetic word includes, are those of which we also read in Isaiah: The day of the Lord shall come, incurable, of fury and of wrath, to lay the whole world waste, and to take away the sinners from it. But how great will the distress be, that its spoils are divided in the midst of it? It often happens that what has been plundered in a city by a sudden assault is divided outside, in the field or in the wilderness, lest perhaps an enemy come upon them. But here so great a weight of evils will press down that what has been plundered is divided in the middle of the city, so secure is the victory. And not only shall Jerusalem be taken, when all the nations have been roused against it in battle, but also the houses of those who dwell in Jerusalem shall be laid waste, and the women shall be violated, to the grief of their masters and husbands, who will be able to prevent neither the pillaging of their houses nor the rape of their wives by the enemy, according to what we read elsewhere: Those who are gathered in the midst of you shall fall by the sword, and they shall dash your sons to pieces in your sight, and they shall plunder your houses, and they shall have your wives; nothing crueller and nothing more wretched than this can be found, that from fear of their own death they dare to defend neither the safety of their sons nor the chastity of their wives. Amos the prophet too says this very thing, threatening Amaziah the impious priest: Your wife shall play the harlot in the city, and your sons and daughters shall fall by the sword, and your ground shall be measured with a line. And all these things shall befall the people of the Jews: For the nations raged, and the peoples meditated vain things. The kings of the earth stood by and the princes came together as one against the Lord and against his Christ. And he laughed at them and derided them, and troubled them in his fury, so much so that the Apostle too, seeing that the years which had been given them for repentance were now completed, and that those who killed the Lord and persecuted the prophets and the apostles nevertheless persisted in denial, said: Wrath has come upon them to the end. Josephus, who wrote the Jewish history, relates most fully that they endured all these things, and things much greater than we read in the prophets; so too Cornelius Tacitus, who set down in thirty volumes the lives of the Caesars after Augustus up to the death of Domitian. And as for what we read in the Hebrew: And Judah shall fight against Jerusalem, for which the LXX translated: And Judah shall be made ready in Jerusalem, let us take it both ways: that Judah, who once confessed the name of the Lord and, under compulsion in the persecutions, persecuted the people of Christ, may himself also be turned to joy. Or at any rate Judah, every one who confesses and is faithful, does not fight against Jerusalem, but makes himself ready in Jerusalem to fight against the adversaries. And this Judah shall gather the riches of all the nations round about, gold, silver and garments exceedingly many. We have often said that gold and silver are to be understood of sense and of speech; hence we ought to take the garments likewise, with which the Church of Christ is adorned, of which it is written: The queen stood at your right hand in gilded clothing, surrounded with variety. Clothed with these garments the throng of believers rejoices and says: I will exult in the Lord, for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation, and with the tunic of gladness. For the Lord had said to her: I clothed with both τριχάπτοις (fine-spun stuffs) and fine linen. By τριχάπτοις let us understand fine garments, which are so beautiful and delicate that they seem to resemble hairs. These garments the Church gathers, so as to have the wherewithal to clothe her people, to whom it was commanded by the true Preacher: At all times let your garments be white.

And so shall be the ruin of the horse, and of the mule and of the camel, and of the ass, and of all the beasts that shall be in those camps, like this ruin. LXX: And this shall be the ruin of the horses and of the mules and of the camels and of the asses, and of all the beasts that shall be in those camps, according to this ruin. These things too the Jews contend are to be fulfilled carnally under their ἠλειμμένῳ (anointed one). Great indeed is the strength of the Lord, that in the camps of the enemies the horses and mules, camels and asses, and all their beasts should fall, just as the men had fallen. A great triumph, a glorious victory, that brute animals should be overcome with God fighting! Let us say, therefore, according to the tropology we have begun, that all the evil things which formerly fought against the Church fall for this reason, that good things may suddenly rise up. In short, he who had formerly been a horse, neighing after his neighbor's wife, and was carried away, unrestrained, to lust and lay in the foulness of pleasures, when he has begun to follow chastity, it shall be said to him: Arise, let us go hence, and: Arise, come, my neighbor, and in the Apostle: Arise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten you. And when the horses that had fallen before have risen, and have offered the Lord soft backs to sit on, they shall say to him: Mount upon your horses, and your riding is salvation. In accordance with this sense let us take the mules, which are sterile and do not beget offspring, but frolic in pleasure, of whom the psalmist makes mention: Do not become like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding. As, then, those who are prone to lust are called horses, so we shall rightly call mules those who are virgins in the flesh, not in the spirit, who make themselves eunuchs not for the sake of the kingdom of heaven but for the sake of men's pleasure. When, therefore, these mules and these eunuchs have been turned to begetting offspring and have produced spiritual sons, they shall hear through Isaiah: And let not the eunuch say: I am a dry tree. For thus says the Lord to the eunuchs who keep my sabbaths and choose what I have willed and hold fast my covenant: I will give them in my house and within my wall a named place, and a name better than sons and daughters; I will give them an everlasting name which shall not fail. Such mules and she-mules the kings of Israel had, and above all David, who is referred to Christ. If we have understood who are the horses and mules that fall and rise again, let us pass on to the camels, an animal that chews the cud and does not divide the hoof, and let us say that the camels are all the sinners of the earth, who are weighed down by the heavy load of their sins, and seem to themselves to read the holy scriptures, but do not divide the hoof, chewing over the divine sayings and neglecting the things that are written. But more rightly can the people of the Jews be called a camel, which itself also meditates on the law of God, and chews it over and turns it in its breast, but does not divide the hoof so as to believe in the Father and in the Son; and it is unclean in this, that it by no means separates the letter from the spirit, the shadow from the truth, and it carries the burdens of the law and hears through the prophet: Woe, sinful nation, a people full of offences. Of a camel of this kind, which chews the cud and does not divide the hoof, it is said in Proverbs too, as though to a son: He who leaves off keeping his father's discipline shall meditate on evil sayings. After the camel let us pass on to the ass, which once was carried along unbridled, and was unclean, and had many masters, and slipped over precipices, so that, having fallen, it might suddenly rise again and carry the Lord and Savior, and enter holy Jerusalem, and the throng of little ones who believed might receive him in triumph. This ass the Savior is said to have tied to the vine and the vineyard, of which it is written in the psalms: You brought a vine out of Egypt; you cast out the nations, and planted it. To which the vinedresser himself speaks: I planted you a fruitful vine, wholly true. All the beasts and living creatures too, which the prophet includes under one name, are to be interpreted according to the nature of each, and this befits the Son of God more than the things which the people of the Jews presume in their foolish prophesying.

And all who are left of all the nations that came against Jerusalem shall go up from year to year to worship the king, the Lord of hosts, and to celebrate the feast of tabernacles. LXX: And it shall be, whoever are left of all the nations that came against Jerusalem shall go up every year to worship the king, the Lord Almighty, and to celebrate the feast of tabernacles. All, he says, who are left of the nations that came against Jerusalem shall go up every year to worship the king, the Lord of hosts, and to celebrate the feast of tabernacles. These things too the Jews, with empty hope, promise will come to pass in the kingdom of a thousand years. The origin of this solemnity is as follows. When the people of Israel had gone out of Egypt through the vast, terrible and wide wilderness, in which there was no house, farmstead, town or cave, they made themselves tabernacles and tents — which are now called papiliones from their likeness to a little bird —, in which they might stay with their wives and children, and take their food, and avoid the heat of the sun by day and by night the damp and the cold and the harm of the dew; and it was commanded that in the seventh month, on the fifteenth day of the month, the solemnity of tabernacles should be held: And when your son, he says, asks you tomorrow, saying: What do these tabernacles mean? You shall answer him: For a long time we sojourned in Egypt, out of which the Lord led us into the wilderness, and therefore we set up tabernacles, so that we may remember the benefits of God at all times, now that we have begun to dwell in cities. He commanded also that they make the tabernacles of the most beautiful wood, which the Jews call citron, and of the branches of palms and the boughs of the thickest tree, and of the willow, and of the poplar. We have laid the foundations of the history, so that from these we may pass to spiritual things. As long as we are advancing and in the race and the contest, we dwell in tabernacles, striving with our whole mind for this, that from tabernacles we may pass to a firm and stable dwelling, that is, the house of God. Hence the holy man also says in the psalm: Woe is me, that my sojourning is prolonged, and: I am a stranger and a pilgrim, as all my fathers were. This is the speech of one who is in Egypt and still set in the world. But he who goes out of Egypt, which in Hebrew is called mesraim and is interpreted tribulation, and enters the wilderness of the vices, takes up his journey and says in the psalm: I will pass over into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, as far as the house of God. For it is a wonderful thing to refuse to dwell with the Egyptians, and, Pharaoh having been drowned, to long to enter the land of promise. Hence he also says elsewhere: How beloved are your tabernacles, O Lord of powers. My soul longs and faints for the courts of the Lord, and a little after: Blessed are all who dwell in your house; for ever and ever they shall praise you. The voice, for, of exultation and salvation is in the tabernacles of the just. And in Proverbs we find it written: The houses of the just remain, and the tabernacles of those who act rightly shall stand. Of the houses he promises that they remain, of the tabernacles that they shall stand. The holy man also says in another place: One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, and see the delight of the Lord, and visit his temple. He who dwells in tabernacles of this kind, and hastens to go from the tabernacles to the courts, and from the courts to the house, and from the house to the temple of the Lord, ought to celebrate the solemnity of tabernacles with the most beautiful wood of wisdom, of which it is said in Proverbs: It is a tree of life to all who draw near to it, and to those who lean upon it, as upon the Lord of firmness. And with the branches of palms, in which the sign of victory and the reward of virtue are contained; and with the boughs of the thickest tree, which the Jews understand as the myrtle, because of the mortification of vices and lusts. Hence myrrh too is offered as a gift to the Lord and Savior by the magi. And with the branches, he says, of the willow and of the poplar, which some call one tree; and the very name of the wood, which in Greek is called ἀγνὸς, indicates chastity. Physicians, and those who have written on the natures of trees and herbs, say that if anyone drinks the flower of the willow, or of the poplar, mixed with water, all heat in him grows cold, and the vein of lust is dried up, and he can no longer beget children. Whoever has been sheltered by the branches of such trees, let him keep the solemnity of tabernacles, passing through the sixth month, which is referred to the world, and keeping in the seventh a spiritual sabbath, on the fifteenth day of that same month, when the moon of the night is at its fullest, and all its darkness is dispelled by clear light. These things we have said briefly, having long had in view the size of the books, so that we may pass on to what remains.

On that day what is upon the bridle of the horse shall be holy to the Lord; and the cauldrons in the house of the Lord shall be like bowls before the altar. And every cauldron in Jerusalem and in Judah shall be sanctified to the Lord of hosts. LXX: On that day what is upon the bridle of the horse shall be holy to the Lord Almighty; and the cauldrons that are in the house of the Lord shall be like bowls before the house of the altar. And every cauldron in Jerusalem and in Judah shall be holy to the Lord Almighty. The Hebrew word mesuloth Aquila and Theodotion interpreted as βυθὸν, that is, depth; Symmachus as περίπατον σύσκιον, that is, a shady walk. The Seventy alone translated χάλινον, that is, bridle, and we too have followed them in this place, so as not to seem to bring something new into a well-known question. When I asked a Hebrew what it meant, he told me that we ought not to read mesuloth but mesaloth, which means the trappings of horses and warlike adornment, and that apart from this place the word is found in no volume whatever of the holy scriptures; that a bridle, moreover, is called resen in the Hebrew tongue, and not mesuloth, which the LXX translated; and that the sense is: At the time of the perpetual solemnity and of the kingdom of Jerusalem, when all things are pacified and tranquil, there will be no need at all of cavalry, which is the strongest kind of fighting force, but all the adornment and beauty of the trappings is to be devoted to the worship of the Lord. So they may say. Let us refer the depth of the horses and their σύσκιον walk, shady or dark, to mystical knowledge, which David too, like a most excellent horse, boasted that he had, saying: The uncertain and hidden things of your wisdom you have made manifest to me. And the Apostle: O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God. How inscrutable are his judgments, and untraceable his ways. From this depth the prophet cried to the Lord, and he heard him. In these depths and darkness God set his hiding place; into this darkness and these divine mysteries Moses entered in the thick cloud of Mount Sinai, that he might see God; of them David too spoke in another psalm: The judgments of the Lord are a great abyss. These secrets and these mysteries are holy to the Lord, which John the evangelist knew, who dared to say what perhaps the angels did not know: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. And for that reason he is loved by the Lord, because he possessed a most excellent depth; and he had reclined upon the breast of Jesus, whence he had also drawn wisdom. But if we wish to take the bridle, as the LXX translated, as the word of God, let us understand by the bridle him who reins in from vices horses raging with lust, and mules barren and wanton, and restrains them, and does not allow them to rush headlong; of whom it is said: Do not become like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding. With bridle and bit you will bind fast the jaws of those who do not draw near to you. Of this bridle James also speaks: We put bridles into the mouths of horses, and we turn about their whole body, so, that is, that they may walk on a straight road and be able to offer the Lord backs gentle for sitting. Such a bridle and such a word, wrought with a variety of gold and silver, prepares wild horses for the Savior to sit upon, and makes them holy and properly consecrated to his worship. I have heard from someone a thing said with pious intent indeed, but ridiculous: that the nails of the Lord's cross, out of which Constantine Augustus made a bridle for his horse, are called the holy thing of the Lord. Whether this is to be taken so, I leave to the reader's prudence. Let us pass on to the cauldrons, which are to be in the house of the Lord like the bowls of the altar. For every cauldron in Jerusalem and in Judah shall be sanctified to the Lord Almighty. Let them love bronze cauldrons who loved the pots of Egypt and the meat, and the melons and the garlic, and the onions and the cucumbers. Let us turn the Jewish cauldrons, in which the flesh of the victims was cooked, into bowls of spices before the altar of the Lord, of which the bride says to the bridegroom: My kinsman has gone down into my garden, to the bowls of spices, to feed in the gardens and to gather lilies. The garden and paradise into which the bridegroom goes down to the bride is the reading of the holy scriptures, from which he plucks lilies and violets and roses and various spices, to fill the bowls of believing souls and to pour out libations to the Lord from them. When cauldrons of this kind have been turned into bowls of the Lord and can say: We are the good fragrance of Christ, and, instead of the rankness of meats, have begun to bear the various flowers of the virtues, then they will be in Jerusalem and in Judah sanctified to the Lord Almighty; of which we have said quite often that Jerusalem expresses vision of peace, and Judah one who confesses.
