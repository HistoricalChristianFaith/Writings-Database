Hebrew Questions on Genesis

Latin source: Jerome's Hebrew Questions on Genesis, in one book with a preface, notes on selected verses of Genesis 1–49 (written at Bethlehem in the early 390s), from P. de Lagarde's text as reprinted in Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 72 (Turnhout, 1959), pp. 1–56, verified against the page images and collated with Lagarde's own edition (Leipzig, 1868), with the edition's evident misprints corrected; its critical apparatus is not translated. Each note opens with the verse it treats and its lemma, which gives Genesis in the Old Latin, from the Septuagint, that Jerome's readers knew; all Scripture is translated as Jerome quotes it, from his own Latin. Italics mark the lemmata and the other Scripture he quotes, and a verse reference in square brackets marks a quotation inside a note. Headings give the chapter and verse of each lemma as numbered in the edition's margin. Greek is kept as printed, with an English gloss in square brackets where Jerome gives none, and Hebrew words keep Jerome's own transliteration. This English translation is released into the public domain.

Preface

I, who at the beginning of my books ought to set out the subject matter of the work that follows, am compelled first to answer slanders, suffering something like Terence, who used to give the prologues of his comedies to the stage in his own defense. For Luscius Lanuvinus, like our own Luscius, was pressing hard upon him, and charged the poet with being, as it were, a thief of the public treasury. The bard of Mantua too suffered this same thing from his rivals, so that, when he had translated certain verses of Homer word for word, he was called a plunderer of the ancients. To them he replied that it was a mark of great strength to wrench the club from the hand of Hercules. And even Tully, who stood on the citadel of Roman eloquence, the king of orators and the illuminator of the Latin tongue, is accused of extortion by the Greeks. No wonder, then, if unclean swine grunt against me, a paltry little man, and trample pearls under their feet, when envy has blazed up against most learned men, men who ought to have overcome ill will by their glory. But this befell them deservedly, whose eloquence thundered in the theaters, in the senate house, in the assembly, from the rostra: for strength out in the open always has rivals, and the lightnings strike the highest mountains: but me, removed far from cities, the forum, lawsuits and crowds, even so (as Quintilian says) envy has found out in my hiding. Hence I beseech the reader (if anyone yet shall read these things too, if anyone, captivated by love, shall read them) not to look for eloquence, not for the charm of orators, in the books of Hebrew Questions, which I have planned to write on the whole of holy Scripture, but rather to answer our enemies on our behalf himself, that pardon should be granted to a new work. For just as we, lowly and poor, neither have riches nor deign to accept them when offered, so let them too know that they cannot have the knowledge of the Scriptures. That is, the riches of Christ, together with the riches of the world. It will therefore be the business of our study either to refute the errors of those who entertain various suspicions about the Hebrew books, or to restore to their own authority the things that seem to teem with faults in the Latin and Greek copies, and also to explain, by the reasoning of the native tongue, the etymologies of things, of names and of regions which do not resonate in our speech. And so that the correction may be recognized more easily, we first set out the testimonies themselves as they stand among us. And from comparison with what follows we shall show what is in them either less, or more, or otherwise. Nor indeed do we convict the Seventy translators of errors, as the envious carp, nor do we think our labor a censure of them. For they were unwilling to disclose to Ptolemy, king of Alexandria, all the mystical things in the holy Scriptures, and above all those which promised the coming of Christ, lest the Jews should seem to worship a second god too: and that follower of Plato held them in high esteem for this very reason, that they were said to worship one God. But the evangelists too, and our Lord and Savior as well, and also Paul the apostle, bring forward many things as if from the Old Testament which are not found in our copies: on these we shall discourse more fully in their proper places. From this it is plain that those exemplars are the truer which agree with the authority of the New Testament; add to this that Josephus too, who sets out the story of the Seventy translators, reports that only the five books of Moses were translated by them, and these we too acknowledge to agree with the Hebrew more than the rest. But those too who arose afterwards as translators, I mean Aquila and Symmachus and Theodotion, have it far otherwise than we read. Lastly (and this can placate even our detractors), let foreign wares sail only to those who want them: let rustics not buy balsam, pepper and the fruit of palms. Of Adamantius, however, I say nothing, whose name (if one may compare small things with great) is more envied than my own, because, though in his homilies, which he speaks to the common people, he follows the common edition, in his tomes, that is, in his larger discussion, overcome by the Hebrew truth and surrounded by the ranks of his own, he sometimes seeks the aid of the foreign tongue. This one thing I say: that I should wish, together with the ill will his name bears, to have his knowledge of the Scriptures as well, caring not a straw for the images and shades of ghosts, whose nature is said to be to frighten little children and to chatter in dark corners.

1:1

In the beginning God made heaven and earth. Most people think, as is written also in the Dispute of Jason and Papiscus, and as Tertullian argues in his book against Praxeas, and as Hilary too affirms in his exposition of a certain psalm, that in the Hebrew it has in the Son God made heaven and earth: but the truth of the matter itself proves this to be false. For both the Seventy translators and Symmachus and Theodotion translated in the beginning, and in the Hebrew it is written bresith, which Aquila renders in the head, and not baben, which is called in the Son. It can therefore be taken of Christ according to the sense rather than according to the translation of the word: he is shown to be the founder of heaven and earth as much on the very front of Genesis, which is the head of all the books, as also in the beginning of John the evangelist. Hence in the Psalter too he says of himself in the head of the book it is written of me, that is, in the beginning of Genesis, and in the Gospel all things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. But this too should be known, that among the Hebrews this book is called bresith, for they have this custom, that they give volumes their names from their beginnings.

1:2

And the Spirit of God was borne over the waters. In place of what is written in our copies, was borne, in the Hebrew it has marahaefeth, which we can call 'was brooding' or 'was cherishing', after the likeness of a bird quickening its eggs with warmth. From this we understand that it is said not of the spirit of the world, as some suppose, but of the Holy Spirit, who himself also is called the life-giver of all things from the beginning. But if the life-giver, then consequently also the creator. And if the creator, then also God: for you will send forth he says your Spirit, and they will be created.

1:10

And the gatherings of the waters he called seas. It should be noted that every gathering of waters, whether they are salt or fresh, is called seas according to the idiom of the Hebrew language. In vain therefore does Porphyry slander the evangelists, alleging that, to work a miracle for the ignorant out of the Lord's having walked upon the sea, they called the lake of Gennesaret a sea, since every lake and gathering of waters is called seas.

2:2

And God finished on the sixth day his works which he had made. In place of the sixth day, in the Hebrew it has the seventh day. We shall therefore press hard on the Jews, who glory in the rest of the sabbath, with this: that already then, in the beginning, the sabbath was broken, since God works on the sabbath, completing his works on it and blessing that very day, because on it he completed all things.

2:8

And the Lord God planted a paradise in eden toward the east. In place of paradise it has garden, that is, gan. Further, eden means delights, for which Symmachus translated 'a flowering paradise'. And also what follows, toward the east, is written in the Hebrew mimizra, which Aquila put as ἀπὸ ἀρχῆθεν, and we can say 'from the outset', while Symmachus put ἐκ πρώτης and Theodotion ἐν πρώτοις, which itself too signifies not the east but the beginning. From this it is most plainly proved that before God made heaven and earth he had already founded paradise, just as we also read in the Hebrew: now the Lord God had planted a paradise in eden from the beginning.

2:11

The name of the one is Fison. They think that this is the river of India, the Ganges.

2:12

Where there is carbuncle and the green stone. In place of carbuncle and the green stone, others translated βδέλλιον [bdellium] and ὄνυχα [onyx].

2:15

And the Lord God took the man and placed him in the paradise of pleasure. In place of pleasure, in the Hebrew it has eden. So here the Seventy themselves have rendered eden as pleasure, while Symmachus, who a little before had translated 'a flowering paradise', has here put ἐν τῷ παραδείσῳ τῆς ἀκτῆς [in the paradise of the shore], which itself too sounds of loveliness and delights.

2:17

But on whatever day you eat of it, you shall die the death. Symmachus rendered it better, saying you shall be mortal.

2:21

And the Lord God sent ἔκστασιν upon Adam. In place of ecstasy, that is, a going-out of the mind, in the Hebrew it has thardema, which Aquila rendered καταφοράν and Symmachus κάρον, that is, a heavy and deep slumber.

2:21

Then there follows and he slept. This very word is used also of the sleep of Jonah as he snored.

2:23

This now is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called woman, because she was taken from man. In Greek and in Latin the sound does not seem to show why she is called mulier (woman) because she was taken from vir (man), but the ἐτυμολογία [etymology] is preserved in the Hebrew speech. For man is called his, and woman hissa. Rightly, then, from his the woman was called hissa. Hence Symmachus too finely wished to keep the ἐτυμολογίαν [etymology] in Greek as well, saying she shall be called ἀνδρίς, ὅτι ἀπὸ ἀνδρὸς ἐλήμφθη, which we can say in Latin: she shall be called virago, because she was taken from vir. Theodotion, however, suspected another etymology, saying she shall be called assumption (a taking-up), because she was taken from man. For hissa, according to a variation of accent, can also be understood as assumption.

3:1

Now the serpent was wiser than all the beasts upon the earth. In place of wise, in the Hebrew it has arom, which Aquila and Theodotion rendered πανοῦργον, that is, wicked and shifty. And so this word points to cunning and craftiness rather than wisdom.

3:8

And they heard the voice of the Lord God walking in paradise toward evening. In most of the Latins' copies, in place of what we have put here, toward evening, it has after midday: because we cannot translate the Greek expression τὸ δειλινὸν [the afternoon hour] word for word: in place of it, in the Hebrew is written barua haium, which Aquila rendered τῷ ἀνέμῳ τῆς ἡμέρας, that is, in the wind of the day, and Symmachus διὰ πνεύματος ἡμέρας, that is, through the breeze of the day, while Theodotion, more plainly, has ἐν τῷ πνεύματι πρὸς κατάψυξιν τῆς ἡμέρας, so as to show the refreshment of a breathing air once the midday heat was past.

3:14

Upon your breast and your belly you shall walk. The Seventy translators added belly: in the Hebrew, however, it has only breast, so as to lay bare the cunning and the crafty turns of his thoughts: namely, that all his steps were steps of wickedness and fraud.

But also in what follows, you shall eat earth, in place of earth there is written afar, which we can call 'ash' and 'dust'.

3:15

He shall watch your head, and you shall watch his heel. It has it better in the Hebrew: he shall crush your head, and you shall crush his heel. For our steps too are hindered by the snake, and the Lord shall crush Satan under our feet swiftly.

3:16

Multiplying I will multiply your sorrows and your groanings. In place of sorrow and groaning, in the Hebrew it has pains and conceptions.

And your turning shall be to your husband. In place of turning, Aquila translated 'fellowship', Symmachus 'appetite' or 'impulse'.

3:17

Cursed is the earth in your works. Works here signify not those of tilling the countryside, as most people think, but sins, as it has in the Hebrew. And Aquila does not disagree, saying cursed is the ground because of you, and Theodotion cursed is adama in your transgression.

3:20

And Adam called the name of his wife Life, because she is the mother of all the living. Why Eve was called Life he shows by this, that she is the mother of all the living. For Eve means life.

3:24

And he cast out Adam and made him dwell over against the paradise of pleasure. And he set the cherubim and the flaming blade which turns, to guard the way of the tree of life. The sense in the Hebrew is far other than is understood here. For it says and he cast out Adam (the Lord, without any doubt) and made the cherubim and the flaming sword dwell before the paradise of pleasure, that it might turn and guard the way of the tree of life. Not that he made Adam himself, whom God had cast out, dwell over against the paradise of pleasure. But that, when he had been cast out, he placed the cherubim and the flaming sword before the doors of paradise to guard the entrance of paradise, so that no one could enter.

4:1

And she conceived and bore Cain, and said: I have acquired (or, I have possessed) a man through God. Cain means acquisition or possession, that is, κτῆσις; hence, expressing its etymology, she says canithi, that is, I have possessed, a man through God.

4:4-5

And God looked upon Abel and upon his gifts, but upon Cain and upon his sacrifices he did not look. And Cain was exceedingly saddened. How could Cain know that God had accepted his brother's gifts and rejected his own, unless that rendering is true which Theodotion put: and the Lord set fire upon Abel and upon his sacrifice, but upon Cain and upon his sacrifice he did not set fire? That fire was wont to come from heaven to devour a sacrifice we read both at the dedication of the temple under Solomon and when Elijah built an altar on Mount Carmel.

4:6-7

And the Lord said to Cain: Why has your face fallen? If you offer rightly but do not divide rightly, have you not sinned? Be still: to you is its turning, and you shall rule over it. We are compelled by necessity to linger rather long over each point, since here too the sense in the Hebrew is far other than in the Seventy translators: for the Lord said to Cain: Why are you angry, and why has your face fallen? If you do well, will it not be forgiven you? And if you do not do well, your sin will sit before the doors, and to you is its fellowship: but rather you rule over it. What he says is this. Why are you angry, and, tormented by the spite of envy against your brother, do you cast your face down to the ground? If you do well, will not every offense of yours be forgiven you, or, as Theodotion says, it will be acceptable, that is, I will accept your gift as I accepted your brother's? But if you do ill, at once sin will sit before your entrance, and you will have such a doorkeeper for companion. But since you are of free will, I admonish you that sin should not rule over you, but you over sin. Now what caused the error in the Seventy translators is this: that sin, that is, hatath, is of the masculine gender in the Hebrew and of the feminine in the Greek, and those who translated it rendered it in the masculine gender, as it was in the Hebrew.

4:8

And Cain said to Abel his brother. There is understood: the things which the Lord had spoken. Superfluous, therefore, is what is found in the volume of the Samaritans and in ours: let us go over into the field.

4:15

Whoever kills Cain shall pay seven vengeances. In place of seven vengeances, Aquila rendered sevenfold, Symmachus the seventh, Theodotion through a hebdomad: on this passage there is extant our letter to Bishop Damasus.

4:16

And he dwelt in the land of Naid. What the Seventy translated Naid is called Nod in the Hebrew, and it means σαλευόμενος, that is, unstable and wavering and of uncertain abode. It is not, therefore, a land of Naid, as the common run of our people think; rather the sentence of God is fulfilled, that he wandered about hither and thither, a vagrant and a fugitive.

4:25

And she called his name Seth: For God has raised up for me another seed in place of Abel, whom Cain killed. Seth is properly called θέσις, that is, placing. Because, then, God placed him in place of Abel, he is therefore called Seth, that is, placing. Then Aquila: and she called he says his name Seth, saying: For the Lord has placed for me a second seed.

4:26

And he called his name Enos: he hoped to call upon the name of the Lord God. Just as Adam means man, so Enos too, according to the variety of the Hebrew language, is called man or male. And fittingly, since he bore this name, it is written of him then was the beginning of calling upon the name of the Lord: although most of the Hebrews think otherwise, that it was then that idols were first fashioned in the name of the Lord and in his likeness.

5:2

Male and female he made them, and he blessed them, and he called their name Adam, that is, man. And the name of man fits the male as much as the female.

5:3

And Adam lived 220 years, and begot after his image and likeness, and called his name Seth. It should be known that up to the flood, wherever in our copies someone is said to have begotten at 200 years and something over, in the Hebrew it has 100 years and the rest that follow.

5:4

And the days of Adam, after he begot Seth, were 700 years. Because he had gone astray in the 200, he consequently put 700 here, whereas in the Hebrew it has here 800, and above, 100.

5:25-27

And Methuselah lived 167 years and begot Lamech. And Methuselah lived, after he begot Lamech, 802 years, and begot sons and daughters. And all the days of Methuselah which he lived were 969 years, and he died. It is a famous question, and one aired in the disputation of all the churches, that by a careful reckoning Methuselah is reported to have lived fourteen years after the flood. For when Methuselah was 167 years old, he begot Lamech. Again, Lamech, when he was 188 years old, begot Noah. And up to the day of Noah's birth the years of Methuselah's life come to 355. But the flood took place in the six hundredth year of Noah's life. And by this, the reckoning being made part by part, the flood is shown to have been in Methuselah's 955th year. But since he was said above to have lived 969 years, no one doubts that he lived fourteen years after the flood; and how then is it true that only eight souls were saved in the ark? It remains, therefore, that, as in very many places, so in this one too there is an error in the number. For indeed both in the Hebrew books and in those of the Samaritans I have found it written thus: and Methuselah lived 187 years and begot Lamech. And Methuselah lived, after he begot Lamech, 782 years. And he begot sons and daughters. And all the days of Methuselah were 969 years, and he died. And Lamech lived 182 years and begot Noah. From the day of Methuselah's birth, then, to the day of Noah's birth there are 369 years: add to these Noah's 600 years, since the flood took place in the six hundredth year of his life: and so it comes about that Methuselah died in the 969th year of his life, in the year in which the flood began.

5:29

And he called his name Noah, saying: This one will make us rest from our works. Noah means rest. Because, then, under him all the former works came to rest through the flood, he was called rest.

6:2

But the sons of God, seeing the daughters of men, that they were good. The Hebrew word eloim is of common number: for both God and gods are alike so called; on which account Aquila ventured to say, in the plural number, sons of gods, understanding by gods the saints or angels. For God stood in the synagogue of gods; and in the midst he judges between gods. Hence Symmachus too, following a sense of this kind, says the sons of the powerful, seeing the daughters of men and so on.

6:3

And the Lord God said: My spirit shall not remain in these men for ever, because they are flesh. In the Hebrew it is written my spirit shall not judge these men for evermore, since they are flesh: that is, because the condition in man is frail, I will not keep them for eternal torments, but here I will pay them back what they deserve. Therefore it bespeaks not the severity of God, as is read in our copies, but his clemency, while the sinner is visited here for his crime. Hence God also, in anger, speaks to certain people: I will not visit their daughters when they have committed fornication, nor their brides when they have committed adultery. And in another place: I will visit their iniquities with a rod and their sins with scourges; but my mercy I will not take away from them.

Furthermore, lest he should seem cruel in this, that he had not given sinners room for repentance, he added [Gen 6:3] but their days shall be a hundred and twenty years, that is, they will have a hundred and twenty years for doing repentance. So it is not human life, as many mistakenly think, that was contracted to a hundred and twenty years, but a hundred and twenty years were given to that generation for repentance; for indeed we find that after the flood Abraham lived a hundred and seventy-five years, and the others more than two hundred and three hundred years. But because they scorned to do repentance, God would not wait for the decreed time; instead, cutting off a span of twenty years, he brought on the flood in the hundredth year of the time appointed for doing repentance.

6:4

Now there were giants on the earth in those days; and after this, as the sons of God went in to the daughters of men, they also bore to them. Those were the giants from of old, persons of renown. In the Hebrew it has thus: the falling ones were on the earth in those days, that is, nifilim. And after this, as the sons of gods went in to the daughters of men, they also bore to them: these were the mighty from the beginning, men of renown. For falling ones or giants Symmachus rendered violent ones. And the name of falling ones suits both the angels and the children of the saints.

6:9

Noah, a just and perfect man in his generation, pleased God. Pointedly he says in his generation, to show that he was just not according to consummate justice, but according to the justice of his own generation. And this is what is said in the Hebrew: Noah, a just man, was perfect in his generations: Noah walked with God: that is, he followed in his footsteps.

6:14

Make yourself an ark of squared timbers. For squared timbers we read bitumened in the Hebrew.

6:16

Gathering, you shall make the ark, and in a cubit you shall finish it above. In place of what stands as gathering, you shall make the ark, in the Hebrew it has you shall make a midday for the ark, which Symmachus rendered more plainly, saying διαφανές, that is, something translucent, you shall make for the ark, meaning a window to be understood.

8:1-2

And the water rested, and the fountains of the abyss and the cataracts of heaven were uncovered. For uncovered fountains all the translators rendered closed and stopped up. And for what follows, [Gen 8:3] the water ceased upon the earth and so on, it is written and the waters returned from the earth, going and returning. Note that, according to Ecclesiastes, all the waters and torrents return through hidden veins to their mother the abyss.

8:6-7

After forty days Noah opened the door of the ark which he had made, and sent out the raven, and having gone out it did not return to him until the water was dried from the earth. For door, window is written in the Hebrew. And of the raven it is said otherwise: he sent out the raven, and it went out, going forth and returning, until the waters were dried from the earth.

9:18

And the sons of Noah who went out of the ark were Shem, Cham and Japheth. Frequently the Seventy translators, unable to turn the letter heth, which sounds a double aspiration, into the Greek language, added the Greek letter chi, to teach us that in words of this kind we ought to aspirate: hence in the present clause too they rendered Cham in place of what is Ham, from whom Egypt also is called Ham in the language of the Egyptians to this day.

9:27

May God enlarge Japheth, and may he dwell in the tents of Shem. From Shem are born the Hebrews, from Japheth the people of the Gentiles. Since, then, the multitude of believers is broad, it is from breadth, which is called Japheth, that this breadth received its name. But when he says and may he dwell in the tents of Shem, it is prophesied of us, who, Israel having been cast out, are engaged in the learning and knowledge of the Scriptures.

9:29

And all the days of Noah came to nine hundred and fifty years. See: after the flood Noah lived three hundred and fifty years. From this it is plain that the hundred and twenty years were, as we said above, given to that generation for repentance, and not fixed for the life of mortals.

10:2

The sons of Japheth: Gomer and Magog and Madai and Javan and Thubal and Mosoch and Thiras. To Japheth the son of Noah were born seven sons, who possessed the land in Asia from Amanus and Taurus, the mountains of Syria Coele and Cilicia, as far as the river Tanais, and in Europe as far as Gadira, leaving their names to places and peoples; of these very many were afterwards changed, the rest remain as they were. Now Gomer are the Galatians, Magog the Scythians, Madai the Medes, Javan the Ionians, who are also the Greeks (hence too the Ionian Sea), Thubal the Iberians, who are also the Spaniards, from whom come the Celtiberians, though some suppose them to be the Italians; Mosoch the Cappadocians, whence also a city among them is called Mazaca to this day (the Seventy translators, however, take Captorim to be the Cappadocians); Thiras the Thracians, whose name has not been much changed. I know that someone has referred Gog and Magog, both in the present passage and in Ezekiel, to the history of the Goths who have lately been roaming in our land: whether this is true is shown by the outcome of the battle itself. And certainly all the learned men of the past were accustomed to call the Goths Getae rather than Gog and Magog. These seven peoples, then, which I have mentioned as coming from the stock of Japheth, dwell toward the region of the north.

10:3

The sons of Gomer: Ascenez and Rifath and Thogorma. Ascenez the Greeks call the Regini, Rifath the Paphlagonians, Thogorma the Phrygians.

10:4-5

The sons of Javan: Elisa and Tharsis, Cethim and Dodanim. From these the islands of the nations were divided in their lands, each according to his tongue and his kindred and his nation. From the Ionians, that is, from the Greeks, are born the Eliseans, who are called Aeolians: hence also the fifth tongue of Greece is called Aeolic, which they call πέμπτην διάλεκτον. Tharsis Josephus takes to be the Cilicians, saying that θ, the letter of aspiration, was corrupted by later generations into tau: hence also their metropolis is called the city of Tarsus, made glorious by the apostle Paul. Cethim are the Citians, from whom even today a city of Cyprus is named Citium. Dodanim are the Rhodians: for so the Seventy translators rendered it. Let us read the books of Varro on antiquities, and of Sinnius Capito, and the Greek Phlegon, and the other most learned men, and we shall see that almost all the islands, and the shores of the whole world, and the lands near the sea were occupied by Greek settlers, who (as we said above) possessed all the maritime places from the Amanus and Taurus mountains as far as the British ocean.

10:6

The sons of Cham: Chus and Mesraim and Fut and Canaan. Chus is called Ethiopia by the Hebrews to this day, Mesraim Egypt, Fut the Libyans, from whom also a river of Mauretania is called Fut up to the present, and all the region around it Futensian. Many writers, Greek as well as Latin, are witnesses of this. But why the ancient name of Libya has settled in only one part of the clime, while the rest of the land has been called Africa, is not a matter to discuss in this place or at this time. Canaan, furthermore, obtained the land which the Jews afterwards possessed, when the Canaanites had been cast out.

10:7

The sons of Chus: Saba and Evila, Sabatha, Rehema, Sabathaca. Saba, from whom come the Sabaeans, of whom Vergil says, to the Sabaeans alone belongs the frankincense rod; and elsewhere, and a hundred altars glow with Sabaean incense. Evila are the Gaetulians, in a part of more remote Africa, bordering on the desert. Sabatha, from whom come the Sabatheni, who are now named Astabari. Rehema and Sabathaca, however, have gradually lost their ancient names, and what names they now have in place of the old ones is unknown.

10:7

The sons of Rehema: Saba and Dadan. Here Saba is written with the letter sin, but above with samech, from whom we said the Sabaeans were named. So here Saba means Arabia. For in the seventy-first psalm, where we have the kings of the Arabs and of Saba shall offer gifts, in the Hebrew it is written the kings of Saba and Saba: the first name with sin, the second with samech. Dadan is a people of Ethiopia in the western region.

10:8

And Chus begot Nemrod. He began to be mighty on the earth. And a little after, [Gen 10:10] and there was he says as the head of his kingdom Babel and Arach and Achad and Chalanne in the land of Sennaar. Nemrod the son of Chus was the first among the people to seize an unaccustomed tyranny, and he reigned in Babylon, which was called Babel from the fact that there the tongues of those building the tower were confused. For Babel means confusion. And he reigned also in Arach, that is, in Edessa, and in Achad, which is now called Nisibis, and in Chalanne, which afterwards, its name being changed, was called Seleucia by King Seleucus, or at any rate the city which is now called Κτησιφῶν [Ctesiphon].

10:11

Out of that land went forth Assur and built Nineveh and the city Rooboth. From this land sprang the empire of the Assyrians, who, from the name of Ninus son of Belus, founded Ninus, a great city, which the Hebrews call Nineveh. To its ruin or its repentance the whole prophecy of Jonah pertains. But when he says Nineveh and the city Rooboth, let us not think that there are two cities; rather, since Rooboth means streets, it should be read thus: and he built Nineveh and the streets of the city.

10:13-14

And Mesraim begot Ludim and Anamim and Labaim and Nefthuim and Fetrosim and Chasloim, from whom came forth Philistiim and Captorim. Except for Labaim, from whom the Libyans were afterwards named, who had previously been called Futhei, and Chasloim, who were afterwards called Philistiim, whom we corruptly call Palestinians, the other six peoples are unknown to us, because, overthrown in the Ethiopian war, they have passed into oblivion of their former names. They possessed the land from Gaza as far as the furthest borders of Egypt.

10:15-18

And Canaan begot Sidon his first-born, and Chettaeus and Jebusaeus and Amorraeus and Gergesaeus and Evaeus and Aracaeus and Sinaeus and Aradius and Samaraeus and Amathaeus. Of Canaan the first-born was Sidon, from whom a city in Phoenicia is called Sidon. Then Aracaeus, who founded Arcae, a town situated opposite Tripolis at the roots of Lebanon. Not far from it there was another city named Sini, which afterwards, overthrown by the shifting fortune of wars, has kept for the place only its former name. The Aradii are those who possessed the island of Aradus, separated from the coast of Phoenicia by a narrow strait. The Samaraei are Emesa, a famous city of Syria Coele. Amath is called, up to our own time, by the Syrians as well as by the Hebrews just as it was called among the ancients; the Macedonians, who reigned in the east after Alexander, named it Epiphania. Some think that Antioch was so called. Others, though not truly, yet consoling their opinion with a name that resembles, as it were, the true one, think that the first station from Antioch for those going to Edessa is called Emas, and that it is the same place that was called Emath among the ancients.

10:19

And the boundary of the Canaanites was from Sidon until you come to Gerar, as far as Gaza, for those going to Sodom and Gomorrah and Adama and Seboim, as far as Lecem. Since the other cities, namely Sidon and Gerar and Sodom and Gomorrah and Adama and Seboim, are known to all, this alone seems worth noting: that Lece is the very place which is now called Callirhoe, where hot waters burst forth and flow down into the Dead Sea.

10:22

The sons of Shem: Elam and Assur and Arfaxad and Ludim and Aram. These hold the part of Asia from the river Euphrates as far as the Indian ocean. Now there is Elam, from whom come the Elamites, the princes of Persia. Of Assur it has already been said before that he founded the city Ninus. Arfaxad, from whom come the Chaldeans. Ludim, from whom Lydia. Aram, from whom come the Syrians, whose metropolis is Damascus.

10:23

The sons of Aram: Hus and Ul and Gether and Mes. Hus, the founder of Trachonitis and Damascus, held the rule between Palestine and Coele Syria; after him the Seventy translators in the book of Job, where in the Hebrew is written the land of Hus, rendered the region Ausitis, as if Husitis. Ul, from whom come the Armenians. Gether, from whom come the Acarnanians or Carians. Furthermore Mes, for whom the Seventy translators said Mosoch: these are now called Maeonians.

10:24-25

Arfaxad begot Sela, and Sela begot Heber. To Heber were born two sons: the name of the one was Phaleg, because in his days the earth was divided, and the name of his brother Jectan. Heber, from whom come the Hebrews, by a kind of prophecy gave his son the name Phaleg, which means division, from the fact that in his days the tongues were divided in Babylon.

10:26-29

Jectan begot Helmodath and Salef and Asermoth and Jare and Aduram and Uzal and Decla, Ebal, Abimahel, Seba, Ofir, Evila, Jobab. The later names of these peoples I have not been able to find; but up to the present, because they are far from us, either they are called as they were at first, or what names have been changed is unknown. They possessed, from the river Cophen, the whole region of India which is called Jeria.

11:28

And Haran died before his father in the land in which he was born, in the region of the Chaldeans. In place of what we read, in the region of the Chaldeans, in the Hebrew it has in ur Chesdim, that is, in the fire of the Chaldeans. Now the Hebrews relate, taking occasion from this, a tale of this kind: that Abraham was thrown into the fire because he would not worship the fire which the Chaldeans revere, and that, freed by God's help, he fled from the fire of idolatry (as in what follows it is written that Terah went out with his offspring from the region of the Chaldeans, in place of what stands in the Hebrew, from the burning of the Chaldeans); and that this is what is now said: Haran died before the sight of Terah his father, in the land of his birth, in the fire of the Chaldeans: namely, that, refusing to worship fire, he was consumed by fire. And afterwards the Lord speaks to Abraham: I am he who brought you out of the fire of the Chaldeans.

11:29

And Abram and Nahor took wives for themselves: the name of Abram's wife was Sarai, and the name of Nahor's wife Milcah, the daughter of Haran. Now the father of Milcah is himself the father of Iscah. Haran, the son of Terah, the brother of Abraham and Nahor, begot two daughters, Milcah and Sarai, surnamed Iscah, δυώνυμον [two-named]. Of these Nahor took Milcah as his wife, and Abraham Sarai; for marriages between paternal uncles and the daughters of their brothers had not yet been forbidden by law, since among the first human beings they were contracted even between brothers and sisters.

12:4

Now Abram was seventy-five years old when he went out of Harran. An insoluble question arises. For if Terah, the father of Abraham, while he was still in the Chaldean region, begot Abraham at seventy years, and afterwards died in Harran in the two hundred and fifth year of his age, how is Abraham now, going out of Harran after the death of Terah, recorded to have been seventy-five years old, when from the birth of Abraham to the death of his father there are shown to have been a hundred and thirty-five years? True, then, is that tradition of the Hebrews which we mentioned above: that Terah went out with his sons from the fire of the Chaldeans, and that Abraham, hemmed in by the Babylonian fire because he would not worship it, was freed by God's help; and that from that time the days of his life and the span of his age are reckoned to him, from when he confessed the Lord, spurning the idols of the Chaldeans. It may also be, however, since Scripture has left it uncertain, that Terah set out from Chaldea and came to Harran a few years before he met his death, or at any rate that he came to Harran immediately after the persecution and stayed there a longer time. If anyone, therefore, is opposed to this explanation, let him seek another solution; and then he will rightly reject what we have said.

12:9-10

And setting out, Abram went away into the desert; and a famine came upon the land. Both in the present place and in very many other places, in place of desert toward the south is written in the Hebrew. This, then, we ought to note.

12:15-16

And the princes of Pharaoh saw her and praised her to Pharaoh, and brought her into Pharaoh's house; and he did well by Abram on her account: and he had sheep and herds and asses and menservants and maidservants, mules and camels. Although it is not force but the will that stains the body of holy women, and Sarai can be excused because, in a time of famine, alone in foreign places, with her husband conniving, she could not resist the king, yet the foul necessity can also be excused in another way: that, according to the book of Esther, whichever of the women had pleased the king, among the ancients, was anointed for six months with oil of myrtle, and for six months was among various spices and the treatments of women, and then at last went in to the king. And so it may be that, after Sarai had pleased the king, while her going in to the king was being prepared over a year, Pharaoh both gave many gifts to Abraham and was afterwards struck by the Lord, she still remaining untouched by his bed.

13:1-4

And Abram went up out of Egypt, he and his wife, and all that was his, and Lot with him, into the desert. Now Abram was very rich in cattle and in silver and in gold; and he went away, whence he came, into the desert as far as Bethel. Fittingly, being set free from Egypt, he is said to have gone up. But what follows runs counter to this sense: how could he, on going out of Egypt, have been very rich? This is resolved by the Hebrew truth, in which it is written Abram was exceedingly heavy, that is, βαρὺς σφόδρα: for he was weighed down by the burden of Egypt. And although these seem to be riches of cattle, of gold and of silver, yet, if they are Egyptian, they are heavy to a holy man. Accordingly, he did not (as we read in the Seventy) go away, whence he had come, into the desert as far as Bethel, but, as it is written in the Hebrew, he went away on his journey through the south as far as Bethel. For this is why he set out from Egypt: not to enter the desert, which he had left behind together with Egypt, but to come through the south, which is opposite to the north, to the house of God, where his tent had been, in the midst of Bethel and Ai.

13:13

And the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners exceedingly in the sight of God. Here, in the Seventy translators, in the sight of God has been added superfluously: since the inhabitants of Sodom were wicked and sinners among men, whereas that man is said to be a sinner in the sight of God who can seem just among men, in the way that it is set down in praise of Zechariah and Elizabeth that they were both just in the sight of God, and in the Psalter it is said no one living shall be justified in your sight.

13:14-15

Lift up your eyes and look from the place where you now are, to the north and to the south and to the east and to the sea: for all the land which you see, to you will I give it and to your seed. He has set down the four regions of the world: the east and the west, the north and the south. And since this is read in all the Scriptures, let it suffice to have said it once here: the sea is always put for the west, because the region of Palestine is so situated that it has the sea on its western side.

14:2-3

And the king of Bale, this is Segor. All these came together at the salt valley, that is, the sea of salt. Bale in the Hebrew language is called κατάποσις, that is, a swallowing up. The Hebrews therefore relate that this same city is named Salissa in another place of the Scriptures, and again is called μοσχάδα τρισσεύουσαν, that is, a three-year-old heifer, because, of course, it was swallowed up by a third earthquake; and that from the time when Sodom and Gomorrah, Adama and Seboim were overthrown by the divine fire, it has been called the little one: since Segor means little, which in the Syrian language is called Zoara. And the valley of the salt-pits (as is written in this same book), in which there were formerly wells of bitumen, after the wrath of God and the rain of sulphur was turned into the Dead Sea, which by the Greeks is called λίμνη, ἀσφαλτῖτις (that is, the lake of bitumen).

14:5

And they cut down the giants in Astaroth Carnaim, and strong nations together with them, and the Ommaei in the city of Sauhe, before they came to Sodom. The four kings, setting out from Babylon, killed the giants, that is, the Rafaim, all the mighty men of Arabia, and the Zozim in Hom and the Emim in the city of Sauhe, which is so called to this day. Now Zozim and Emim mean the terrible and the dreadful: for which the Seventy, translating the sense rather than word for word, put very strong nations. Moreover, bahem, for which they said μετ' αὐτοῖς (that is, with them), they thought was written with he, led by the likeness of the letter, whereas it is written with heth. For bahem is written with three letters. If it has he in the middle, it means in them; but if heth (as in the present passage), it signifies a place, that is, in Hom.

14:7

And returning they came to the fountain of judgment, this is Cades. This is said by anticipation, because it was afterwards so called. And it signifies a place near Petra, which is named the fountain of judgment because there God judged the people.

14:7

And they struck all the country of the Amalekites, and the Amorite dwelling in Asason thamar. This is the town which is now called Engaddi, fertile in balsam and palms. Moreover, Asason thamar in our language sounds as the city of palms. For thamar means palm. And it should be known that, for what follows a little after, [Gen 14:8] and they drew up their battle line against them for war in the valley of the salt-pits, in the Hebrew it has in the valley of Sedim: which Aquila translates τῶν πρινεώνων [of the holm-oaks], Theodotion τῶν ἀκτῶν [of the shores], signifying pleasant groves.

14:11

And they took all the horsemen of Sodom and Gomorrah. For horsemen, in the Hebrew it has rachus, that is, substance.

14:13

And one who had fled told Abram the passer-over: and he was dwelling at the oak of Mambre the Amorite, the brother of Eschol and the brother of Aunan, who were sworn allies of Abram. For what we have put, the passer-over, in the Hebrew it is written the Hebrew: for by this word passer-over is expressed. And as to his saying at the oak of Mambre the Amorite, we read better in the Hebrew at the oak of Mamre the Amorite, the brother of Eschol and the brother (not of Aunan, as the Seventy translated, but) of Aner: so as to show that Mambre and Eschol and Aner were Amorites and brothers, allies of Abraham.

14:14

And he pursued them as far as Dan. To the town of Phoenicia which is now called Paneas. And Dan is one of the sources of the Jordan. For the other is called Ior, which means ῥεῖθρον, that is, a stream. So when the two sources, which are not far from each other, are joined into one stream, it is thereafter called the Jordan.

14:18-19

And Melchizedek, king of Salem, brought forth bread and wine, and he was priest of the most high God, and he blessed him. Since our little work is, once and for all, a collection either of Hebrew questions or of traditions, let us therefore set down what the Hebrews think about this man. They say that this man is Shem, the son of Noah, and by reckoning up the years of his life they show that he lived until Isaac, and that all the firstborn of Noah, until Aaron discharged the priesthood, were high priests. Moreover, the king of Salem means the king of Jerusalem, which was formerly called Salem. But the blessed Apostle, in writing to the Hebrews, recalling Melchizedek as without father and mother, refers him to Christ, and through Christ to the Church of the Gentiles (for all the glory of the head is referred to the members), because, having the foreskin, he blessed Abraham the circumcised, and in Abraham Levi, and through Levi Aaron, from whom the priesthood afterwards came: from which he wishes it to be gathered that the priesthood of the Church, which has the foreskin, blessed the circumcised priesthood of the synagogue. And as to his saying you are a priest for ever according to the order of Melchizedek, our mystery is signified in the word 'order': not at all through Aaron, by sacrificing irrational victims, but by the offering of bread and wine, that is, the body and blood of the Lord Jesus.

15:2-3

Lord God, what will you give me? And I go without children: and the son of Masec, my house-born maidservant, this is Damascus Eliezer. And Abram said: Behold, you have not given me seed: and the son of my house-born maidservant will be my heir. Where we have and the son of Masec, my house-born maidservant, will be my heir, in the Hebrew is written mesech bethi, which Aquila translated υἱὸς τοῦ ποτίζοντος οἴκου μου, that is, the son of him who gives drink to my house, and Theodotion ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἐπὶ τῆς οἰκίας μου, that is, and the son of him who is over my house. What he is saying is this: I shall die without children, and the son of my steward or bailiff, who manages everything and distributes the rations to the household, and is called Damascus Eliezer, he will be my heir. Moreover, Eliezer means my God is helper. By him, they say, Damascus was both founded and named.

15:7

I am God, who brought you out of the region of the Chaldeans. This is what we already said a little before: that in the Hebrew it has who brought you out of ur chesdim, that is, out of the fire of the Chaldeans.

15:10-11

And he laid them facing one another: but the birds he did not divide. And the fowls came down upon the carcasses and upon their divided parts: and Abram sat with them. The explanation of this mystery does not belong to the present little work: this much only we say, that for what we have set down, in the Hebrew it has and the fowls came down upon the carcasses: and Abram drove them away. For by his merit Israel was often set free from distress.

15:12

And at the setting of the sun an ἔκστασις fell upon Abram. For ecstasy, in the Hebrew we read tardema, that is, καταφορά, which above we rendered as deep sleep.

15:16

And in the fourth generation they shall return here. There is no doubt that this means those who were of the seed of Abraham. The question is asked how it is written in Exodus: and in the fifth generation the sons of Israel went out of the land of Egypt. On this passage we have published a small volume.

16:2

Behold, the Lord has shut me up, so that I do not bear: go in therefore to my handmaid, that I may have sons from her. Note carefully that the begetting of sons is written in the Hebrew as building: for there we read go in to my handmaid, if by some means I may be built up from her. And perhaps this is what is said in Exodus: God blessed the midwives, and they built houses for themselves.

16:7

And the angel of the Lord found her by a fountain of water in the desert, at the fountain on the way of Sur. Fittingly, the Egyptian woman was hastening to go by the way of Sur, which leads through the wilderness to Egypt.

16:11

And she called his name Ishmael, because God has heard my humiliation. Ishmael means the hearing of God.

16:12

He will be a rustic man: his hand upon all, and the hand of all upon him: and he will dwell over against the face of all his brothers. For rustic, it has written in the Hebrew fara, which means wild ass. And it signifies that his seed would dwell in the wilderness, that is, the Saracens, wandering and with no fixed dwellings, who raid all the nations on whose flank the desert borders, and are attacked by all.

17:3-5

And he spoke to him, saying: Behold, my testament is with you, and you shall be the father of a multitude of nations, and your name shall no longer be called Abram, but your name shall be Abraham, because I have made you father of many nations. It should be noted that wherever we read testament in the Greek, there in the Hebrew speech is covenant or pact, that is, berith. Now the Hebrews say that God, from his own name, which among them is the four-lettered one, added the letter he to Abraham and Sarah: for he was first called Abram, which means exalted father, and afterwards he was called Abraham, which means father of many: for what follows, of nations, is not contained in the name but is understood. Nor should one wonder why, when among the Greeks and ourselves the letter A seems to have been added, we have said that the Hebrew letter he was added: for it is the idiom of that language to write with E but to read as A: just as, on the contrary, they often pronounce the letter A as E.

17:15

And God said to Abraham: Sarai your wife, you shall not call her Sarai, but Sara shall be her name. Those are mistaken who think that Sara was first written with one R and that afterwards another R was added to her; and, because among the Greeks R is the number one hundred, they suppose many absurdities about her name: whereas, surely, however they would have her name changed, it ought to have a Hebrew, not a Greek, explanation, since the name itself is Hebrew. And no one who names somebody in one language takes the ἐτυμολογίαν [etymology] of the word from another. Sarai, then, was first called with sin, res, ioth: so, when ioth, that is, the element I, was taken away, the letter he was added, which is read as A, and she was called Saraa. And the reason the name was so changed is this: that before she was called my princess, the mother of the household of one house only, but afterwards she is called princess absolutely, that is, ἄρχουσα.

17:16

For there follows I will give you a son from her and I will bless him: and he shall be for nations, and kings of peoples shall be from him. And pointedly, not as we read in the Greek, God said to Abraham: Sarai your wife, her name shall not be called Sarai, but in the Hebrew it has you shall not call her name Sarai, that is, you shall not say to her "You are my princess": for she is now to be the princess of all nations. Some very wrongly suppose that she was formerly called leprosy and afterwards princess: whereas leprosy is called sarath, which in our language indeed seems to have some likeness, but in Hebrew is utterly different. For it is written with sade and ain and res and thau: which plainly differs greatly from the three letters above, that is, sin, res and he, with which Saraa is written.

17:17

And Abraham fell upon his face and laughed and said in his heart: Shall a son be born to a man of a hundred years, and shall Sarah, at ninety years, give birth? And a little after, [Gen 17:19] and you shall call his name Isaac. There is a diversity of opinion, but one etymology, as to why he was called Isaac. For Isaac means laughter. Some say that because Sarah laughed, he was therefore called laughter, which is false. But others say it was because Abraham laughed, which we also approve. For after his son was called Isaac with reference to Abraham's laughter, then we read that Sarah too laughed. Yet it should be known that four in the Old Testament were called by their own names, without any veil, before they were born: Ishmael, Isaac, Solomon and Josiah. Read the Scriptures.

18:6

And he said to her: Hasten, mix three measures of fine flour. Since three measures seem to be spoken of here absolutely, and the measure is uncertain, I have therefore added that in the Hebrew it has three sata, that is, three amphoras, so that we may recognize the same mystery both here and in the Gospel, where a woman is said to leaven three sata.

18:10

And he said: Returning, I will come to you at this time and at the hour, and Sarah shall have a son. For hour we read life in the Hebrew, so that the order or sense is: I will return to you at the time of life: as if he had said, If I live, if life has accompanied me: but this is said ἀνθρωποπαθῶς [in human fashion], as the rest also is.

18:12

And Sarah laughed within herself, saying: It has not yet happened to me until now, and my lord is old. It is read far otherwise in the Hebrew: and Sarah laughed within herself, saying: After I have been worn out, has pleasure come to me? At the same time note that where we have put pleasure, eden is written in the Hebrew. Symmachus translated this place thus: after I have grown old with age, has youth come to me?

18:30

And he said: Is it, Lord, if I speak? which in Greek is written μήτι, κύριε, ἐὰν λαλήσω. A second time Abraham spoke thus to the Lord: which does not seem to make plain what he is saying. In the Hebrew, therefore, it is written more fully: do not, I pray, be angry, Lord, if I speak. For since by his questioning he seemed to press the Lord hard for an answer, he tempers what he asks with a preface.

19:14-15

And he spoke to his sons-in-law, who had taken his daughters. Since afterwards the two daughters of Lot are said to have been virgins, of whom he himself had said a little earlier to the Sodomites behold, my two daughters, who have not known a man, and now Scripture records that he had sons-in-law, some suppose that those who had had husbands remained in Sodom, and that those who were virgins went out with their father. Since Scripture does not say this, the Hebrew truth must be set out, in which it is written Lot went out and spoke to the bridegrooms who were going to take his daughters. So the virgin daughters had not yet been joined in marriage.

19:21

And he said to him, Behold, I have admired your face. In the Hebrew it has behold, I have accepted your face; that is: I assent to your prayers. Symmachus, translating this according to the sense, says ὁράσει ἐδυσωπήθην τὸ πρόσωπόν σου [in the sight I have respected your face].

19:28

And behold, a flame was going up from the earth like the vapor of a furnace. For this we read in the Hebrew behold, citor was going up like the ἀναθυμίασις of a furnace: which we can call vapor, or smoke, or embers.

19:30

And Lot went up from Segor and dwelt on the mountain, and his two daughters with him: for he was afraid to dwell in Segor. The question is asked why, when at first he preferred Segor to flight to the mountain and wished it to be spared as his dwelling place, he now moves again from Segor to the mountain. We shall answer that the conjecture of the Hebrews about Segor is true: that, having been frequently overthrown by earthquake, it was called first Bale and afterwards Salissa, and that Lot was afraid, saying: If, while the other cities were still standing, this one was often overthrown, how much more will it be unable to be saved now, in the common ruin? And that this occasion of unbelief also gave the beginning to his intercourse with his daughters. For he who had seen the other cities overthrown and this one standing, and himself rescued by the help of God, certainly ought not to have doubted about what he had heard was granted to him. Therefore what is said in excuse of the daughters, that they thought the human race had failed and for that reason lay with their father, does not excuse the father.

19:33

Finally, what follows, and he did not know when he had slept with her and when she had risen from him, the Hebrews mark with points above, as being incredible, and something the nature of things does not admit, that anyone should have intercourse without knowing it.

19:36-38

And the two daughters of Lot conceived by their father, and the firstborn bore a son: and she called his name Moab. He is the father of the Moabites to this day. And the younger, she too bore a son and called his name Ammon, that is, son of my people. He is the father of the sons of Ammon. Moab means 'from the father', and the whole name has its etymology; but Ammon, for which the reason of the name is given, as it were, as 'son of my race' or (as is better in the Hebrew) 'son of my people', is derived in such a way that it is partly the sense of the name and partly the word itself. For 'my people' is called Ammi, from which the Ammonites are named.

20:12

For truly she is my sister by my father, but not by my mother. That is, she is the daughter of his brother Haran, not of his sister. But since in the Hebrew it has truly she is my sister, the daughter of my father and not the daughter of my mother, and it sounds more as though she was the sister of Abraham, we say in his excuse that at that time such marriages had not yet been forbidden by the law.

21:9

And Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, whom she had borne to Abraham, playing. What follows, with Isaac her son, it does not have in the Hebrew. This is therefore explained in two ways by the Hebrews: either that he made idols in play, according to that which is written elsewhere, the people sat down to eat and drink, and they rose up to play; or that against Isaac, as being of greater age, he claimed the rights of the firstborn for himself in jest and play. This indeed Sarah heard and did not bear: and this is proved from her own words, when she says [Gen 21:10] cast out this handmaid with her son. For the son of the handmaid shall not be heir with my son Isaac.

21:14

And he took loaves and a skin of water and gave them to Hagar, putting them on her shoulder, and the little one, and sent her away. When Isaac was born, Ishmael was thirteen years old. And after Isaac's weaning the boy is driven from the house with his mother. Among the Hebrews, however, opinion varies: some assert that the time of weaning was fixed at the fifth year, and others claim the twelfth year. We therefore, to choose the shorter age, have reckoned that Ishmael was cast out with his mother after eighteen years, and that it was not fitting for one who was already a young man to have sat on his mother's neck. True, therefore, is that idiom of the Hebrew language, that every son, in comparison with his parents, is called an infant and a little one. Nor should we wonder that a barbarian language has its own peculiarities, since even today at Rome all sons are called infants. Abraham therefore placed the loaves and the skin on Hagar's shoulder: and having done this, he gave the boy to his mother, that is, he delivered him into her hand, entrusted him to her, and so sent her out of the house.

21:15-17

As for what follows, and she cast the boy under a fir tree, and going away she sat down opposite, far off, about a bowshot. For she said, "I will not see the death of my little one," and sat opposite him, and what is immediately joined to it, the boy cried out and wept: and God heard the voice of the boy from the place where he was: and the angel of God said to Hagar from heaven and the rest, let it trouble no one: for in the Hebrew, after what is written, I will not see the death of my little one, we read that Hagar herself sat opposite the boy and lifted up her voice and wept, and God heard the voice of the little one. For while the mother wept and wretchedly awaited the death of her son, God heard the boy, about whom he had made a promise to Abraham, saying but the son of your handmaid too I will make into a great nation. Besides, the mother herself was lamenting not her own death but her son's. God therefore spared him for whom the weeping had been.

21:18

Finally, in what follows it is said Rise and take up the boy and hold his hand. From which it is clear that he who is held was not a burden to his mother but a companion. And in that he is held by the hand of his parent, a solicitous affection is shown.

21:22

And Abimelech said, and Ochozath his groomsman, and Phicol the chief of his army. Except for Abimelech and Phicol, the third name that is read here is not found in the Hebrew volume.

21:30-31

And he said, These seven sheep you shall take from me, that they may be a testimony for me that I dug this well. Therefore he named the name of that place Well of the oath, because there they both swore. Where we read here well of the oath, in the Hebrew it has ber sabee. Now there is a double reason why it was so called: either because Abimelech received seven ewe-lambs from the hand of Abraham, for seven is called sabee; or because they swore there, since an oath too is likewise called sabee. And if we read this name above, before this cause arose, let us know that it was said by prolepsis, as also Bethel and Galgala, which were certainly called otherwise until the time when they were so named. It should be noted, moreover, both from the earlier passages and from the present place, that Isaac was not born at the oak of Mambre, or in the vale of Mamre, as it stands in the Hebrew, but in Gerar: where Bersabee too is a town to this day. This province, not long ago, through the division of the governors of Palestine, was called Salutaris. Of this matter Scripture is witness, which says [Gen 21:34] and Abraham dwelt in the land of the Philistines.

22:2

And God said to him, Take your only-begotten son, whom you love, Isaac, and go into the high land, and offer him there as a whole burnt offering on one of the mountains which I shall tell you. It is difficult to turn the idiom of the Hebrew language into Latin speech. Where it is now said go into the high land, in the Hebrew it has moria, which Aquila translated τὴν καταφανῆ, that is, 'bright', and Symmachus τῆς ὀπτασίας, that is, 'of the vision'. The Hebrews therefore say that this is the mountain on which the temple was afterwards founded, on the threshing floor of Ornas the Jebusite, as it is also written in Chronicles: and they began to build the temple in the second month, on the second day of the month, on Mount Moria. It means 'illuminating' and 'shining' for this reason, that there is the dabir (that is, the oracle of God), and the law, and the Holy Spirit, who teaches men the truth and inspires the prophets.

22:3-4

And he went to the place which God had told him, on the third day. It should be noted that from Gerar to Mount Moria, that is, the seat of the temple, is a journey of three days, and that consequently he is said to have arrived there on the third day. Wrongly, therefore, do some think that Abraham at that time lived at the oak of Mambre, since from there to Mount Moria is scarcely one full day's journey.

22:13

And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and behold, a ram behind him was held in the thicket sabech by its horns. Eusebius of Emesa said a ridiculous thing in this place, saying: sabech is the name for a he-goat which, with upright horns, raises itself up high to crop the leaves of a tree. Aquila, again, rendered it συχνεῶνα, which we can call a briar or a thorn-brake and (to render the force of the word) thickets dense and interwoven with one another. Hence Symmachus too, led into the same opinion: and there appeared he says a ram after this, held fast in a net by its horns. But to some it seems that in this place at least the Seventy and Theodotion translated better, who put the name itself, sabech, saying in the thicket sabech by its horns: for, they say, συχνεὼν, or 'net', which Aquila and Symmachus put, is written with the letter sin, whereas here the letter samech has been placed; from which it is clear that the word sabech is not a rendering of dense stems and of thickets woven together like a net, but sounds the name of a shrub, which is so called in Hebrew. But I, inquiring diligently, have often found συχνεῶνα written with the letter samech.

22:14

And Abraham called the name of that place "the Lord sees", so that they say today "on the mountain the Lord sees". Instead of what it has here, sees, in the Hebrew will be seen is written. This, moreover, has passed into a proverb among the Hebrews, so that, whenever they are placed in distress and wish to be lifted up by the help of the Lord, they say "on the mountain the Lord will be seen", that is: as he had mercy on Abraham, he will have mercy on us too. Hence also, in token of the ram that was given, they are accustomed even now to sound a horn.

22:20-22

And they told Abraham, saying, Behold, Milcah too has borne sons to Nahor your brother: Hus his firstborn, and Buz his brother, and Camuhel the father of the Syrians, and Caseth. The firstborn of Nahor, Abraham's brother, born of Milcah his wife, the daughter of Haran, was Hus: from whose stock Job descended, as it is written at the opening of his volume, there was a man in the land of Hus, Job by name. Wrongly, therefore, do some reckon that Job is of the race of Esau: since what is found at the end of his book, that it was translated from the Syrian language, and that he is the fourth from Esau, and the rest that is contained there, is not found in the Hebrew volumes. The second born of Milcah was Buz, whom the Seventy chose to render Bauz. And of his race, as the Hebrews relate, is that Balaam the soothsayer who in the book of Job is called Elihu: at first a holy man and a prophet of God, afterwards, through disobedience and desire for gifts, while he longs to curse Israel, he is designated by the name of soothsayer; and it is said in the same book, and Elihu the son of Barachiel the Buzite was angry, descending, that is, from the root of this Buz. Camuhel, moreover, is the father of Damascus: for Damascus itself is called Aram, which here has been written as Syria, and it is read under that very name in Isaiah. Caseth, too, is the fourth, from whom the Casdim, that is, the Chaldeans, were afterwards named.

23:2

And Sarah died in the city of Arboc, which is in the valley; this is Hebron in the land of Canaan. This which is set down here, which is in the valley, is not found in the authentic copies. The name of the city, Arboc, has also been gradually corrupted by writers and readers. For we must not think that the Seventy translators rendered the name of a Hebrew city in a barbarous and corrupt manner, and otherwise than it is called in their own tongue. For Arboc means nothing at all. But it is called arbee, that is, 'four', because there Abraham and Isaac and Jacob were buried, and Adam himself, the first of the human race: as will be shown more clearly in the book of Joshua.

23:6

Hear us, lord: you are a king from God to us; in our choice sepulchre bury your dead. Instead of 'king' it has in the Hebrew 'prince' or 'leader': for nasin means not king but leader.

23:16

And Abraham heard Efron, and Abraham weighed out to Efran the silver of which he had spoken in the ears of the sons of Heth. In the Hebrew, as we have set it down here, his name is written first Efron, then Efran. For after he had been won over by the price to sell the sepulchre and accept the silver, although Abraham pressed him, the letter uau, which among them is read for o, was taken away from his name, and instead of Efron he was called Efran: Scripture signifying that he was not of consummate and perfect virtue who could sell the memorials of the dead. Let those therefore know, who make a trade of selling sepulchres, and are not compelled to accept a price but extort it even from the unwilling, that their name is changed and something of their merit perishes, since even he is secretly reproved who accepted it unwillingly.

24:9

And the servant put his hand under the thigh of Abraham his lord, and swore to him concerning this word. The Hebrews relate that he swore by his sanctification, that is, by circumcision; but we say that he swore by the seed of Abraham, that is, by Christ, who was to be born of him, according to the evangelist Matthew, who says the book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham.

24:22

And the man took a golden earring, its weight a didrachm. Bace, which in this place is written for didrachm, is a half-ounce; but secel, which in the Latin tongue is corruptly called siclus (shekel), has the weight of an ounce.

24:43

Behold, I stand over the fountain of water, and the daughters of the men of the city will come out to draw water: and there shall be a virgin, to whom I shall say, Give me a little water to drink from your water jar, and so on. In the Hebrew it is written behold, I, standing beside the fountain of water, and there shall be a young girl who will come out to draw, and I shall say to her, Give me a little water to drink from your water jar, and so on. For 'young girl', moreover, which in the Greek language is called νεᾶνις, we read there alma, which indeed is also found in Isaiah. For in that place where in our copies it is written behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear, Aquila translated behold, a young girl shall conceive and bear, and in the Hebrew we read behold, an alma shall conceive and bear. It should be noted, therefore, that the word alma is never written except of a virgin, and it has the etymology ἀπόκρυφος, that is, 'hidden'. For it is written in Job, and wisdom, whence shall it be found? and what is the place of understanding? and it is hidden from the eyes of every living thing. Where we have just said it is hidden, in the Hebrew, because of the inflection of the word, it is formed differently and is said naalma. Something similar to this, although it is inflected in the masculine gender, is also written in the books of Kingdoms, in the person of Elisha speaking to Gehazi: and the Lord has hidden it from me. Therefore alma, which means 'hidden', that is, a virgin guarded with exceeding care, seems to me to carry greater praise than 'virgin'. For according to the Apostle one can be a virgin in body and not in spirit; but a hidden one, who is a virgin, has an ἐπίτασιν [an intensification] of virginity, so that she is both a virgin and hidden; and she who is hidden, according to the idiom of the Hebrew language, is consequently a virgin as well, whereas it does not immediately follow that she who is a virgin is hidden. This same word we also read in Exodus of Miriam the sister of Moses, a virgin. Let the Jews, then, show alma set down anywhere in the Scriptures where it means only a young girl and not a virgin: and we grant them that what is said among us in Isaiah, behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear, signifies not a hidden virgin but a young girl already married.

24:59

And they sent away Rebecca their sister and her substance, and the servant of Abraham, and the men who were with him. In the Hebrew it has and they sent away Rebecca their sister and her nurse, and the servant of Abraham, and his men. For it was fitting that a virgin setting out for her marriage without her parents should be cherished by the solace of a nurse.

24:62-63

And he dwelt in the land of the south: and Isaac went out to exercise himself in the field toward evening. The land of the south signifies Gerar, from which he had once been led away by his father to be sacrificed. But where it says and he went out to exercise himself in the field, which in Greek is ἀδολεσχῆσαι [to meditate], in the Hebrew we read and Isaac went out to speak in the open country, as evening was now declining. It signifies, moreover, in accordance with the fact that the Lord prayed alone on the mountain, that Isaac too, who was a type of the Lord, went out of the house to prayer as a just man, and either at the ninth hour or before sunset offered spiritual victims to God.

24:65

And she took a theristrum and covered herself. A theristrum is what is called a mantle, a kind of garment Arabian even now, with which the women of that province are veiled.

25:1-6

And Abraham added and took a wife, and her name was Keturah: and she bore him Zamram and Jecsan and Madan and Madian and Jesboc and Sue. And Jecsan begot Saba and Dadan. And the sons of Dadan were Asurim and Latusim and Laomim. And the sons of Madian were Gefar and Afer and Saba and Enoch and Abida and Aledea. All these were the sons of Keturah. And Abraham gave all that he had to Isaac, but to the sons of the concubines whom he had he gave gifts, and sent them away from Isaac his son, while he was still living, into the east, to the eastern land. Keturah in the Hebrew tongue means 'joined' or 'bound'. For this reason the Hebrews suspect that, with her name changed, she is the same as Hagar, who after Sarah's death passed from concubine to wife. And the age of Abraham, already spent, seems to be excused, so that the old man should not be charged with having wantoned in a new marriage after the death of his aged wife. We, leaving aside what is uncertain, say this: that the sons of Abraham born of Keturah, according to the historians of the Hebrews, occupied τρωγλοδύτιν [the Troglodyte country] and the Arabia which is now called εὐδαίμων [Felix, 'Happy'], as far as the borders of the Red Sea. It is said, moreover, that one of the descendants of Abraham, who was called Afer, led an army against Libya, and there, having conquered his enemies, settled; and that his descendants, from the name of their ancestor, called it Africa. Witness to this is Alexander, who is called Polyhistor, and Cleodemus, surnamed Malchus, who retell a barbarian history in the Greek tongue. As for what it says, and the sons of Dadan were Asurim and Latusim and Laomim: they think Asurim is rendered 'merchants', Latusim 'those who hammer out the metals of bronze and iron', and Laomim φυλάρχους, that is, princes of many tribes and peoples. Others contend that the Syrians were called after this Asurim, and that the regions of India were occupied by many of the sons of Abraham by Keturah.

25:8

And Abraham died in a good old age, old and full, and was gathered to his people. Wrongly has it been added in the Seventy translators, and failing, Abraham died, since failing and being diminished does not befit Abraham. That too which we have set down, in a good old age, old and full, is given in the Greek copies as full of days. Although this seems to set out the sense, namely that he died full of light and of the works of the day, yet it serves the ἀναγωγὴν [the anagogical (higher) sense] better if full is set down simply.

25:13-18

And these are the names of the sons of Ishmael in their names and in their generations. The firstborn of Ishmael, Nabaioth, and Cedar and the rest, up to the place where it says, and they dwelt from Evila as far as Sur, which is over against the face of Egypt for those going toward the Assyrians; in the sight of all his brothers he fell. Twelve sons are born to Ishmael, of whom the firstborn was Nabaioth, after whom the whole region from the Euphrates as far as the Red Sea is called Nabathena to this day; it is a part of Arabia. For their families too, and their towns and villages and small forts and tribes, are known by the name of each one of them: of these, Cedar is in the desert, and Duma is another region, and Theman toward the south, and Cedema is called the tract toward the east. As for what we read at the end of this passage according to the Seventy, he dwelt over against the face of all his brothers, truer is that which we have set down, he died in the presence of all his brothers. That is, he died in the hands of all his sons, his children surviving him and none snatched away by death before him. That brothers are called so in place of sons, Jacob also shows when he says to Laban, what is my sin, that you have pursued after me, and that you have searched all my vessels? what have you found of all the vessels of your house? let it be set before my brothers and your brothers, and let them judge between us. For we cannot believe, as Scripture records, that Jacob had any brothers with him apart from his children.

25:21-22

And Rebecca his wife conceived, and her sons moved within her. For 'moving' the Seventy translators set down ἐσκίρτων, that is, they were playing or kicking; which Aquila translated the sons were being crushed together in her womb, but Symmachus διέπλεον, that is, they were borne along on the surface like a ship.

25:25

And the first came out red, all hairy like a skin. Where we have set down 'hairy', in the Hebrew it has seir. Hence Esau, as we also read elsewhere, was called Seir, that is, hairy.

25:30

And Esau said to Jacob, Give me a taste of this red pottage, for I am fainting. Therefore his name was called Edom. Red or tawny is called edom in the Hebrew language. Therefore, from the fact that he sold his birthright for red food, he received the name of tawny, that is, Edom.

26:12

And Isaac sowed in that land, and found in that year a hundredfold of barley. Although Isaac sowed in a foreign land, still I do not think that he had so great a fertility of barley. Hence I think that is better which is found in the Hebrew, and which Aquila also translated, and he found in that year a hundredfold reckoned, that is, ἑκατὸν εἰκασμούς. For although 'estimation' and 'barley' are written with the same letters, yet estimations are read saarim, but barleys sorim. And Scripture, by keeping silent about the kind of crop that was multiplied a hundredfold, seems to me to have shown the multiplication of all virtues in him. Accordingly there follows: [Gen 26:13] and the Lord blessed him, and the man became great, and he walked on, going and magnified, until he became exceedingly great. But I do not know whether the good fortune of multiplied barley could make anyone glorious.

26:17

And Isaac went away from there and came into the valley of Gerar and dwelt there. In place of 'valley' it has 'torrent' in the Hebrew. For Isaac, after he had been magnified, could not dwell in a valley. But he dwelt at the torrent of which it is written, he drinks of the torrent in the way, from which Elijah too drank in the time of famine. But because Elijah was not perfect as Christ is, that torrent therefore dried up for him. But our Lord too was betrayed at a torrent, consecrating our regeneration and the sacrament of baptism.

26:19

And the servants of Isaac dug in the valley of Gerar, and found there a well of living water. Here too 'torrent' is written in place of 'valley'. For a well of living water is never found in a valley.

26:21

And they dug another well, and they quarrelled over it also: and he called its name Enmities. In place of 'enmities', which Aquila and Symmachus translated τὴν ἀντικειμένην and ἐναντίωσιν, that is, the adverse and the contrary, in the Hebrew it has satanan. From this we understand that satanan means 'contrary'.

26:22

And they dug another well: and they did not strive with them, and he called its name Breadth. In place of 'breadth' in the Hebrew it has rooboth, which proves what we said above, he built the city of Nineveh and rooboth, that is, its streets.

26:26

And Abimelech went to him from Gerar, and Ochozath his groomsman, and Phicol the chief of his soldiery. In place of 'Ochozath his groomsman' in the Hebrew it has the company of his friends, so that it signifies not so much a man as the crowd of friends that had come with the king, among whom was also Phicol, the chief of his army.

26:32-33

And the servants of Isaac came and told him of the well which they had dug, and said to him, We have found water, and he called its name Fullness. I do not know how it comes to be found in the Seventy translators, and the servants of Isaac came and told him of the well which they had dug, and said to him, We have not found water: and he called its name Oath. For what ἐτυμολογία [etymology] is it for it to be called 'oath' because they had not found water? On the contrary, in the Hebrew, with whose rendering Aquila and Symmachus also agree, it signifies this: that they found water, and that for this reason the well itself was named Fullness and the city called Bersabee, that is, the well of fullness. For although above we asserted that Bersabee was so named from the word for 'oath' or from the number seven of the sheep, which is called sabee, yet now, from the fact that water was found, Isaac, alluding to the name of the city, which was so called, altered the letter a little, and in place of the hissing sin of the Hebrews, with which sabee begins, put the Greek sigma, that is, the Hebrew samech. Otherwise, too, according to the law of allegory, it would by no means be fitting that after so many wells, at the end of his virtues, Isaac should have found no water at all.

27:11

Behold, Esau my brother is a hairy man, and I am a smooth man. Where we read 'hairy', in the Hebrew it is written seir. Hence afterwards the region of Mount Seir, in which he lived, was so called: we have spoken of this above as well.

27:15

And Rebecca took the garments of Esau her elder son, which were precious, with her at home. In this place too the Hebrews relate that the firstborn performed the office of priests and had a priestly garment, clothed in which they offered victims to God, before Aaron was chosen for the priesthood.

27:35

And Esau said, Justly was his name called Jacob, for behold, he has supplanted me a second time. Jacob means 'supplanter'. So from the fact that he deceived his brother by craft, he alluded to the name of him who was called Jacob before for this reason, that at his birth he had grasped his brother's heel.

27:40

And you shall serve your brother: and the time shall be when you shall put off and loose his yoke from your neck. It signifies that the Idumaeans would serve the Jews, and that the time would come when they would throw off the yoke of servitude from their neck and resist their rule. But according to the Seventy translators, who said, but it shall be, when you have put off and loosed the yoke from your neck, the sentence seems to hang and not to be complete.

28:19

And Jacob called the name of that place Bethel, and Ulammaus was the name of the city before. From what he had said above, how terrible is this place: this is nothing other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven, he now gives the place a name: and he called it Bethel, that is, house of God; which place was called Luza before, which means 'nut' or ἀμύγδαλον. Hence some ridiculously think that the Hebrew word ulam is the name of the city, though ulam means 'before'. So the order of the reading is this: and he called the name of that place Bethel, and before, Luza was the name of the city. All the ancient Scriptures are full of the word ulam or elem, which signifies nothing other than 'before' or 'formerly', or 'porch' or 'lintel' or 'doorposts'.

29:27

Complete therefore the week of this one, and I will give you this one also. After Jacob, deceived by fraud, had taken Leah as his wife in place of Rachel, he is told by his father-in-law Laban to fulfil seven days after the wedding of the elder sister, and so to receive Rachel, for whom he was to serve again for another seven years. It was not, then, as some wrongly suppose, after another seven years that he took Rachel as his wife, but after the seven days of the wedding of his first wife. For there follows: [Gen 29:30] and he went in to Rachel: and he loved Rachel more than Leah, and he served him another seven years.

29:32

And she conceived and bore a son and called his name Reuben. For brevity of reading I wish to give the etymologies of the names of all the patriarchs together. And she called he says his name Reuben, saying, Because the Lord has seen my humiliation. Reuben means 'son of vision'.

29:33

And she conceived he says another son and said, Because the Lord has heard me, in that my husband held me in hatred, and he has given me this one also: and she called his name Simeon. From the fact that she was heard, she gave Simeon his name. For Simeon means 'hearing'.

29:34

Of the third, moreover, there follows: and she conceived again and bore a son and said, Now my husband will be with me, because I have borne him three sons: therefore she called his name Levi. Where we read my husband will be at my side, Aquila translated my husband will be joined to me, which in Hebrew is said illaue, and is rendered otherwise by the teachers of the Hebrews, so that they say, "my husband will escort me". That is: I have no doubt of my husband's love for me; he will be my companion in this life, and his love will lead me and escort me all the way to death, for I have borne him three sons.

29:35

She conceived and gave birth and bore a son and said, "Now over this I will confess to the Lord", and for that reason she called his name Judah. Judah means 'confession'. So from 'confession' the name of the confessor was given. Yet here confession is taken for thanksgiving or for praise, as often in the psalms, and in the Gospel, I confess to you, Lord, Father of heaven and earth, that is, I give you thanks, or I glorify you.

30:5-6

And Bilhah conceived and bore Jacob a son: and Rachel said, "The Lord has judged me and has heard my voice and has given me a son"; therefore she called his name Dan. She expressed the cause of the name, so that, from the fact that the Lord had judged her, she gave the son of her handmaid the name of judgment: for Dan means 'judgment'.

30:7-8

And Bilhah, Rachel's handmaid, conceived again and bore Jacob a second son: and Rachel said: "God has made me dwell in a dwelling with my sister, and I have prevailed": and she called his name Naphtali. A cause of the name Naphtali is set out here far different from the one written in the Book of Hebrew Names. Hence Aquila too says συνανέστρεψέν με ὁ θεὸς συναναστροφήν [God has turned me about with a turning], in place of which in the Hebrew it is written neptule eloim neptalti. Hence from 'turning' or from 'comparison', since it sounds both, 'comparison' or 'turning', that is, Naphtali, she gave the son his name.

30:10-11

As for what follows, and Zilpah, Leah's handmaid, bore Jacob a son: and Leah said: "In fortune", and she called his name Gad: where we have set down in fortune, and in Greek it is said ἐν τύχῃ, which can be called 'outcome', in the Hebrew it has bagad, which Aquila renders a girding came, while we can say 'in readiness'. For ba can sound both as the preposition in and as he came. So from outcome or readiness, which is called gad, the son of Zilpah was called Gad.

30:12-13

There follows: and Zilpah, Leah's handmaid, bore Jacob a second son: and Leah said, Blessed am I, because the women call me blessed, and she called his name Asher, riches. 'Riches', that is, πλοῦτος, has been wrongly added, since the etymology of the name Asher is laid open by the authority of Scripture, which says, Blessed am I, and the women call me blessed, and from the fact that she is called blessed by men, she called her son blessed. So Asher means not 'riches' but 'blessed', at least in the present place. For in others, owing to the ambiguity of the word, riches too can be so called.

30:17-18

And God heard Leah, and she conceived and bore Jacob a fifth son, and Leah said, "God has given me my reward, because I gave my handmaid to my husband": and she called his name Issachar. The Seventy translators gave the etymology of this name as est merces (there is a reward). Certainly it must not be supposed, as many wrongly read it by adding a pronoun, that it is written thus, is est merces (he is the reward); rather, the whole name means est merces, there is a reward. For is means est (there is), and sachar merces (reward). And this was so because with her son Reuben's mandrakes she had bought for herself her husband's going in, which was due to Rachel.

30:19-20

There follows: and Leah conceived again and bore Jacob a sixth son, and Leah said: "God has endowed me with a good dowry: at this time my husband will dwell with me, because I have borne him six sons", and she called his name Zebulun. Where we have set down will dwell with me, and the Seventy translated will love me, in the Hebrew it has iezbuleni. And the sense is: because I have borne Jacob six sons, I am therefore now secure: for my husband will dwell with me. Hence my son too is called 'dwelling'. Wrongly, then, and by force, is Zebulun rendered 'flow of the night' in the Book of Names.

30:21

And after this she bore a daughter and called her name Dinah. This is rendered 'cause', which the Greeks more expressively call δίκην. For she was the cause of the quarrel at Sicima.

After the sons, the names of the parents too must be set down. Leah means 'toiling', Rachel 'sheep'; whose son Joseph, from the fact that his mother had wished another to be added to her, is called 'increase'.

30:32-33

I will pass through all your flock today; separate from it every spotted and parti-coloured beast, and every beast of one colour among the lambs, and the spotted and parti-coloured among the goats, and it shall be my wage: and my justice shall answer for me tomorrow, when my wage comes before you: everything that is not spotted and parti-coloured among the goats and among the lambs shall be theft with me and so on. In the Seventy translators the sense is greatly confused, and to the present day I have been able to find none of our writers who would set out clearly what is said in this place. You want me, says Jacob, to serve you another seven years as well: do what I ask. Separate all the parti-coloured and spotted, sheep as well as goats, and hand them over into the hands of your sons; and in turn, out of each flock, give me the white and the black beasts, that is, those of one colour. If, then, anything spotted is born of the white and the black, which are of one colour, it shall be mine; but if anything of one colour, yours. I ask no difficult thing. The nature of the flocks is on your side, that white are born of white and black of black; on mine is my justice, while God looks upon my humiliation and my toil. Laban gladly seized the option given him and, doing as Jacob asked, set a journey of three days between Jacob and his own sons, so that no trickery might arise from the nearness of the flocks. So Jacob devised a new trick and fought with natural art against the nature of the white and the black beasts. For taking three rods, of poplar and of almond and of pomegranate (although the Seventy have rods of storax and of nut and of plane), and stripping them in part, he gave the rods a varied colour, so that wherever he had left the bark on the rod the old colour remained, but where he had taken the bark off, a white colour was laid bare. Jacob therefore kept watch, and at the time when the flocks were mounted, and after the heat of the day went eagerly to drink, he set the parti-coloured rods in the troughs, and letting in the rams and the he-goats, in that very eagerness for drinking he had the sheep and the she-goats mounted, so that from the twofold desire, while they drink eagerly and are mounted by the males, they might conceive offspring such as the shadows of the rams and he-goats mounting from above which they beheld in the mirror of the waters. For from the rods set in the troughs the colour of the images too was varied. Nor is it surprising that this is the nature of females in conceiving, that they produce such young as they have looked upon, or conceived in mind, in the final heat of pleasure as they conceive, since this very thing is said to happen also in the herds of mares among the Spaniards; and Quintilian, in that controversy in which a matron was accused of having given birth to an Ethiopian, argues in her defence that this is the nature of conceptions which we have described above. And after the spotted and parti-coloured kids and lambs had been born from the white flocks and those of one colour, Jacob separated them and had them kept far from the original flock; but if any were born of one colour, that is, white or black, he handed them over into the hands of Laban's sons: and he set the rods which he had stripped in the troughs where the waters were poured out, and they came to drink opposite the flocks, so that they might conceive at the time when they came to drink.

30:41-42

And the flocks conceived facing Jacob's rods, the rods which he had set before the flocks in the troughs, that they might conceive at them: and among the late sheep he did not set them. And the late ones became Laban's and the early ones Jacob's. This is not found in the Seventy translators, but in place of 'late' and 'early' they translated something else, I know not what, which does not bear on the sense. But what Scripture says is this: Jacob, prudent and shrewd, kept justice and fairness even in his new art. For if the flocks had produced all the lambs and kids spotted, there was some suspicion of trickery, and the envious Laban would have openly opposed this. So he tempered everything in such a way that he himself would receive the fruit of his labour and Laban would not be stripped entirely. Whenever the sheep and goats were mounted in the first season, because the spring offspring is better, he set the rods before them, so that spotted young would be born. But whatever sheep and goats sought the male late, he did not set them before their eyes, so that beasts of one colour would be born. And whatever was born first was his, because it was parti-coloured and spotted; whatever later, Laban's. For a beast of one colour, black as well as white, sprang up. But in that place where it is written that they might conceive at the rods, and in the Hebrew it has iaeahamena, I cannot express the force of the Hebrew word except by circumlocution. For iaeahamena properly denotes the final heat in coupling, by which the whole body is shaken, and the end is near for one who is consummating pleasure.

31:7-8

And your father has lied to me and has changed my wage ten times, and God did not grant him to harm me. If he says, "This speckled flock shall be your wage," the whole flock will be born speckled. And if he says, "Those of one color shall be your wage," the whole flock will be born of one color. Where we have put he has changed my wage ten times, the Seventy translators put by ten lambs, led by I know not what opinion, since the Hebrew word moni means number rather than lambs. Indeed, from what follows as well this sense is the better proved: that at each breeding Laban always changed the terms. If he saw a speckled flock being born, after the breeding he would say: I want speckled ones to be born for me in future. Again, when he had seen beasts of one color being born (for Jacob, having heard this, did not put the rods in the troughs), he would say that the beasts should produce their future young of one color for him. And why say more? Up to ten times the terms for his own flock, or rather Jacob's, were always being changed by Laban. And whatever he had proposed to himself should be born was turned into the opposite color. But lest ten times of bearing in six years should seem incredible to anyone, read Vergil, in whom it is said: twice-pregnant flocks. And the nature of Italian sheep and of those of Mesopotamia is reported to be one and the same.

31:19

And Rachel stole her father's idols. Where we now read idols, in the Hebrew therafim is written, which Aquila translates μορφώματα, that is, figures or images. And this is said so that we may know what therafim means in the book of Judges.

31:21

And he crossed the river and came to Mount Galaad. Not that the mountain was called Galaad at that time, but by anticipation, as we have often said, it is called by the name by which it was named afterwards.

31:41

And you have changed my wage by ten lambs. The same error as above: for number must be read in place of lambs.

31:46-47

And Jacob said to his brothers, Let us gather stones. And when the stones had been gathered they made a heap and ate upon it, and Laban called it heap of testimony, and Jacob called it heap of witness. In the Hebrew tongue a heap is called gal, and testimony aad. Again, in the Syrian tongue a heap is called igar, testimony sedutha. Jacob therefore called the heap of testimony, that is, Galaad, in the Hebrew tongue, while Laban called the very same thing, that is, the heap of testimony, igar sedutha in the speech of his own people. For he was a Syrian, and had exchanged the ancient tongue of his forefathers for the speech of the province in which he lived.

32:2-3

And the angels of God met him. And Jacob said, when he saw them: "These are the camps of God," and he called the name of that place Camps. Where camps is put here, in the Hebrew it has manaim, so that we may know, if ever it is set down untranslated in another place, what place it signifies. And fittingly, as he is about to go to meet his hostile brother, he is received by choirs of angels accompanying him.

32:10-11

And Jacob said, God of my father Isaac, O Lord, who said to me: "Return to your land and I will bless you": I am less than all your mercy and all your truth, which you have shown for your servant. Where we have put I am less, something else, I know not what, which disturbs the sense, is contained in the Greek and Latin copies.

32:28-29

And he said to him, What is your name? And he said: Jacob. And he said to him, Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but your name shall be called Israel, because you have prevailed with God and you shall be strong with men. Josephus, in the first book of the Antiquities, thinks that he was called Israel because he stood against the angel: which I, examining it carefully, could find nowhere at all in the Hebrew. And what need is there for me to seek out the opinions of individuals, when he who gave the name himself sets out its etymology: There shall not be called he says your name Jacob, but Israel shall be your name. That is why Aquila translates ὅτι ἦρξας μετὰ θεοῦ [for you have ruled with God], Symmachus ὅτι ἤρξω πρὸς θεόν [for you have ruled toward God], the Seventy and Theodotion ὅτι ἐνίσχυσας μετὰ θεοῦ [for you have prevailed with God]. For Sarith, which is derived from the word Israel, means prince. So the sense is this: your name shall not be called supplanter, that is, Jacob, but your name shall be called prince with God, that is, Israel. For as I am a prince, so you too, who were able to wrestle with me, shall be called a prince. And if you were able to fight with me, who am God or an angel (since most interpret it in various ways), how much more with men, that is, with Esau, whom you ought not to fear. But the rendering in the Book of Names, where Israel means 'a man seeing God' or 'a mind seeing God', worn smooth in almost everyone's speech, seems to me to have been interpreted not so much truly as forcibly. For Israel here is written with these letters, iod sin res aleph lamed, which means prince of God or upright of God, that is, ἐυθύτατος θεοῦ. But man seeing God is written with these letters: man is written with three letters, aleph iod sin, so that it is pronounced eis; seeing with three, res aleph he, and it is pronounced raha; further, el with two, aleph and lamed, and it means God or strong. Although, therefore, those who translated Israel as a man or a mind seeing God are of great authority, and the shadow of their eloquence overwhelms us, we are led rather by the authority of Scripture and of the angel, or of God, who himself called him Israel, than by that of any worldly eloquence whatever.

32:30-31

That also which follows afterwards, and he blessed him there, and Jacob called the name of that place Face of God: For I have seen God face to face, and my soul has been saved, in the Hebrew is called Fanuhel, so that we may know that this is the very place which in the other volumes of holy Scripture is read in the Greek as Fanuhel, just as it is written in the Hebrew.

33:1-3

And he divided the children among Leah and Rachel and the two handmaids, and he put the handmaids and their sons first, but Leah and the children last, and Rachel and Joseph last, and he himself passed on before them. He did not make three troops, as most suppose, but two. Indeed, where we have he divided, Aquila put ἡμίσευσεν, that is, he halved, so that he made one wedge of the handmaids with their little ones and another of Leah and Rachel, who were free, with their sons, and had the handmaids go first and the free women second, while he himself, ahead of both flocks, went to meet his brother to bow down before him.

33:17

And Jacob built a house there, and for his flocks he built tents. Therefore he called the name of that place Tents.

Where we have tents, in the Hebrew we read socoth. And there is to this day a city of this name beyond the Jordan, in the district of Scythopolis, about which we have written in the Book of Places.

33:18

And Jacob came to Salem, a city of Sychem, in the land of Canaan, when he had come from Mesopotamia of Syria. A difficulty arises: how the city Sychem can be called Salem, when Jerusalem, in which Melchizedek reigned, was formerly called Salem. Either, then, both cities are of one name (which we can find of many places in Judaea as well, so that the same name of a city and of a place occurs in one tribe and in another), or certainly we say that this Salem, which is named here in place of Sychem, is here interpreted as completed and perfect, while that which was afterwards called Jerusalem is translated in our language as peaceful. For with the accent slightly shifted this word sounds as either. The Hebrews relate that the thigh of the limping Jacob grew strong there and was healed, and that for this reason the same city obtained the name of the cured and perfected one.

34:20-21

And Emmor came, and Sychem his son, to the gate of their city, and they spoke to the men of the city, saying, These men are peaceable towards us. Where the Seventy translators have here rendered peaceable, Aquila translated ἀπηρτισμένους, that is, completed and perfect, in place of which in the Hebrew we read salamin. From this it is plain that what we said above about Salem is true.

34:25

And they entered the city carefully and killed every male. Where in the Greek we read ἀσφαλῶς, that is, carefully, in the Hebrew bete is written, that is, boldly and confidently.

35:6

And Jacob came to Luzam in the land of Canaan, which is Bethel. See, it is most plainly proved that Bethel was formerly called not Ulammaus, as was said above, but Luzam, that is, ἀμύγδαλον.

35:8

And Deborah, Rebecca's nurse, died and was buried near Bethel. If Rebecca's nurse, Deborah by name, died, as the Seventy translators too have rendered here, and the Hebrew word itself is menecha, we cannot know why there they put property and here nurse.

35:10

And God said to him, Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel shall be your name, and he called his name Israel. Earlier the name is by no means imposed on him by the angel; rather, it is foretold that it is to be imposed by God. What, therefore, is promised there as future is here shown to be fulfilled.

35:16

And it came to pass, while he was drawing near to Chabratha in the land of Canaan, to come to Efratha, Rachel gave birth. The Hebrew word chabratha, in what follows, where Jacob speaks to Joseph, but when I was coming from Mesopotamia of Syria, your mother Rachel died in the land of Canaan, on the road of the hippodrome, as I was coming to Ephratham, the Seventy translators, meaning I know not what, rendered as hippodrome: and straightaway there, where in the Hebrew it is said [Gen 35:19] and they buried her on the road of Ephratha, this is Bethlehem, the Seventy translators again put hippodrome in place of Ephratha: whereas, surely, if chabratha is a hippodrome, Ephratha cannot be a hippodrome. Aquila, however, translated this as follows: and it came to pass καθ'-ὁδὸν τῆς γῆς, that is, on the journey of the land, entering into Ephratham. But it is better if it is translated, "in the chosen time of the land, when he was entering Ephratham". Further, it signifies the springtime, when all things burst into flower and it is the chosen time of the year, or when those passing along the road pluck and choose from the neighboring fields whatever comes to hand, enticed by the various flowers. Ephratha and Bethlehem, moreover, are the name of one city, with a closely similar interpretation. For indeed it is rendered as fruitful and as house of bread, on account of that bread which says that it came down from heaven.

35:18

And it came to pass, when her soul was leaving her (for she was dying), she called his name Son of my pain, but his father called his name Benjamin. In the Hebrew a likeness of name resounds: for son of my pain, the name the dying mother gave, is said Benoni, while son of the right hand, that is, of strength, the name to which Jacob changed it, is called Benjamin. Hence those who think that Benjamin means son of days are mistaken. For while the right hand is called iamin and ends in the letter N, days are indeed themselves called iamim, but they end in the letter M.

35:21

And Israel set out: and he pitched his tent beyond the tower of Ader. The Hebrews hold that this is the place where the temple was afterwards built, and that the tower of Ader signifies the tower of the flock, that is, of the gathering and assembly: as the prophet Micah also testifies, saying, and you, cloudy tower of the flock, daughter of Sion, and so on; and that at that time Jacob had his tents beyond the place where the temple was afterwards built. But if we follow the order of the route, the place of the shepherds is near Bethlehem, where either the flock of angels sang at the birth of the Lord, or Jacob pastured his flocks, giving the place its name, or (which is nearer the truth) by a kind of prophecy the future mystery was already then being shown.

35:27

And Jacob came to Isaac his father at Mambre, the city of Arbee, this is Hebron. In place of arbee the Seventy have plain, although Hebron is situated on a mountain. And the same city is also called Mambre, having been so named of old after the friend of Abraham.

36:4

And Ada bore Eliphaz to Esau. This is the Eliphaz whom Scripture remembers in the volume of Job.

36:19

These are the sons of Esau, and these their chiefs: he is Edom: and these are the sons of Seir. Esau and Edom and Seir are the name of one man, and why he is called by various names has been said above.

36:20

And as for what follows, and the Horites inhabiting the land, and the rest: having enumerated the sons of Esau, he goes back further and sets out who were the chiefs in the land of Edom before Esau, from the race of the Horites, who in our language are rendered free men. Let us read Deuteronomy carefully, where it is written more plainly how the sons of Esau came and killed the Horites and possessed their land as an inheritance.

36:22

And the sons of Lotan were Borri and Omman, and the sister of Lotan was Themna. This is the Themna of whom it was said above, and Themna was the concubine of Eliphaz, the firstborn son of Esau, and of her Amalek was born. And he made mention of the Horites for this reason: because the firstborn of Esau's sons had taken a concubine from among their daughters. And as for what is said, Theman and Cenez and Amalek and the rest, let us understand that names were afterwards given from these men to the regions of the Idumaeans.

36:24

This is Ana, who found Iamin in the desert, when he was pasturing the asses of Sebeon his father. Many and various things are argued among the Hebrews about this passage; among the Greeks, indeed, and among our own writers there is silence on it. Some think that the seas are called iamim: for seas are written with the same letters with which this word too is written here. And they will have it that he, while pasturing his father's asses in the desert, found gatherings of waters, which according to the idiom of the Hebrew language are called seas: that is to say, he found a pool, a thing hard to find in the wilderness. Some think that hot springs are signified by this word, after the neighboring Punic language, which borders on the Hebrew. There are those who judge that wild asses were let in by him to the she-asses, and that he himself discovered this kind of mating, so that from them very swift asses might be born, which are called iamim. Most think that he was the first to have herds of mares mounted by asses in the desert, so that from this the new animals, mules, might be born against nature. Aquila translated this place thus: this is Ana, who found σὺν τοὺς ἠμίν. And Symmachus similarly τοὺς ἠμίν, a rendering which signifies the plural number. But the Seventy and Theodotion translated alike, saying τὸν ἰαμίν, which indicates the singular number.

36:33

And Jobab the son of Zare, from Bosra, reigned in his stead. Some suspect that he is Job, as is added at the end of that very volume. The Hebrews, on the contrary, assert that he was born of the stock of Nahor, as has already been said above.

37:3

And Israel loved Joseph above all his sons, because he was the son of his old age, and he made him a many-colored tunic. For 'many-colored tunic' Aquila translated a tunic ἀστράγαλον, that is, a tunic reaching to the ankles; Symmachus a tunic with sleeves: either because it came down as far as the ankles and was set off by the craftsman's hands with a wonderful variety, or because it had sleeves; for the ancients mostly wore sleeveless tunics.

37:28

And they sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for twenty gold pieces. For 'gold pieces' the Hebrew has silver pieces; nor was it fitting that the Lord should be sold for a cheaper metal than Joseph.

37:36

But the Midianites sold Joseph in Egypt to Phutiphar, the eunuch of Pharaoh, the chief cook. In very many places Scripture mentions chief cooks (that is, princes of the cooks) in place of masters of the army: for μαγειρεύειν in Greek means to slay. Joseph, then, was sold to the prince of the army and of the warriors, not to Petefre, as is written in the Latin, but to Phutiphar the eunuch. Here the question is asked how he is afterward said to have a wife. The Hebrews relate that Joseph was bought by this man for his exceeding beauty for a shameful service, and that after his virile parts had been withered by the Lord, this man was afterward chosen, according to the custom of the hierophants, for the high priesthood of Heliopolis; and that Asenath, whom Joseph afterward took as his wife, was his daughter.

38:5

And again she bore a son, and she called his name Selom; and she was in Chazbi when she bore them. Here a Hebrew word has been set down as the name of a place, which Aquila translated as a thing, saying and she called his name Selom. And it came to pass that she lied in childbearing, after she had borne him. For after she had borne Selom, her childbearing stopped. Chazbi, then, does not mean the name of a place, but a lie. Hence in another place too it is written the work of the olive shall lie, that is, the olive shall yield no fruit.

38:12

And Judah, having been comforted, went up to those who were shearing his sheep, he and Hiras his shepherd, the Odollamite. For 'shepherd' we read 'friend'. But the word is ambiguous, because both nouns are written with the same letters; but 'friend' is read re, 'shepherd' ro.

38:14

And she sat at the gate of Enan, which is on the way to Thamna. The Hebrew word enaim means eyes. So it is not the name of a place, but the sense is: she sat at a fork in the road, or at a crossroads, where the traveler has to look more carefully to see which way to take.

38:26

And Judah recognized them and said, She is more righteous than I, because I did not give her to Selom my son. The Hebrew has she has been justified rather than I: not that she was righteous, but that in comparison with him she had done less evil, by seeking not wanton shamefulness but children.

38:29

And behold, his brother came out, and she said, Why has the wall been divided on your account? and she called his name Phares. For 'wall' Aquila and Symmachus translated 'division', which in Hebrew is called Phares. So from the fact that he divided the little membrane of the afterbirth, he received the name of division. Hence the Pharisees too, who had separated themselves from the people as being righteous, were called the divided.

38:30

After this his brother came out, on whose hand was the scarlet thread; and she called his name Zara. This name means rising. So whether because he appeared first, or because very many righteous men were born of him, as is contained in the book of Chronicles, he was called Zara, that is, rising.

40:1

After these words the chief of the wine-stewards of the king of Egypt sinned. Where we have put 'chief of the wine-stewards', in the Hebrew it is written masech, the word which we read a while ago in the name of Abraham's servant, and which we can, following common usage, call the cupbearer. Nor let the office be thought mean, since among barbarian kings to this day it is a mark of the highest dignity to have handed the king his cup, and the poets too keep writing of Catamitus and Jove, that he bound his darling over to this office.

40:9-10

And behold, a vine before me, and on the vine three stocks, and it was budding. By 'three stocks' the Hebrew language means three shoots and three branches or layers, which they call sariagim.

40:16

And I seemed to myself to be carrying three baskets of groats on my head. For 'three baskets of groats' the Hebrew has three hampers of flour.

41:2

And behold, out of the river there came up seven cows, beautiful to look upon and choice in their flesh, and they were feeding in achi. Twice in Genesis achi is written, and it is neither a Greek word nor a Latin one. But even the Hebrew itself has been corrupted: for it is said in ahu, that is, in the marsh. But because among the Hebrews the letter u and I are alike and differ only in size, the Seventy translators rendered ahi for ahu, and, following their custom of expressing the double aspiration of the Hebrew letter heth, they joined to it the Greek letter chi.

41:16

And Joseph answered Pharaoh, saying, Without God there shall be no answer of salvation to Pharaoh. In the Hebrew it has otherwise: without me God shall answer peace to Pharaoh. Indeed Symmachus, as is his way, translated more plainly: not I, but God shall answer peace to Pharaoh.

41:29

Behold, seven years of great abundance shall come in all the land of Egypt. I wonder how the Seventy, who now translate most correctly the Hebrew word sabee, which we rendered above as abundance or satiety in the case of the well that Isaac's servants dug last, rendered it there as oath: since it can be understood as oath, and seven, and satiety, and abundance, as the place and the sequence require. And in what follows too, wherever abundance is read, the same word is written in the Hebrew.

41:43

And a herald cried out before him, and he set him over all the land of Egypt. For this Aquila translated and he cried out in his sight a bending of the knee; Symmachus, rendering the very Hebrew word, says and he cried out before him abrech. Hence it seems to me that what is to be understood is not so much a herald, or a bending of the knee, which can be taken of saluting or worshipping Joseph, as what the Hebrews relate, saying that this word is translated tender father. For Ab means father, rech delicate or most tender: Scripture signifying that in wisdom he was indeed the father of all, but in age a most tender youth and a boy.

41:45

And Pharaoh called Joseph's name Zapfanethfane, and gave him Asenath the daughter of Phutiphare, priest of Heliopolis, as his wife. Although in Hebrew this name sounds as 'finder of hidden things', yet since it is given by an Egyptian, it ought to be explained from that language. So in the Egyptian tongue Zapfanethfane, or (as the Seventy chose to render it) Psontonphanech, means savior of the world, because he freed the earth from the threatened destruction of famine. But it should be noted that he took as his wife the daughter of the man who had once been his master and buyer, who at that time was high priest of Heliopolis. For it was not lawful for the priests of that idol to be anything but eunuchs, so that the suspicion of the Hebrews about him, which we mentioned earlier, is proved true.

41:50-52

And to Joseph two sons were born before the years of famine came, whom Asenath the daughter of Phutiphare, priest of Heliopolis, bore to him. And Joseph called the name of the firstborn Manasseh, because: God has made me forget all my labors and all my father's house. And the name of the second he called Ephraim, because: God has made me increase in the land of my lowliness. Observe, because of the question that is to be put a little later about Joseph's sons, that before the time of the famine, when Jacob entered Egypt, Joseph had only two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim: calling him Manasseh from his having forgotten his labors (for so forgetfulness is called in Hebrew), and Ephraim because God had increased him; for from this word too increase is translated into our language.

43:11

And carry down gifts to the man: some resin and honey, incense and stacte and terebinth and nuts, or, as Aquila and Symmachus translated, almonds. We have set down this passage so that we may know that where our copies have 'incense', in the Hebrew it is nechota, which Aquila translated storax; from which the house of nechota, which is read in Isaiah, is most plainly understood as the storehouse of incense or of storax, because various spices were laid up in it.

43:34

And they drank and were made drunk with him. It is an idiom of the Hebrew language to put drunkenness for satiety, as in that place: in its droppings the budding thing shall be made drunk, meaning, beyond doubt, the earth watered by the rains.

44:1-2

And put the silver of each one in the mouth of the sack, and put my silver condy into the sack of the younger. For sack, the Hebrew has a rawhide boot or a leather bag. For condy, that is, cup, which we also read in Isaiah, Aquila translated goblet, Symmachus bowl.

45:9-10

Come down therefore to me; do not stand still; and you shall dwell in the land of Gesen of Arabia. Here 'of Arabia' has been added: for it is not found in the Hebrew volumes, and from this the error has also spread that the land of Gesen is in Arabia. Moreover, if (as it is in our copies) gesem is written with a final M (which by no means pleases me), it signifies a land rained upon. For gesem is rendered 'rain'.

45:17

And Pharaoh said to Joseph, Say to your brothers, Do this: load your wagons, and go into the land of Canaan. For wagons, which the Seventy and Theodotion rendered τὰ πορεῖα [the carriages], the others translated beasts of burden.

45:21

And he gave them provisions for the way. The word seda, which here all with one voice rendered ἐπισιτισμόν, that is, provisions or rations, is also found in the Psalter. For where our people read blessing I will bless her widow, although in most copies some read θήραν instead of widow (that is, χήρα), in the Hebrew it has seda, that is, blessing I will bless her provisions. Moreover θήρα can sound more like hunting than like the fruits of the earth, although it is the custom of the Egyptians to call spelt, too, θήραν, which they now, in corrupted form, call atheran.

46:26-27

All the souls, therefore, who entered Egypt with Jacob and who came forth from his thighs, besides the wives of Jacob's sons, sixty-six souls; and the sons of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt, nine souls. All the souls, therefore, who entered Egypt with Jacob, seventy-five. That, apart from Joseph and his sons, sixty-six souls which came forth from the thighs of Jacob entered Egypt, there is no doubt. For so both the number, reckoned up little by little one by one, confirms it, and it is found in the Hebrew volumes. But as for what we read in the Seventy, [Gen 46:27] and the sons of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt, nine souls, let us know that in the Hebrew there are two instead of nine. For Ephraim and Manasseh were born in Egypt of Asenath the daughter of Phutiphare before Jacob entered Egypt and the time of famine came on. But that too which we read above, and there were sons of Manasseh, whom his Syrian concubine bore to him, Machir; and Machir begot Galaad; and the sons of Ephraim, the brother of Manasseh, Sutalaam and Taam; and the sons of Sutalaam, Edem, has been added, since what we read of afterwards is described as done, as it were, by anticipation. For at the time when Jacob entered Egypt, Ephraim and Manasseh were not of such an age that they could beget sons. From this it is manifest that all the souls which entered Egypt from the thighs of Jacob were seventy, since sixty-six entered afterwards and found three souls in Egypt, namely Joseph with his two sons, while the seventieth was Jacob himself. This matter, lest we seem to speak against the authority of Scripture, even the Seventy translators rendered in Deuteronomy: that with seventy souls Israel entered into Egypt. If anyone therefore resists our opinion, he will make Scripture contradict itself. For the Seventy translators themselves, who here said that seventy-five souls entered Egypt, by πρόλημψιν [prolepsis, anticipation], with Joseph and his descendants, record in Deuteronomy that only seventy went in. But if, on the contrary, this is set against us, how in the Acts of the Apostles, in Stephen's address, it is said to the people that seventy-five souls entered Egypt, the excuse is easy. For holy Luke, who is the writer of that history, sending out the volume of the Acts of the Apostles among the Gentiles, ought not to write anything contrary to that Scripture which had already been published among the Gentiles. And certainly, at that time at least, the authority of the Seventy translators was held in greater repute than Luke, who among the nations was regarded as unknown and lowly and of no great credit. But this is to be observed generally, that wherever the holy apostles or apostolic men speak to the peoples, they mostly make use of those testimonies which had already been published among the Gentiles, although many relate that Luke the evangelist, being a proselyte, did not know Hebrew letters.

46:28

And he sent Judah before him to Joseph, that he might meet him at the city of heroes in the land of Ramesse. In the Hebrew it has neither city of heroes nor land of Ramesse, but only Gosen. Some of the Jews assert that Gosen is now called the Thebaid.

47:11

And as for what follows afterwards, he gave them to possess the best land of Egypt in Ramesse, they hold that the Arsenoite district was once so called.

47:31

And he said to him, Swear therefore to me. And he swore to him, and Israel worshiped toward the top of his staff. In this place too some vainly pretend that Jacob worshiped the top of Joseph's scepter, namely that, honoring his son, he worshiped his power, whereas in the Hebrew it reads very differently: and Israel worshiped, he says, at the head of the bed: namely that, after his son had sworn to him, secure about the petition he had asked, he worshiped God toward the head of his bed. For the holy man, devoted to God and weighed down by old age, had his bed so placed that the very posture of his lying was ready for prayer without any difficulty.

48:1

And it was told to Joseph, Behold, your father is sick; and he took his two sons with him, Manasseh and Ephraim. From this place is shown what we said above, that Joseph up to that time had only two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh. For if, after many years, when Jacob his father was dying, he brought only two sons for the blessing, surely at that time, when his sons, little ones still at the breast, had not been able to beget, he could not have had grandsons from them at the entry of his father and brothers.

48:2

And Israel, strengthened, sat upon the bed. The reason why the Seventy translators rendered the same word now one way and now another, I do not know: this one thing I say boldly, that the very word metta, which here they translated as bed, they named staff rather than bed above, where we said that Jacob worshiped.

48:5-6

And now, behold, your two sons, who were born to you in the land of Egypt before I came to you in Egypt, are mine: Ephraim and Manasseh shall be to me as Reuben and as Simeon. But those whom you shall beget after these shall be yours; they shall be called by the name of their brothers. If anyone was in doubt that seventy souls of the sons of Israel entered Egypt, and that Joseph, at the time when Jacob entered, had not nine but only two sons, it is confirmed by the present passage, since Jacob himself says that he had two sons, not nine. And when he says Ephraim and Manasseh shall be to me like Reuben and Simeon, he signifies this: as Reuben and Simeon will be two tribes and will be called by their own names, so Ephraim and Manasseh will be two tribes, and will beget two peoples, and so will inherit the land of promise, just as my sons too. But the remaining sons, he says, whom you will beget after my death (showing that they were not yet begotten at that time), [Gen 48:6] shall be yours; they shall be called by the name of their brothers in their inheritance. They will not, he says, receive land separately, nor will they have their own allotted portions, like the other tribes, but they will be mingled into the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh as appended peoples.

48:22

And I have given you Sicima, a choice portion above your brothers, (more than to your brothers) which I took from the hands of the Amorites with my sword and arrow. Sicima has been declined according to Greek and Latin custom; otherwise in Hebrew it is called Sychem, as John the evangelist also testifies, although an error has taken root, so that it is read, faultily, as Sychar: and it is now Neapolis, the city of the Samaritans. Since, then, Sychem in the Hebrew language means shoulder, he beautifully alluded to the name, saying and I will give you one shoulder. For instead of choice, that is, ἐξαιρέτῳ, aad, that is, one, is written in the Hebrew. And when he says that he possessed it with bow and sword, here he calls justice bow and sword, through which, a pilgrim and a stranger, he deserved to be delivered from danger after Sychem and Emmor had been killed. For he feared (as we read above) that the neighboring towns and fortresses would rise up against him because of the overthrow of the allied city: and the Lord did not grant them to harm him. Or certainly it is to be understood thus: I will give you Sicima, which I bought with my strength, that is, with money which I acquired with much labor and sweat. And when he says above your brothers, he shows that he gave it to the tribe of Joseph without lot. Indeed Joseph was buried in that same place: and his tomb is seen there to this day.

49:3-4

Reuben, my firstborn: you are my strength and the beginning of my sons: hard to bear, and hard in insolence and injury; like water, may you not boil over. For you went up upon your father's bed; then you defiled my couch, when you went up. In the Hebrew it is written thus: Reuben, my firstborn, my strength, and head among my children: greater in carrying and greater in might: you were poured out like water; add no more. For you went up to your father's bed and defiled the couch in going up. And this is the sense: you are my firstborn, greater among the children, and you sat (in your place) according to the order of your birth, to receive the inheritance which was owed by right to the firstborn, the priesthood and the kingship: this indeed is shown in the carrying of the burden and in the very great might. But because you sinned and, like water that is not held in a vessel, you were poured out by the onrush of pleasure, therefore I command you to sin no further and to be in the number of your brothers, paying the penalty for your sin, in that you have lost the rank of the firstborn.

49:5-6

Simeon and Levi, brothers, have accomplished the iniquity of their device: let my soul not come into their counsel, and in their assembly let my liver not be zealous: for in their fury they killed men, and in their desire they hamstrung a bull. We are compelled by necessity, in keeping with the plan of this work, to go over at greater length the things that disagree with the Hebrew truth. For there one reads Simeon and Levi, brothers, vessels of iniquity their weapons. Into their secret let my soul not enter, and in their assembly let my glory not be dissolved: for in their fury they killed a man, and in their lust they dug under a wall. And he signifies that it was not by his counsel that they killed Sychem and Emmor, men bound by treaty, and against the law of peace and friendship shed innocent blood, and, carried away by cruelty as by a kind of fury, overthrew the walls of a hospitable city.

49:7

Hence he goes on and says cursed be their fury, because it was insolent, and their anger, because it was hard: I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel. For Levi did not receive an inheritance of his own, but among all the tribes had a few cities to dwell in; and of Simeon it is written in the book of Joshua that he too did not obtain an allotted portion of his own, but received something from the tribe of Judah, while in Chronicles it is written more plainly that, when he had multiplied and had no place of possession, he went out into the desert. Some interpret prophetically the men killed as the apostles, and the bull hamstrung by the Pharisees as Christ.

49:8

Judah, your brothers shall praise you. Because Judah means confession or praise, it is consequently written Judah, your brothers shall confess you or [Gen 49:9] your brothers shall praise you. The sons of your father shall worship you. A lion's whelp is Judah; from the shoot, my son, you have gone up. Lying down like a lion and like a lion's whelp: who shall rouse him? Although it is a great mystery concerning Christ, yet according to the letter it is prophesied that kings would be born from Judah through the stock of David and that all the tribes would worship him. For he does not say the sons of your mother, but the sons of your father. And where he says from the shoot, my son, in the Hebrew it has from captivity, my son, you have gone up, to show that he would lead captive peoples and (according to a more sacred understanding) that he ascended on high and led captivity captive. Or (which I think better) the captivity signifies the passion, the ascent the resurrection.

49:11

Binding his foal to the vine, and with a cord the foal of the she-ass. In the Hebrew it has thus: binding his foal to the vine, and to the sorec, my son, his she-ass: namely, that he joined the foal of the she-ass on which Jesus sat, that is, the people of the Gentiles, to the vineyard of the apostles, who are from the Jews, and bound to the sorec, that is, the choice vine, the she-ass on which he sat, the Church gathered from the nations. And when he says my son, he makes an ἀποστροφὴν [an apostrophe, a turning to address] to Judah himself, that Christ would do all these things. But this too must be known, that where we read binding his foal to the vine, instead of foal one can read in the Hebrew his city, the Church being indicated in other words according to the same sense, of which it is written elsewhere a city set on a mountain cannot be hidden and the rush of the river makes glad the city of God.

49:14-15

Issachar has desired the good, resting in the midst of the lots: and seeing that rest was good and the land that it was fat, he put his shoulder under to labor and became a husbandman. In the Hebrew it is written thus: Issachar, a bony ass, lying down between the borders: and seeing that rest was good and the land that it was beautiful, he bowed his shoulder to carry: and became one serving under tribute. Because above he had said of Zebulun that he would possess the shores of the great sea and would reach Sidon too and the other cities of Phoenicia, he now returns to the inland province and, by his blessing, makes Issachar an inhabitant, because he is to possess the most beautiful region in Galilee, next to Naphtali. And he calls him a bony ass and a shoulder given over to carrying, because he would labor very greatly in working the land and in conveying to the sea the burdens that were produced within his borders, bringing tributes to kings as well. The Hebrews say that it is signified by a metaphor that, meditating on the holy Scriptures day and night, he gave his zeal to laboring, and therefore all the tribes serve him, bringing gifts as to a master.

49:17-18

Dan shall judge his people, as one of the tribes of Israel. Let Dan become a serpent in the way, a basilisk in the path, biting the hoofs of the horse, and his rider shall fall backward. I will await your salvation, O Lord. Samson, a judge of Israel, was of the tribe of Dan. This, then, is what he says: now, seeing in the spirit Samson your Nazirite growing his hair and triumphing over his slain enemies, in that, in the likeness of a serpent and a basilisk besieging the ways, he allows no one to pass through the land of Israel, but even if some rash man, trusting in his own strength as in the swiftness of a horse, should wish to ravage it in the manner of a robber, he will not be able to escape. And he says the whole of it through the metaphor of the serpent and the rider. Seeing, then, your Nazirite so strong, and that he too died on account of a harlot and in dying slew our enemies, I thought, O God, that he himself was Christ your Son; but because he died and did not rise again, and Israel was again led captive, another Savior of the world and of my race must be awaited by me, that he may come for whom it is laid up, and he shall be the expectation of the nations.

49:19

Gad, a robber shall rob him: and he himself shall rob the heel. We have translated according to the Hebrew. But where we have put robber, there gedud is written, so as to allude to the name Gad, which can be expressed more significantly as εὔζωνος, that is, girded or unencumbered. And the whole of it is this: that, returning ahead of Reuben and the half-tribe of Manasseh, after fourteen years, to the sons whom he had left in possession beyond the Jordan, he found a great war of the neighboring nations against them and, having conquered the enemies, fought bravely. Read the book of Joshua son of Nun and Chronicles. I am not unaware that there are more mysteries in the blessings of the patriarchs, but that does not belong to the present little work.

49:21

Naphtali, a loosened shoot, giving beauty in generation. In the Hebrew it is written thus: Naphtali, a well-watered field, giving words of beauty, signifying that hot waters spring up in that very tribe, or that, lying above the lake of Gennesaret, it is watered by the stream of the Jordan. But the Hebrews would have it that the well-watered field and the words of beauty are prophesied on account of Tiberias, which was seen to have knowledge of the Law. Moreover, where we have put a well-watered field and the Seventy στέλεχος, that is, a loosened shoot, in the Hebrew one reads aiala selua, which can also be translated a hart let loose, showing the swiftness of a richer land in its early crops. But it is better if we refer everything to the teaching of the Savior, since it was there above all that he taught: as is also written in the Gospel.

49:22-26

Joseph, a son increased, a son increased over a spring; daughters walking with measured step upon the wall. And they embittered him and contended: and those who had arrows were angry against him. His bow sat in the strong one, and the bonds of his hands were broken, by the hands of the mighty one of Jacob: thence the stone of Israel shall be fed by the God of your father and the rest. Because the Seventy translators disagree in very many things, we have set down, in place of their translation, what is in the Hebrew. And the sense of the passage is: O Joseph, who are so called because the Lord has increased you for me, or because you are to be greater among your brothers (for the tribe of Ephraim was indeed the strongest, as we read in the books of Kingdoms and of Chronicles), O, I say, my son Joseph, who are so beautiful that the whole throng of the girls of Egypt gazes at you from the walls and towers and windows, your brothers envied you and provoked you to anger, having the arrows of spite and wounded by the darts of jealousy. But you placed your bow and your weapons of fighting in God, who is a strong champion, and your bonds, with which your brothers bound you, were loosed and broken by him, so that from your seed the tribe of Ephraim might be born, strong and stable and unconquered like a harder stone, ruling also over the ten tribes of Israel.

49:27

Benjamin, a ravening wolf, in the morning shall still eat, and at evening shall give food. Although it is a most manifest prophecy concerning Paul the apostle, that in his youth he persecuted the Church and in his old age was a preacher of the Gospel, yet in the Hebrew it reads thus: Benjamin, a ravening wolf (or seizing), in the morning shall eat the prey and at evening shall divide the spoils. Which the Hebrews expound thus: the altar on which the sacrifices were offered, and at whose base the blood of the victims was poured out, was in the portion of Benjamin. This, then, they say, is what it signifies: that the priests offer the sacrifices in the morning and at evening divide the things which have been brought to them by the people according to the Law; they set the bloodthirsty wolf, the voracious wolf, upon the interpretation of the altar, and the division of the spoils upon the priests, who, serving the altar, live from the altar.
