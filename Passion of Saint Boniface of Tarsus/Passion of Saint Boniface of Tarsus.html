Passion of Saint Boniface of Tarsus

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the Greek Passion of Boniphatios (BHG 279–280), by Stratis Papaioannou, appears in his anthology Saints at the Limits: Seven Byzantine Popular Legends (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 78; Harvard University Press, 2023; ISBN 978-0-674-29079-2), available from the publisher. The Passion is translated complete, with Papaioannou’s edition of the Greek text on facing pages; the volume gathers seven popular Byzantine saints’ legends, with an introduction and notes on the text and its manuscripts. The legend tells of Boniface, the steward and lover of Aglaïs, a rich lady of Rome, a man given to drink and pleasure but generous to the poor. Aglaïs sends him to the East to bring back relics of the martyrs; at Tarsus he sees Christians being tortured, declares himself a Christian, and is tortured and beheaded. His companions carry his body back to Rome, where Aglaïs, converted, builds a shrine for it and spends the rest of her life in penance. The Greek Church keeps the feast of Boniface and Aglaïs on 19 December. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 November 2023.)
