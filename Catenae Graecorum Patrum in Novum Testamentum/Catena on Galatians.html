Catena on Galatians

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Greek catena on Galatians printed by J. A. Cramer in Catenae Graecorum Patrum in Novum Testamentum, vol. 6 (Oxford, 1842), has been translated in full by Bill Berg as Paul’s Letter to the Galatians: A Fourth-Century View: Cramer’s Catena of Commentary from John Chrysostom, Theodore of Mopsuestia, Severian of Gabala, and Eusebius of Emesa (Consolamini Publications, 2015, printed through CreateSpace; ISBN 978-1-5116-0794-0), with a short introduction and the translator’s notes on the Greek text in footnotes; the publisher has no web page for it, but it is listed at Open Library. A catena (Latin for “chain”) is a commentary made by stringing together excerpts from earlier interpreters, each headed with its author’s name. This one comments on Paul’s letter passage by passage, drawing mainly on John Chrysostom, Theodore of Mopsuestia, Severian of Gabala and Eusebius of Emesa; some of its extracts survive nowhere else and appear in English here for the first time. Berg arranges the letter under the twelve headings into which it was divided in antiquity. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 23 April 2015.)
