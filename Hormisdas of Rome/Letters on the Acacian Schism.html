Letters on the Acacian Schism

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. English translations of a selection of the key letters of the Acacian schism, including the Libellus (formula of faith) of Hormisdas, are posted on the Ubi Petrus Ibi Ecclesia blog in “The Libellus of Hormisdas and the Failure of Policy” (2022, updated 2024). The translator is not named, and the texts are taken from Thiel’s Epistolae Romanorum pontificum genuinae and the Collectio Avellana. Its licence is not stated. This is a selection, not the complete correspondence, and the blogger says more letters will be added over time. The letters include those of Popes Anastasius II and Symmachus to the East; Hormisdas’ instructions to his legates Ennodius and Fortunatus in 515, sent with the Libellus; his letters to the emperors Anastasius and Justin, to Justinian, to Patriarch Epiphanius of Constantinople and to the monks of Syria Secunda; and letters to Hormisdas from Justin, Justinian, the patriarchs John II and Epiphanius, and the deacon Dioscorus. The schism divided Rome from Constantinople from 484 to 519. It began when Pope Felix III condemned Patriarch Acacius of Constantinople, who backed the emperor Zeno’s Henoticon, and it ended when Patriarch John II signed the Libellus, which declares that the Catholic faith has always been kept unblemished in the apostolic see. A short introduction on the blog discusses how the Libellus was later used in arguments for papal authority. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 14 August 2024.)
