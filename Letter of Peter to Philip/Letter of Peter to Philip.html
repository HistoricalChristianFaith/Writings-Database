Letter of Peter to Philip

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The copy of the letter in Codex Tchacos is published, with the Coptic text and an English translation, in The Gospel of Judas, Together with the Letter of Peter to Philip, James, and a Book of Allogenes from Codex Tchacos: Critical Edition, with introductions, translations and notes by Rodolphe Kasser, Gregor Wurst, Marvin Meyer and François Gaudard (National Geographic, Washington, 2007; ISBN 978-1-4262-0191-2); no publisher's page for it is still online (see its Open Library record). It is an edition of the whole codex, in which the letter is the first of the four texts. The other surviving copy, in Nag Hammadi Codex VIII, is translated by Frederik Wisse in The Nag Hammadi Library in English, ed. James M. Robinson, and by Marvin Meyer in The Nag Hammadi Scriptures (HarperOne, 2007). The Letter of Peter to Philip is a Gnostic writing, probably composed in Greek in the late second or early third century and preserved only in Coptic. It opens as a letter in which Peter calls Philip back to the company of the apostles, and then becomes an account of their gathering on the Mount of Olives, where the risen Christ appears to them as a light and a voice and answers their questions about the deficiency of the aeons, the powers that oppose them, and how they are to fight them; it ends with the apostles going out to preach. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 November 2007.)
