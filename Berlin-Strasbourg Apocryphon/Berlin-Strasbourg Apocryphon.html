Berlin-Strasbourg Apocryphon

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Alin Suciu, with a new edition of the Coptic text of all three manuscripts, is in his doctoral thesis, Apocryphon Berolinense/Argentoratense (Previously Known as the Gospel of the Savior). Reedition of P. Berol. 22220, Strasbourg Copte 5-7 and Qasr el-Wizz Codex ff. 12v-17r with Introduction and Commentary (Université Laval, 2013), which can be downloaded free as a PDF from Theses Canada (Library and Archives Canada); the copy Suciu first posted on his own blog is no longer online. Its licence is not stated. The work, long known as the Gospel of the Savior or the Unbekanntes Berliner Evangelium, survives in fragments of two Sahidic Coptic manuscripts, a parchment codex of the seventh or eighth century in Berlin (P. Berol. 22220) and a papyrus codex of about 600 in Strasbourg (Copte 5–7), together with a reworked extract of its Hymn of the Cross in a Nubian codex from Qasr el-Wizz. Told by the apostles in the first person plural, it recounts a revelation of the Saviour to them shortly before the Crucifixion, much of it taken up by a hymn that Christ sings to the cross while the apostles answer “Amen”. Suciu argues that it is not an early gospel but one of the Coptic “apostolic memoirs”, composed after the Council of Chalcedon (451). Other English translations: Alin Suciu, The Berlin-Strasbourg Apocryphon: A Coptic Apostolic Memoir (Wissenschaftliche Untersuchungen zum Neuen Testament 370; Mohr Siebeck, 2017; ISBN 978-3-16-155106-2), available from the publisher; Charles W. Hedrick and Paul A. Mirecki, Gospel of the Savior: A New Ancient Gospel (California Classical Library; Polebridge Press, 1999; ISBN 978-0-944344-68-2), covering the Berlin papyrus only.
