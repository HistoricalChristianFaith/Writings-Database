Chronicle

No finished English translation of this work is available. A provisional English translation by Alexander Hourani, The chronicle of the year 693 in Sinaitic Arabic 597: English translation (dated 13–16 February 2026), made from his own transcription of the Arabic manuscript, is posted free on the Internet Archive together with the transcription: see “The chronicle of the year 693 in Sinaitic Arabic 597” (PDF and Word files). It runs from the opening of the chronicle (fol. 85b) to its end (fol. 155b), with gaps where leaves are missing or the manuscript is damaged or illegible. The archive.org item carries the Creative Commons Public Domain Mark, but no other licence is stated, and the translation is a working draft that Hourani is still revising, so it is linked here rather than copied; the archive.org copy is the most up to date. Hourani prefers to call it the Chronicle of 693. The chronicle is an anonymous Christian world chronicle in Arabic, preserved in a single thirteenth-century manuscript at St Catherine’s Monastery, Mount Sinai (Sinai Arabic 597), where it follows the book of Daniel and the Proverbs of Solomon. Adrian Pirtea, who identified it and named it the “Maronite Chronicle of 713” (Medieval Worlds 23, 2025, pp. 155–167, open access), takes it to be a translation of a lost Syriac work composed in 712–713, probably in Monothelete circles close to the Monastery of Mar Maron; Hourani regards it as an abridged translation of the Syriac chronicle of Theophilus of Edessa. It runs from Adam to 692–693, reckoning by the Seleucid era, and shares much material with the lost “Eastern source” used by Theophanes, Agapius of Mabbug, Michael the Syrian and the Syriac Chronicle of 1234, so it is a new independent witness to the early Arab conquests. Pirtea has a critical edition and annotated translation in preparation.
