Passion of Marius, Martha, Audifax and Abachum

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, by Michael Lapidge, appears in his The Roman Martyrs: Introduction, Translations, and Commentary (Oxford Early Christian Studies; Oxford University Press, 2018; ISBN 978-0-19-881136-7), pp. 420–435, available from the publisher. The Passion (BHL 5543) is translated complete, with Lapidge’s introduction and commentary, as one of the forty Latin passions of the martyrs of Rome collected in the volume; the Latin text is printed in the Acta Sanctorum under 19 January. The legend, probably composed in the sixth century, tells of Marius and Martha, a noble Persian couple, who come to Rome with their sons Audifax and Abachum in the reign of Claudius to pray at the shrines of the apostles, help Christians held in prison, and bury the bodies of martyrs. Arrested and tortured, they refuse to give up their faith; Marius and his sons are beheaded on the Via Cornelia and Martha is drowned in a well, and a Roman lady buries their remains on her estate. Several chapters tell of Valentine, a Roman priest, who heals the blind adopted daughter of the officer Asterius and baptizes his household before being beheaded on the Via Flaminia on 14 February; these chapters were later excerpted as a separate Passion of St Valentine of Rome (BHL 8465). Pearse has quoted the Valentine chapters (6–11 and 15) of Lapidge’s translation, with his own renderings of the variant endings of the excerpt.
