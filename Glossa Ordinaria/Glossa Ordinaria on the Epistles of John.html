Glossa Ordinaria on the Epistles of John

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The section of the Gloss on the three letters of John was translated by a team from the Glossa Ordinaria Project (Jacob Cerone, Sarah Van Der Pas, Richard Keith Frazine, Matthew Zawadzki and others) under the editorship of John D. Litteral, as Glossa Ordinaria In English: The Epistles of St. John 1-3 (self-published through CreateSpace, 2014; ISBN 978-1-4953-4806-8); it is described on the Glossa Ordinaria Project’s page for 1–3 John (archived copy). It is a complete translation of this section: for each verse of 1, 2 and 3 John it gives the biblical text followed by both the marginal and the interlinear glosses. The Glossa Ordinaria is the standard Latin Bible commentary of the Middle Ages, put together in the twelfth century, largely in the circle of Anselm of Laon, from short excerpts of the Fathers and later writers, which were copied in the margins and between the lines of the biblical text; it was long wrongly ascribed to Walafrid Strabo. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 June 2014.)
