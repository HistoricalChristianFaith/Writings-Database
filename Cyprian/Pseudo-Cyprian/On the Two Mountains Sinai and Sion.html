On the Two Mountains Sinai and Sion

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the De duobus montibus Sina et Sion by Stephen C. Carlson, made from Clara Burini’s Latin edition (Pseudo Cipriano: I due monti Sinai e Sion, Biblioteca Patristica 25, Fiesole, 1994), was first placed online free as a Google Docs document in 2007; that document is no longer available, but Carlson’s translation, with a short introduction, is now posted on his Academia.edu page. Its licence is not stated. The work is a short Latin treatise of uncertain authorship, probably written in North Africa in the third century and handed down among the works of Cyprian, whose style and theology it does not share. Reading the two mountains as figures, it sets Sinai, where the law was given to the Jews, against Sion, the figure of the new covenant in Christ, to argue that Christianity has taken the place of Judaism, making it an early example of Latin writing adversus Iudaeos; it also preserves some unusual traditions, among them a derivation of the name Adam from the four quarters of the world, and a reference to an otherwise unknown letter of John.
