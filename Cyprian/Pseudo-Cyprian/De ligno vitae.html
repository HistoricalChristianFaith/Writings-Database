De ligno vitae

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete poem is translated by Carolinne White, under the title De ligno crucis, in Early Christian Latin Poets (The Early Church Fathers; Routledge, 2000; ISBN 978-0-415-18782-4 hardback, 978-0-415-18783-1 paperback), pp. 136–139, an anthology of Christian Latin poetry from the fourth to the sixth centuries with an introduction to each poet; it is available from the publisher. The poem, of 69 lines and by an unknown author of about the late fourth century, was handed down among the works of Cyprian and of Tertullian, and is known variously as De ligno vitae (The Tree of Life), De ligno crucis (The Tree of the Cross) and De Pascha (On Easter). It describes the cross as a tree planted at Golgotha, the centre of the world, from a barren stump: its shoot springs up again on the third day, grows for forty days until it touches heaven, puts out twelve branches over the whole earth, and after fifty more days pours down heavenly dew; beneath it flows a spring around which all peoples gather, but they may pick its fruit only after washing in the spring, and the poem ends with some who fall back into the mire and others who keep the truth and on the seventh day come to bathe in the sacred spring. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 28 April 2010.)
