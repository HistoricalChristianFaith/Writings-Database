Life of Saint Pantaleon the Recluse

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation appears in the anthology Saints of Ethiopia (Scriptorium Press, 2024; ISBN 979-8-3009-0117-2), pp. 51–77, for which the publisher’s listing names no translator; it is available from the publisher. It is a complete and fully annotated translation of the Life as edited by Carlo Conti Rossini in the Corpus Scriptorum Christianorum Orientalium (CSCO 26, Scriptores Aethiopici 17, pp. 39–56), probably made from Conti Rossini’s Latin translation rather than directly from the Ge‘ez; the volume also contains texts on Frumentius, Moses the Ethiopian, the Martyrdom of Arethas and the Life of Yared, with an introduction on the origins of the Ethiopian Church. The Life is a fifteenth-century Ge‘ez account of Abba Pantaleon (Pantelewon), one of the Nine Saints, monks from the Roman world who came to Aksum towards the end of the fifth century. He shut himself up in a tiny cell on a hill near Aksum, where he stood in prayer for many years and worked miracles; King Kaleb sought his blessing before his campaign against the Jewish king of Himyar in South Arabia, and afterwards gave up his throne to become a monk beside him. The short notice on Pantaleon in the Ethiopian Synaxarion, a different work, is translated in E. A. Wallis Budge, The Book of the Saints of the Ethiopian Church, vol. 1 (Cambridge, 1928), under the month of Ṭeḳemt. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 19 December 2024.)
