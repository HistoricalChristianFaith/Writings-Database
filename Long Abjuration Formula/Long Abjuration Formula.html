Long Abjuration Formula

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Greek text is edited with a complete English translation by Samuel N. C. Lieu in Manichaeism in Mesopotamia and the Roman East (Religions in the Graeco-Roman World 118; Brill, Leiden, 1994; ISBN 978-90-04-09742-1), pp. 235–253, in his study “An Early Byzantine Formula for the Renunciation of Manichaeism” (first published in Jahrbuch für Antike und Christentum 26, 1983), where it is set out side by side with the Seven Chapters against the Manichaeans; the book is available from the publisher. The book collects Lieu’s studies of Manichaeism; the formula is given there in full, in Greek and English, with commentary. The Long Abjuration Formula (also called the Long Anathema, or the Long Formula for the Renunciation of Manichaeism) is a Greek formula of the Byzantine Church, compiled in the ninth or tenth century, to be pronounced by a Manichaean received into the Church. In a long series of anathemas the convert renounces Mani, his disciples and successors, the books of the sect and its teachings, and also related writings such as the Theosophy of Aristocritus, which it says tried to show that Judaism, paganism, Christianity and Manichaeism are one and the same doctrine. Much of its wording is taken from the sixth-century Seven Chapters, probably by Zacharias of Mitylene. The Greek text was first printed by Cotelier in his edition of the Apostolic Fathers, from which it passed into the Patrologia Graeca (cols. 1461–1472), and it is also in Alfred Adam, Texte zum Manichäismus (1954), pp. 97–103. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 4 January 2021, who quotes Lieu’s version of its anathema on Aristocritus.)
