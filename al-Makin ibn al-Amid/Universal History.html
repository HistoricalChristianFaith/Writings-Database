Universal History

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first part has been edited and translated by Martino Diez as al-Makīn Ǧirǧis Ibn al-ʿAmīd: Universal History. The Vulgate Recension. From Adam to the End of the Achaemenids (Arabic Christianity: Texts and Studies 6/1; Brill, Leiden, 2024; ISBN 978-90-04-54998-2), a critical edition of the Arabic text with a facing English translation and an introduction on the author’s life, the recensions of the work, and its sources and later influence; it is available from the publisher. It is a partial translation: it covers only the opening section of the chronicle (chapters 1–91), from Adam to the end of the Persian Achaemenid kings, and not the rest of the pre-Islamic history or the second half on the Islamic period. Al-Makīn Ǧirǧis Ibn al-ʿAmīd (1205–1273) was a Coptic Christian official in Egypt who compiled a world history in two halves: the first runs from the Creation to the rise of Islam and is drawn largely from Byzantine and Syriac material that had passed into Arabic, and the second covers Islamic history down to his own time. The second half was printed in 1625 by Thomas Erpenius with a Latin translation; the first half had never been printed before Diez’s edition. The work was widely read by Arabic-speaking Christians, in Ethiopia, by Muslim historians and in early modern Europe. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 29 November 2023; see also his post of 16 December 2013 on the “Testimonium Flavianum” in al-Makīn.)
