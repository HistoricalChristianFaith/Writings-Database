Legend of the Seven Sleepers (Coptic)

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the Coptic (Sahidic) version of the legend, by James Drescher, was published with the Coptic text and a commentary in Three Coptic Legends: Hilaria, Archellites, the Seven Sleepers (Supplément aux Annales du Service des Antiquités de l’Égypte, Cahier 4; Cairo: Institut Français d’Archéologie Orientale, 1947), pp. 100–119 (text pp. 32–67, printed alongside the Greek). The book can be read free online as a scan on the Internet Archive, and was formerly offered page by page as images on the Coptic Library site of calloflove.net, now preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine (the original domain no longer hosts the site). Its licence is not stated. Drescher translates the one complete Coptic text, a Pierpont Morgan manuscript; two other Coptic fragments survive, in the Vatican and in Vienna. He judges it a translation of a Greek text older than, but of the same family as, the Greek versions published by M. Huber, and his commentary sets out its agreements with them. The legend tells how, when the emperor Decius came to Ephesus and ordered all to sacrifice to the gods, seven young Christians gave away their goods and hid in a cave in the mountain outside the city, where they fell asleep; Decius had the mouth of the cave walled up. Awaking after many years, they sent one of their number into the city to buy bread, and his old coins betrayed him; he was brought before the bishop and the proconsul, and the youths’ awakening was proclaimed as a witness to the resurrection of the dead. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 July 2016.)
