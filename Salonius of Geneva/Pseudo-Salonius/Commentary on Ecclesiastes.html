Commentary on Ecclesiastes

No public-domain English translation of this work is available online. The only English translation known is William Pollard’s A misticall exposition of doctor Salonius, borne at Vienna and bishopp of Fraunce, upon the Ecclesiastes of Salomon, in manner of a dialogue, dated 1615. It is public domain by age, but its text is not online: Google Books has only a catalogue record (182 pages). A reader’s comment on Pearse’s post, citing WorldCat, identifies the copy as an unprinted manuscript of 91 leaves in the Brotherton Library, University of Leeds (Brotherton Collection MS Gen 5), written out by hand while Pollard, who is otherwise unknown, was in a London prison; it had not been digitized at the time. The commentary, the Expositio mystica in Ecclesiasten, is a dialogue in which Salonius, bishop of Geneva in the mid-fifth century and son of Eucherius of Lyon, answers questions put by his younger brother Veranus (another commenter notes that the speakers’ names were added by the sixteenth-century editor Brassicanus). It reads the book of Ecclesiastes spiritually: “a time to weep, and a time to laugh”, for example, is taken to mean that we weep in this present life and will laugh and rejoice in the new Jerusalem. The Latin text, together with Salonius’ exposition of Proverbs, is in Migne, Patrologia Latina 53, cols. 967–1012. Pearse thinks the attribution to Salonius is spurious, and the Vetus Latina list of Latin church writers (Frede/Gryson) classes the work as Pseudo-Salonius, later than 800; the same text is also transmitted under the name of Honorius of Autun (Patrologia Latina 172, cols. 311–348). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 24 October 2013.)
