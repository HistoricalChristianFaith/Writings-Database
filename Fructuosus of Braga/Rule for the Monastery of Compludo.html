Rule for the Monastery of Compludo

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The English translation, by Claude W. Barlow, is “Rule for the Monastery of Compludo,” in Iberian Fathers, Volume 2: Braulio of Saragossa, Fructuosus of Braga (The Fathers of the Church 63; Catholic University of America Press, 1969; ISBN 978-0-8132-0063-7; reissued 2014 in paperback, ISBN 978-0-8132-2630-9, and as an e-book, ISBN 978-0-8132-1163-3), with an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher. The book gives the complete Rule, together with Fructuosus’s General Rule for Monasteries, the monastic Pact, and his letter to King Receswinth, as well as the letters and other writings of Braulio of Saragossa. Fructuosus of Braga (d. 665), a Visigothic nobleman who became a monastic founder in north-western Spain and later bishop of Dumium and archbishop of Braga, wrote this Rule (Regula monachorum Complutensis) for Compludo, the first of his foundations. It regulates the monks’ prayer, work, food and obedience to the abbot, and sets out penalties for faults; the penalty in chapter 16 for a monk or cleric who molests boys was later circulated in canon-law collections under the name of Basil the Great, and was quoted as Basil’s by Peter Damian. The Latin text is printed in the Patrologia Latina, vol. 87, an edition Barlow judged “far from satisfactory, often corrupt.” (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 July 2018.)
