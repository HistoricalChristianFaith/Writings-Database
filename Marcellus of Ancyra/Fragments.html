Fragments

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation of all the surviving fragments of Marcellus’ book against Asterius the Sophist, made by several translators working with Glen L. Thompson from Markus Vinzent’s edition (Markell von Ankyra: Die Fragmente, 1997), is offered free on the Fourth Century Christianity website of Wisconsin Lutheran College, at “Marcellus of Ancyra Fragments”, one page per fragment, numbered after Vinzent (Pr and 1–128) with Klostermann’s numbers alongside, each giving the ancient writer who quotes it. The site is copyrighted and its licence for reuse is not stated. Marcellus, bishop of Ancyra (d. c. 374), took part in the Council of Nicaea and was a determined opponent of Arius’ supporters; his book against Asterius, which has not survived as a whole, was answered by Eusebius of Caesarea in his Against Marcellus and On Ecclesiastical Theology and most of its fragments survive as quotations in those two works. In them Marcellus argues that the Word is eternally in God rather than a second being produced by the Father, that the titles “Son”, “image” and “first-born” belong to the Word made flesh, and that at the end the Son will hand over the kingdom and the Word return into God. Other English translations: Kelley McCarthy Spoerl and Markus Vinzent, Eusebius of Caesarea, Against Marcellus and On Ecclesiastical Theology (The Fathers of the Church 135; Catholic University of America Press, 2017; ISBN 978-0-8132-2991-1), which includes the fragments quoted by Eusebius, available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 July 2015.)
