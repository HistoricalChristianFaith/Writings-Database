On the Correction of Peasants

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the De correctione rusticorum by Angus Graham (2001), made from Claude W. Barlow’s Latin edition (Martini Episcopi Bracarensis Opera Omnia, 1950, pp. 183–203), was offered free on Graham’s website as a Word document (BRAGRUSE.DOC) inside a zip file of Martin’s works; the site has disappeared, but his Martin of Braga page and the zip file are preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. Its licence is not stated. Martin, bishop of Braga in the Suevic kingdom of Gallaecia (d. 579), wrote the work around 572 as a letter to Bishop Polemius of Astorga, giving him a model sermon for country people: it retells the creation, the fall of the rebel angels and the flood, explains the pagan gods as demons who took the names of wicked men, and condemns surviving customs such as naming the days of the week after the gods, beginning the year on the calends of January, lighting candles at rocks, trees, springs and crossroads, and watching for omens, before recalling the hearers to their baptismal vows and to keeping Sunday. Other English translations: Claude W. Barlow, “Reforming the Rustics”, in Iberian Fathers, Volume 1: Martin of Braga, Paschasius of Dumium, Leander of Seville (The Fathers of the Church 62; Catholic University of America Press, 1969; ISBN 978-0-8132-2828-0), available from the publisher.
