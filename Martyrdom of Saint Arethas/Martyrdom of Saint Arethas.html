Martyrdom of Saint Arethas

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation, as “The Martyrdom of Saint Arethas”, appears in the anthology Saints of Ethiopia (Scriptorium Press, 2024; ISBN 979-8-3009-0117-2), pp. 78–122, for which the publisher’s listing names no translator; it is available from the publisher. It is a complete translation, in nine chapters, of the Greek text (BHG 166–166b) printed by J. F. Boissonade in his Anecdota Graeca, vol. 5 (Paris), pp. 1–62, and appears to be the first in English; the volume also contains texts on Frumentius, Moses the Ethiopian, Pantaleon the Recluse and the Life of Yared, with an introduction on the origins of the Ethiopian Church. Written in Greek in the sixth century, and drawing on earlier accounts preserved in Syriac, the Martyrdom tells of Arethas (al-Ḥārith), the aged leader of the Christians of Najran in South Arabia, and his companions, who were put to death around 523 when the Jewish king of Himyar, Dhu Nuwas, took the city and massacred its Christians; it goes on to tell how the emperor Justin I urged Kaleb, king of Aksum, to avenge them, how Kaleb invaded South Arabia and overthrew Himyarite rule, and how he afterwards gave up his throne to become a monk. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 19 December 2024.)
