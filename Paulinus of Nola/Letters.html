Letters

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete letters were translated by P. G. Walsh as Letters of St. Paulinus of Nola, with an introduction and notes, in two volumes (Ancient Christian Writers; Newman Press, 1966–67): Volume 1: Letters 1–22 (ACW 35; ISBN 978-0-8091-0088-0) and Volume 2: Letters 23–51 (ACW 36; ISBN 978-0-8091-0089-7); both are available from the publisher, Paulist Press. Paulinus (c. 354–431), a wealthy Aquitanian senator and former governor who gave away his estates and settled with his wife Therasia beside the shrine of St. Felix at Nola in Campania, later becoming its bishop, kept up a wide correspondence from there. His fifty-one surviving letters, most of them written between the mid-390s and the 410s, go to Sulpicius Severus, Delphinus and Amandus of Bordeaux, Victricius of Rouen, Augustine and Alypius, and other friends in Gaul, Italy and Africa. They discuss the renunciation of wealth, the ascetic and monastic life, Scripture and friendship in Christ, and describe the cult of Felix and the churches Paulinus built and decorated at Nola and Fundi. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 11 December 2009.)
