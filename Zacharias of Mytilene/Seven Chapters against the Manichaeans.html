Seven Chapters against the Manichaeans

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete Greek text, with an English translation, introduction and commentary, is edited by Samuel N. C. Lieu as “An Early Byzantine Formula for the Renunciation of Manichaeism: The Capita VII contra Manichaeos of ‹Zacharias of Mitylene›,” first published in Jahrbuch für Antike und Christentum 26 (1983), pp. 152–218, and reprinted in revised form in his Manichaeism in Mesopotamia and the Roman East (Religions in the Graeco-Roman World 118; Leiden: Brill, 1994; repr. 1999; ISBN 978-90-04-09742-1), pp. 203–304; it is available from the publisher. The Seven Chapters is a short Greek formula of abjuration, probably composed in the early sixth century by Zacharias of Mytilene: seven chapters of anathemas by which a convert renounces Mani, his disciples and writings, and the chief Manichaean teachings, such as the two principles and the sect’s accounts of creation and of man; among the books it condemns is the Theosophy of Aristocritus. It is preserved in a manuscript on Mount Athos (Vatopedi 236), where Marcel Richard found it in 1977, and it was later drawn on by the longer Byzantine formula of anathemas against the Manichaeans. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 4 January 2021.)
