Letter 43

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The letter was translated in full by Sebastian Brock in “Two Letters of the Patriarch Timothy from the Late Eighth Century on Translations from Greek,” Arabic Sciences and Philosophy 9 (Cambridge University Press, 1999), pp. 233–246, with a detailed commentary; the same article also translates Letter 48, and it is available from the publisher. In this short letter to the priest and teacher Pethion, Timothy reports that, at the command of the caliph al-Mahdi, Aristotle’s Topics has been translated from Syriac into Arabic with the help of the teacher Abū Nūḥ, and asks Pethion to search out, discreetly, any commentaries or scholia on the Topics, the Sophistical Refutations, the Rhetoric and the Poetics, together with other books: the second volume of a Syriac version of Gregory of Nazianzus revised by Athanasius of Balad, an anonymous Platonist treatise on the principles of bodies (since identified as Porphyry’s On Principles and Matter), Nemesius’ On the Nature of Man, and Dionysius the Areopagite. It is a glimpse of the Graeco-Syriac-Arabic translation movement at work in early Abbasid Baghdad. The Syriac text, with a German translation, is edited by Martin Heimgartner, Die Briefe 42–58 des Ostsyrischen Patriarchen Timotheos I (CSCO 644–645; Peeters, 2012). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 July 2024.)
