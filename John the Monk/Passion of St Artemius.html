Passion of St Artemius

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the Artemii Passio (“The Ordeal of Artemius”, BHG 170–171c, CPG 8082) by Mark Vermes, with an introduction and notes by Samuel N. C. Lieu, is included in the anthology From Constantine to Julian: Pagan and Byzantine Views. A Source History, edited by Samuel N. C. Lieu and Dominic Montserrat (London and New York: Routledge, 1996; ISBN 978-0-415-09336-1, paperback), pp. 224 ff., alongside the Origo Constantini, the Panegyric of 310, a Byzantine life of Constantine and Libanius’ Oration 59; it is available from the publisher. The Passio tells the story of Artemius, a commander under Constantius II and governor of Egypt, who was put to death at Antioch under Julian the Apostate; much of it is taken up with his defiant speeches to the emperor. It is a work of hagiography from the eighth or ninth century, attributed in the manuscripts to a monk named John (sometimes called John of Rhodes) or to John of Damascus, and it is valued chiefly because its author drew heavily on the lost Church History of Philostorgius, preserving passages of it, among them the last oracle of Delphi. The Greek text is in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 96, cols. 1252–1320. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 11 September 2018.)
