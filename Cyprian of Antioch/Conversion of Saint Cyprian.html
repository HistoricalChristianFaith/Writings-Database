Conversion of Saint Cyprian

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Greek text is translated in The Conversion of Saint Cyprian: The Unabridged Greek Acts (Montreal: Scriptorium Press, 2023; ISBN 979-8866756322), published under the press's name without a named translator, with an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher. It is a complete translation of both Greek accounts of the conversion: the shorter Acts (BHG 452) as Book I, and the longer first-person account, also called the Confession, as Book II; the separate Martyrdom is not included. The legend tells of Cyprian, a sorcerer of Antioch, who called up demons to win the Christian virgin Justina for a client, saw them defeated each time by her prayer and the sign of the cross, then burned his books of magic and was baptized. The story was already circulating in the fourth century, and Gregory of Nazianzus mixes it with the life of Cyprian of Carthage in his Oration 24; the Scriptorium Press introduction likewise takes the two Cyprians to be one man. A public-domain translation of the Syriac version of the legend, the conversion and martyrdom together, is Agnes Smith Lewis, Select Narratives of Holy Women (Studia Sinaitica 10; London, 1900), pp. 185–203; Anthony Alcock's translation of the Coptic Confession and Martyrdom is available on this site under "Confession and Martyrdom (Coptic)". (Noted by Roger Pearse, 3 December 2024.)
