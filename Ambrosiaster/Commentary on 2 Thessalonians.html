Commentary on 2 Thessalonians

Latin source: the commentary on the Second Epistle to the Thessalonians by the anonymous Roman writer called Ambrosiaster (written at Rome about 366–384), from the Maurist text of 1690, printed under the name of Ambrose in J.-P. Migne, Patrologia Latina 17 (Paris, 1845), cols. 453–462, verified against the page images and collated in full with the Maurist edition (Paris, 1690). Migne's misprints are corrected where the Maurist edition he reprints or Ballerini's edition (Milan, 1877) has the right reading, as are his misprinted Scripture references and verse labels. On 2:5 the comment reads fatus est ("he said") with Vogels' critical text and Atto of Vercelli, where Migne and the Maurists print factus est. The lemmata are translated from Ambrosiaster's own Old Latin text of Paul, and all other Scripture as he quotes it, from his own Latin; italics mark the lemmata and the other Scripture he quotes. The edition's verse labels are given as (2 Thess. chapter:verse). This English translation is released into the public domain.

Prologue

Since in the first epistle given to the Thessalonians he wrote, among other things, some things also about the coming of the Lord and about the resurrection of the saints, he now writes another to them, in which he signifies, although obscurely (for he could not write openly), concerning the abolition of the Roman kingdom, and concerning the appearance and condemnation of the Antichrist, and concerning the restlessness of certain brothers.

Chapter I

(2 Thess. 1:1–3) Paul, and Silvanus, and Timothy, to the church of the Thessalonians in God our Father, and in the Lord Jesus Christ. Grace to you and peace from God our Father, and the Lord Jesus Christ. He writes to them in solemn words; for the head of the epistle is contained in the customary manner of address; and they are the same three in whose name it is written, as also in the first epistle (1 Thess. 1:1).

We ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers, as is fitting, because your faith grows exceedingly, and the love of each one of you all towards one another is multiplied. They profess themselves debtors to God in thanksgiving for the progress of these men; and to show that it was no ordinary debt, he says: as is fitting, so that they might testify that great thanks are to be returned to God for so infinite a gift. For after receiving the first epistle they persevered in being better than they had been, so that they appeared rich and abundant in that which, after devotion to the Lord and God, we have as a commandment, that we love one another (John 13:34).

(2 Thess. 1:4–5) So that we ourselves glory about you in the churches of God for your patience and faith in all your persecutions and afflictions, which you endure as an example of the just judgment of God, to this end, that you may be counted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you also suffer. He testifies that his glory lies in their way of life and in the persecution they have endured, so much so that their examples are set forth among the other churches, so that by imitating them these may grow in devotion to suffering for Christ, and may deserve to obtain what has been promised to the saints on the day of the just judgment of God, for love of whom they did not hesitate to despise the world. For these very afflictions are examples of future deserts: in those who suffer, unto glory; but in those who persecute, unto perdition.

(2 Thess. 1:6–9) Since indeed it is just with God to repay affliction to those who oppress you, and to you who endure affliction, rest with us at the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven with the angels of his power, in a fire of flame, giving vengeance on those who have not known God, and on those who do not obey the Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ, who will pay the penalty in eternal destruction from the face of the Lord, and from the glory of his power. Since he said that an example of the just judgment of God appears, whether upon the good or upon the wicked, he therefore went on to say: Since indeed it is just with God, that he should render to each according to his works. What is so just as that those who in this world oppress the good, and make them exiles by persecutions, should in the future suffer the same things that they do, while the others are at rest with the rest of the saints, who, coming out of great tribulation, will receive the eternal kingdom at the coming of the Lord from heaven, when he begins to come with his heavenly army and with fire as his minister to give vengeance on the pagans, who do not know that God is the Father of Jesus Christ, and on the Jews, who, though they say that they know God, do not believe the Gospel of Christ, which God the Father gave him to hand on to his servants? All these the fire will burn up by the presence of the Lord and the glory of his majesty, so that they may pay the due penalties of destruction for ever, always suffering, yet never wholly failing, so that the penalty itself in a way always generates them, by which they may be consumed.

(2 Thess. 1:10) When he comes to be glorified in his saints, and to be made wonderful in all who have believed; because our testimony concerning you has found faith, on that day of the coming of the Lord. It contains a twofold sense; for he will come to punish the wicked and to glorify the good. For he himself will be seen glorious and wonderful towards those who believe through the example and faith of the apostles, when they have been crowned, the Gospel rendering testimony to them on the day of the Lord; but he will appear severe towards the unbelieving, when they begin to be confined in eternal penalties. For the crown of the disciples is the glory of the teacher, and his truth is the penalty of the unbelieving, because what was preached was not believed.

(2 Thess. 1:11–12) In which also we pray always for you, that our God may count you worthy of his calling, and fulfil every good pleasure of goodness, and the work of faith in power, so that the name of our Lord Jesus Christ may be glorified in you, and you in him, according to the grace of our God, and of the Lord Jesus Christ. To his joy he adds prayers also, that, because they are devoted, God may deign to help them, so that they may complete the work they have begun, to the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ, so that the perseverance of the disciples may be a testimony to the teacher, unto glory. For the authority of the teacher lies in the confidence of the disciples, when they make themselves more worthy in the grace they have received.

Chapter II

(2 Thess. 2:1–4) But we ask you, brothers, by the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, and of our gathering together to him, that you be not easily moved from your mind, nor be troubled, either by spirit, or by word, or by epistle as if sent from us, as though the day of the Lord were at hand. Let no one deceive you in any way; for unless the failing comes first, and that man of sin is revealed, the son of perdition, who opposes, and is exalted above all that is called God, or that is worshipped, so that he sits in the temple of God, showing himself as though he were God. He beseeches them not to accept lightly and easily the opinion that the coming of the Lord is, as it were, imminent; and he gives such commands that, even if anyone has spoken through the spirit as a prophet, he ought not to be believed; nor is assent to be given if it comes through a treatise, or through an epistle written perhaps in the name of the apostles. For dissemblers, in order to deceive, are accustomed to forge an epistle under the name of some illustrious man, so that the authority of the name may commend what could not be accepted on its own account. He does this so that, while they were found off their guard in this very disturbance, they might not be led astray into worshipping the devil, who will boast these things for this reason: that, appearing under the name of the Savior and deceiving the saints, he may wish to be worshipped, so as to deceive those who believe in Christ. But so that most shameless Satan may not have the place or occasion of deceiving which he thinks he has, he has marked out the time and the signs of the coming of the Lord: that the Lord will not come before the failing of the Roman kingdom takes place, and the Antichrist appears, who will kill the saints, freedom having been restored to the Romans, yet under his own name. For knowing that the Lord will come to crush him, he will usurp his name for himself; and so that his kingdom may seem to be true, he will draw with him those who are to perish together with him, so that he sits in the house of the Lord in the seat of Christ, and asserts that he himself is God, not the Son of God. Hence in the Gospel the Lord says to the Jews: I have come in the name of my Father, and you have not received me; if another comes in his own name, him you will accept (John 5:43). Wherefore it is to be expected that he will come from the circumcision, or circumcised, so that the Jews may have the confidence to believe him. And so, in writing to the Thessalonians, the Apostle teaches all that they ought to be cautious in a matter of this kind.

(2 Thess. 2:5–6) Do you not remember that when I was still with you, I used to tell you these things? And now you know what holds him back: to this end, that he may be revealed in his own time. He says that this remains: that he who is to come before the Lord be revealed, so that then the Lord too may be expected to come; which he said above as if under a veil, saying: Unless the failing comes first, which, as I recalled above, is to be understood as the abolition of the Roman kingdom; so that when it has failed, and the Antichrist has come, then the coming of the Lord may be believed to be arriving.

(2 Thess. 2:7) For the mystery of iniquity is already at work, only that he who now holds may hold, until such time as he is taken out of the midst. The mystery of iniquity was begun by Nero, who out of zeal for idols killed the apostles at the instigation of his father the devil, and runs down to Diocletian, and most recently Julian, who with a certain art and subtlety could not complete the persecution he had begun, because it had not been granted from above. For Satan uses these ministers so that meanwhile, under cover of the throng of gods, he may make a mockery of the manifestation of the one true God in order to seduce men, as long as the Roman kingdom stands; this is what he says: Until he is taken out of the midst.

(2 Thess. 2:8–9) And then that wicked one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill with the spirit of his mouth, and will destroy with the brightness of his presence: whose coming is according to the working of Satan, in all power, and signs and portents of falsehood. He says that the Antichrist will appear after the failing of the Roman kingdom, as has been mentioned. For in the last time the devil, knowing that his destruction is at hand (and, as the Roman kingdom fails, he too will be sent down from the heavens, cast out upon the earth, as is said in the Apocalypse of the apostle John), will suborn for himself one in whom and through whom, performing certain signs of power by the permission of the just God, he may commend himself, so as to be worshipped as though he were God. For he will imitate God, so that, just as the Son of God, born or made man, demonstrated his divinity by signs and powers, so Satan too will appear in a man, so as to show himself God by powers of falsehood, as was said above. And this is the revelation of the mystery of iniquity: when the Antichrist has appeared, he will be recognized to be, as it were, the God of those whom formerly the common people worshipped as gods at his nod, he himself being the first or highest of them, which he will make credible by the powers of his signs. And he spoke of portents of falsehood for this reason, because he wishes himself to be believed through signs to be what he is not, so as to deceive those whom he is going to ensnare; and this has been foretold to the saints so that they may know what to beware of.

(2 Thess. 2:10) And in all the deceit of iniquity, for those who are perishing, because they did not receive the love of the truth, that they might be saved. He says that the deceit of the iniquity of his prodigies prevails with those who are going to perish; for because they spurned the love of the truth, through which they could have been saved when the apostles preached, they have been handed over to the devil; for being unwilling to be saved, they are deserted by God.

(2 Thess. 2:11) And for this reason God will send them the working of error, so that they believe falsehood, in order that all may be judged who did not believe the truth, but consented to iniquity. The working of error is to entrust one's faith to false things, so that those who were unwilling to agree that the brightness of light is day think that darkness ought to be called day; so that, made guilty of a more wilful offence, they may be condemned without contradiction as enemies of the truth and abettors of iniquity.

(2 Thess. 2:12–13) But we ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers beloved by the Lord, because God has taken you up from the beginning unto salvation, in sanctification of the spirit and faith of the truth, to which he called you through our Gospel, for the acquiring of the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ. In this place the foreknowledge of God resounds, because he knows the minds of all before their birth; for it is not hidden from him who are going to believe. Therefore he knows from the beginning that those will be faithful who, believing, bring increase to the faith, not loss; for, sanctified through the faith of the truth of the Gospel of the Son of God, they are acquired for the increase of the glory of the body of Christ, as he says in another epistle too: that all things, he says, may grow unto the increase of God (Col. 2:19). For whoever, having deserted the devil, take refuge in the faith of Christ bring increase to God in the body of Christ, though before they had brought loss.

(2 Thess. 2:14) Therefore, brothers, stand firm, and hold our traditions, which you have learned, whether by word or by our epistle. So that the foreknowledge of God may remain unto their salvation, he therefore warns that they must stand and persevere in the tradition of the Gospel, so that they may take care lest, growing torpid in the works of God through sloth and idleness, they fail to complete what they have begun. For he foreknows that they believe and can remain on this condition: if they are roused by frequent admonition.

(2 Thess. 2:15–16) And may our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God and our Father, who loved us, and gave eternal consolation and good hope in grace, console your hearts, and establish them in every good work and word. Since the Father and the Son are one power, and one divinity and substance, he therefore did not hesitate to name first our Lord Jesus Christ, then God, our Father by his condescension, not by truth of nature, who loved us so much that he gave his Son for us (John 3:16), God for men, the Lord for servants, the true Son for adopted sons; so that his death might be our life, and his resurrection our justification, and his second coming the repose of our life, and glory for ever; so that this hope might be the consolation of present afflictions, and through it, grounded, they might grow in good works and teaching.

Chapter III

(2 Thess. 3:1–2) As for what remains, brothers, pray for us, that the word of the Lord may run and be glorified, as also among you, and that we may be delivered from importunate and wicked men; for faith is not of all. For the rest he urges them to pray that God may deign to direct his teaching on a tireless course and to pour it through the mouth of his Apostle into the ears of the hearers, and that he would restrain and calm the seditions of evil men who distrust the truth, and that confidence in speaking might grow in the Apostle, for its fruit and the conversion of many.

(2 Thess. 3:3) But God is faithful, who will establish them and guard them from evil. Since God has promised that his help will not be lacking in need to the faithful and to men of good life, for their strengthening, and these were emulators of the good as much in faith as in work; therefore he calls God faithful in his promises, because his providence was over them.

(2 Thess. 3:4) And we trust in the Lord concerning you, that what we have commanded you both do and will do. On this account they deserved that he should trust they would be kept safe by God's protection; therefore he does not doubt that the things he commanded them in the name of the Lord are being done by them and will be done.

(2 Thess. 3:5) And may the Lord direct your hearts in the love of God and the patience of Christ. He wishes for them from God this, which he does not doubt can be granted: that in the love of God their heart may advance to awaiting the coming of the Lord, so that, the world being condemned, the coming of him who conquered the world may be longed for.

(2 Thess. 3:6) And we charge you, brothers, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you separate yourselves from every brother walking intemperately, and not according to the tradition which you received from us. Since in every people not all are obedient to the word of teaching, following not the Law's pleasure but their own pleasure and counsel; on this account he commands that they be shunned, so that they may recognize that they themselves are going astray.

(2 Thess. 3:7–9) For you yourselves know how you ought to imitate us, since we did not live intemperately among you, nor did we eat bread from anyone for nothing; but in labor and toil, working night and day, so that we might not be a burden to any of you: not because we did not have the power, but that we might give ourselves as a pattern to you for imitating us. He says that they are not ignorant of his work and labor, which he carried out diligently to the end, so as not to need another's bread, that he might give them an example for imitating him. For he would not be blameworthy if he received bread for the care of the body from those to whose souls he was supplying spiritual food, which would procure for the body too immortality with glory. For then freedom can be unfettered, if one subjects himself to no one for the sake of the belly or of money, content with his own bread; above all because it advances more to praise if, when it is permitted and there is the power of taking, one refrains: such a man can benefit those who hear him; for they will have reverence for this strictness, and the things commanded by him cannot be passed over.

(2 Thess. 3:10) For when we were with you, we commanded you this, that if anyone does not wish to work, neither let him eat. He taught not only by words, but exhorted also by deeds; for it belongs to a fit teacher to fulfil in works what he teaches in words. For then learners know that what they hear is true, if they do not see it neglected by the teacher. For although it is not unknown that what is taught is manifestly true, yet if it begins to be neglected by the teacher, it will hardly profit the hearers: for works persuade more than words. Wherefore those are to be repaid with great rewards who, while their teachers are negligent, profit from words alone both in work and in understanding. So he was a pattern to those in the people who were of moderate or slender means, that they might learn how not to lose their freedom. Hence Solomon also: Seldom, he says, shall you bring your foot to your friend, lest, sated with you, he hate you (Prov. 25:17). For he who frequently comes to another's table, given over to idleness, must flatter the one who feeds him; whereas our religion calls men to freedom. And therefore if anyone, he says, scorns to eat, let him cease from work: but since no one can live without food, let him apply himself to laboring, so that, living by his own choice, he may have God favorable.

(2 Thess. 3:11) For we have heard that some walk among you intemperately, doing no work, but acting curiously. Those who desire to be idle make it their business that an occasion may arise by which they may make their entrance desirable to the houses of the rich; walking about, they subtly gather up deeds and the opinions of words, knowing what each one wishes to hear about what, so that they may be fed gladly and even sought after. This practice is very far removed from the discipline of the Lord; for the belly is the god of those who provide their necessities with a foul care.

(2 Thess. 3:12) Now those who are such we charge and exhort in the Lord Jesus Christ, that working with quietness they eat their own bread. To persuade them more easily, interposing the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, he exhorts them to obey, since it would be useful to them.

(2 Thess. 3:13–15) But you, brothers, do not fail in well-doing. And if anyone does not obey our word through the letter, mark him, and do not live in company with him, that he may be confounded with shame: and do not regard him as an enemy, but admonish him with counsels, as a brother. Because above he commanded that each one ought to eat his own bread, lest perhaps some of them should seize on it as an occasion for stinginess and unkindness, he therefore at once added: But you, brothers, do not grow weary doing good. For it is not he who is kind and generous that comes under reproach, but he who, though he can bear labor, wishes to spend his life in idleness. If the authority of the letter does not correct him, he commands that he be marked and that no one live in company with him, so that, put to shame, being shunned by all, he might submit himself to the precepts of the Apostle. Yet he wishes this to be done without anger and insult, so that he be patiently avoided; yet in such a way that, if the matter requires speaking with him, he should be admonished to suffer himself to be corrected and no longer to despise the precepts of the Apostle: and he wishes also that no familiarity be kept with him until he becomes obedient. For if he is provoked to anger by insults, he will become worse than he is; for he will be stirred up to contention and will begin to want to defend his error, and afterwards to be ashamed to amend himself, lest he seem afterwards to confess what before he excused.

(2 Thess. 3:16) Now may the God of peace himself give you peace always in every place. The Lord be with you all. Always, as is his way, he follows them with a good wish, as his dearest sons, desiring that peace always remain with them, which is the guide to eternal life. For the Lord promised that he would be with those who are peacemakers.

(2 Thess. 3:17) The greeting itself is mine, by the hand of Paul, which is the sign in every letter: so I write. Because of interpolators and corrupters of the Scriptures, he testifies that he always subscribes the greeting with his own hand in every letter of his, so that a letter which was not subscribed by his hand could not be credited under his name.

(2 Thess. 3:18) The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. This greeting was written by the hand of the Apostle, in which he wishes the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ to be with them. Amen.
