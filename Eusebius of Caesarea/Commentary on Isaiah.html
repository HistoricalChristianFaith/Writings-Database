Commentary on Isaiah

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete commentary was translated by Jonathan J. Armstrong, edited by Joel C. Elowsky, as Eusebius of Caesarea: Commentary on Isaiah (Ancient Christian Texts; IVP Academic, 2013; ISBN 978-0-8308-2913-2), the first English translation of the work, made from Joseph Ziegler’s critical edition (GCS, 1975), with an introduction by the translator; it is available from the publisher. The commentary, the earliest Christian commentary on Isaiah to survive, works through the whole book, setting the readings of Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion beside the Septuagint and moving between the historical sense of the prophecies and their fulfilment in Christ, the fall of Jerusalem and the calling of the Gentiles into the Church. Long known only from excerpts in the catenae, it was recovered in the twentieth century from the margins of a manuscript in Florence. Eusebius draws on Origen’s lost commentary on Isaiah, at points noting where its books ended, and refers to his own Onomasticon of biblical place names.
