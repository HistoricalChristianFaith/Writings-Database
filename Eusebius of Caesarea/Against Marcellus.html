Against Marcellus

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Kelley McCarthy Spoerl and Markus Vinzent in Eusebius of Caesarea: Against Marcellus and On Ecclesiastical Theology (The Fathers of the Church 135; Catholic University of America Press, 2017; ISBN 978-0-8132-2991-1), the first English translation of both works; it is available from the publisher. Eusebius wrote the two books Against Marcellus soon after Marcellus, bishop of Ancyra, was deposed by a council at Constantinople in 336, to defend that verdict: working through long quotations from Marcellus’s own treatise against Asterius the Sophist, he charges him with teaching, like Sabellius, that Father and Son are a single person, and, like Paul of Samosata, that Christ was a mere man, and with misreading the biblical texts at issue in the debates around Nicaea. Because Marcellus’s book is otherwise lost, these quotations are the main source for his thought. Eusebius followed the work with the three books of the Ecclesiastical Theology. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 29 August 2013, updated 2020.)
