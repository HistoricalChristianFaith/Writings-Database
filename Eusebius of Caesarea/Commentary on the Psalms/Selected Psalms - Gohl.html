Selected Psalms - Gohl

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Justin Gohl has made free English translations of Eusebius’s commentary on a number of whole psalms, the first in English, and posted them from 2020 on his Sophiaphile blog, each with the Greek text from Migne’s Patrologia Graeca beside the English: Psalm 8; Psalm 22 (23); Psalm 44 (45); Psalm 52 (53); Psalm 57 (58); Psalm 62 (63); Psalm 63 (64); Psalm 73 (74); Psalm 81 (82); Psalm 87 (88); Psalm 90 (91), in two parts (part 1, part 2); Psalm 92 (93); Psalm 94 (95); and the “hypotheseis” (summaries) of the Songs of Ascents, Psalms 119–133 (120–134). Psalms are numbered as in the Septuagint, with the Hebrew numbering in parentheses. Many of these are also gathered in one document on Academia.edu, “Eusebius of Caesarea, Commentary on the Psalms: A Translation of Selected Psalms” (a free Academia.edu login is needed to download it). Their licence is not stated. Eusebius’s Commentary on the Psalms, one of his longest works, survives complete for Psalms 51–95 and otherwise in extracts preserved in the catenae; it reads the psalms chiefly as prophecies of Christ and of the Church gathered from the nations, while attending to their titles, to the history of David, and to the other Greek versions of Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion. Andrew Eastbourne’s public-domain translation of the commentary on Psalm 51 is hosted in this collection. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 October 2020, updated 6 September 2021.)
