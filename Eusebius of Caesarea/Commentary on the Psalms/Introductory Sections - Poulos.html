Introductory Sections - Poulos

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. In 2010 Alex Poulos posted free English translations of the short introductory sections that stand at the head of the commentary in Migne’s Patrologia Graeca (vol. 23, cols. 66–76), each with the Greek text, on his blog (first at mapoulos.wordpress.com, now alexpoulos.com): “Eusebius on the Inscriptions of the Psalms: Some Abbreviated Teaching” (PG 23.66, with Migne’s Latin version); “A Hypothesis of Eusebius Pamphili”, on the authors and divisions of the Psalter; the “hypotheses”, the one-line statements of the theme of each psalm, for Psalms 1–15 (PG 23.68); “Eusebius on the Psalms”, in part 1 (PG 23.72–73) and part 2 (PG 23.73–76); and “Eusebius of Caesarea on the Interlude”; his structural overview of the commentary links them all. The translator says modestly that they “aren’t very good” and that he made them to help his own study of the text. Their licence is not stated. These prefatory pieces explain the terms “psalm”, “ode”, “psalm of an ode” and “ode of a psalm” used in the titles; tell how David, bringing the ark to Jerusalem, appointed Asaph, Heman, Ethan and Jeduthun to lead the singing before it, and explain the diapsalma (the Hebrew selah) as the pause that came when the inspiration of the Spirit briefly ceased and the singers fell silent; note the differences between the Hebrew and Greek Psalters; and account for the psalms not standing in historical order by their having been lost in times of idolatry and gathered again, perhaps by Ezra. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 7 July 2010.)
