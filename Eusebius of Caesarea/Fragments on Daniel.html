Fragments on Daniel

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation by Peter Papoutsis, made for John Literal, was posted with the Greek text on Roger Pearse’s blog (2 January 2014), with Literal’s permission; its licence is not stated. The fragments come from a catena and are ascribed to Eusebius of Caesarea; the Greek as posted carries the column marker of Migne’s Patrologia Graeca, vol. 24 (col. 528). The first is headed by the words of Psalm 29:7 (“The voice of the Lord dividing the flame of fire”), which it applies to the fire of the Babylonian furnace that spared the three youths (Daniel 3), but all of them deal with the Book of Daniel: the following ones read the Son of Man coming on the clouds and the saints receiving the kingdom (Daniel 7) as the general judgement and Christ’s everlasting reign, and connect them with Paul on the Lord’s descent from heaven (1 Thessalonians 4) and on the man of sin (2 Thessalonians 2); the last takes the cutting off of the anointing and the strengthening of the covenant for one week (Daniel 9:26–27) as the ending of the old sacrifices after Christ’s own sacrifice. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 2 January 2014.)
