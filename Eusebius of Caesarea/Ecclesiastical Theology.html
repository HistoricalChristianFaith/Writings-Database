Ecclesiastical Theology

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Kelley McCarthy Spoerl and Markus Vinzent in Eusebius of Caesarea: Against Marcellus and On Ecclesiastical Theology (The Fathers of the Church 135; Catholic University of America Press, 2017; ISBN 978-0-8132-2991-1), the first English translation of both works; it is available from the publisher. A short section, Book III, chapters 4–6, on the Holy Spirit, was earlier translated by John Mackett in his dissertation Eusebius of Caesarea’s Theology of the Holy Spirit (PhD, Marquette University, Milwaukee, 1990), pp. 225–244. In the three books of the Ecclesiastical Theology, written in the late 330s as a sequel to his two books Against Marcellus, Eusebius moves from attacking Marcellus of Ancyra to setting out his own teaching: that the Son is a living and distinct being, begotten by the Father before all ages and not a mere word or power within God, and that the Holy Spirit is a third, subordinate being, created through the Son; along the way he answers Marcellus’s reading of the key biblical texts. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 29 August 2013, updated 2020; Pearse reproduces Mackett’s version of III.4–6 in a post of 30 August 2013.)
