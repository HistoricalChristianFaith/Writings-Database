History of Saint Monacella

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Huw Pryce, printed with his new edition of the Latin text, is “A New Edition of Historia Divae Monacellae”, Montgomeryshire Collections 82 (1994), pp. 23–40, the journal of the Powysland Club; the volume can be read free in the National Library of Wales’s Welsh Journals Online (vol. 82), where the article can also be downloaded as a PDF. Its licence is not stated. The Historia Divae Monacellae is a short Latin legend of St Melangell (in Latin, Monacella) of Pennant Melangell in Powys, written in the late Middle Ages (its use of the word divus points to the late fifteenth century or later) and surviving only in copies of the sixteenth to nineteenth centuries. It tells how in 604 Prince Brochwel Ysgithrog, hunting at Pennant, found a hare sheltering under the hem of a virgin at prayer, from which his hounds retreated howling; how she told him that she was the daughter of an Irish king and had fled a marriage arranged by her father, and had lived there alone for fifteen years; and how he gave her the land as a perpetual sanctuary, where she lived thirty-seven more years among tame hares and gathered a community of virgins, and where those who violated her sanctuary met with divine vengeance. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 27 May 2026, who quotes Pryce’s translation in full.)
