On the Ages of the World and of Man

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Leslie George Whitbread, is in Fulgentius the Mythographer (Ohio State University Press, 1971; ISBN 0-8142-0162-8), pp. 177–231, a volume that translates, with introductions and notes, all five Latin works ascribed to Fulgentius the mythographer, following Rudolf Helm’s Teubner edition (1898). The publisher has made the book freely available through the Ohio State University Knowledge Bank (book page, PDF); the printed book is marked “All rights reserved”, and no open licence is stated for the free copy. Fabius Planciades Fulgentius was a Latin writer of Vandal North Africa in the late fifth or early sixth century, sometimes, though not certainly, identified with Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe. In On the Ages of the World and of Man (De aetatibus mundi et hominis), a work some late manuscripts assign to Fulgentius of Ruspe, the author summarizes world history, drawing on the Bible, Eusebius and Orosius, from Adam down to the Roman emperors up to Valentinian III, matching the ages of the world to the stages of a human life. Each of the fourteen surviving sections is a lipogram, written without one letter of the alphabet in turn (the first without A, the second without B, and so on to O); the work ends abruptly, and it is unclear whether the twenty-three sections it seems to promise were ever written. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 March 2009, and again on 13 July 2015.)
