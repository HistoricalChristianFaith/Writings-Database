The Explanation of Obsolete Words

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Leslie George Whitbread, is in Fulgentius the Mythographer (Ohio State University Press, 1971; ISBN 0-8142-0162-8), pp. 155–175, a volume that translates, with introductions and notes, all five Latin works ascribed to Fulgentius the mythographer, following Rudolf Helm’s Teubner edition (1898). The publisher has made the book freely available through the Ohio State University Knowledge Bank (book page, PDF); the printed book is marked “All rights reserved”, and no open licence is stated for the free copy. Fabius Planciades Fulgentius was a Latin writer of Vandal North Africa in the late fifth or early sixth century, sometimes, though not certainly, identified with Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe. The Explanation of Obsolete Words (Expositio sermonum antiquorum) is a short glossary written at the request of the author’s patron: sixty-two entries, each defining a rare or archaic Latin word (terms for burial customs, divination and sacrifice, foods, boats, utensils and the like) and illustrating it with a quotation from a named ancient author, with scraps of antiquarian lore along the way. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 March 2009, and again on 13 July 2015.)
