The Mythologies

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Leslie George Whitbread, is in Fulgentius the Mythographer (Ohio State University Press, 1971; ISBN 0-8142-0162-8), pp. 13–102, a volume that translates, with introductions and notes, all five Latin works ascribed to Fulgentius the mythographer, following Rudolf Helm’s Teubner edition (1898). The publisher has made the book freely available through the Ohio State University Knowledge Bank (book page, PDF); the printed book is marked “All rights reserved”, and no open licence is stated for the free copy. Fabius Planciades Fulgentius was a Latin writer of Vandal North Africa in the late fifth or early sixth century, sometimes, though not certainly, identified with Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe. The Mythologies (Mitologiae), his chief work, is in three books, each with a prologue: the first a long, whimsical and autobiographical one in which the Muse Calliope visits the author on his country estate and offers to guide him. He then retells fifty classical myths (22, 16 and 12 chapters in the three books), reading each as a moral allegory, mostly by way of etymologies of the characters’ names, and hints at a Christian purpose in stripping the Greek tales of their fiction. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 March 2009, and again on 13 July 2015.)
