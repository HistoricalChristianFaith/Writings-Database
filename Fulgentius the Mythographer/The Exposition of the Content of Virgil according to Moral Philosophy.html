The Exposition of the Content of Virgil according to Moral Philosophy

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Leslie George Whitbread, is in Fulgentius the Mythographer (Ohio State University Press, 1971; ISBN 0-8142-0162-8), pp. 103–153, a volume that translates, with introductions and notes, all five Latin works ascribed to Fulgentius the mythographer, following Rudolf Helm’s Teubner edition (1898). The publisher has made the book freely available through the Ohio State University Knowledge Bank (book page, PDF); the printed book is marked “All rights reserved”, and no open licence is stated for the free copy. Fabius Planciades Fulgentius was a Latin writer of Vandal North Africa in the late fifth or early sixth century, sometimes, though not certainly, identified with Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe. In the Exposition of the Content of Virgil (Expositio Virgilianae continentiae) the author summons the shade of Virgil, who lays bare the hidden meaning of the Aeneid as an allegory of human life, the adventures of Aeneas standing for the stages from birth and childhood to full manhood. The work was widely used in the Middle Ages as a model for allegorical reading of the classics; Whitbread adds supplementary notes. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 March 2009, and again on 13 July 2015.)
