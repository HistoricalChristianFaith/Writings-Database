The Spiritual Meadow

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English version of the Latin translation made by Ambrogio Traversari (Ambrosius Camaldulensis) in the fifteenth century, in 219 chapters as printed in Heribert Rosweyde’s Vitae Patrum (1615 and 1628) as Book X, was made by the Revd Benedict Baker (d. 2004) and is free online on his Vitae Patrum website (contents). Its licence is not stated. Baker’s version is a free, readable rendering rather than a close translation: in places it paraphrases and softens the Latin (in chapter 45, for instance, it leaves out the elder’s saying that it would be better to enter every brothel in the city than to give up venerating the icon of Christ and his Mother). The Pratum Spirituale (“Spiritual Meadow”) of John Moschus (c. 550–619), a monk of Palestine who travelled with his disciple Sophronius through the monasteries of Palestine, Syria, Egypt and the islands, and finished the book at Rome, is a collection of short stories and sayings of the monks, hermits and devout lay people he met or heard of, written down in the reign of Heraclius, around the Persian capture of Jerusalem in 614. Other English translations: John Wortley, The Spiritual Meadow (Pratum Spirituale) by John Moschos (Cistercian Studies 139; Cistercian Publications, 1992; ISBN 978-0-87907-539-2), a complete translation of the Greek text in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 87, with supplementary tales from the manuscripts, available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 October 2021, who compares chapter 45 in both versions.)
