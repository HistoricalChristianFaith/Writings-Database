History of the Vandal Persecution

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by John Moorhead as Victor of Vita: History of the Vandal Persecution (Translated Texts for Historians 10; Liverpool University Press, 1992; ISBN 978-0-85323-127-1), with an introduction and notes, from Karl Halm’s Latin edition in the Monumenta Germaniae Historica; it is available from the publisher. Victor, bishop of Vita in the province of Byzacena, wrote the Historia persecutionis Africanae provinciae in three books around 484, when the Arian Vandal kings were still ruling Roman Africa. It traces the sufferings of the Catholic clergy and people from the Vandal crossing into Africa in 429 through the reigns of Geiseric and his son Huneric: churches seized, bishops exiled and confessors tortured or killed, and on to the conference of bishops at Carthage in 484 and the persecution that followed it. It preserves the decrees of Huneric and the Book of the Catholic Faith that the Catholic bishops presented at that conference, whose quotation of 1 John 5:7 is one of the earliest witnesses to the “three heavenly witnesses”. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 29 September 2018.)
