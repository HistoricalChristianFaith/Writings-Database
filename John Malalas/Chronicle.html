Chronicle

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, by Elizabeth Jeffreys, Michael Jeffreys and Roger Scott, with other contributors, is The Chronicle of John Malalas: A Translation (Byzantina Australiensia 4; Melbourne: Australian Association for Byzantine Studies, 1986; ISBN 978-0-9593636-2-3), now distributed by Brill (e-book ISBN 978-90-04-34460-0) and available from the publisher. It is a complete translation of all eighteen books. John Malalas, a Greek-speaking official of Antioch writing in the sixth century, composed the earliest surviving Byzantine world chronicle, running from Adam to the later years of Justinian. Written in plain, near-colloquial Greek and arranged by reigns, it mixes biblical and classical legend, Roman and imperial history, and a great deal of local detail about Antioch and Constantinople, including earthquakes, buildings, games and riots such as the Nika riot of 532; it was widely used by later chroniclers. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 June 2012, who quotes book 12, chapter 10 from it and remarks that Malalas is not online in English; see also his posts of 25 July 2019 and 4 January 2021.)
