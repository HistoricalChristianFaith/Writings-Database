Commentary on the Gospel of John

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete commentary was translated by Marco Conti and edited by Joel C. Elowsky as Commentary on the Gospel of John (Ancient Christian Texts; IVP Academic, 2010; ISBN 978-0-8308-2906-4); it is available from the publisher. It is the first complete English translation, made from the Syriac version of the commentary. The Greek fragments alone were translated earlier by George Kalantzis, Theodore of Mopsuestia: Commentary on the Gospel of John (Early Christian Studies 7; Strathfield, NSW: St Pauls Publications, 2004; ISBN 978-0-9577483-9-2). Theodore, bishop of Mopsuestia in Cilicia (d. 428) and the leading exegete of the Antiochene school, wrote the commentary in Greek. Because his writings were condemned at the Council of Constantinople in 553, the Greek original survives only in fragments quoted in later collections, and the whole work is known only from a Syriac translation made in the later fifth century, edited by J.-M. Vosté (1940). Theodore works through the Gospel passage by passage, paying close attention to its literal and historical sense and keeping a careful distinction between what is said of Christ as God and as man. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 June 2009.)
