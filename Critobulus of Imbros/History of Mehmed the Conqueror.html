History of Mehmed the Conqueror

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, by Charles T. Riggs, is History of Mehmed the Conqueror by Kritovoulos (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1954; its US copyright was renewed in 1982); it was reissued in the Princeton Legacy Library in 2019 (ISBN 978-0-691-19790-6 paperback, 978-0-691-19791-3 hardcover) and is available from the publisher. It is a complete translation, made from Karl Müller’s Greek text in the Fragmenta Historicorum Graecorum, vol. 5 (1870), before the critical edition by Diether R. Reinsch (Corpus Fontium Historiae Byzantinae 22, 1983). Michael Critobulus (Kritoboulos) was a Greek notable of the island of Imbros who submitted to the Ottomans and governed the island for Sultan Mehmed II. He wrote, in a classicizing Greek modelled on Thucydides and with a dedication to the sultan, a history of Mehmed’s reign from his accession in 1451 to 1467. It is one of the main accounts of the siege and capture of Constantinople in 1453, including the sack of the city, and goes on to describe Mehmed’s repopulation of the city and his campaigns in Serbia, the Morea, Trebizond and the Aegean. The work survives in a single manuscript, found in the library of the Topkapı Palace in Istanbul in 1865.
