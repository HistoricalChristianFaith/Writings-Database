Chronicon Paschale

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, by Michael Whitby and Mary Whitby, is Chronicon Paschale 284–628 AD (Translated Texts for Historians 7; Liverpool University Press, 1989; ISBN 978-0-85323-096-0), with an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher. It is a partial translation: it covers only the final section of the chronicle, from the accession of Diocletian in 284 to 628, and not the earlier parts or the opening treatise on Easter. The Chronicon Paschale (“Easter Chronicle”) is an anonymous Greek world chronicle compiled at Constantinople around 630, in the reign of Heraclius, by a writer who lived through its last decades; it runs year by year from Adam onward and takes its name from its long introductory discussion of how the date of Easter is calculated. Its later entries are an important contemporary witness to events in the capital, such as the Nika riot of 532 and the Avar siege of 626. The complete Greek text, with Du Cange’s Latin translation, is printed in Dindorf’s Bonn edition (1832) and in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 92.
