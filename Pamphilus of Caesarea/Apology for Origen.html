Apology for Origen

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Thomas P. Scheck, is Apology for Origen, with the Letter of Rufinus On the Falsification of the Books of Origen (Fathers of the Church 120; Catholic University of America Press, 2010; ISBN 978-0-8132-0120-7; paperback 2020, ISBN 978-0-8132-2774-0), with an introduction and notes; it is available from the publisher. It translates all of the work that survives. Pamphilus, the presbyter and teacher of Caesarea who kept Origen's library there, wrote the Apology in prison during the Great Persecution, with the help of his pupil Eusebius, shortly before his martyrdom in 310. Of its original six books only the first remains, and that only in the Latin version made by Rufinus in 397; in it Pamphilus answers the charges brought against Origen's teaching on the Trinity, the Incarnation, the resurrection, the soul and other points, chiefly by quoting Origen's own words, so that it also preserves passages from his lost commentaries. The volume adds Rufinus' short treatise arguing that Origen's writings had been corrupted by heretics. Rufinus' preface to his translation and that treatise (in the older Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers version) are hosted here under Tyrannius Rufinus.
