Commentary on the Gospels

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete commentary was translated by H. A. G. Houghton, in association with Lukas J. Dorfbauer, as Fortunatianus of Aquileia: Commentary on the Gospels. English Translation and Introduction (Corpus Scriptorum Ecclesiasticorum Latinorum, extra seriem; Berlin and Boston: De Gruyter, 2017; ISBN 978-3-11-052420-8). It was made from Dorfbauer’s critical edition (CSEL 103, 2017) and is the first translation of the work into any modern language. The book is open access, free to download from De Gruyter and from the OAPEN Library. It is published under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives 4.0 licence, not in the public domain. Fortunatianus, an African by birth, was bishop of Aquileia in the mid-fourth century. Jerome mentions his brief commentary on the Gospels, but the work was then lost for more than a thousand years. Only a few fragments were known until 2012, when Dorfbauer recognised the whole text in a ninth-century manuscript in Cologne Cathedral Library (Codex 17). It is the earliest surviving Latin commentary on the Gospels. It opens with a “rule of the four Gospels”, linking the evangelists with the four living creatures and the four rivers of Paradise. A longer exposition of the opening of Matthew follows, and then a commentary in 160 short chapters on almost all of Matthew, part of Luke and the opening of John. The commentary reads the Gospel narrative figuratively, as a picture of Christ and the Church. Its biblical quotations follow the Old Latin version rather than the Vulgate. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 26 August 2017.)
