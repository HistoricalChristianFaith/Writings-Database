On the Blessings of the Patriarchs

Latin source: Ambrose's commentary on the blessings Jacob gave his sons (Genesis 49), with Moses' blessings of the tribes (Deuteronomy 33), written at Milan about 390–391 as a sequel to his treatise on Joseph, from the critical text of C. Schenkl, Corpus Scriptorum Ecclesiasticorum Latinorum 32/2 (Vienna, 1897), pp. 125–160, verified against the page images and compared in sample with the Benedictine edition as reprinted in Migne, PL 14, with two misprints of punctuation in the edition corrected. Scripture is translated as Ambrose quotes it, from his own Old Latin text; italics mark the Scripture he quotes. The chapter and section numbers are the Maurist ones shared by all editions; the Maurist chapter summaries are not translated. The one psalm Ambrose names by number keeps the Latin (LXX) number, with the Hebrew number in parentheses. Schenkl's supplements are read into the text, and his critical apparatus is not translated. This English translation is released into the public domain.

Chapter I

1. First of all, how great a reverence we learn to render to parents, when we read that he who was blessed by his father was blessed, and he who was cursed was cursed! Therefore God granted this grace to parents, that the piety of children might be called forth. The prerogative of parents, then, is the discipline of children. Honour your father, then, that he may bless you. Let the pious man honour his father out of gratitude, the ungrateful out of fear. Even if a father is poor and has no store of riches to leave to his children, he has nevertheless the inheritance of the last blessing, by which he may bestow the wealth of sanctification upon his successors. And it is much more to become blessed than to become rich.

2. Joseph was eager to receive the blessing. And so he also brought his sons Manasseh and Ephraim, whom Jacob blessed, so that, since he had twelve sons and Paul was to be the thirteenth apostle, chosen as it were later, a thirteenth tribe might be sanctified from his grandsons Manasseh and Ephraim, divided between the two, in order that Paul might not be found outside the number of the fathers' tribes, he who, the eminent preacher of the Old and the New Testament, could readily prove that the inheritance of the fatherly blessing had profited him too.

3. How glorious are the mysteries in this: that Joseph, taking his sons whom he had received in Egypt, Ephraim at his own right but at the left of Israel his father, and Manasseh at his own left but at the right of Israel, brought them before his father; but Israel, stretching out his right hand, laid it upon the head of Ephraim, who was the younger and stood at his grandfather's left, and laid his left hand upon Manasseh, who was at the right, and so, with his hands exchanged, he blessed them! In this Joseph kept the order of nature, so as to give more to the elder son, just as Isaac too desired to confer the blessing on Esau, the first son; but he believed that the younger son, as a type of the younger people, was to be preferred, just as he himself had been preferred by his mother.

4. And so Manasseh is signified, by Latin interpretation, as "from forgetfulness," because the people of the Jews forgot their God who made them, and whoever of that people believes is recalled as it were from forgetfulness. Ephraim, on the other hand, by the interpretation of his name promises fruitfulness of faith, he who increased the father, as Joseph himself says: For God has increased me in the land of my humiliation. This is proper to the younger people, which is the body of Christ, increasing the father and not forsaking its own God.

5. And so the old man declared that this mystery is spiritually about the peoples. For when his son thought he had erred through the fault of dimmer sight, he wished to turn his hands round, saying: Not so, father; for this one is the firstborn. Lay your right hand upon his head. And he would not, but said: I know, son, I know. This one too shall become a people, and this one shall be exalted, but his younger brother shall be greater than he, and his seed shall be a multitude of nations. And so he prophesied that Ephraim was to be preferred to his elder brother by the order of the blessing as well, saying: In you shall Israel be blessed, saying: God make you as Ephraim and Manasseh. And therefore, although they were grandsons, they were adopted into the place of sons, so that they might not be without a share in their grandfather's blessing.

Chapter II

6. When this blessing had been performed, he called his sons as well. And he who earlier had preferred the younger to the elder began with the first, so that in the one case he might give precedence to the grace of the mystery, and in this keep the order of age; at the same time because earlier, in the two, he had blessed them all, with all their posterity and the offspring of those to come, lest either a repeated blessing of the people should seem superfluous or the former should be judged weak. And rightly he says that what he confers this second time is an announcement of the things that were to come to pass in later times rather than a blessing.

7. And so he began thus: Reuben, my firstborn, you are my strength and the beginning of my sons, hard to bear and hard and headstrong, you have done outrage. Like water, do not boil over; for you went up to your father's bed: then you defiled the couch where you went up. Does he not seem to be rebuked rather than blessed? And therefore it is a prophecy rather than a blessing. For a prophecy is the announcement of things to come, while a blessing is the votive bestowal of sanctification and of graces.

8. The Jews, too, think that the old man says these things to his son Reuben because he lay with Bilhah, his father's concubine, and defiled his father's couch — but they are easily refuted, for this had been done earlier —, whereas Jacob promises that he will tell the things that were to come in the last days, not those that had been done. So the patriarch's statement is consistent and agrees with itself: seeing the Lord's future passion at the hands of the persecuting Jews, he curses the monstrous audacity of the firstborn people, which, unbelieving and not subject to the law of God, and not knowing how to bear the yoke of Christ with its hard neck, assailed the author of life not only with the crime of murder but also with the injury of sacrilegious insult.

9. Do not boil over, he says, like water, so that it may not burst out into greater madness and the heat of fury and insanity not allow the sinners to come to their senses, but that they may do penance for their offences, since the hard people went up to the father's bed and defiled the holy couch, that is, by fastening to the gibbet of the cross the flesh of the Lord Jesus our creator, in which, as in a kind of bed and fatherly couch, his saints rest in saving refreshment. Who then would deny that this was said of the people, when all these things fit the people? For it was the people that was called Israel the firstborn, the people that was said to be of a hard neck; of it Moses said: But you are a people of hard neck. And in truth, who is so hard and so headstrong and so outrageous as the people of the Jews, who scourged with whips and pierced with nails the Lord Jesus, through whom they had seen the dead raised and the blind given light, though they could not deny his divine works? That this was committed at the instigation of the scribes and the high priests, with Caiaphas, the chief of the priests, raging alongside them, the course of the Gospel has declared; and therefore the holy prophet shrank from any share in so great a crime, lest through the succession of their descent they should dare to claim for themselves fellowship with so great a patriarch.

Chapter III

10. The just man bears witness and disowns a posterity that would not keep its father's affection, and because he foresaw the crime, he shudders at the contagion of their counsel. Into their counsel, he says, let my soul not come, and into their assembly let my inmost parts not strive, for in their anger they slew a man and in their desire they hamstrung a bull. What he says, he says indeed of his sons Simeon and Levi, but under their name he signifies the tribes, which were called by the same name. This most beautiful interpretation the Jews turn aside, and they think that Jacob rebukes his sons for this reason: that on account of the violation of their sister these two, wishing more than the other brothers to take vengeance on the Shechemites, pretended that they wished to enter into friendship with them, and therefore persuaded them that they ought to be circumcised, so that by a common religion peace between them and the fellowship of the kinship to be contracted might be confirmed. But they fell upon them when they had been circumcised, while their wounds were still fresh and their strength feeble, loosened by pain, and on the arrival of the third day they slew them, broken as they were. And perhaps these two were avengers above the rest for this reason too, that they are the founders of the scribes and the priests; for none ought more than the wise and the priests to defend chastity.

11. But in this too the Jews err. For these men had set before their father the grounds of their grievance, asserting that, though at that youthful age, they had been the vindicators of wounded piety and the avengers of violated chastity. This holy Jacob certainly could not condemn, because they did not suffer their sister to be left unavenged in the position of a harlot, she who had both lost her virginity and had not the consolation of vengeance; especially since he himself so approved the deed that, when he possessed Shechem, he gave it at his death to his most beloved son Joseph, saying to him: I give you Shechem as a special portion above all your brothers, which I took from the hands of the Amorites with my sword and bow. This fact cannot be denied; yet we can interpret that by Shechem shoulders are signified, and by shoulders works. So he chose holy Joseph above the rest as the heir of good works, whose works his brothers could not equal. For who could equal the deeds of Christ?

12. Then, moreover, immaculate and chaste, he carried off spoils from this earthly lodging and from the authors of impurity, capturing a place given over to debaucheries and crimes for the sojourning of the saints by heavenly words and the spiritual sword, so that where formerly the inhabitants of wantonness and the princes of luxury dwelt, where there had been the incitements of lust and the kindlings of wickedness, there holy priests may now teach the lessons of chastity, and very many examples of virginal integrity may shine forth with a kind of brightness of the light on high.

13. It is tribes, then, that are designated by the names of the patriarchs, because from the tribe of Simeon are the scribes, from the tribe of Levi the chief priests, who brought their wickedness to completion in the Lord's passion, filling up the whole measure of their fathers' impiety. They devised a counsel against the Lord Jesus, to kill him, as Isaiah says: Woe to their soul, for they have devised an evil counsel against themselves, saying: Let us bind the just one, for he is useless to us. They slew the prophets and apostles who announced the coming of the saving Lord and proclaimed the glory of his passion and resurrection. They afterwards in their desire, because they longed for earthly crimes and shrank from divine fellowship, from chastity of body, sobriety of mind, contempt of money, the gain of grace, hamstrung the bull, that bull, namely, which puts forth horns and hoofs, which the poor see and rejoice, since by the word of God he has exalted the horn of his people, with which he both drove back the enemies and won the prize of the heavenly crown. This is the bull by which the Church is figured, in the moon, which is fuller at the time when, resting as it were on the horns of a bull, it encloses the space of the whole world.

14. Yet he seems to join to this prophecy the grace of a blessing as well. For both in saying to the firstborn Reuben: Do not boil over like water he set a bound to the sin, because water is wont rather to cleanse offences and to call our minds back from every heat of the vices; and again, in saying to Simeon and Levi: I will divide you in Jacob and scatter you in Israel he showed that they were to be redeemed by the gathering of the Gentiles; for when the shepherd was struck, that flock which had before been gathered was scattered, so that he who was not might enter in, and thus all Israel might be saved. And we ought to believe this especially on account of the tribe of Levi, because the Lord Jesus is seen to draw his descent from that tribe according to his taking up of the body. From that tribe are the priests Levi and Nathan, whom holy Luke, in the book of the Gospel which he himself wrote, has counted among the Lord's ancestors. For the priest of the Father and the chief of all priests, as it is written: You are a priest for ever, had to claim the succession of a priestly origin.

15. Hence Moses too blessed this tribe, saying: Give to Levi the lot of his suffrage, and his truth to the holy man. Moses blessed the tribe of Reuben as well, as you have it written: Let Reuben live and not die, and let him be many in number. For he would not have blessed them if he had known them to be unworthy of blessing according to the patriarch's sentence. Certainly, what the one passed over briefly, this one completed.

Chapter IV

16. And because the tribes of Judah and Levi were joined by the intermingling of their descent, Matthew therefore describes his family from the tribe of Judah. And the Apostle says: For our Lord sprang from Judah, so that from the tribe of Levi a priestly inheritance, full of holiness, might be reckoned to him, and from the tribe of Judah, from which came David and Solomon and the other kings, the splendour of a royal succession might shine forth, in order that the same one might be shown by the testimony of the Scriptures to be king and priest. And rightly holy Jacob poured himself out over the grace of Judah, saying: Judah, your brothers shall praise you; your hands shall be upon the back of your enemies, the sons of your father shall worship you. Judah is a lion's whelp. From a shoot, my son, you have come up for me. Lying down you slept like a lion and like a whelp: who will rouse him? A judge shall not be lacking from Judah, nor a prince from his thighs, until he comes for whom it is laid up, and he is the expectation of the nations: binding his ass to the vine, and to the haircloth the colt of his ass, he will wash his robe in wine and his mantle in the blood of the grape: his eyes are cheerful from wine and his teeth whiter than milk.

17. The address seems indeed to be directed to Judah the patriarch, but it is that later Judah, the true confessor, who is expressed, who was born of that tribe, who alone is praised by his brothers, of whom he says: I will declare your name to my brothers, Lord by nature, brother by grace, whose hands, which he stretched out to an unbelieving people, are upon the back of the enemies. For with the same hands and by the same passion he protected his own and subjugated the adverse powers, and made subject to himself all who have no share in faith and piety. Of these the Father says to the Son: And you shall rule in the midst of your enemies. It was their own malice that made them enemies, not Christ's will. In this is the great grace of the Lord. For the spiritual wickednesses which formerly were wont to bend our neck under the yoke of captivity, so that David himself wrote that he in some way felt the hands of those who triumphed over him, saying: Upon my back sinners have wrought, these same are now made subject to Christ's triumph and subjected as it were to his hands, that is, to his deeds and works, and undergo the hardship of a perpetual captivity. He it is, surely, who is worshipped by the sons of his father, when he is worshipped by us, whom he himself has allowed to call upon his Father as our Father, he whose servant it is a virtue to be.

18. Judah is a lion's whelp. Has he not plainly both expressed the Father and declared the Son? What could be so plain, to teach that God the Son is of one nature with the Father? The one is a lion, this one a lion's whelp. By a lowly comparison the unity of the same nature or power is understood. A king proceeded from a king, a strong one from a strong one. Because he foresaw that there would be those who would maintain that the Son was of a younger age, he meets them by adding: From a shoot you have come up for me. Lying down you slept like a lion and like a whelp. And elsewhere you have it that the whelp himself is the lion of the tribe of Judah. So because he had said "whelp," he did well to put "lion" at once, that is to say: Let not your ears be caught because they heard "whelp"; I have expressed a son, I did not say a lesser one. He too is a lion, like the Father. Let them hear that he called him both lion and whelp: lion, as being of perfect and full power, whelp, as being a son, lest anyone on hearing it should think the Son not equal to the Father. The Son is not so praised as to be separated from the Father. He who confesses him Son proves him equal.

19. Wonderfully, too, he expressed his incarnation, saying: From a shoot you have come up for me, because like a shrub of the earth he sprouted in the virgin's womb, and like a flower of good fragrance, sent forth from his mother's womb with the splendour of a new light, he came up for the redemption of the whole world, as Isaiah says: There shall come forth a rod from the root of Jesse, and a flower shall come up from the root. The root is the family of the Jews, the rod Mary, the flower of Mary Christ. Rightly a rod, for she is of royal race, of the house and country of David, and her flower is Christ, who abolished the stench of worldly filth and poured in the fragrance of eternal life.

20. You have, then, the incarnation; receive the passion. Lying down, he says, you slept like a lion, when he lay in the sepulchre, at rest as in a kind of sleep of his body, as he himself says: I slept and took my rest and rose up, because the Lord will uphold me. Hence Jacob too says: Who will rouse him? That is: him whom the Lord will uphold. Who else is there to raise him again, unless he raise himself again by his own power and the Father's? I see him born by his own authority, I see him dead by his own will, I see him sleeping by his own power. He who did all things by his own choice, whose help will he need in order to rise again? He himself, then, is the author of his own resurrection, who is the arbiter of death, who is awaited by the nations.

21. Therefore, until he come, a leader shall not fail from Judah, so that until his birth the faith of the royal succession might be kept uncorrupted. For afterwards, as we have shown in the treatise we composed on the Gospel, the succession, adulterated through Herod, lost the prerogative of its dignity. For because they denied the true king, they began to have false ones. So this is what the patriarch says: the inheritance of an inviolate succession, carried down through kings, will be kept among the judges or kings of the Jews, until he come for whom it is laid up, that he may gather the Church of God out of the assembly of all nations and the devotion of the Gentile peoples; that is, it awaits him, what is owed is reserved for him, to him the prerogative of so great a grace is accorded.

22. And he is the expectation of the nations. He said more than if he had said: The nations await him, because all the hope of the Church rests upon him. Therefore it is said to Moses: Loose the sandal from your feet, lest he, who was being chosen as leader of the people, should be believed to be the Bridegroom of the Church. Therefore Joshua son of Nun loosed his sandal, so that he too might keep the grace of so great an office for the one who was to come. Therefore John says: After me comes a man the strap of whose sandal I am not worthy to loose; therefore he says: He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices with joy, that is: He alone is the husband of the Church, he is the expectation of the nations; yielding to him the union of nuptial grace, the prophets loosed their sandals. He is the Bridegroom, I am the Bridegroom's friend: I rejoice because he has come, because I hear the nuptial voice, because I hear now no longer the harsh punishments of sinners, the harsh torments of the Law, but the remission of crimes. The voice of gladness, the sound of joy, the exultation of the wedding feast is what we hear.

23. This is he who is binding his ass to the vine, and to the haircloth the colt of his ass, so that the gathering of the nations, formerly slack and negligent, but now devoted through Christ, may have the fervour of the Holy Spirit, and that we, like a fruitful branch, may be bound by certain bonds of indissoluble faith to that everlasting vine, that is, to the Lord Jesus, who says: I am the vine, my Father is the husbandman. This is that mystery, that he ordered the colt of the ass to be loosed in the Gospel, and the Lord Jesus himself sat on it, so that, bound to the vine, it might rest in the everlasting sweetness of the saints.

24. He will wash, he says, his robe in wine. A good robe is the flesh of Christ, which covered the sins of all, took up the offences of all, hid the errors of all; a good robe, which clothed all with the garment of gladness. He washed this robe in wine when, as he was being baptized in the Jordan, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove and remained upon him. By this is signified that the fullness of the Holy Spirit was undivided in him and did not withdraw. Hence the evangelist too says that full of the Holy Spirit the Lord Jesus returned from the Jordan. Jesus washed his robe, then, not to wash away filth of his own, which there was not, but ours, which there was. And so he added: and his mantle in the blood of the grape, that is, in the passion of his body he washed the Gentiles with his blood; for the Gentiles are the mantle of the Word, as it is written: As I live, says the Lord, unless I put them all on like a garment, and elsewhere: Like a cloak you will change them, and they shall be changed. Not, then, sins of his own, which there were not, but our offences, which we committed, he cleansed with his own blood. And rightly he said "grape," because like a grape he hung on the wood. He is the vine, he is the grape: the vine clinging to the wood, the grape because his side, opened by the soldier's lance, sent forth water and blood. For so John said, that there came forth from him water and blood, water for washing, blood for the price. The water washed us, the blood redeemed us.

25. And therefore the prophet says: His eyes are cheerful from wine and his teeth whiter than milk, signifying the prophets and the apostles. For some, like the eyes of Christ, foresaw and announced his coming, of whom he himself says: Abraham saw my day and was glad, and one of the prophets says: I saw the Lord of Sabaoth; and seeing him they were filled with spiritual gladness: but others, that is the apostles, whom the Lord cleansed from every stain of sins, were made whiter than milk, and no spot darkened them afterwards. For milk is temporal, but the grace of the apostles abides for ever, who, preparing for us those spiritual and heavenly foods, have fattened the inward parts of the inner mind. There are also those who think that the shining commandments of the Lord, which were brought forth from the divine mouth, became for us like milk, nourished by which we come to the nourishment of the heavenly bread. Hence Paul too says: I gave you milk to drink, not food; for you were not yet able. The Corinthian at the beginning of faith is given a drink of milk; those saints whose faith is proclaimed in the whole world are strengthened, like weaned children, with more solid food.

Chapter V

26. Zebulun shall dwell by the sea, and he beside the approaches of the ships, and he will stretch as far as Sidon. The very interpretation of the name promises better things, because the Latin rendering signifies deliverance from the things of night, which is surely a good thing and belongs to him who hopes under the wings of the Lord, whom his truth encompasses, so that he may not be afraid of the terror of the night and of the business that walks about in darkness.

27. Here, then, Zebulun, he says, shall dwell by the sea, so that he may see the shipwrecks of others while himself free from danger, and may look upon others tossing in the straits of this world, carried about by every wind of doctrine, while he himself remains immovable by the root of faith, just as holy Church, rooted and grounded in faith, looks upon the storms of the heretics and the shipwrecks of the Jews, since they denied the helmsman they had had. So she dwells near the waves, but is not moved by the waves, and is ready to give help rather than exposed to danger, so that if any, driven by heavy storms, wish to flee to the harbour, the Church may be at hand as a harbour of salvation, to call those in peril with outstretched arms into the bosom of her tranquillity, showing them a place of safe anchorage. The churches, then, in this world, like seaports spread along the shores, come to meet those in distress, saying that a refuge has been prepared for believers, where ships shaken by the winds may be brought in.

28. In these churches are the princes of Zebulun and the princes of Naphtali, as Psalm 67 (68) teaches, of whom some are deliverers from the night-storm, crying: Let us therefore cast off the works of darkness and put on the armour of light, while others are of apostolic breadth, who can say: Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians, our heart is enlarged. These are they who, when they were in darkness, saw a great light, as the prophet testifies, saying: The land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, the way of the sea beyond the Jordan. The people that sat in darkness have seen a great light. Those who sat in the region of the shadow of death, for them a light has risen.

29. So the Lord our God set the heirs of this patriarch as watchmen beside the approaches of the ships, and his watchful care and spiritual succession stretches as far as Sidon, that is, reaches to the Gentiles, so that the Lord's mercy may lighten the sins of the nations. For Sidon is the firstborn son of Ham, of him, namely, who for his irreverence against filial piety toward his father was condemned by his father's curse. So the deliverance from the night, set as it were on the watch of a spiritual bulwark, so that no one may run upon the rocks of this life, stretches even to sinners of the gravest crimes; and the Sidonians themselves, who formerly, because of their violent superstition, were called hunters of crimes, as the interpretation teaches, it absolved, dissolving the inheritance of the curse and bestowing the inheritance of the blessing, so that where the guilt was greater, there grace may now be more abundant.

Chapter VI

30. Issachar desired the good, resting between the lots. And seeing rest, that it is good, and the land, that it is rich, he set his shoulder to labour and became a husbandman. Issachar is called "reward," and therefore he is referred to Christ, who is our reward, because we purchase him for ourselves, for the hope of eternal salvation, not with gold and silver but with faith and devotion. Hence David too says of him: Behold, the inheritance of the Lord, sons, the reward, the fruit of the womb. And Moses says of him: the reward of those who dwell about the sea. This is he who desired the good from the beginning and did not know how to desire what is evil. Of him Isaiah too says: Before the child knows how to call father or mother, he does not trust in wickedness, choosing what is good. He rested between the lots of the Old and the New Testament, or in the midst of the prophets. And therefore he appeared in the middle between Moses and Elijah, to show us that he has rest in their words, through whom very many, renouncing their sins, believe in the living God, or that they themselves are witnesses of his resurrection and of his blessed repose.

31. And so, in order to call the Gentiles to the grace of his resurrection — for this is the rich and fertile land, which brings forth eternal fruits, fruits a hundredfold and sixtyfold — he put his shoulder under the labour, subjecting himself to the cross, that he might bear our sins. And therefore the prophet says: whose rule is upon his shoulder, that is, the power of the divinity is above the passion of the body, or the cross rising above the body. So he set his shoulder, bending to the plough, patient in undergoing every insult, so subjected to labour that he might be wounded for our iniquities and made weak for our sins. And he became a husbandman, knowing how to sow his land with good wheat and to plant fruit-bearing trees with deep roots.

Chapter VII

32. Dan shall judge his people as one tribe of Israel. And Dan became for him a serpent sitting in the way and biting the horse's heel in the path, and the rider shall fall backward, awaiting salvation from the Lord. The simple understanding indeed holds this: that the tribe of Dan also gave a judge in Israel. For after Joshua son of Nun there were judges of the people from various tribes. And Samson too was of the tribe of Dan, and he judged for twenty years. But the prophecy does not signify him, but Antichrist, who, to come from the tribe of Dan, a savage judge and a monstrous tyrant, will judge the people. Like a serpent sitting in the way, he will try to cast down those who walk the way of truth, desiring to trip up the truth. For this is to bite the horse's heel: that the horse, hurt by the spreading of the venom and wounded by the serpent's tooth, should lift up its heel, just as Judas the traitor, tempted by the devil, lifted up his heel against the Lord Jesus, to cast down the rider, who cast himself down that he might raise up all. He fell, therefore, not thrown down on his face as one dozing, but backward, so that, stretching himself forth from the things that went before to those that lie ahead, he might await salvation from the Lord; for he knew that he was to be raised again, and therefore he was waiting to raise up Adam who lay fallen.

33. Hence, when we are running well in the way, let us beware lest somewhere a serpent lurk in the path and undermine the step of the horse, that is, of our body, and suddenly cast down the rider while he sleeps. For if we keep watch, we ought to be on our guard in some measure and to avoid the serpent's bites. Let not, therefore, the sleep of negligence overwhelm us, the sleep of the world; let not the sleep of riches overwhelm us, lest it be said of us too: they have slept their sleep, and all the men of riches have found nothing. But there are also sleeping riders, of whom it is written: they who mounted horses have slumbered. If avarice wounds your breast, if lust inflames it, you are asleep, rider, and therefore you cannot rein in your body, that is, your horse. Keep watch, then, so that even if you have been cast down, that is, if you have died, you may not fall asleep. For those who sleep their sleep find nothing; but you, await salvation from the Lord, look to it as one that is coming, that you may find the grace of the resurrection. Judas was sleeping: in fact he did not hear the words of Christ. Judas was sleeping, and indeed the sleep of riches, since he was seeking a reward from the betrayal. The devil saw him sleeping and overwhelmed by the heavy sleep of avarice: he cast himself into his heart, wounded the horse, cast down the rider, whom he separated from Christ.

34. Moses blessed this tribe, saying: Dan is a lion's whelp, and he will escape from Bashan, that is, from confusion. Hence, according to the Greek, from which the Latin was translated, we ought rather to understand that Dan himself became a serpent sitting in the way. Dan is interpreted 'judgment', and therefore this tribe underwent the grave peril of judgment; the serpent that slipped into it is Antichrist, who would wound with his venom one who was running. But nevertheless that tribe itself will be delivered from confusion when it has confessed the rider rising again, who says: whoever has been put to confusion over me before men, I too will put him to confusion before my Father, who is in heaven. But whoever has confessed me before men, I too will confess him before my Father, who is in heaven.

Chapter VIII

35. Gad, temptation will tempt him, and he will tempt them close at their feet. The temptation is the gathering and the cunning of the scribes and priests, who tempted the Lord Jesus about Caesar's tribute and about John's baptism, as Scripture has taught; and the Lord Jesus, in his justice, turned the temptation back upon them. Close at their feet, that is, answering at once, without any deliberation, so that he himself might the more hem in those who tempted him. For when they said: By what authority do you do these things? he did not answer what was asked, but himself also put a question, saying: I also will ask you one word. If you tell me, I also will tell you by what authority I do these things. Again, when they said: Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar or not? he said: Why do you tempt me, hypocrites? Show me the coin of the census. And when they offered it, he asked again: Whose is the image and the superscription? They say to him: Caesar's. There, therefore, he bound them by their own words, he constrained them by their own pledge — then he said to them: Render the things that are Caesar's to Caesar, and the things that are God's to God — so that they could not go against their own words. And so they marvelled and went away from him. But it is no wonder if he answered close at their feet, who saw what lay before his feet.

36. Moses set forth clearly that this prophecy of holy Jacob is about Christ; for thus he says: Blessed is he who increased Gad. Like a lion he rested, breaking arms and princes. And he saw from his beginning that there the land of the princes was divided, of the princes gathered together with the princes of the peoples; the Lord did justice, and the judgment of Israel.

37. We recognize, therefore, the one who rested like a lion when he had broken the arms of the mighty, because from the beginning he saw the divisions of those who tempted him. There, therefore, is the chasm of the earth that is to swallow up the slanderers, where the temptation of the unbelievers is.

Chapter IX

38. Asher, his bread is rich, and he will give food to princes. Asher, by Latin interpretation, signifies riches. Who, then, is rich, except where there is the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God? Who is rich, except the Lord Jesus, who always abounds and never fails? Poor he came into this world, and he abounded for all; he filled everyone. How great in riches is he who by his poverty made all men rich! But he was poor for our sake, rich with the Father; poor, that he might deliver us from want, as the Apostle teaches, saying: for your sakes he became poor, though he was rich, that by his want you might be made rich. His want enriches, his fringe heals, his hunger satisfies, his death gives life, his burial raises up. This, then, is the rich treasure whose bread is rich. And well is it called rich, for whoever eats it will not be able to hunger. This bread he gave to the apostles, that they might divide it to the people of the believers, and today too he gives it to us, the bread which he himself, as priest, consecrates daily with his own words. This bread, then, has become the food of the saints.

39. We can also take it of the Lord himself, who gave us his own flesh, as he himself says: I am the bread of life. Your fathers ate manna in the desert and died; but this is the bread which came down from heaven, so that, if anyone eats of it, he may not die. And lest anyone should think that he speaks of this death which comes through the separation of soul and body, and should rightly doubt, since he knows that the holy apostles died by this death, he added: I am the living bread, who came down from heaven. If anyone eats of this, he will live for ever, that is: I did not speak above of temporal life, nor of the death of this life; for even if someone has died by that death, yet if he has received my bread, he will live for ever. For he receives it who proves himself; and he who receives it will not die the sinner's death, because this bread is the remission of sins.

40. Moses too prophesied most beautifully in his blessings, saying: Blessed of sons is Asher, and he will be acceptable to his brothers, and he will dip his foot in oil. Iron and bronze will be his sandal, and as your days are, so will your strength be. There is no one such as your God is in heaven, your helper, both great and comely, of the firmament, and God of the beginning protecting, and through the strength of mighty arms. And he will cast out the enemy from before your face, saying: perish. And Israel will dwell trusting, alone, upon the land of Jacob in grain and wine, and the heaven will be to you with a cloud of dew.

Chapter X

41. Naphtali is a vine let loose, putting forth beauty in its shoot. One vine-twig is cut off, because it seems useless, lest the vine run riot to no purpose in an exuberance of vine-twigs; another is cut back for a little while and let loose, that it may bear fruit, and its beauty is put forth in its generation, because, while it raises itself toward the heights, it embraces the vine, and, climbing to the top, it clothes, as it were, the necks of the vines' yoke with the necklace of a precious branch. That one too is a beauty in its generation, because with full branches it overflows with much fruit.

42. This is beautiful, but far more beautiful is that which it signifies: the branch clinging to the spiritual vine, whose vine-twig we are, and we can bear fruit if we abide in the vine; but if not, we are cut off.

43. The holy patriarch Naphtali was an abounding branch. Hence Moses says: Naphtali is the fullness of those who receive; he will be filled with blessing from the Lord, he will possess the sea and the south-west, expounding what Jacob had said, that he is a vine let loose, that is, freed from the bonds of death through the grace of faith. In him is signified the people of God, called to the freedom of faith and the abundance of grace, spread throughout the whole world, a people that clothes the yoke of Christ with good fruit and embraces the wood of that true vine, that is, the mysteries of the Lord's cross, and does not fear the danger of confessing him, but rather, even when set in persecutions, glories in the name of Christ.

44. He is truly let loose from bonds who is bound by no tie of fear — whence the prophet too says: they will go out and leap like calves loosed from bonds — and therefore he puts forth beauty in his shoot, because, placed in a place of pasture and brought up beside the water of refreshment, through the sacraments of his regeneration he sprouts the good beauty of the Word and is taken up into that most beautiful grace of Christ, who can increase the loveliness of your beauty.

45. Finally, he who is beautiful above all gives what he has, because no one can give what he does not have; and therefore it was said: the Lord has reigned, he has put on beauty. For by grace he put on the beauty of the Church, which, laying aside in that washing the foulness of all sins, has shone with the splendour of heavenly grace. And therefore the Bridegroom says of her: who is this that looks forth like the dawn, fair as the moon, chosen as the sun, a wonder like an array?

Chapter XI

46. Now, to conclude the history with a kind of epilogue, as it were, as divine Scripture too is brought to its close, let us set out the prophecy of holy Jacob concerning the name of holy Joseph: my son to be increased, Joseph, my son to be increased, to be envied, my younger son, return to me. Taking counsel against him, they cursed, and the masters bent the bow against him. And their bows were crushed with might, and the sinews of the arms of their hands were loosed by the hands of the mighty one of Jacob; and from there Israel prevailed from the God of your father. And my God helped you and blessed you with the blessing of heaven from on high and with the blessing of the earth that holds all things, on account of the blessing of the breasts and of the womb, the blessings of your father and mother. He prevailed above the blessings of the abiding mountains and the desires of the everlasting hills: they will be upon the head of Joseph and upon the crown of those brothers over whom he was set.

47. What reason is there why the father honoured his son Joseph more richly than all his sons, except that he saw in him the mysteries that prefigured Christ? Hence, blessing him who was expected rather than him who was seen, he says: my son to be increased, Joseph. Who is to be increased, except Christ, whose grace is ever increased and whose glory has no end to its progress? Of him John too says: he must increase, but I must decrease, because through that saving and perfect name of his grace has been heaped up and has abounded in this world — my son to be increased — and therefore, because they saw him increasing, his brothers began to envy him; and the intelligible Joseph, too, met with jealousy from those whom he cherished the more. Indeed, he said: I have not come except to the lost sheep of the house of Israel. And they said: we do not know where he is from. He was healing them, and they were denying him.

48. My son, he says, the younger. Younger indeed, for he was almost the last by birth. Indeed Scripture too says: Jacob loved him, because he was the son of his old age. This too refers to Christ. For as the world was growing old and already setting, the Son of God, shining upon it, came late through the childbearing of the Virgin Mary. As a son of old age he took up a body, according to the mystery, he who was always with the Father before the ages.

49. Hence the Father says to him: return to me, calling back to heaven from the earth him whom he had sent for our salvation. And so, by raising up his only-begotten Son, he made void the counsel of those who cursed — whence Isaiah too says: vain is the counsel of your spirit — and the reproaches which they aimed as though shooting arrows he loosed, every one. He crushed the might of those who trusted in their own powers and not in God. Hence, he says, Israel prevailed from the God of your father. And my God helped you. Who is it that strengthened Israel and helped the Son, except God the Father alone, who said: Jacob my servant, I will receive him; Israel my chosen, let my soul receive him?

50. And he blessed him with the blessing of heaven from above and with the blessing of the earth that holds all things; for he subjected all things to him, the heavenly things as the blessing of heaven and the earthly things as the blessing of the earth, that he might be lord over both men and angels.

51. And so in that body, contemptible as it seemed, you prevailed, he says, on account of the blessing of the breasts and of the womb, the blessings of your father and mother. By the breasts he meant either the two Testaments, in one of which he was announced and in the other shown forth — and well are they called breasts, since the Son nourished us as it were with a spiritual milk, brought us up and offered us to God — or he means the breasts of Mary, which were truly blessed, from which the holy Virgin drew a drink of milk for the people of the Lord. Hence that woman in the Gospel too says: blessed is the womb that bore you, and the breasts that you sucked! But as for his saying: the blessing of the womb of your father and mother, if we choose to understand the womb of Mary alone, the reason why he joined both blessings will remain hidden — for he could have spoken of the mother's womb alone — but I think it finer that, according to the spiritual mystery, we should understand both generations of the Lord Jesus, both according to the divinity and according to the flesh, because before the ages he was begotten of the Father. Hence the Father too says: my heart has poured forth a good word, because he proceeded from that inmost and incomprehensible substance of the Father and is always in him. Hence the evangelist too says: no one has ever seen God, except the only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father; he has declared him. As, then, the bosom of the Father is understood spiritually as an inmost secret, as it were, of the Father's love and nature, in which the Son ever is, so too the womb of the Father is that of a spiritual, inward hidden place, from which, as from a womb that gives birth, the Son proceeded. Indeed, we read in different places now of the womb of the Father, now of his heart, with which he poured forth the Word, now of his mouth, from which justice proceeded, from which wisdom came forth, as she herself says: I came forth from the mouth of the Most High. Thus, since no single thing is defined and all express one thing, it signifies the spiritual mystery of the Father's generation rather than any bodily member. But as we understand that generation from the Father, so also, for the completeness of faith, let us understand the generation from Mary, when the womb of the mother is blessed, that virginal womb of Mary, surely, which brought forth for us the Lord Jesus. Of it the Father says through Jeremiah the prophet: before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you came forth from your mother's womb I sanctified you. The prophet, therefore, declared a twofold substance in Christ, of divinity and of flesh, the one from the Father, the other from the Virgin, yet in such a way that he was not without his share in his divinity when he was born of the Virgin and was in the body.

52. Hence too he grew strong over all the mountains and the desires of the everlasting hills. For above all those men of lofty merit, patriarchs and prophets and apostles, and even beyond sun, moon and archangels, he shone forth like the light of heaven, as he himself says: the disciple is not above the master, nor the servant above the lord. For which of them was there to whom all things were subject, they to whom he himself gave what they were? In him all his saints are blessed, because he is over the heads of all, the head of all — for the head of the woman is the man, the head of the man is Christ — and over the crowns of all, because he is the surpassing crown of all things; but he is the highest crown of the just, whom he calls brothers, acquired through grace and a certain fellowship of regeneration. Hence too we understand as the brothers of Joseph rather those of whom he says in the psalm: I will declare your name to my brothers, in the midst of the Church I will sing to you.

53. Indeed Moses too, when he was about to complete the course of this life, in blessing the tribe of Joseph was surely blessing not that Joseph, already dead, but Christ, as you have it written of Joseph: from the blessing of the Lord is his land, from the bounds of heaven and from the dew and from the deeps of the springs below, and according to the season, the yield of the sun's course, and from the months as they meet, and from the top of the mountains from the beginning, and from the top of the everlasting hills, and at the season of the fullness of the earth; and from him who was seen in the bush may it come upon the head of Joseph and upon his crown. Honoured among his brothers. The firstborn of a bull is his beauty, the horns of a unicorn. With them he will toss the nations together, to the end of the earth. These are the myriads of Ephraim and these the thousands of Manasseh.

54. The blessing is the same, which holds all the fullness of heavenly and earthly things and the special grace of Christ, who, seen in the bush, said to Moses himself: loose the sandal from your feet; who is God over all, because he is the head of the body, the Church, he who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in all things he himself may hold the primacy, since in him it pleased all fullness to dwell. And therefore to him alone the prerogative of this blessing is granted, from the summit of heaven to the end of the earth. For he is before all, in whom all things hold together, who also through the blood of his cross brought all things to peace, whether those on earth or those in heaven. Bearing his type, holy Joseph is beautifully blessed, that he may be honoured among his brothers.

55. The firstborn of a bull is his beauty, having the horns of a unicorn, with which he will toss the nations. And a good bull he is, as an offering for sins and the victim of the whole world, that he might bring all things to peace. His beauty is holy; for every firstborn is holy, as we have shown elsewhere; hence Levi deserved to be called firstborn not by order of age but by the prerogative of a sacred succession. And truly holy is his beauty, of whom it is written as he rises again: beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, because he is the firstborn from the dead, having the horns of a unicorn. But since he said horns, we must ask how he came to put 'of a unicorn', since the unicorn itself is not found among the kinds of wild beasts, as those skilled in such things say. And therefore we ought rather to take it as the one Word, because the substantial Word of God is one, not many words. Hence Hannah too says: the Lord judges the ends of the earth, and he will give strength to the king and will exalt the horn of his Christ, and Isaiah says: a vineyard was made for the beloved on a horn in a fertile place, since in the only-begotten Son of God the Church flourishes, holding the one Word of God, in whom is the fullness of power and wisdom, by whose fertility the harvest of faith has sprung up. This Word the saints follow.

56. Ten thousands, he says, of Ephraim and thousands of Manasseh, that is: that he may be lord both of the Jews and of the Gentiles, and from both peoples acquire for himself the fullness of the Church. And therefore holy Jacob placed his right hand upon Ephraim, because you read her saying in the Song of Songs: my kinsman is white and ruddy, chosen out of ten thousands. Indeed, the young women too extolled David, the forefather of Mary, from whose line Christ was born through the Virgin's childbearing, in ten thousands, but Saul in thousands, although out of reverence they ought rather to have given the greater weight to the king. He, then, who has exalted the horn of Christ by confessing his glory himself also receives horns. Hence the saints too are called unicorns in a little verse of the psalm: and the beloved like the son of unicorns. For just as an animal of this kind, when its horns spring up, signifies the advance of a fuller age, so when horns have begun to sprout from a certain head of our soul, they seem to signify the advance of a more perfect virtue, and they grow until they are complete. With this horn the Lord Jesus broke the nations in pieces, that he might crush superstition and restore salvation, as he himself says: I will strike and I will heal. And therefore the prophet, as an imitator of this bull, says: in you we will toss our enemies, that is, destroying every height that exalts itself against the knowledge of God. And therefore, according to the Law, the clean animals have horns; for the Law is spiritual. For those who can drive back the allurements of this world by the word of God and the observance of virtue seem to be armed with horns, as with certain weapons of their head. And rightly, too, the power of wondrous speech is called a trumpet of horn, which kindles the good soldiers of Christ to battle, so that we may carry off spoils from the enemy, the devil. We are, then, in the battle line, and we see very many of our own held captive in the enemy's camp. These we must free from the most heavy yoke of slavery.

Chapter XII

57. The devil has very many wolves, which he sends against the sheep of Christ. And therefore the intelligible Joseph, to save his sheep, seized the very enemy who was coming to plunder the sheep, the wolf Paul, making of the persecutor a teacher. Of him Jacob speaks, as it is written: Benjamin is a ravening wolf; in the morning he will still eat, and at evening he will divide food to the princes. He was a wolf when he was scattering and devouring the sheep of the Church; but he who had come as a wolf was made a shepherd. He was a wolf while he was Saul, when he entered into houses and dragged men and women off to prison. He was a wolf when, breathing threats and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, he sought letters from the chief priests, that he might fall upon the little servants of Christ. Him, as he roamed like a wolf in the darkness of night, Jesus blinded by pouring light around him. Therefore Rachel, when she bore Benjamin, called his name son of my sorrow, prophesying that from that tribe Paul would come, who in the time of his persecution would afflict the sons of the Church and torment their mother with heavy sorrow. But yet the same man afterwards, at a later time, divided food to the princes, preaching the word of God to the Gentiles and calling very many to faith, men who, while he disputed, received the grace of the Lord, such as the legate of the proconsul Paulus and Publius the prince.

58. Beautifully, too, did Moses say, when he blessed the tribe of Benjamin: the beloved of the Lord will dwell trusting, and God will overshadow him all the days, and the beloved of the Lord will rest between his shoulders. He also was made a vessel of election; for he was converted in no other way than by the mercy and love of the Lord. Hence he himself too, ascribing nothing to his own merit but attributing everything to Christ, says: for I am the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God. But by the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace in me was not needy. He dwelt trusting in the house which before he was laying waste; he dwelt in the tabernacles of Christ, he who before had wandered like a wolf in the woods; and God overshadowed him when Christ was seen by him. Although with open eyes he saw nothing, yet he saw Christ. And rightly did he see him present, whom he also heard speaking. That overshadowing is not of blindness, but of grace. Indeed, it is said to Mary: the Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.

59. And he will rest between his shoulders, that is, between good acts and precious works. For above, too, you have it that Issachar set his shoulder to labour and became a husbandman. Imitating him, Paul set the young plantings of faith, and therefore, as a good husbandman, he said: I planted, Apollos watered.
