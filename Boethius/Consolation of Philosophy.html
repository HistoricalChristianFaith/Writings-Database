Consolation of Philosophy

This work is not yet hosted here. The complete public-domain English translation by W. V. Cooper (The Consolation of Philosophy, The Temple Classics; J. M. Dent and Company, London, 1902), with his notes, was published online by the University of Virginia Library’s Electronic Text Center; that site has closed, but its copy is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine, both as the entire work and Book 5 alone. Another public-domain translation, by H. R. James (1897), is available from Project Gutenberg. Boethius, the Roman statesman and Christian philosopher, wrote the work in prison around 524, while awaiting execution on a charge of treason under the Ostrogothic king Theodoric. In five books of prose alternating with verse, Philosophy appears to him as a woman and leads him from complaint against Fortune to the recognition that true happiness is found only in God, the highest good; she then takes up providence and fate and why the wicked seem to prosper, and in the last book reconciles chance and human free will with God’s foreknowledge, teaching that God sees all things at once in an eternal present. Other English translations: Joel C. Relihan, Consolation of Philosophy (Hackett, 2001; ISBN 978-0-87220-583-3), available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 October 2013, who quotes Cooper’s version of Book 5 on God’s eternal present.)
