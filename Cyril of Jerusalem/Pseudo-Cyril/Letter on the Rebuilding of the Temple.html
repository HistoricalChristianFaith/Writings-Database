Letter on the Rebuilding of the Temple

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The letter was edited from the Syriac, with an English translation, introduction and notes, by Sebastian P. Brock, “A letter attributed to Cyril of Jerusalem on the rebuilding of the Temple”, Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African Studies 40.2 (1977), pp. 267–286; the article is available from Cambridge University Press (also on JSTOR), and is reprinted as no. XI in Brock’s Fire from Heaven: Studies in Syriac Theology and Liturgy (Variorum Collected Studies; Ashgate, 2006). It gives the complete text of the letter. Preserved only in Syriac, the letter is written in the name of Cyril, bishop of Jerusalem, and describes the earthquake of May 363 that put an end to the Emperor Julian’s attempt to have the Jews rebuild the Temple, listing the towns damaged by it; that list has proved useful to archaeologists of Roman Palestine. It is generally thought to have been composed after Cyril’s death, perhaps early in the fifth century, by someone who knew Jerusalem well, though its authenticity has also been defended. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 May 2015.)
