The Reckoning of Time

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Faith Wallis as Bede: The Reckoning of Time (Translated Texts for Historians 29; Liverpool University Press, 1999; ISBN 978-0-85323-693-1), with an introduction and a chapter-by-chapter commentary; it is available from the publisher. A second, revised edition, with a thoroughly revised translation and a new introduction and commentary, appeared in 2025 (ISBN 978-1-83624-427-1; publisher's page). Written in 725, De temporum ratione is Bede's textbook of computus: it explains the divisions of time from the moment to the year, the courses of the sun and moon, the calendars of the Hebrews, Greeks, Romans and English, the tides, and above all the calculation of the date of Easter, and it ends with a chronicle of the six ages of the world. Its fifteenth chapter, on the months of the English, is our only source for the pagan Old English month names, including Eosturmonath and the goddess Eostre from whom Bede derives the English word "Easter". (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 March 2024.)
