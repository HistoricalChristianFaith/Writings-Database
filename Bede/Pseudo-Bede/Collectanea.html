Collectanea

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was edited and translated by Martha Bayless and Michael Lapidge, with contributions from a team of other scholars, as Collectanea Pseudo-Bedae (Scriptores Latini Hiberniae 14; Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies, 1998; ISBN 978-1-85500-160-2), which gives the Latin text with an English translation and commentary; it is available from the publisher. The Collectanea (also called Excerptiones Patrum, CPL 1129) is an anonymous miscellany, opening "Dic mihi, quaeso, quae est illa mulier", of riddles, question-and-answer pieces, sayings and short excerpts drawn from the Fathers and other sources. It was printed among the works of Bede in Johann Herwagen's Basel edition of 1563 (reprinted in Migne, PL 94, cols. 539–560), but no manuscript of it is known to survive, and it is probably a compilation of the eighth century or later. It is the source of the famous saying that Rome will stand as long as the Colosseum stands. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 27 April 2024.)
