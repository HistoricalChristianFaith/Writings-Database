Metrical Calendar of York

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A critical edition with a complete English translation by Kazutomo Karasawa appears as Appendix 2 (pp. 138–153) of his The Old English Metrical Calendar (Menologium) (Anglo-Saxon Texts 12; Cambridge: D. S. Brewer, 2015; ISBN 978-1-84384-409-9; paperback 2021, ISBN 978-1-84384-599-7), a book chiefly devoted to the Old English poem; it is available from the publisher. The Metrical Calendar of York is a poem of 83 hexameter lines which runs through the year month by month, naming the feasts of the principal saints and their dates in the Roman reckoning. It is preserved in the manuscripts as the Martyrologium Bedae (or Martyrologium poeticum), but it cannot be by Bede, since it records events after his death; it was probably composed at York in the eighth century, perhaps by Alcuin, and the name now used for it was given by Dom André Wilmart. It is the earliest witness to the celebration of All Saints on 1 November. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 8 October 2019.)
