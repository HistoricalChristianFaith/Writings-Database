Preliminary Talks and Declamations

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The twelve declamations of Choricius, together with his preliminary talks (dialexeis), are translated in Rhetorical Exercises from Late Antiquity: A Translation of Choricius of Gaza’s Preliminary Talks and Declamations, edited by Robert J. Penella (Cambridge University Press, 2009; ISBN 978-0-521-84873-2; paperback 2019, ISBN 978-1-108-72992-5), available from the publisher. Penella wrote the introduction and translated the preliminary talks; the declamations were translated by D. A. Russell, Simon Swain, Malcolm Heath, George A. Kennedy, William Reader and Terry L. Papillon, and Eugenio Amato contributes an epilogue on the later fortunes of Choricius and his works. Choricius, a Christian who succeeded his teacher Procopius at the head of the school of rhetoric at Gaza in the sixth century, wrote the declamations as model speeches on set themes from myth, history and imagined law-cases: among them Priam objecting to the marriage of Polyxena to Achilles, Miltiades defending himself after the failed siege of Paros, a Spartan protesting against Praxiteles’ statue of Aphrodite, and Patroclus begging Achilles to return to battle. Most of the preliminary talks are short, informal addresses that introduced a declamation to its audience. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 8 December 2009.)
