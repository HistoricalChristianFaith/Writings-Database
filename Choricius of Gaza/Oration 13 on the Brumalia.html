Oration 13 on the Brumalia

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation by Roberta Mazza, published as “Choricius of Gaza, Oration XIII: Religion and State in the Age of Justinian” in E. Digeser, R. M. Frakes and J. Stephens (eds), The Rhetoric of Power in Late Antiquity: Religion and Politics in Byzantium, Europe and the Early Islamic World (London and New York: I.B. Tauris, 2010), pp. 172–93, has been made freely available by the translator on Academia.edu; its licence is not stated. Choricius, the sixth-century Christian rhetor of Gaza, composed the speech (Oration XIII in the edition of Foerster and Richtsteig) for the Brumalia of the emperor Justinian, the old Roman midwinter festival that Justinian kept on a lavish scale with banquets and spectacles; as Robert Penella notes, the sophist compares the emperor to Zeus but says nothing of his Christianity. Mazza’s chapter sets the translation beside a survey of what the sources tell us about the bruma and the Brumalia. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 8 December 2009, in an update of 22 December 2016.)
