Life of Saint Petroc

No public-domain English translation of this work is available online. The only English translation is by Gilbert H. Doble (d. 1945), who first printed it in 1928 in his pamphlet Saint Petroc, Abbot and Confessor (Cornish Saints series); that first edition is in the public domain in the United States, but no copy of it is known to be online. Doble later revised the translation in further editions of the pamphlet (the third apparently in 1939), and the final version was reprinted in The Saints of Cornwall, Part 4, edited by Donald Attwater, and again in facsimile as The Saints of Cornwall, Volume 4: Newquay, Padstow & Bodmin (Llanerch Press, 1998; ISBN 978-1-86143-044-1), pp. 132–163, available from the publisher. It gives a complete, rather loose translation of the “First Life” (BHL 6639), from the sixteenth-century manuscript of the Breton abbey of Saint-Méen now in Paris (BNF lat. 9889, fols. 142r–150r), together with translations of selected passages of the longer “Second Life”, which came to light in the twentieth century in a manuscript at Gotha (Memb. I 81), where it differs from the first. Petroc was a sixth-century British abbot whose cult centred on Padstow and Bodmin, with churches dedicated to him across Cornwall, Devon and Somerset. The First Life was probably written at Bodmin in the eleventh century and the Second Life in the twelfth; both tell of the cult of the saint as it stood in the Middle Ages rather than of his own times. Paul Grosjean edited the Latin texts in Analecta Bollandiana 74 (1956), pp. 131–188 (the Gotha dossier) and 470–496 (the Saint-Méen dossier).
