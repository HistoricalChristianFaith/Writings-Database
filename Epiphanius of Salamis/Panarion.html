Panarion

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Frank Williams as The Panarion of Epiphanius of Salamis (Nag Hammadi and Manichaean Studies; Brill, Leiden), first in two volumes (NHMS 35, 1987; NHMS 36, 1994) and then in a second, revised edition: Book I (Sects 1–46) (NHMS 63, 2009; ISBN 978-90-04-17017-9) and Books II and III. De Fide (NHMS 79, 2013; ISBN 978-90-04-22841-2). Selected passages were also translated by Philip R. Amidon, The Panarion of St. Epiphanius, Bishop of Salamis: Selected Passages (Oxford University Press, 1990; ISBN 978-0-19-506291-5). Written about 375–378, the Panarion (“medicine chest”) describes and refutes eighty heresies, from the divisions of the world before Christ and the sects of the Samaritans and the Jews down to the Arians, Manichaeans, Origenists and Messalians of Epiphanius’ own day, offering an antidote to the poison of each, and ends with a summary of the faith of the Church (De Fide). It is the main or only source for many early sects, such as the Ebionites, the Nazoraeans and several Gnostic groups, and quotes a number of documents in full, among them Ptolemy’s Letter to Flora. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 December 2008.)
