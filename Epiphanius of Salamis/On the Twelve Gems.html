On the Twelve Gems

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The nearly complete Old Georgian version of the De gemmis was edited and translated into English by Robert P. Blake, together with the fragments of the Armenian version, in Epiphanius De Gemmis: The Old Georgian Version and the Fragments of the Armenian Version, which also contains the Coptic-Sahidic fragments edited by Henri de Vis (Studies and Documents 2; London: Christophers, 1934); a scan of the whole book is freely viewable at the Internet Archive. Its licence is not stated. Epiphanius wrote the treatise near the end of his life (about 394) at the request of a bishop Diodorus, probably of Tyre. It deals with the twelve precious stones set in the breastplate of the Jewish high priest (Exodus 28:17–21): it describes each stone, its colour and the places where it is found, together with the properties popularly ascribed to it, assigns each to one of the twelve tribes of Israel in the order of the patriarchs’ birth, and draws out an allegorical meaning. The Greek original survives only in epitomes and excerpts, and the old Latin version contains barely half of the work, so the Georgian is the fullest witness to the text. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 December 2008.)
