Writings against Images

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. English translations of the five texts against images attributed to Epiphanius (the postscript of his letter to John of Jerusalem; the treatise against those who make images of Christ, the Mother of God, the angels and the prophets; the Dogmatic Letter; the letter to the Emperor Theodosius; and his testament addressed to the members of his church) form chapter 2 of Steven Bigham, Epiphanius of Salamis, Doctor of Iconoclasm? Deconstruction of a Myth (Patristic Theological Library 3; Orthodox Research Institute, 2008; ISBN 978-1-933275-28-4), available from the publisher. The rest of the book examines the dispute over whether the texts are genuine, first between the iconoclasts and their opponents (John of Damascus, the Second Council of Nicaea of 787, Nicephorus of Constantinople and Theodore the Studite) and then among modern scholars, chiefly Karl Holl, who defended them, and George Ostrogorsky, who rejected them; Bigham concludes that the texts are either forgeries or have been misread. The first of the five, the account of how Epiphanius tore down a painted curtain in a church at Anablatha, also survives in Jerome’s Latin version of the letter to John of Jerusalem (Jerome, Letter 51, §9). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 August 2012.)
