Homily on Holy Saturday

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation, made from the Greek text printed by André Vaillant (“L’homélie d’Épiphane sur l’ensevelissement du Christ”, Radovi Staroslavenskog instituta 3, Zagreb, 1958), was published in 2020 by Holy Cross Monastery, West Virginia (Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia), on its website as “Sermon of St. Epiphanius of Cyprus for Holy Saturday”; the translator is not named and its licence is not stated. The homily, on the divine burial of the body of Christ, on Joseph of Arimathea, and on the Lord’s descent into hell, for Holy Saturday (CPG 3768; Greek text in PG 43, 439–464), is attributed to Epiphanius of Cyprus but is not the work of Epiphanius of Salamis: Vaillant ascribed it to a later archbishop of Cyprus of the same name, who attended the Council in Trullo in 691. It opens with the “great silence” on earth while the King sleeps, contrasts the burial with the Nativity, addresses Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus as they take down and bury the body, and, drawing on the apocryphal Gospel of Nicodemus, describes Christ’s descent into hell to set free Adam, Eve and the righteous dead. Other English translations: extracts, as the second reading of the Office of Readings for Holy Saturday, in The Liturgy of the Hours, also given on the Vatican website. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 19 April 2025.)
