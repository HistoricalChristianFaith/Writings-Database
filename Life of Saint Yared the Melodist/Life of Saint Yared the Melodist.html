Life of Saint Yared the Melodist

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An abridged English translation, as “The Life of Saint Jared the Melodist”, appears in the anthology Saints of Ethiopia (Scriptorium Press, 2024; ISBN 979-8-3009-0117-2), pp. 123–131, for which the publisher’s listing names no translator; it is available from the publisher. It is a partial translation: the Ge‘ez Life, edited by Carlo Conti Rossini in the Corpus Scriptorum Christianorum Orientalium (CSCO 26, pp. 3–21), is a long text, and the editors have abbreviated it, adding E. A. Wallis Budge’s translation of the corresponding notice in the Ethiopian Synaxarion; the volume also contains texts on Frumentius, Moses the Ethiopian, Pantaleon the Recluse and the Martyrdom of Arethas, with an introduction on the origins of the Ethiopian Church. Yared, a sixth-century deacon of Aksum, is honoured as the founder of the chant of the Ethiopian Church and of its musical notation. In the tradition he is a slow pupil who, after watching a caterpillar try again and again to climb a tree, returns to his teacher and is given understanding of the Scriptures; he is caught up to heaven and learns the songs of the angels, composes the hymns of the church year, and, after singing before King Gabra Masqal, asks leave to become a monk and ends his life in the desert. The Life itself is of uncertain date. Budge’s Synaxarion notice, which is in the public domain, is in his The Book of the Saints of the Ethiopian Church (Cambridge, 1928), vol. 3, pp. 875–877. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 19 December 2024.)
