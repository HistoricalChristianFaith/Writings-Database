Book of Paradise (Syriac, Anan-Isho)

A public-domain English translation of this work exists but has not yet been transcribed here. E. A. Wallis Budge edited the Syriac text of the recension of ʿAnan-Ishoʿ of Beth ʿAbhe, from Lady Meux MS. 6, with a complete English translation, in The Book of Paradise, being the Histories and Sayings of the Monks and Ascetics of the Egyptian Desert by Palladius, Hieronymus and Others (2 vols.; London: printed for Lady Meux by W. Drugulin, Leipzig, 1904; 500 copies for private circulation): the English translation fills volume I and the Syriac text volume II, both scanned at archive.org. Public domain (published 1904). Budge reissued the translation alone as The Paradise or Garden of the Holy Fathers (2 vols.; London: Chatto & Windus, 1907), whose second volume contains the sayings. The English runs to more than a thousand pages, too long to be reproduced here in one go. ʿAnan-Ishoʿ, a monk of the monastery of Beth ʿAbhe in the seventh century, gathered the Syriac versions of the classic accounts of Egyptian monasticism into a work in two parts. The first holds the histories: Athanasius’s Life of Antony, the Lausiac History of Palladius (“Paradise”), a second set of lives ascribed to Palladius, and the History of the Monks in Egypt, which the Syrians ascribed to Jerome. The second holds the Sayings of the Fathers, translated from Greek and Latin sources: more than a thousand sayings arranged by subject, in chapters on such themes as flight from men and dwelling in the cell, fasting, reading the Scriptures and prayer, mourning for sin, poverty, patient endurance, obedience, watchfulness, love, humility, fornication and repentance, followed by questions and answers on the ascetic life. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 October 2018.)
