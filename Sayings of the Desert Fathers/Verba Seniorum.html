Verba Seniorum

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Latin collection was translated by Benedicta Ward as The Desert Fathers: Sayings of the Early Christian Monks (Penguin Classics; London: Penguin, 2003; ISBN 978-0-14-044731-6), with an introduction; it is available from the publisher. An earlier selection from the same collection is in Helen Waddell, The Desert Fathers (London: Constable, 1936), which is still in copyright. The Verba Seniorum (“Sayings of the Elders”) is the Latin systematic collection of the Sayings of the Desert Fathers, translated from the Greek in sixth-century Rome by the deacon Pelagius and continued by the subdeacon John; it was printed by Heribert Rosweyde as Books V and VI of his Vitae Patrum (Antwerp, 1615; reprinted in the Patrologia Latina, vol. 73, cols. 851–1022). Like the Greek systematic collection, it arranges the sayings by subject, in chapters on such themes as stillness, self-control, discretion, patience, obedience, humility, charity and visions, and it was through this Latin version that the desert tradition was chiefly known in the medieval West.
