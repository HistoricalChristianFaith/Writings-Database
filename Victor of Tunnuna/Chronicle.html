Chronicle

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An annotated English translation of the Chronicle by John R. C. Martyn is included in his Arians and Vandals of the 4th–6th Centuries: Annotated Translations of the Historical Works by Bishops Victor of Vita (Historia Persecutionis Africanae Provinciae) and Victor of Tonnena (Chronicon), and of the Religious Works by Bishop Victor of Cartenna (De Paenitentia) and Saints Ambrose (De Fide Orthodoxa contra Arianos), and Athanasius (Expositio Fidei), with a preface by David O’Brien (Newcastle upon Tyne: Cambridge Scholars Publishing, 2008; ISBN 978-1-84718-991-2), a collection in which the Chronicle is one of five works translated; the publisher’s page for the book is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. Victor, bishop of Tunnuna in North Africa, opposed Justinian’s condemnation of the Three Chapters and spent the rest of his life in confinement, at Carthage, in the Balearic Islands, at Canopus in Egypt and finally in Constantinople, where he died around 570. His Latin chronicle carried on the chronicle tradition of Eusebius, Jerome and Prosper of Aquitaine; the part from 444 to 566 survives, and it is a chief source for the Arian Vandal kingdom in Africa, the Eutychian controversy and the struggle over the Three Chapters, including the career of Primasius of Hadrumetum. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 28 June 2019.)
