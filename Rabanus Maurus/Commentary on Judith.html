Commentary on Judith

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation is in Saints of the Old Testament: Rabanus Maurus’ Commentary on Ruth, Judith, and Esther (Scriptorium Press, 2024; ISBN 9798870443119), one volume containing complete translations of all three of Rabanus’s commentaries, with an introduction on his life and exegesis and the short life of Rabanus by Johannes Trithemius; the translator is not named. It is available from the publisher, whose site also offers a free sample. The commentary on Judith, given there with its dedication to the Empress, a prefatory poem and sixteen chapters covering the whole book, was written around 834 and sent, together with the commentary on Esther, to the Empress Judith, wife of Louis the Pious, whom Rabanus urged to take the biblical heroine as her model. Like his other commentaries it explains the book section by section, chiefly in an allegorical sense, drawing on earlier Fathers such as Augustine, Gregory the Great and Bede. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 3 December 2024.)
