Compendium of Heretical Fables

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only complete English translation, of all five books, is contained in Glenn Melvin Cope's doctoral dissertation, An Analysis of the Heresiological Method of Theodoret of Cyrus in the “Haereticarum fabularum compendium” (The Catholic University of America, 1990), which has never been published as a book; copies are available from ProQuest Dissertations & Theses (order no. 9027656). The title of the thesis does not reveal that it includes the translation. The Haereticarum fabularum compendium (CPG 6223), written by Theodoret in the early 450s, after the Council of Chalcedon, is a short handbook of heresies: its first four books survey the heretical sects from Simon Magus and the Gnostics through the Ebionites, Montanists and Novatians down to Arius, Eunomius, Nestorius and Eutyches, drawing largely on earlier heresiologists such as Irenaeus, Hippolytus and Eusebius, and its fifth book sets out the orthodox teaching on God, creation, Christ and the last things against them. The Greek text is printed in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 83. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 June 2011; see also his posts of 2 December 2013 and 12 December 2013.)
