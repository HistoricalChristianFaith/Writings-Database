Commentary on the Gospels

No complete English translation of this work is available. Extracts were translated into English by Dudley Loftus (1619–1695), who calls the author “Dionysius Syrus”, and these are in the public domain (published 1672 and 1695). Both books are freely readable online in the CC0 transcriptions of the Text Creation Partnership (EEBO-TCP); the page images are not open. A Clear and Learned Explication of the History of our Blessed Saviour Jesus Christ: Taken out of above Thirty Greek, Syriack, and other Oriental Authors, by way of Catena (Dublin: Samuel Lee, 1695; Wing D1524; EEBO-TCP text) translates the commentary on the first three and last three chapters of Matthew: the genealogies, the conception, birth and flight into Egypt, the baptism, and the passion, burial and resurrection, with the author’s Latin prefatory words to his catena on the four Gospels. The Exposition of Dionysius Syrus … upon the Gospel of St. Mark (Dublin: Joseph Wilde, 1672; Wing D1525; EEBO-TCP text) translates the commentary on Mark, followed by shorter pieces from the commentary (on the Beatitudes, the Lord’s Prayer, the Gospel canticles and other questions), other Syriac, Armenian and Greek texts, and a Latin version of Dionysius’ introduction to his commentary on the four Gospels. Loftus also made a Latin translation of the whole commentary from a manuscript at Trinity College, Dublin, which was never published and survives in manuscript in the Bodleian Library. Dionysius bar Salibi (d. 1171), Syriac Orthodox metropolitan of Amida, wrote a very large exposition of the four Gospels that works largely as a catena, giving the interpretations of earlier Greek and Syriac writers (among them a passage of Papias and comments of Eusebius) alongside his own. The Syriac text, with a Latin translation, is edited in the Corpus Scriptorum Christianorum Orientalium, beginning with J. Sedláček’s volume on Matthew (CSCO 16, 1906; archive.org). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 4 August 2009.)
