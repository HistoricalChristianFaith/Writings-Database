Book of Ceremonies

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Ann Moffatt and Maxeme Tall as Constantine Porphyrogennetos: The Book of Ceremonies (Byzantina Australiensia 18, in two volumes; Australian Association for Byzantine Studies, Canberra, 2012; ISBN 978-1-876503-42-0), printed facing the Greek text of Reiske’s Bonn edition (1829); it was reissued in paperback by Brill (Leiden, 2017; ISBN 978-90-04-34492-1) and is available from the publisher. Chapter II.42, the list of the imperial tombs in the Church of the Holy Apostles at Constantinople, is also translated by Glanville Downey in “The Tombs of the Byzantine Emperors at the Church of the Holy Apostles in Constantinople”, Journal of Hellenic Studies 79 (1959), pp. 27–51, and is edited and translated, together with the other surviving lists of the tombs, by Philip Grierson in “The Tombs and Obits of the Byzantine Emperors (337–1042)”, Dumbarton Oaks Papers 16 (1962), pp. 1–63 (JSTOR; Dumbarton Oaks has posted a scan of the article on the Internet Archive). Paul Stephenson translated some further excerpts on his website, which is now preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine (De Cerimoniis excerpts). The Book of Ceremonies (De cerimoniis aulae Byzantinae) was compiled in the circle of the emperor Constantine VII Porphyrogenitus (913–959) and partly revised under Nikephoros II Phokas. It is a handbook of the Byzantine court: it sets out, often step by step, the processions, receptions and banquets of the imperial year, the emperor’s part in the great feasts of the church, the ceremonies for coronations, promotions to office and the reception of foreign envoys, and a miscellany of lists and records, among them the catalogue of the emperors’ sarcophagi in the two imperial mausolea of the Holy Apostles.
