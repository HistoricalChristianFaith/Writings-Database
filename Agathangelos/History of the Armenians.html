History of the Armenians

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the Armenian text, by Robert W. Thomson, is Agathangelos: History of the Armenians (State University of New York Press, Albany, 1976; ISBN 978-0-87395-323-8; paperback reissue ISBN 978-1-4384-5174-9), with an introduction, commentary and a photographic reprint of the Armenian text; it is available from the publisher. It is a partial translation of the work as it stands: it leaves out the long central section known as the Teaching of Saint Gregory (§§259–715), which Thomson translated separately as The Teaching of Saint Gregory: An Early Armenian Catechism (Harvard Armenian Texts and Studies 3, 1970; revised edition 2001). The History, attributed to an otherwise unknown secretary of King Trdat named Agathangelos and put together in the fifth century, tells how Armenia became Christian: the sufferings of Gregory the Illuminator, the martyrdom of the virgins Hṙipʿsimē and Gayanē, the king’s affliction and cure, and Gregory’s preaching, consecration at Caesarea and baptism of the nation. It blends remembered tradition with legend, and its other versions in Greek, Arabic and Syriac do not all derive from the surviving Armenian text. S. C. Malan’s The Life and Times of S. Gregory the Illuminator (1868) is not a translation of this text but of an eighteenth-century Armenian retelling of the story. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 22 August 2011.)
