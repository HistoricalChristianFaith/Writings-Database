On the Acts of the Apostles

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, by Richard J. Schrader with Joseph L. Roberts III and John F. Makowski, is Arator’s On the Acts of the Apostles (De actibus apostolorum) (American Academy of Religion Classics in Religious Studies 6; Scholars Press, Atlanta, 1987; ISBN 978-1-55540-133-7, also issued as 978-1-55540-134-4), a complete translation of the poem with an introduction on Arator and his epic; Scholars Press has since closed, and the book can be traced through its WorldCat record. Arator, a north Italian who served as an official at the Gothic court of Ravenna who became a subdeacon at Rome under Pope Vigilius, wrote this single Latin hexameter poem in two books, also known as the Historia apostolica: the first follows Peter and the second Paul through selected episodes of the Acts of the Apostles, each retold briefly and then expounded at length in allegorical, moral and number-symbolic terms. It is framed by verse letters to Florianus and to Vigilius and a closing letter to Parthenius. A subscription in several manuscripts records that Arator read the poem aloud to clergy and people in the church of San Pietro in Vincoli in Rome over four days in April–June 544, to repeated calls for encores, and it was much read and commented on in the Middle Ages. The Latin text is in the Patrologia Latina, vol. 68, and in McKinlay’s edition (CSEL 72, 1951). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 26 December 2018.)
