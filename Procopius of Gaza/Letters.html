Letters

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Roger Pearse has shared online the working file he made for an abandoned translation project: an unchecked machine translation (slightly modified Google Translate output of Federica Ciccolella’s 2010 Italian translation, with a selection of her notes machine-translated by DeepSeek as endnotes to each letter), which he describes as having no scholarly value and asks readers not to quote. It covers all 174 letters in the numbering of Eugenio Amato (ed.), Rose di Gaza: gli scritti retorico-sofistici e le Epistole di Procopio di Gaza (Hellenica 35; Edizioni dell’Orso, Alessandria, 2010; ISBN 978-88-6274-233-7), and is available as Word, PDF and EPUB files on the Internet Archive; its licence is not stated. Procopius of Gaza (d. c. 528), a Christian sophist who taught rhetoric at Gaza and also wrote commentaries on the Old Testament, left a collection of short, polished letters to friends, former pupils, fellow rhetoricians, clergy and officials, full of classical allusion, which give a vivid picture of educated life in the eastern provinces around the year 500; the collection ends with an exchange of letters with Megethius. Letter 81, written to a Jerome living in Egypt, came down among the letters of St Jerome as the spurious Epistle 150. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 April 2026; an earlier post of 11 December 2025 gives unchecked DeepSeek versions of Letters 9, 81, 86, 91 and 124, all addressed to Jerome.)
