Synopsis of Histories

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by John Wortley as John Skylitzes: A Synopsis of Byzantine History, 811–1057 (Cambridge University Press, 2010; ISBN 978-0-521-76705-7; paperback 2011, ISBN 978-1-107-40474-8), with introductions by Jean-Claude Cheynet and Bernard Flusin and notes by Cheynet; it is available from the publisher. Written in the late eleventh century by John Skylitzes, a senior official at Constantinople, the Synopsis takes up the history of the Byzantine empire where Theophanes the Confessor left off and carries it from the death of Nikephoros I in 811 to the fall of Michael VI in 1057, reign by reign. In his preface Skylitzes criticises earlier historians, among them Demetrius of Cyzicus, whose history is now lost, for writing with partisan aims, and promises a plain narrative drawn from their accounts. Much of the later Byzantine chronicle of George Kedrenos is copied from it almost word for word, and its best-known manuscript, the Madrid Skylitzes, is illustrated with several hundred miniatures. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 December 2013, and 28 October 2026, who quotes the preface from Wortley’s version.)
