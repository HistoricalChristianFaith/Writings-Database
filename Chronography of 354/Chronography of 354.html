Chronography of 354

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An online edition of the whole compilation, put together by Roger Pearse in 2006, is free on his website at tertullian.org, with an introduction to the online edition and a page for each of its sixteen parts: (1) title page and dedication; (2) images of the personified cities of Rome, Alexandria, Constantinople and Trier; (3) images of the emperors and the birthdays of the Caesars; (4) the seven planets with a calendar of the hours; (5) the signs of the zodiac; (6) the Philocalian calendar; (7) portraits of the emperors; (8) the consular fasti to 354; (9) the dates of Easter from 312 to 411; (10) the prefects of the city of Rome from 254 to 354; (11) the burial dates of the bishops of Rome from 255 to 352; (12) the commemoration dates of the martyrs; (13) the bishops of Rome (the Liberian Catalogue); (14) the fourteen regions of the city; (15) the Liber generationis, or Book of Generations; and (16) the Chronicle of the City of Rome. The edition is mostly the Latin text rather than a translation: of the pages checked (parts 1, 6, 12 and 13), the calendar (part 6) gives the Latin as edited by Mommsen in Inscriptiones Latinae Antiquissimae (1893) and the commemorations of the martyrs (part 12) the Latin of Mommsen’s MGH Chronica Minora I (1892), each with English notes, and the title page (part 1) is a photograph with notes; of the pages checked, only the Liberian Catalogue (part 13) has an English translation, facing the Latin, and that translation is given in this collection as Bishops of Rome (The Liberian Catalogue). Each page states “All material on this page is in the public domain - copy freely”, and Pearse’s introduction to the online edition, on the manuscripts and the order of the parts, draws almost all its information from Michele Renee Salzman, On Roman Time (1990). The Chronography is a lavishly illustrated book of calendars and lists made in 354 for a wealthy Roman Christian named Valentinus by the calligrapher and artist Furius Dionysius Filocalus. The original is lost; the work survives only in later, partial copies, most of them descended from a lost Carolingian manuscript, some with the pictures and some with the text alone. Alongside the civic calendar and the lists of consuls and city prefects it contains some of the earliest documents of the Roman church: the Easter table, the anniversaries of the bishops and martyrs of Rome (the list of martyrs opens with the earliest notice of Christ’s birth being kept on 25 December), the catalogue of the popes down to Liberius, and a Latin version of the chronicle of Hippolytus. Pearse’s translations of the poems that accompany the months of the calendar are given separately in this collection as Poems of the Months. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 21 February 2022, who calls his compilation the only publication that presents the work as a whole, in order.)
