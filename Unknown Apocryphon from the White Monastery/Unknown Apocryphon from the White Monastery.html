Unknown Apocryphon from the White Monastery

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The surviving text was edited, with the Coptic text and an English translation of everything that survives, by Alin Suciu and Einar Thomassen, “An Unknown ‘Apocryphal’ Text from the White Monastery”, in Paola Buzi and Alberto Camplani (eds.), Christianity in Egypt: Literary Production and Intellectual Trends. Studies in Honor of Tito Orlandi (Studia Ephemeridis Augustinianum 125; Institutum Patristicum Augustinianum, Rome, 2011; ISBN 978-88-7961-135-0), pp. 477–499; the volume is listed by the publisher, and Suciu has posted an offprint of the article on his blog (its licence is not stated). The work is known only from two consecutive parchment leaves (pages 11–14) of a Sahidic Coptic codex from the White Monastery near Sohag in Upper Egypt, apparently in the hand of the scribe Zacharias, who dated another of his codices to 1005/6: the first leaf is in the Schøyen Collection in Norway (MS 1991), and the second is in Paris (Bibliothèque nationale, Copte 1317, fol. 294). Its title and author are lost, and the name used here is only a description. What survives is a dialogue set after the crucifixion, in which Jesus, who is never named, tells his disciples that as he has conquered, so will they: he insists that he suffered as one fully human as well as divine, and that they share in his power because he is their head, root and leaven, working out the image of the head and the limbs with some medical detail. It matches no known apocryphon; the editors note that its echoes of fifth-century Christological debate tell against an early date, and that the dialogue may have been set within a Coptic sermon or martyrdom rather than in a gospel. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 January 2011.)
