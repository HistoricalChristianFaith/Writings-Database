Letter to Apollonius

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The surviving text was edited with an English translation by John Barns and Henry Chadwick, “A Letter Ascribed to Peter of Alexandria”, The Journal of Theological Studies, n.s. 24 (1973), pp. 443–455, available from the publisher and on JSTOR; it translates all that survives, with Barns’s edition of the Coptic and Chadwick’s historical introduction. Only the opening of the letter is preserved, in Coptic, on a single damaged parchment leaf of the ninth or tenth century from the White Monastery near Sohag (Paris, BnF copte 131(1), fol. 1r–v, digitized on Gallica), and it breaks off in mid-sentence. Its heading presents it as a letter of Peter, archbishop of Alexandria, to Apollonius, bishop of Siout (Asyut), written on hearing that he had sacrificed to the idols in the persecution under Diocletian: Peter grieves over the shepherd the enemy has led astray, rebukes him for arguing with his persecutors instead of turning to him for help, recalls how he brought Basil the Persian, a worshipper of the sun, moon, water and fire, to the faith through his tears, and pleads with Apollonius to return; whether the letter is really Peter’s is uncertain. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 30 July 2026.)
