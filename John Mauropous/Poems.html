No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Floris Bernard and Christopher Livanos, is in The Poems of Christopher of Mytilene and John Mauropous (Dumbarton Oaks Medieval Library 50; Harvard University Press, 2018; ISBN 978-0-674-73698-6), which gives a revised Greek text with a facing English translation and notes; it is available from the publisher. The volume translates Mauropous’ collection of poems as it survives in its main manuscript, Vaticanus graecus 676, which is thought to have been copied under the poet’s own supervision, and prints it alongside the Various Verses of his contemporary Christopher of Mytilene. John Mauropous (c. 1000–c. 1080) was a teacher at Constantinople, whose pupils included Michael Psellos, and a court orator under Constantine IX Monomachos, before being sent away as metropolitan of Euchaita; late in life he retired to a monastery in the capital. He arranged the collection himself: alongside epigrams for icons and buildings and poems for patrons and friends, it includes reflective pieces in which he shapes an account of his own life and career, all written in the Byzantine twelve-syllable line. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 7 October 2026.)
