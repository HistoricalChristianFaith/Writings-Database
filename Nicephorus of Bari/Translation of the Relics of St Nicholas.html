Translation of the Relics of St Nicholas

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation by Charles W. Jones of the complete Latin account by Nicephorus, made from the text of the Vatican manuscript (Vat. lat. 5074) as edited by Nitti di Vito (1937), is reproduced free online by the St. Nicholas Center as “The Translation of Saint Nicholas, Confessor” (formerly at this address), with headings and illustrations added by the Center. Its licence is not stated: the page reprints the translation by permission from Jones’ Saint Nicholas of Myra, Bari, and Manhattan: Biography of a Legend (University of Chicago Press, 1978; ISBN 978-0-226-40699-2), pp. 176–193, which remains in copyright. Nicephorus, a cleric of Bari, wrote the earliest account of how the body of St Nicholas was carried from Myra to Bari in the spring of 1087, apparently within days of its arrival on 9 May and at the request of the judge Curcorius and other leading men of the city. He tells how merchants of Bari trading to Antioch, learning that the Venetians meant to do the same, hurried to Myra, overcame the resistance of the monks who guarded the shrine, broke open the tomb and took away the bones amid the lament of the townspeople; he then follows the voyage home, the dispute in Bari over where the relics should rest, and the many cures reported at the shrine in the days after their arrival. Later medieval copies, in the “Vatican” and “Beneventan” recensions, mix his account with that of the Archdeacon John. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 4 January 2024.)
