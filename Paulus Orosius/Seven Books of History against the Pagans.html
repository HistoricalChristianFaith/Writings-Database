Seven Books of History against the Pagans

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Irving Woodworth Raymond, Seven Books of History against the Pagans: The Apology of Paulus Orosius (Columbia University Press, 1936), had its copyright renewed and remains in copyright in the United States. Two later complete translations are in print: Roy J. Deferrari, The Seven Books of History against the Pagans (Fathers of the Church 50; Catholic University of America Press, 1964; ISBN 978-0-8132-0050-7; paperback ISBN 978-0-8132-1310-1), available from the publisher; and A. T. Fear, Orosius: Seven Books of History against the Pagans (Translated Texts for Historians 54; Liverpool University Press, 2010; ISBN 978-1-84631-473-5; paperback ISBN 978-1-84631-239-7), with an introduction and full notes, available from the publisher. Orosius, a Spanish priest and friend of Augustine, wrote the work at Augustine's request around 417, after the sack of Rome by Alaric, to answer the pagan charge that the disasters of the age were the fault of the Christians. Running from the creation and the earliest empires down to his own day, it sets out to show that the world before Christ had suffered wars, plagues and calamities at least as great. It was the first Christian history of the secular world rather than of the Church, and it was one of the most widely read history books of the Middle Ages. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 19 June 2009; see also his post of 31 May 2012.)
