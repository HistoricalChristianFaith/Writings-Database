Book of Allogenes

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Coptic text, with an English translation, is published in The Gospel of Judas, Together with the Letter of Peter to Philip, James, and a Book of Allogenes from Codex Tchacos: Critical Edition, with introductions, translations and notes by Rodolphe Kasser, Gregor Wurst, Marvin Meyer and François Gaudard (National Geographic, Washington, 2007; ISBN 978-1-4262-0191-2); no publisher's page for it is still online (see its Open Library record). It is an edition of the whole codex, in which this is the last of the four texts. The Book of Allogenes (also called the Temptation of Allogenes) is a Gnostic work, originally in Greek, which survives only in this badly damaged Coptic copy, occupying eight pages of Codex Tchacos (pp. 59–66); its title is lost, and it is a different text from the Nag Hammadi tractate Allogenes. In the better-preserved part, after a prayer that seems to be spoken by Allogenes ("the Stranger"), Satan appears to him and offers him food, silver, gold and garments, but Allogenes refuses him, saying that he seeks his Father, who is above all the aeons, and that he does not belong to Satan; the fragmentary end seems to describe his being taken up into a luminous cloud. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 November 2007.)
