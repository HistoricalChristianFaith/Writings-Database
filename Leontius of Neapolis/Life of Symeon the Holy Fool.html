Life of Symeon the Holy Fool

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Derek Krueger, the first in English, appears as the appendix to his study Symeon the Holy Fool: Leontius’s Life and the Late Antique City (The Transformation of the Classical Heritage 25; University of California Press, 1996; ISBN 978-0-520-08911-2). It was made from Lennart Rydén’s Greek text as printed in A. J. Festugière’s edition (Vie de Syméon le Fou et Vie de Jean de Chypre, Paris, 1974). The whole book can be read free online in the publisher’s UC Press E-Books Collection; its licence is not stated, and it remains in copyright. Leontius, bishop of Neapolis (Limassol) in Cyprus in the seventh century, tells the story of Symeon of Emesa, a sixth-century monk who, after many years as a hermit in the desert near the Dead Sea with his companion John, went to the Syrian city of Emesa and lived there as a “fool for Christ”. He behaved outrageously in public, earning mockery and blows, while secretly healing the sick, rescuing sinners and foretelling events, and hid his holiness until his death. The Life became an influential model for later holy fools in Byzantium and Russia. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 5 August 2023.)
