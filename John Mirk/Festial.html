Festial

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Festial was written in Middle English, and the original text is freely available in Theodor Erbe’s edition, Mirk’s Festial: A Collection of Homilies, by Johannes Mirkus (John Mirk), Part I (Early English Text Society, extra series 96; London: Kegan Paul, Trench, Trübner, 1905), edited from Bodleian MS Gough Eccl. Top. 4 with variant readings from other manuscripts; a scan is on the Internet Archive here. The edition is in the public domain (published 1905). This first part gives the text and a glossary; the promised second part, with introduction and notes, never appeared. A newer critical edition by Susan Powell, from British Library MS Cotton Claudius A. II, was published by the Early English Text Society in 2009–2011. John Mirk, an Augustinian canon of Lilleshall in Shropshire active around 1400, wrote the Festial as a ready-made book of sermons for parish priests, arranged by the feasts of the church year from Advent onward: Sundays and seasons, the great feasts of Christ and the Virgin, and the days of the apostles and of saints such as George, Margaret, Winifred, Alkmund and Katherine, ending with sermons on the dedication of a church and on the Lord’s Prayer (Erbe adds five further pieces from the Claudius manuscript, on marriage, burial, the Hail Mary and miracles of the Virgin). Each sermon tells the people why the day is kept and illustrates it with legends and short exemplary stories, many drawn from the Golden Legend; the work was one of the most often printed English books before the Reformation. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 31 May 2021, who quotes, slightly modernised, the passage of the sermon for Michaelmas (p. 258) that places an apparition of St Michael at St Michael’s Mount in Cornwall.)
