On the Error of Profane Religions

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation of the De errore profanarum religionum, with an introduction and commentary, by Richard E. Oster (Julius Firmicus Maternus: De errore profanarum religionum. Introduction, translation and commentary, Master’s thesis, Rice University, 1971), is free online as a PDF in Rice University’s repository. Its licence is not stated beyond the repository’s general notice that copyright is held by the author. Julius Firmicus Maternus, a Roman senator who had earlier written the Mathesis, a handbook of astrology, wrote this work as a Christian convert around 346–350 and addressed it to the emperors Constantius II and Constans. The first part (chapters 1–17) argues that the gods of the pagan cults, among them Isis and Osiris, Attis and Cybele, Dionysus and Mithras, are nothing but deified natural elements or dead men and women, and mocks their myths and rites; the second part (chapters 18–29) quotes the Greek watchwords and formulas of the mystery cults and treats them as the devil’s imitations of Christian symbols such as the rock, the bridegroom and the sacred meal. Throughout, Firmicus urges the emperors to suppress paganism by force, and the work is valued as a source for the mystery religions of late antiquity. Other English translations: Clarence A. Forbes, Firmicus Maternus: The Error of the Pagan Religions (Ancient Christian Writers 37; Newman Press, 1970; ISBN 978-0-8091-0039-2), complete, with notes, available from the publisher.
