Memoriale Sanctorum

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Aymenn Jawad Al-Tamimi, with an introduction and notes, is free online on his website in three parts: Preface and Book One (24 September 2020), Book Two (6 November 2020) and Book Three (28 November 2020), each since revised; it is made from the Latin text in Juan Gil’s Corpus Scriptorum Muzarabicorum (Madrid, 1973), checked against Lorenzana’s edition (Madrid, 1785). Its licence is not stated. The Memoriale Sanctorum (“Memorial of the Saints”), Eulogius’s largest work, was written at Córdoba in the 850s, during the executions of Christians by the authorities of the Umayyad emirate for apostasy from Islam or for denouncing the Prophet. The first book defends those who died as true martyrs against Christian critics and Muslim taunts; the second tells the stories of the martyrs of 850–852, several of whom Eulogius knew, down to the death of the amir ʿAbd al-Raḥmān II; and the third describes the measures taken against the Christians under his son Muḥammad I and the further martyrdoms of 853–856. Other English translations: Kenneth Baxter Wolf, The Eulogius Corpus (Translated Texts for Historians 71; Liverpool University Press, 2019; ISBN 978-1-78962-079-5), a translation of all of Eulogius’s works together with Paul Alvarus’s Life of Eulogius, available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 June 2021.)
