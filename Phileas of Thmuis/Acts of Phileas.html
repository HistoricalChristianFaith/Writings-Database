Acts of Phileas

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Acts are translated by Herbert Musurillo in his anthology The Acts of the Christian Martyrs (Oxford Early Christian Texts; Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1972; ISBN 978-0-19-826806-2; now issued as The Acts of the Christian Martyrs, Volume II, ISBN 978-0-19-884336-8), available from the publisher. The anthology prints the Greek version of the Acts in the Bodmer papyrus (P. Bodmer XX), whose translation begins at pp. 330–331, and the ancient Latin version, each with an English translation on facing pages. A second, different Greek version, in the Chester Beatty papyrus (P. Chester Beatty XV), was first edited and translated by Albert Pietersma in The Acts of Phileas, Bishop of Thmuis (Including Fragments of the Greek Psalter): P. Chester Beatty XV (With a New Edition of P. Bodmer XX, and Halkin’s Latin Acta) (Cahiers d’Orientalisme 7; Geneva: Patrick Cramer, 1984), available from its publisher. Phileas, a learned bishop of Thmuis in the Nile Delta, was tried at Alexandria before the prefect Culcianus during the Great Persecution under Diocletian and beheaded. The Acts record, in the form of question and answer, his last appearance in court, where he refuses to sacrifice and argues with the prefect about sacrifice and the resurrection, and about Paul compared with Plato; only the Latin version keeps the ending, in which the high official Philoromus takes his part and is condemned with him. The Bodmer text also contains the prefect’s taunt that “Pierius saved many by submitting”, which scholars have taken as proof that the Alexandrian scholar Pierius lapsed in the persecution. The letter that Phileas wrote from prison to his flock at Thmuis, preserved by Eusebius (Church History 8.10), is a separate work. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 3 September 2026.)
