On the Astrolabe

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English version of the treatise by Jessie Payne Smith Margoliouth, made with F. Nau’s edition and French translation (Le traité sur l’astrolabe plan de Sévère Sabokt, 1899) and including Nau’s notes, was published as “Description of the Astrolabe” in R. T. Gunther, The Astrolabes of the World, vol. 1 (Oxford University Press, 1932), pp. 82–103. Roger Pearse’s transcription of it (2005) is online free at tertullian.org, with a mirror at CCEL. The page marks the transcription as public domain, but the 1932 translation itself is still in copyright in the United States until the end of 2027, so it is not reproduced here. Severus, bishop of Qenneshrin and the leading Syriac scholar of Greek science in the seventh century, wrote the treatise before 660 from Greek sources: a first part describes the flat brass astrolabe and its plates, circles and pointer, and a second explains how to use it to tell the hour by day and by night, to find the positions of the sun, moon and planets and the risings of the signs of the zodiac, and to test whether an instrument is accurately made. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 April 2010.)
