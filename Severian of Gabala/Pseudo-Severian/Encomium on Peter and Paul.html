Encomium on Peter and Paul

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete encomium was translated by Michael E. Foat as “Encomium on SS. Peter and Paul Attributed to Severian of Gabala”, in Leo Depuydt (general editor), Encomiastica from the Pierpont Morgan Library: Five Coptic Homilies Attributed to Anastasius of Euchaita, Epiphanius of Salamis, Isaac of Antinoe, Severian of Gabala, and Theopempus of Antioch, vol. 2: Translation (Corpus Scriptorum Christianorum Orientalium 545, Scriptores Coptici 48; Leuven: Peeters, 1993; ISBN 978-90-6831-575-2), pp. 65–101, listed on Google Books (no page for the volume could be found on the publisher’s site); the Coptic text is in vol. 1 (CSCO 544), pp. 85–130. The anthology gives one homily each by five authors. This Coptic sermon, falsely attributed to Severian of Gabala and listed among his spurious works as an encomium on the twelve apostles (CPG 4281), was composed in Egypt for the feast of the martyrdom of Peter and Paul and of the twelve apostles: it praises all the apostles, with Mark and Luke, first from the Gospel accounts of their calling and then from apocryphal traditions of their missions, the death of Peter and the conversion and disciples of Paul, and gives a long account of Mark, the evangelist of Egypt, that runs parallel to the Martyrdom of Mark. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 July 2018.)
