Speculum Ecclesiae

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. English translations of many of its sermons, by Gerhard Eger and a co-author writing as “Aelredus Rievallensis”, have been posted free on their blog Canticum Salomonis (2020–2022), most with a downloadable PDF of the translation and their Latin text (edited from Patrologia Latina 172 and manuscripts such as Admont 131 and Graz 173); their licence is not stated. The sermons translated there are: Christmas, “Laetentur coeli”; the sermon and bidding prayers for the highest feasts; St Stephen; St John the Evangelist; Candlemas; Ash Wednesday; the Second Sunday of Lent; Laetare Sunday; Palm Sunday; the Invention of the Cross; the Ascension; Pentecost; St Mary Magdalene; the Exaltation of the Cross; All Saints; St Martin; and the general sermon to all estates. Roger Pearse’s public-domain translation of the sermon on St Nicholas is given separately in this folder (“Sermon on St Nicholas”). The Speculum Ecclesiae (Mirror of the Church) is a collection of model sermons that Honorius Augustodunensis compiled in the early twelfth century for priests to preach on the main feasts and some Sundays of the church year; each mixes scriptural allegory with stories of the saint or the feast, often in rhymed prose, and the collection also contains a general sermon, to be repeated through the year, that addresses each order of society in turn. It was widely copied and excerpted in the Middle Ages, was first printed at Cologne in 1531, and is most accessible in Migne’s Patrologia Latina 172, cols. 807–1107; there is no critical edition. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 13 March 2023.)
