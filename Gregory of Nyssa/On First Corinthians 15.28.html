On First Corinthians 15.28

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation of the In illud: Tunc et ipse Filius by Richard (Casimir) McCambly, first published in the Greek Orthodox Theological Review 28.1 (1983), is offered free, with his introduction and notes, as a PDF among his translations of Gregory of Nyssa on the Lectio Divina website; its licence is not stated. In this short treatise Gregory explains Paul’s words “then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him that put all things under him, that God may be all in all” (1 Corinthians 15:28), which his Arian opponents cited as proof that the Son is inferior to the Father. Gregory argues that the subjection belongs not to the Word’s divinity but to his body, the Church and the whole of human nature united to him, which he brings to the Father when evil has been entirely done away and God is all in all. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 May 2019.)
