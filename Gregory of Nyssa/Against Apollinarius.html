Against Apollinarius

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation of the Antirrheticus adversus Apolinarium by Richard (Casimir) McCambly is offered free, with his introduction and notes, as a PDF among his translations of Gregory of Nyssa on the Lectio Divina website; its licence is not stated. Gregory’s longest Christological work is a point-by-point refutation of the Demonstration (Apodeixis) of Apollinarius of Laodicea, which survives chiefly in the excerpts Gregory quotes. Against Apollinarius’ teaching that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind, and that Christ’s flesh came down from heaven, Gregory insists that the Son assumed a complete human nature, soul and mind included, in order to heal the whole of it. Other English translations: Robin Orton, in Anti-Apollinarian Writings (The Fathers of the Church 131; Catholic University of America Press, 2015; ISBN 978-0-8132-2807-5), available from the publisher. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 May 2019.)
