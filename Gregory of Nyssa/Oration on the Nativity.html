Oration on the Nativity

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A draft English translation by Andrew Radde-Gallwitz (2017) of the Oration on the Saviour’s Nativity (In diem natalem Christi, also called In diem natalem Salvatoris; CPG 3194), made from Friedhelm Mann’s critical edition in the Gregorii Nysseni Opera (vol. 10.2, Brill, 1996), is freely available on Humanities Commons; the deposit is marked “All rights reserved”. The final version is printed in Mark DelCogliano (ed.), The Cambridge Edition of Early Christian Writings, Volume 3: Christ: Through the Nestorian Controversy (Cambridge University Press, 2022; ISBN 978-1-107-06213-9), pp. 403–419, available from the publisher. Two other complete English translations can be read online: Beth Dunlop’s, in her unpublished Boston College dissertation The Earliest Greek Patristic Orations on the Nativity (2004), offered as a PDF on dec25th.info with a notice that it is copyrighted and may be read but not copied; and an anonymous one on John Sanidopoulos’s blog Orthodox Christianity Then and Now, whose licence is not stated. Gregory preached this sermon at Christmas. He celebrates the birth of Christ and the wonder of a virgin who becomes a mother and remains a virgin, which he sees foreshadowed in the bush that burned without being consumed, and goes on to the slaughter of the infants of Bethlehem by Herod, from which Migne’s fuller title (Oratio in diem natalem Christi et in infantes qui in Bethleem occisi sunt a Herode) is taken. The Greek text is also printed in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 46, cols. 1128–1149. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 3 October 2025; see also his post of 25 November 2011.)
