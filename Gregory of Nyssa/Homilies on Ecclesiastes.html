Homilies on Ecclesiastes

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete work was translated by Stuart George Hall and Rachel Moriarty in Gregory of Nyssa: Homilies on Ecclesiastes. An English Version with Supporting Studies, edited by Stuart G. Hall (Proceedings of the Seventh International Colloquium on Gregory of Nyssa, St Andrews, 5–10 September 1990; Walter de Gruyter, 1993; ISBN 978-3-11-013586-2), available from the publisher; the translation of all eight homilies, made from Paul Alexander’s critical text in the Gregorii Nysseni Opera (vol. 5), is followed by studies of each homily and of Gregory’s exegesis. An earlier translation of all eight homilies by Richard (Casimir) McCambly, with his introduction, was posted free on the Gregory of Nyssa Home Page at the Sage Colleges and is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine; its licence is not stated. In eight homilies Gregory expounds Ecclesiastes 1:1–3:13, reading the book as teaching the soul to see the emptiness of what passes away and to rise towards God. The fourth homily, on the verse “I got me slaves and slave-girls” (Ecclesiastes 2:7), is one of the sharpest attacks on slavery in ancient literature: Gregory asks what price could be set on a being made in the image of God, and tells the slave-owner that he has made himself master over his own kind in defiance of God. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 31 July 2010; see also his post of 24 January 2019, which quotes the passage on slavery in full.)
