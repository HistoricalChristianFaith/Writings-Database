On the Nativity of the Lord

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by Kurt Simmons (2023), made from the text in the Patrologia Graeca (vol. 96, cols. 1435–1450), is posted on Academia.edu as “Concerning the Nativity of the Lord, to Zachariah, Catholicos of Greater Armenia” (a free Academia.edu login may be needed to download it). Its licence is not stated. The author, traditionally called John of Nicaea (Iohannes Nicaenus), was in fact an Armenian named Vahan, archbishop of Nike in Thrace, who around 861–862 went as the envoy of the patriarch Photius to Zacharias, catholicos of Greater Armenia, and represented Constantinople at the Council of Širakawan (862/63). His letter to Zacharias (De nativitate Domini) explains, among other things, how the Church came to keep Christ’s birth on 25 December, separately from his baptism on 6 January: it repeats the legend that a bishop of Jerusalem asked Pope Julius I to search the Jewish records carried off to Rome by Titus, and that Julius, finding from Josephus that Zechariah’s vision fell on the Day of Atonement in the seventh month, counted the Hebrew and Roman months forward to fix the conception and birth of John the Baptist, the Annunciation on 25 March and the Nativity on 25 December. Other English translations: Roger Pearse translates the passage about Pope Julius and the reckoning of the months (from the Latin version in the Patrologia) in his post “Dubious claims: Pope Julius I decided that Jesus was born on 25 December?” (21 December 2018).
