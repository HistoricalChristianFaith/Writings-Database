Apocalypse of Adam

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation by George W. MacRae, prepared for the Coptic Gnostic Library Project of the Institute for Antiquity and Christianity at Claremont, is posted free, edited for that site, in the Gnostic Society Library at gnosis.org; the site notes that E. J. Brill has asserted copyright on the project’s translations, and no licence for reuse is stated. The Apocalypse of Adam, the fifth tractate of Nag Hammadi Codex V (a Coptic codex of the fourth century; the date of composition is uncertain), is a Sethian Gnostic revelation which Adam gives to his son Seth in his seven hundredth year. Adam tells how he and Eve lost the knowledge of the eternal God when the god who created them divided them in wrath, and foretells how that god will try to destroy the seed of Seth by flood and by fire, how angels will rescue them, and how an Illuminator will come; thirteen “kingdoms” then give false accounts of the Illuminator’s origin, one of them a birth from a rock that has been compared with the birth of Mithras. Other English translations: George W. MacRae, “The Apocalypse of Adam”, in Douglas M. Parrott (ed.), Nag Hammadi Codices V,2–5 and VI with Papyrus Berolinensis 8502, 1 and 4 (Nag Hammadi Studies 11; Brill, 1979; ISBN 978-90-04-05798-2), available from the publisher; “The Revelation of Adam”, in Marvin Meyer (ed.), The Nag Hammadi Scriptures (HarperOne, 2007; ISBN 978-0-06-052378-7).
