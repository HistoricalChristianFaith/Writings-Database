Letter 9 to Salonius

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete letter was translated by Jeremiah F. O'Sullivan in The Writings of Salvian, the Presbyter (The Fathers of the Church 3; New York: Fathers of the Church, Inc., 1947), which also contains Salvian's other eight letters, The Governance of God and the four books To the Church; the volume is now published by the Catholic University of America Press (paperback, 2008; ISBN 978-0-8132-1550-1) and is available from the publisher. Most of the letter is also translated in Alfred Haefner, “A unique source for the study of ancient pseudonymity,” Anglican Theological Review 16 (1934), pp. 8–15 (from Pauly's CSEL text, omitting the long answer to the first question), of which Roger Pearse has posted a PDF. The letter does not come down with Salvian's other letters but is found prefixed to his treatise To the Church (Ad Ecclesiam, c. 440), which had been sent out under the name of “Timothy.” Writing to Salonius, bishop of Geneva and his former pupil, who had asked why the name stood on the books and warned that they might be counted among the apocrypha, Salvian answers three questions, always speaking of the author in the third person: why the work was addressed to the whole Church (because the avarice it attacks had spread through every rank, clergy and bishops included), why the author withheld his own name (to shun human praise and from a low opinion of himself, so that the book would be weighed by its usefulness rather than its author), and why he chose “Timothy” in particular (because the name means “honour of God,” for whose honour the books were written). It is often cited in modern discussions of ancient pseudonymity and forgery. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 9 October 2013; see also his posts of 12 October 2013, 25 October 2013 and 22 November 2013.)
