Martyrdom of Mark

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The Greek Martyrdom of Mark (Martyrium Marci, BHG 1035–1036) is translated by Tobias Nicklas as “The Martyrdom of Mark” in Tony Burke (ed.), New Testament Apocrypha: More Noncanonical Scriptures, vol. 3 (Eerdmans, 2023; ISBN 978-0-8028-7793-2), pp. 375–92, available from the publisher; the complete text is translated there, with an introduction. An earlier translation by Allen Dwight Callahan appeared, under the misleading title “The Acts of Saint Mark: An Introduction and Translation,” in Coptic Church Review 14 (1993), pp. 3–10, and in the appendix to his Harvard dissertation (1992). Older public-domain English translations exist only of versions in other languages: of an Arabic version by Agnes Smith Lewis, The Mythological Acts of the Apostles (Horae Semiticae 4, 1904), pp. 147–51 (archive.org); of the Ethiopic by S. C. Malan (1871) and E. A. W. Budge (1901); and of a seventh-century Coptic papyrus fragment by W. E. Crum, Theological Texts from Coptic Papyri (1913), pp. 65–68 (archive.org). The short legend tells how the lot of preaching in Egypt fell to the evangelist Mark. After preaching in Cyrene and the Pentapolis he comes to Alexandria, where his sandal breaks at the city gate; he heals the cobbler who mends it, Anianus (Ananias in some texts), who is converted with his household and later made bishop. When the pagans of the city turn against the growing church, Mark is seized at Easter, which that year fell on the festival of Serapis, dragged through the streets by a rope round his neck, and imprisoned; in the night an angel and then Christ himself appear to him, and the next day he dies under the same treatment. A storm stops the crowd from burning his body, which the Christians bury with honour. It is a different and much shorter text from the Acts of Mark (BHG 1036m). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 July 2018.)
