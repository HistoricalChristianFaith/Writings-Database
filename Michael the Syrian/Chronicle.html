Chronicle

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first complete English translation of the Syriac text, by Matti Moosa, is The Syriac Chronicle of Michael Rabo (the Great): A Universal History from the Creation (Teaneck, NJ: Beth Antioch Press, 2014; ISBN 978-1-939682-09-3), a large volume of 827 pages with an introduction and brief notes, published for the Archdiocese of the Syriac Orthodox Church for the Eastern United States; it is available from the publisher. Michael, patriarch of the Syriac Orthodox Church from 1166 to 1199, compiled this world history from the Creation down to his own day, setting out secular events, church affairs and miscellaneous notices (portents, earthquakes, famines) side by side in separate columns. He drew on many earlier chroniclers and historians, some of them now lost, and often quotes them at length, while his final books are an eyewitness account of the Crusader era. The Syriac survives in a single manuscript whose beginning is lost; the opening is known from the shorter thirteenth-century Armenian version, Robert Bedrosian’s translation of which is on this site as a separate work. Scholars long relied on J.-B. Chabot’s French translation, published with the Syriac text (Paris, 1899–1910). Roger Pearse has rendered a few passages into English from Chabot’s French on his blog: the preface (as Chabot restored it from the Armenian), 16 June 2007; book 12, chapter 1, 25 July 2009; the passage on the end of the Montanists at Pepuza (book 9, chapter 33), 5 July 2010; and the opening of John of Ephesus’s account of the Justinianic plague, 10 May 2017. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 29 March 2014.)
