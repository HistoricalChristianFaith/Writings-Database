Miracles at Saint Michael's Mount

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Gilbert H. Doble (d. 1945) printed the first edition of the Latin text, with an English translation, in his short undated pamphlet Miracles at St. Michael’s Mount in Cornwall in 1262 (St Michael’s Mount, after 1935; sometimes dated 1945), which is free to read at the Internet Archive. Its licence is not stated in the pamphlet. The text, catalogued as Miracula in Monte S. Michaelis in Cornubia (BHL 5955b), is an untitled note added by a later hand on one of the leaves preceding the chronicle in Avranches, Bibliothèque municipale, MS 159, from the library of Mont-Saint-Michel, to which the Cornish Mount then belonged. In a set formula it records three cures at the church of St Michael’s Mount in 1262: Christina, from near Glastonbury, blind for about six years, who received her sight before High Mass; Matilda, of the parish of Lanescli (Gulval), who had lost consciousness and speech and recovered them on entering the church, an event the writer says he witnessed; and Alice, from the Hereford district, blind for seven years, who was healed before sunrise. It names the prior, Ralph Viel, and three of the house’s socii, and refers to a fourth miracle, the cure of a dumb man, on a page now lost. Doble’s pamphlet adds a commentary on the names and places. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 22 May 2021, who reproduces Doble’s Latin text and translation.)
