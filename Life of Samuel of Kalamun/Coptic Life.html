Coptic Life

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete Coptic Life, attributed to Isaac the Presbyter, was edited and translated by Anthony Alcock as The Life of Samuel of Kalamun by Isaac the Presbyter (Aris & Phillips, Warminster, 1983; ISBN 978-0-85668-219-3), with the Coptic text from Pierpont Morgan Library ms. M 578 and an English translation; the translation was reissued by St Shenouda Press (2020; ISBN 9780648865858). Samuel (597–695) was a monk of Scetis who rejected the Council of Chalcedon and was beaten for it by Cyrus, the Chalcedonian patriarch and governor of Egypt; the Life tells of his expulsion from the Nekloni monastery in the Fayum, his captivity among the Berbers, and his founding of the monastery at Kalamun, which he ruled as abbot for the rest of his life. Part of the first Sahidic fragment published by Amélineau is paraphrased in A. J. Butler, The Arab Conquest of Egypt (Oxford, 1902), pp. 185 ff., available at the Internet Archive. A later Arabic version of the Life was also edited and translated by Alcock (Le Muséon, 1996 and 1998). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 11 October 2011; see also his post of 23 December 2010.)
