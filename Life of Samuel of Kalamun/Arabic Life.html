Arabic Life

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. Anthony Alcock published the Arabic text for the first time, with an English translation, in two articles in the journal Le Muséon (Peeters, Leuven): “The Arabic Life of Anbā Samaw’īl of Qalamūn”, Le Muséon 109 (1996), pp. 321–345, and “The Arabic Life of Anbā Samaw’īl of Qalamūn: II”, Le Muséon 111 (1998), pp. 377–404, which together give the complete text. It is the Arabic version of the Life of Samuel of Kalamun (597–695), the Coptic monk who was beaten for rejecting the Council of Chalcedon, carried off by the Berbers, and founded the monastery at Kalamun in the Fayum. Alcock edited it from a very late copy, a manuscript written in 1945 and kept at the Franciscan Centre of Christian Oriental Studies in Cairo; the Life also survives complete in Coptic and in Ethiopic, and although the Arabic often stands closer to the Ethiopic, Alcock argues that it goes back ultimately to the Coptic original. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 6 October 2011.)
