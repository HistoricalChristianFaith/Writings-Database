Dialogue of a Montanist and an Orthodox

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The complete Greek text, taken from Ficker’s edition, is printed with an English translation by Ronald E. Heine in The Montanist Oracles and Testimonia (North American Patristic Society, Patristic Monograph Series 14; Macon, GA: Mercer University Press, 1989; ISBN 978-0-86554-333-1), pp. 112–27, as one of the testimonia in his collection of the ancient sources for Montanism; a preview is on Google Books. The Dialogue (Dialexis; CPG 2572) is a short anonymous Greek debate, probably of the fourth century and sometimes ascribed to Didymus the Blind, which survives among the works of Athanasius in a manuscript of the Escorial. In it an orthodox Christian answers a Montanist who claims that Montanus brought the perfect gift of the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, promised in Paul’s “when that which is perfect is come”; they go on to argue over prophecy uttered in ecstasy, over whether the prophetesses Priscilla and Maximilla had any right to speak and to write books in their own name, and over the Montanist confusion of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. The Greek text was first edited by Gerhard Ficker, “Widerlegung eines Montanisten”, Zeitschrift für Kirchengeschichte 26 (1905), pp. 447–63, which is online at the Internet Archive. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 3 July 2010.)
