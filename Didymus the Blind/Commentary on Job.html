Commentary on Job

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A partial English translation by Edward F. Duffy, The Tura Papyrus of Didymus the Blind’s Commentary on Job: An Original Translation with Introduction and Commentary (PhD thesis, Graduate Theological Foundation, Donaldson, Indiana, 2000), made from the critical edition of the Greek text by Ursula and Dieter Hagedorn, Ludwig Koenen and Albert Henrichs, was offered free as a PDF on the Graduate Theological Foundation’s website; that link is now dead, but the PDF is preserved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine. Its licence is not stated. It covers only part of the commentary: Didymus’ prologue and his comments on Job 1:1 to 4:21, with an introduction on his theology and notes. The commentary is one of the works of Didymus the Blind (d. c. 398), the head of the catechetical school of Alexandria, that were recovered in 1941 among the papyrus codices found in a quarry at Tura, near Cairo; it expounds the text of Job verse by verse, often drawing a spiritual sense from the literal one, and in the course of it Didymus discusses the immortality of the soul, the place of the devil and of suffering under God’s providence, and the virtue of Job as the model of the just man tested. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 16 March 2011.)
