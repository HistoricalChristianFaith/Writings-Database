Life of Peter the Iberian

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation of the complete work is by Cornelia B. Horn and Robert R. Phenix Jr., John Rufus: The Lives of Peter the Iberian, Theodosius of Jerusalem, and the Monk Romanus (Writings from the Greco-Roman World 24; Society of Biblical Literature, Atlanta, 2008; ISBN 978-1-58983-200-8; also issued in hardback by Brill, ISBN 978-90-04-14686-0), available from the publisher. The volume gives a new critical edition of the Syriac text with an English translation, an introduction on Palestinian monasticism, the christological controversies of the period and the life and writings of John Rufus, and notes; it also contains his accounts of Theodosius of Jerusalem and of the monk Romanus. Peter was a Georgian prince of the fifth century who left the imperial court at Constantinople to become a monk in Jerusalem, was made bishop of Maiuma near Gaza, and became a leader of the monks who rejected the Council of Chalcedon; he died in 491. His Life was written in Greek by his disciple and friend John Rufus; the Greek is lost, and the work survives in a Syriac translation, first edited by Richard Raabe with a German translation (Petrus der Iberer, Leipzig, 1895). (Noted by Roger Pearse, 10 November 2015.)
