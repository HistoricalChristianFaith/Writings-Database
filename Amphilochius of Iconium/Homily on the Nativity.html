Homily on the Nativity

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. A complete English translation, “On the Nativity of Our Great God and Savior Jesus Christ”, made from Cornelis Datema’s critical edition (Amphilochii Iconiensis Opera, Corpus Christianorum Series Graeca 3, 1978), is part of Beth Elise Dunlop’s doctoral dissertation Earliest Greek Patristic Orations on the Nativity: A Study Including Translations (Boston College, 2004; UMI 3122121), pp. 202–210, with an introductory chapter on the homily at pp. 112–132. The dissertation can be read free online as a scanned PDF from the dec25th.info website (archived copy), which also contains Dunlop’s translations of the Christmas sermons of Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa and John Chrysostom. It is not openly licensed: the dissertation is copyright by the author with all rights reserved, and the site offers it to be read but not copied. Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium from about 373 and a cousin of Gregory of Nazianzus and friend of Basil, preached this sermon for the feast of 25 December, probably some years after the feast had reached Cappadocia in the 380s. It is an exuberant festal encomium: taking Isaiah’s words “the Lord Himself will come and save us” as its theme, it addresses the prophet and sets the expectation of the men of old beside the wonders of the birth itself — the Virgin, the star, the angels’ song to the shepherds and the gifts of the magi. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 25 November 2011.)
