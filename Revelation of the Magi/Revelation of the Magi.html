Revelation of the Magi

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The first English translation, by Brent Landau, is Revelation of the Magi: The Lost Tale of the Wise Men’s Journey to Bethlehem (HarperOne, 2010; ISBN 978-0-06-194703-2), a complete annotated translation from the Syriac with an introduction and commentary written for a general reader; it is available from the publisher. The book is based on Landau’s Harvard Th.D. dissertation, The Sages and the Star-Child: An Introduction to the Revelation of the Magi, An Ancient Christian Apocryphon (2008), which includes his edition of the Syriac text and a more technical annotated translation. The Revelation of the Magi is an apocryphal account of the Nativity told in the voice of the Magi themselves: they are a line of sages descended from Seth, living in a far-eastern land called Shir, who keep Adam’s prophecy of a coming star, and the star that leads them to Bethlehem proves to be Christ himself. Landau dates its earliest form to the late second or early third century, with later additions that bring in the apostle Thomas. The full text survives only in Syriac, as part of the eighth-century Chronicle of Zuqnin (Vatican Syriac 162); a much shorter version of the story is summarised in the Latin Opus Imperfectum in Matthaeum. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 24 November 2010.)
