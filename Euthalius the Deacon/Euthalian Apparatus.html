Euthalian Apparatus

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. An English translation by Vemund Blomkvist, printed with von Soden’s Greek text and a commentary, is Euthalian Traditions: Text, Translation and Commentary (Texte und Untersuchungen zur Geschichte der altchristlichen Literatur 170; De Gruyter, 2012; ISBN 978-3-11-029179-7); it is available from the publisher. The book edits and translates the apparatus as a whole, with Greek and English on facing pages, and studies how its different parts read the apostolic writings. The “Euthalian apparatus” is the body of introductory and reference material that accompanies Acts, the Catholic Letters and the Letters of Paul in many medieval Greek New Testament manuscripts, and that also passed into Syriac, Armenian, Georgian and Slavonic. It is made up of a prologue to each of the three groups of books, which the manuscripts ascribe to a certain Euthalius, called “the deacon” or “bishop of Sulca” (the prologue to Paul’s letters frames a summary of the letters with an account of Paul’s life and death, dating his martyrdom to the thirteenth year of Nero); a division of the text into lections for church reading and into short lines (stichoi) of regular length; lists of chapter headings and of Old Testament quotations; summaries (hypotheses) of each book; a Martyrium Pauli; and, in some manuscripts, a colophon recording that the copy was compared with one in the library of Caesarea written in the hand of Pamphilus. Nothing certain is known of Euthalius, who is later than Eusebius’ Chronicle, to which the prologues refer. The Greek was first edited by L. A. Zacagni in 1698 and is reprinted in the Patrologia Graeca, vol. 85, cols. 627–790. The list of chapter headings for Acts, which the Ante-Nicene Fathers prints under the name of Pamphilus of Caesarea, is in this collection as An Exposition of the Chapters of the Acts of the Apostles. (Noted by Roger Pearse, 24 January 2017, who quotes most of Blomkvist’s version of the prologue to Paul.)
