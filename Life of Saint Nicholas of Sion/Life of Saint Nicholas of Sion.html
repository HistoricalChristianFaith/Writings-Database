Life of Saint Nicholas of Sion

No public-domain English translation of this work is available. The only English translation, by Ihor Ševčenko and Nancy Patterson Ševčenko, is The Life of Saint Nicholas of Sion (The Archbishop Iakovos Library of Ecclesiastical and Historical Sources 10; Brookline, Mass.: Hellenic College Press, 1984; ISBN 978-0-917653-02-5; later reissued by Holy Cross Orthodox Press, ISBN 978-0-917653-03-2), which gives the Greek text (BHG 1347) and the English translation complete on facing pages; a copy may be borrowed from the Internet Archive’s lending library. The Life, written by an anonymous author, probably a monk of his monastery, not long after the saint’s death, tells of Nicholas of Sion, a native of the village of Pharroa in Lycia, who became abbot of the monastery of Holy Sion in the hills near Myra and died in 564, shortly after being made bishop of Pinara. It recounts his pilgrimages to the Holy Land, on one of which he calms a storm at sea and raises a sailor who has fallen from the mast, his felling of a tree held sacred by the local people, his healings and exorcisms in the villages of Lycia, and the coming of the great plague of Justinian’s reign. It is a rare picture of rural life in sixth-century Asia Minor, and an early witness to the cult of St Nicholas of Myra; several of its episodes were later transferred to the Lives of that more famous namesake.
